Q1Meaning and effect of phrases · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read the passage below.The bazaar woke slowly that Friday. By nine, though, it had become a living river of people, and Ayesha, clutching her mother's list, was swept along with it. Stall-holders barked out prices over one another. Bolts of silk spilled their colours across wooden counters, and the air was thick with cardamom, frying dough and diesel from the delivery rickshaws that nosed their way through the crowd like impatient goats.At the spice stall, old Karim sat as still as a stone in the middle of all the movement. He weighed out turmeric with the slow care of a jeweller, tipping the tiny brass scale until it settled exactly level. When Ayesha handed him the list, he peered at it over his glasses and smiled, as if it were a letter from an old friend. 'Your mother still buys the best saffron,' he said. 'Tell her the red chillies are good this week, but the cumin is not what it was.' Ayesha nodded seriously, as though she had been trusted with a secret.

  1. (a) Explain the meaning and effect of the phrase 'a living river of people'. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the meaning and effect of the phrase 'as still as a stone in the middle of all the movement'. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Meaning: a huge crowd moving continuously through the bazaar. Effect: the metaphor suggests the crowd flows constantly and powerfully, as if it has a will of its own; Ayesha is helpless in it ('swept along'); creates a sense of energy and bustle.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
  2. (b) Meaning: Karim does not move at all while everything around him is busy. Effect: the simile contrasts his calm with the chaos of the market, making him seem solid, dependable, timeless, perhaps wise; he becomes the fixed centre of the scene.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
Q2Meaning and effect of phrases · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read the passage below.Grandmother's kitchen was the warmest room in the house, and not only because of the stove. On winter evenings we gathered there like moths around a lamp, drawn by the smell of onions turning golden in ghee and the comfortable rattle of her bangles as she worked.She never used a recipe. She measured salt in the hollow of her palm and judged the heat of a pan by holding her hand above it, the way a doctor feels for a fever. When she rolled out rotis, the dough obeyed her completely, spreading into perfect circles under the quick, sure strokes of her rolling pin. My own attempts looked like maps of countries that did not exist.She would laugh, take the rolling pin gently from my hand and say, 'Again.' I must have rolled a hundred bad rotis in that kitchen before I made a good one. She ate every single one of the bad ones without complaint, as though each of them were a small gift, and told my mother I was a natural.

  1. (a) Explain the meaning and effect of 'we gathered there like moths around a lamp'. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the meaning and effect of 'My own attempts looked like maps of countries that did not exist.' [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Meaning: the family were naturally drawn into the kitchen and crowded round. Effect: the simile suggests an irresistible attraction to warmth, light and comfort; makes the kitchen and grandmother seem the glowing heart of the home.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
  2. (b) Meaning: the narrator's rotis were irregular, strange shapes rather than circles. Effect: humorous, self-mocking image; contrasts the narrator's clumsiness with the grandmother's skill and so emphasises her expertise; affectionate tone.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
Q3Meaning and effect of phrases · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read the passage below.The flood came at night. By the time Jamal's father hammered on the door, the water had already crept over the courtyard step and was licking at the legs of the charpai. Outside, the canal that had dawdled lazily past their fields all summer had become a brown, muscular thing, shouldering aside fences and swallowing the lane whole.They carried what they could to the roof: the goat, a sack of flour, the tin trunk with the family papers. From up there, under a sky that seemed to sag with rain, Jamal watched the village shrink into a scattering of islands. Somewhere a dog was barking without hope. His mother sat with her arms around his little sister, saying nothing, her eyes fixed on the water as though she could hold it back by staring. At dawn the rain finally stopped. A boat appeared between the trees, rowed by two men from the next village, and Jamal's father stood up and waved both arms until they saw him.

  1. (a) Explain the meaning and effect of 'a brown, muscular thing, shouldering aside fences'. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the meaning and effect of 'as though she could hold it back by staring'. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Meaning: the canal has turned into a fast, powerful flood that pushes over fences. Effect: personification makes the water seem like a strong, aggressive creature or bully; 'thing' makes it unnatural and frightening; contrasts with its earlier lazy movement to show how threatening it has become.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
  2. (b) Meaning: she watches the water fixedly and without moving. Effect: suggests her desperation and helplessness; she has no real power over the flood, so the image is almost pitiful; conveys her determination to protect her children and her silent fear.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.

Q6Meaning and effect of phrases · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Read the passage below.The stadium was a bowl of noise. Hassan stood at the end of his run-up, ball in hand, and tried to hear only his own breathing. He wiped the ball on his trousers and took a long breath.Hassan began to run. The crowd seemed to lean forward as one creature. His feet found their rhythm on the dry grass, and for a moment the world narrowed to a strip of earth, twenty-two yards long, with a single figure waiting at the end of it. He released the ball. It left his hand clean and angry, swung late and crashed into the off stump with a sound like a door slamming on the whole afternoon.For a heartbeat there was nothing. Then the bowl of noise overflowed.

  1. (a) Explain the meaning and effect of 'the world narrowed to a strip of earth, twenty-two yards long'. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the meaning and effect of 'a sound like a door slamming on the whole afternoon'. [3]

Show answer
  1. (a) Meaning: Hassan concentrated entirely on the pitch and the batsman, ignoring everything else. Effect: conveys intense focus under pressure, like a camera zooming in; shuts out the noisy crowd; creates suspense before the delivery.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
  2. (b) Meaning: the ball hit the stump with a loud crack that took the last wicket and ended the match. Effect: the simile suggests finality, that the contest is over instantly and decisively; the sharp sound contrasts with the tension; implies the opponents' hopes are shut out.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
  3. (c) It frames the passage and shows a change: the crowd was already loud but now, after the wicket, the noise becomes uncontrollable/too great to contain (1); the image of overflowing suggests an explosion of joy and release of tension (1).

  2. (b) It conveys an overwhelming, powerful, booming applause, showing how deeply the audience was moved and how completely their attitude changed.

  3. (c) It suggests the audience was fidgeting, bored or impatient and not paying attention to Asad (not just making sound), which makes their later stillness show how completely he won them over.
