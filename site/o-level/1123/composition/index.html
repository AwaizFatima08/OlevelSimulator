Q1Descriptive (scene, place or event) · Core · Paper 2 style · 25 marks

Composition title: Describe the scene at a busy railway station in a large city just before dawn and then at the height of the morning rush hour. (350–450 words)

  1. (a) Write your composition on the title above. Write 350 to 450 words. [25]
Show answer
  1. (a) Structure: 1) the empty, quiet station before dawn; 2) first signs of life (stalls opening, first passengers); 3) the rush hour at its height; 4) a closing image that links back to the opening (e.g. the pigeons now scattered by the noise).Planning ideas: Before dawn (any three): a sweeper pushing a broom along an empty platform; a single tea stall hissing as the kettle boils; the hum of strip lights; a guard yawning; pigeons on the rafters; cold air and the smell of diesel. Rush hour (any three): crowds pressing towards the doors; porters in red shirts shouting; announcements echoing; the smell of fried snacks and sweat; trains screeching in; vendors calling prices.Example paragraph: 'At four o'clock the station belongs to the pigeons. They shuffle along the iron beams above platform three while, below them, a lone sweeper drags his broom in slow arcs, raising small clouds of dust that glitter under the strip lights. The only sound, apart from the buzz of the lamps, is the tea-seller's kettle beginning its thin whistle. The air smells of cold metal and yesterday's diesel.'
    Content and structure (10 marks): 9–10 = a clear structure that moves through time or space; the two settings/moments are clearly contrasted; precise, focused detail rather than a list; a sense of atmosphere that changes; 7–8 = relevant and well organised, with mostly precise detail and some development; 5–6 = relevant but uneven: some detail is general or listed, structure is simple; 3–4 = thin or partly off the task, weak structure; 1–2 = little relevant content.Style and accuracy (15 marks): 13–15 = wide, precise vocabulary; varied sentence lengths and openings used for effect; imagery used sparingly and freshly (not clichés); accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar; present or past tense kept consistent; 10–12 = fluent and mostly accurate, some range of vocabulary and sentence types; 7–9 = generally clear, plain vocabulary, errors that do not obscure meaning; 4–6 = frequent errors, limited vocabulary and sentence control; 1–3 = errors often obscure meaning.Avoid turning the description into a story with a plot.

Composition title: Describe the scene in an examination hall in the ten minutes before an important exam begins and during the final ten minutes of the exam. (350–450 words)

  1. (a) Write your composition on the title above. Write 350 to 450 words. [25]
Show answer
  1. (a) Structure in time: entering the hall; the paper handed out; (brief link through the middle of the exam); the final ten minutes; time called. The words fall into the silence like a stone into a well. Pens freeze in mid-air. Somewhere behind me a chair creaks, and a long breath escapes from a hundred chests at once.'
