  4. (d) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'emerged' as it is used in paragraph 2?A shoutedB came outC hidD ran away [1]
  5. (e) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'swiftly' as it is used in paragraph 3?A slowlyB gentlyC quicklyD strangely [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B could no longer be seen
    The driver had to steer by distant dunes because the track could not be seen any more. A and D describe a track that still exists; 'vanished' means it had disappeared from view.
  2. (b) A extreme
    The metal door burned Asad's arm, so the heat was extreme. B contradicts the burning; C and D are not meanings of 'intense'.
  3. (c) B falling apart
    Crumbling walls are slowly breaking into pieces – the fort is ancient. D is tempting because the walls are 'still enormous', but that describes size, not condition.
  4. (d) B came out
    The guard came out of a small gate. C is the opposite; A and D do not fit 'from a small gate'.
  5. (e) C quickly
    The air cooled so quickly that Asad needed his jacket at once. A is the opposite; B ('gently') does not explain his sudden need for a jacket.
Q26Vocabulary in context (MCQ) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Read Text A, then answer the questions. Paragraph numbers are shown in square brackets.Text A: The bakery at dawn[1] Long before the call to the dawn prayer, the bakery on Qissa Khwani Bazaar in Peshawar was ablaze with light. Inside, Gul Khan slapped rounds of dough against the inner wall of the tandoor with a speed that seemed almost careless, though he never burned his hand.[2] His apprentice, a slight boy of twelve, struggled to keep pace, kneading the dough in a huge tin bowl. Gul Khan rarely praised him, but occasionally, when a batch came out perfectly, he would grunt in a way the boy had learned to interpret as approval.[3] By sunrise a queue stretched along the street: schoolchildren, labourers and old men who had bought their bread here for half a century. The fragrance of fresh naan drifted across the bazaar, and even the stray dogs sat patiently, waiting for the inevitable scraps.[4] When the last naan had been sold, Gul Khan wiped his hands on his apron, poured two cups of green tea and handed one to his apprentice without a word. The boy understood that this, too, was a kind of praise.

  1. (a) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'ablaze' as it is used in paragraph 1?A on fireB brightly litC very hotD crowded [1]
  2. (b) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'slight' as it is used in paragraph 2?A unimportantB thin and smallC nervousD young [1]
  3. (c) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'interpret' as it is used in paragraph 2?A translateB ignoreC understandD repeat [1]
  4. (d) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'fragrance' as it is used in paragraph 3?A tasteB steamC pleasant smellD warmth [1]
  5. (e) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'inevitable' as it is used in paragraph 3?A unexpectedB certain to comeC deliciousD small [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B brightly lit
    The bakery was 'ablaze with light', so it was brightly lit. A is the literal meaning but the bakery was not burning; C is tempting because of the tandoor, but the phrase is about light.
  2. (b) B thin and small
    A 'slight boy' is thin and small in build. A is another meaning of 'slight' (a slight problem) that does not fit a person; D is true of a twelve-year-old but is not what the word means.
  3. (c) C understand
    The boy had learned to understand the grunt as approval. A is the language sense of 'interpret' and does not fit a grunt; B and D do not fit 'as approval'.
  4. (d) C pleasant smell
    The fragrance of fresh naan drifted across the bazaar – a pleasant smell. B could drift, but 'fragrance' refers to smell, not vapour.
  5. (e) B certain to come
    The dogs waited patiently because scraps were certain to come. A is the opposite; their patience shows they knew the scraps would arrive.
Q27Vocabulary in context (MCQ) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Read Text A, then answer the questions. Paragraph numbers are shown in square brackets.Text A: Below the surface[1] Lina had dived many times off the coast of Aqaba, but she had never seen the reef so vivid. Shoals of tiny orange fish darted between branches of coral, and a turtle drifted past, utterly indifferent to the divers who hovered nearby.[2] At twenty metres she noticed that her diving partner, Omar, was gesturing urgently. He pointed to his gauge: the needle showed his air was running low far sooner than expected. Lina signalled calmly, and they began their ascent together, rising slowly and stopping as they had been trained.[3] Back on the boat, Omar admitted that he had neglected to check his tank before the dive. The instructor's reprimand was brief but sharp, and Omar did not argue.[4] Later, as the boat headed back to the harbour, Omar sat quietly at the stern. Lina handed him a bottle of water and told him that every diver makes a mistake once; the important thing was never to make the same one twice.

  1. (a) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'vivid' as it is used in paragraph 1?A quietB brightly colouredC damagedD deep [1]
  2. (b) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'indifferent' as it is used in paragraph 1?A unfriendlyB unconcernedC frightenedD different [1]
  3. (c) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'ascent' as it is used in paragraph 2?A agreementB journey upwardsC dive downwardsD escape [1]
  4. (d) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'neglected' as it is used in paragraph 3?A refusedB failedC rememberedD tried [1]
  5. (e) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'reprimand' as it is used in paragraph 3?A punishmentB praiseC telling-offD question [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B brightly coloured
    The orange fish and coral show that the reef looked brightly coloured. C is the opposite of the description; A and D are not meanings of 'vivid'.
  2. (b) B unconcerned
    The turtle drifted past paying no attention to the divers – it was unconcerned. A suggests hostility, which the turtle did not show; D is a confusion with a similar-looking word.
  3. (c) B journey upwards
    They began rising slowly to the surface. A confuses 'ascent' with 'assent'; C is the opposite (descent).
  4. (d) B failed
    Omar failed to (did not) check his tank. A suggests he chose not to, which the text does not say; C is the opposite.
  5. (e) C telling-off
    The instructor's words were 'brief but sharp' – a telling-off. A is too strong: no penalty is mentioned, only sharp words; B is the opposite.
Q28Vocabulary in context (MCQ) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Read Text A, then answer the questions. Paragraph numbers are shown in square brackets.Text A: The final game[1] The hall was hushed. At the last table of the junior chess championship in Kuala Lumpur, Arif sat opposite the defending champion, a composed girl from Singapore who had not lost a game all week. Her moves were deliberate, each one placed on the board with a small, decisive click.[2] For two hours the position was balanced. Then, on move forty, Arif saw a chance: if he sacrificed his knight, her king would be exposed. It was a gamble; if he had miscalculated, he would lose. His hand hovered over the piece. He hesitated, checked the line once more, and made the move.[3] The champion studied the board for a long time. At last she extended her hand across the table – a gesture of resignation – and the spectators, who had been told to remain silent, broke into spontaneous applause.[4] Arif shook the champion's hand. Only afterwards, when his coach hugged him in the corridor, did he notice that his own hands were shaking.

  1. (a) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'composed' as it is used in paragraph 1?A writtenB calm and self-controlledC proudD made up of parts [1]
  2. (b) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'deliberate' as it is used in paragraph 1?A careful and intentionalB quickC discussed at lengthD aggressive [1]
  3. (c) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'exposed' as it is used in paragraph 2?A shown to everyone watchingB open to attackC trapped in a cornerD removed from the board [1]
  4. (d) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'resignation' as it is used in paragraph 3?A leaving her jobB admitting defeatC sadnessD politeness [1]
  5. (e) Which of the following is closest in meaning to 'spontaneous' as it is used in paragraph 3?A unplannedB loudC politeD long [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B calm and self-controlled
    The champion had not lost all week and placed her pieces with decisive clicks – she was calm and in control. A and D are other meanings of 'composed' that do not describe a person; C is not supported by the text.
  2. (b) A careful and intentional
    Each move was placed carefully and on purpose. C is the verb sense ('to deliberate'), which does not fit 'her moves were'; B contradicts the careful placing.
  3. (c) B open to attack
    Sacrificing the knight would leave her king without protection, open to attack. A is a general meaning of 'exposed' that misses the danger; C describes a different situation – a king can be exposed without being cornered.
  4. (d) B admitting defeat
    In chess, offering a hand at this point is a sign of conceding the game. A is the workplace meaning; D is tempting because offering a hand is polite, but the gesture here means she accepted defeat.
  5. (e) A unplanned
    The spectators had been told to stay silent, so their applause was unplanned and unprompted. B may also be true of applause, but it is not the meaning of 'spontaneous'.
Q29Finding a word or phrase · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read Text A, then answer the questions. Paragraph numbers are shown in square brackets.Text A: Three days before the wedding[1] Three days before her cousin's wedding, the courtyard of Grandfather's house in Sialkot had been transformed. Strings of tiny lights zigzagged between the walls, and marigold garlands hung from every window. Aunts arrived by the hour, each carrying a suitcase and an opinion.[2] In the kitchen, a hired cook stirred a vast pot of biryani with a paddle the size of an oar, while three cousins argued over the order of the dances. Somebody's phone played the same song again and again.[3] Amid all the commotion, only Grandfather seemed untroubled. He sat on his charpai in the corner, sipping tea and greeting each new arrival with the same slow smile, as if weddings happened every week.[4] By the evening of the first day, the cousins had finally agreed on the dances, the cook had produced enough biryani to feed the whole street, and the lights were switched on for the first time. The courtyard glowed like a lantern, and even the most critical aunt was heard to say that it was 'not bad at all'.

  1. (a) Paragraph 1: Identify a single word that shows the courtyard had changed completely. [1]
  2. (b) Paragraph 1: Which phrase suggests, with humour, that every aunt was ready to give advice? [1]
  3. (c) Paragraph 2: Identify a phrase that emphasises how enormous the cook's spoon was. [1]
  4. (d) Paragraph 3: Identify a single word that sums up the noise and activity in the house. [1]
  5. (e) Paragraph 3: Which phrase shows that Grandfather was completely calm about the wedding? Explain your choice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Transformed.
    1 mark. Only this word.
  2. (b) '(each carrying a suitcase and) an opinion'.
    1 mark. Accept 'each carrying a suitcase and an opinion'. Must include 'an opinion'.
  3. (c) 'a paddle the size of an oar'.
    1 mark. Accept 'the size of an oar'.
  4. (d) Commotion.
    1 mark. Only this word.
  5. (e) 'as if weddings happened every week' (or 'untroubled'); it suggests he treated the great event as ordinary and was not excited or anxious.
    1 mark for a correct phrase ('as if weddings happened every week' or 'seemed untroubled'); 1 mark for the explanation.
Q30Finding a word or phrase · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read Text A, then answer the questions. Paragraph numbers are shown in square brackets.Text A: Fog in the channel[1] By midnight the fog had swallowed the ferry completely. From the bridge, Captain Ruiz could not see the bow of his own ship, only a wall of grey that pressed against the windows. Every two minutes the foghorn bellowed, and every two minutes the fog seemed to absorb the sound and give nothing back.[2] The young officer beside him, on his first crossing, kept his eyes fixed on the radar screen, where a cluster of green dots crept slowly towards the centre. His knuckles were white on the edge of the console.[3] 'Fishing boats,' said the captain, in the unhurried voice of a man ordering breakfast. 'They'll move.' He reduced speed until the ship was barely crawling and gave a small order to the helmsman.[4] Ten minutes later the dots had slid harmlessly past on either side. Only then did the young officer realise he had been holding his breath.

  1. (a) Paragraph 1: Identify a single word that suggests the fog was like a creature devouring the ship. [1]
  2. (b) Paragraph 1: Which phrase shows that the fog seemed to deaden the noise of the foghorn? [1]
  3. (c) Paragraph 2: Identify a phrase that shows the young officer's tension. [1]
  4. (d) Paragraph 3: Identify the phrase that shows how calm the captain was, and explain why it is effective. [2]
  5. (e) Paragraph 4: Which phrase shows that the danger passed without any damage? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Swallowed.
    1 mark. Only this word.
  2. (b) 'the fog seemed to absorb the sound and give nothing back'.
    1 mark. Accept 'absorb the sound' or 'give nothing back'.
  3. (c) 'His knuckles were white on the edge of the console'.
    1 mark. Accept 'knuckles were white'. Also accept 'kept his eyes fixed on the radar screen' only with 'fixed'.
  4. (d) 'in the unhurried voice of a man ordering breakfast'; comparing a dangerous moment to an everyday, trivial action shows he was completely relaxed / unworried.
    1 mark for the phrase; 1 mark for explaining the contrast between danger and an ordinary routine task.
  5. (e) 'slid harmlessly past'.
    1 mark. Must include 'harmlessly'.
