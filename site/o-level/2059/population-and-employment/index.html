Q1Population growth and structure · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by the term death rate? [1]
  2. (b) A district has a birth rate of 27 per 1,000 and a death rate of 7 per 1,000. Calculate its rate of natural increase as a percentage. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why death rates in Pakistan have fallen since 1950. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The number of deaths in a year for every 1,000 people in the population.
    B1 deaths per 1,000 per year.
  2. (b) (27 − 7) ÷ 10 = 2% per year
    M1 27 − 7 = 20 per 1,000; A1 2%.
  3. (c) Vaccination programmes against diseases such as measles, tuberculosis and polio mean fewer children die young; more hospitals, basic health units and Lady Health Workers give people access to treatment; cleaner drinking water and better sanitation reduce diseases such as cholera and diarrhoea; improved food supply since the Green Revolution reduces malnutrition.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: vaccination; medical facilities and medicines; clean water and sanitation; better nutrition; health education.
Q2Population growth and structure · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State what happens to birth rates and death rates in Stage 2 of the demographic transition model. [2]
  2. (b) Pakistan is often described as being between Stage 2 and Stage 3 of the model. Explain why its birth rate is now falling slowly. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Birth rates stay high; death rates fall rapidly, so the population grows quickly.
    B1 birth rate high; B1 death rate falls rapidly.
  2. (b) More girls are going to school and women who are educated tend to marry later and have fewer children; family planning services and contraception are more widely available, especially in cities; as child death rates fall, parents no longer need many children to make sure some survive; in towns children are costly to raise and educate rather than helping on the farm.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: female education / later marriage; family planning; lower infant mortality; urbanisation / cost of children; women working outside the home.
Q3Population growth and structure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Approximate population (millions) of a province by age group:0–14: 8.615–64: 13.165+: 0.9

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the province's population aged 0–14. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) The dependency ratio = (population aged 0–14 + population aged 65+) ÷ population aged 15–64 × 100. Calculate the dependency ratio. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Describe the shape of the population pyramid you would expect for this province. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.6 ÷ 22.6 × 100 = 38.1%
    M1 8.6/22.6; A1 38.1%.
  2. (b) (8.6 + 0.9) ÷ 13.1 × 100 = 72.5
    M1 9.5/13.1 × 100; A1 72.5.
  3. (c) A wide base because of the large number of children, narrowing steadily upwards (triangular / concave sides), with a very narrow top because few people live beyond 65.
    B1 wide base; B1 narrow top / tapering shape.
Q4Population growth and structure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Population of Pakistan at four censuses (approximate, millions):1981: 84.31998: 132.42017: 207.72023: 241.5

  1. (a) Calculate the increase in population between 1998 and 2023. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage increase in population between 1998 and 2023. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why Pakistan's population has continued to grow rapidly. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 241.5 − 132.4 = 109.1 million
    B1 109.1 million.
  2. (b) 109.1 ÷ 132.4 × 100 = 82.4%
    M1 109.1/132.4 × 100; A1 82.4%.
  3. (c) Birth rates remain high, especially in rural areas, because many families want sons to work on the land and support parents in old age; early marriage gives women a long child-bearing period; death rates have fallen because of better health care, so natural increase is high; a very young population means many people are entering child-bearing age (population momentum); use of contraception remains low.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: high birth rate (rural, sons, old-age security, religious or cultural beliefs); early marriage; falling death rate; young population / momentum; low contraceptive use; limited female education.
Q5Population growth and structure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe two measures the government has used to reduce the birth rate in Pakistan. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why birth rates are higher in rural areas than in cities in Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Family planning centres and free or cheap contraceptives; Lady Health Workers visiting homes to advise families; media campaigns on small families; encouraging girls' education; involving religious leaders to support birth spacing.
    1 mark each for two measures.
  2. (b) Children help with farm work and looking after animals from a young age, so large families are seen as an economic benefit; rural women have lower literacy and marry younger, so they have more children; family planning services and clinics are fewer and further away; infant mortality is higher, so parents have more children to make sure some survive; traditional and religious attitudes against contraception are stronger.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: children as farm labour; old-age security; low female literacy / early marriage; poor access to family planning; higher infant mortality; traditions.
Q6Population growth and structure · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 11 marks
  1. (a) A useful estimate of the time a population takes to double is 70 ÷ (annual growth rate in %). Pakistan's population grew at about 2.5% a year between 2017 and 2023. Estimate how many years it would take to double at this rate. [1]
  2. (b) Explain two problems that rapid population growth causes for the government of Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) 'Pakistan's large and young population is an asset rather than a problem.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 70 ÷ 2.5 = 28 years
    B1 28 years.
  2. (b) It must provide more schools, teachers and hospitals every year just to keep up, so quality falls and class sizes rise; millions of young people enter the labour market each year, and not enough jobs are created, so unemployment and underemployment rise; demand for food, water and housing rises, putting pressure on irrigation water and causing growth of katchi abadis.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: services (education, health); jobs; food and water; housing; budget pressure / debt.
  3. (c) Asset: a large young workforce can raise production and attract investment if it is educated and skilled (demographic dividend); a big home market helps industries such as textiles and food; young workers abroad send remittances; young people quickly adopt technology, supporting the IT sector and freelancing. Problem: many young people lack education and skills, so they cannot find good jobs; high dependency ratio strains family incomes; pressure on schools, hospitals, water and housing; unemployment can lead to social unrest. Judgement: e.g. it can be an asset only if the government invests in education, skills and job creation.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.

Q9Employment and unemployment · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by seasonal unemployment? [1]
  2. (b) Give two examples of seasonal work in Pakistan. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two ways in which rural families in Pakistan cope with seasonal unemployment. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Unemployment that happens at certain times of the year when work is not available, e.g. between farming seasons.
    B1.
  2. (b) Any two: cotton picking (autumn); wheat harvesting (April–May); sugar cane cutting and crushing (winter); work in hill tourist resorts in summer; fishing seasons.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) Family members migrate temporarily to cities to work as labourers, sending money home; they take up cottage industries such as weaving, embroidery or pottery during slack seasons; they keep livestock, which provides milk and income all year; they borrow from landlords or shopkeepers until the next harvest, which can lead to debt.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: temporary migration; cottage industries; livestock; borrowing / debt; working in the informal sector.

Q11Employment and unemployment · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by underemployment? [1]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why many educated young people in Pakistan's cities are unemployed. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) Suggest one way in which the government could reduce unemployment among young people. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) When people work fewer hours than they want, or in jobs that do not use their skills and qualifications.
    B1.
  2. (b) The number of graduates is growing faster than the number of new jobs, because economic growth has been slow; many degrees do not match the skills employers need, such as technical and IT skills, so graduates cannot find suitable work; job seekers often prefer government or office jobs and wait for them rather than take lower-status work; recruitment can depend on contacts, so those without connections struggle.

  3. (c) Technical and vocational training (e.g. TEVTA courses) linked to industry needs; IT and freelancing training; cheap loans for young entrepreneurs; attract investment to SEZs.
    B1 any valid suggestion.
Q12Employment and unemployment · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain two reasons why a smaller proportion of women than men work outside the home in Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) Explain two benefits to Pakistan of increasing the number of women in paid work. You should develop your answer. [4]

  1. (a) Cultural traditions in many areas expect women to look after the home and children, so families may not allow them to take paid work; lower female literacy and fewer technical skills limit the jobs available; lack of safe, affordable transport and fear of harassment make travelling to work difficult; few workplaces offer day-care or flexible hours.

  2. (b) It would add more workers and skills to the economy, raising production and national income; women earning money increase family incomes, which can pay for children's education and health; working women tend to marry later and have fewer children, reducing population growth; industries such as garments and IT gain skilled workers.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: bigger workforce / higher GDP; family income and child welfare; lower birth rate; skills for industries; women's independence and status.

Q18Migration · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by seasonal migration? [1]
  2. (b) Describe the seasonal movement of herders and their animals in the northern mountains (transhumance). [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why families from the Thar Desert migrate for part of the year. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Moving away from home for part of the year and returning, e.g. for harvest work or to find grazing.
    B1.
  2. (b) In summer herders take sheep, goats and cattle up to high mountain pastures as the snow melts; in autumn they bring them down to the valleys or plains, where it is warmer and fodder is stored for winter.
    B1 upward in summer to high pastures; B1 downward in autumn/winter.
  3. (c) When the monsoon fails, wells and ponds dry up and grazing disappears, so families move with their animals to the irrigated barrage areas where water and fodder are available; there is little paid work in the desert, so they move to work as labourers at harvest time (e.g. wheat, cotton, sugar cane) in irrigated districts; drought causes food shortages, so they move to be near markets and relief.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: drought / lack of water; grazing for livestock; harvest work in irrigated areas; food shortage; return after rains.
Q19Migration · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 11 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by 'brain drain'? [1]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why many skilled Pakistanis emigrate to countries such as the UK, Canada and the Gulf states. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) 'Emigration of skilled workers does more harm than good to Pakistan.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) The emigration of highly educated and skilled people (e.g. doctors, engineers, IT experts) to other countries.
    B1.
  2. (b) Salaries for doctors, engineers and IT specialists are many times higher abroad, so they can support their families better; there are more opportunities for training, research and promotion; economic and political uncertainty, inflation and power cuts at home push people out; relatives already abroad help them settle and find work.

(c) Harm: Pakistan loses doctors, nurses and engineers that cost the country a lot to train, so hospitals and universities are short of staff; it reduces the skills needed for development and new businesses; families are separated. Good: emigrants send large remittances (Pakistan receives tens of billions of US dollars a year), which support the balance of payments and families; some return with new skills, savings and contacts and start businesses; it reduces unemployment among graduates; the diaspora invests in property and projects in Pakistan. Judgement: e.g. it does more good while remittances are high, but Pakistan should encourage return and train more professionals.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.

Q21Urbanisation and living conditions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe the living conditions in a typical katchi abadi. [3]
  2. (b) Explain two ways in which living conditions in katchi abadis can be improved. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer

Q22Urbanisation and living conditions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

The population of a large city in Pakistan grew from about 7.1 million to about 13.0 million in 20 years.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in the city's population over the 20 years. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the average number of people added to the city each year. [1]
  3. (c) Explain two problems this growth causes for public transport in the city. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (13.0 − 7.1) ÷ 7.1 × 100 = 83.1%
    M1 5.9/7.1 × 100; A1 83.1%.
  2. (b) 5.9 million ÷ 20 = about 295,000 people a year
    B1 295,000.
  3. (c) The number of commuters rises faster than buses and roads are added, so buses and vans are overcrowded and unsafe; more private cars and motorbikes cause traffic jams and long journey times, raising fuel use and air pollution; building mass transit systems is very expensive and takes years, so services lag behind demand.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: overcrowded buses; congestion; pollution; cost and time of new systems; long commutes from outer settlements.
Q23Urbanisation and living conditions · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Explain two reasons why the population of large cities in Pakistan is growing faster than the national population. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'The best way to reduce the problems of Pakistan's large cities is to develop rural areas.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cities grow from both natural increase and rural–urban migration, as people move in for jobs, education and health care; many migrants are young adults, so they add to births in the city; city boundaries expand and absorb surrounding villages; people displaced by floods or conflict often move to cities.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: rural–urban migration (pull factors); young migrants raise births; boundary expansion; displacement by disasters/conflict.
  2. (b) For: if villages had more jobs (agro-based industry, small businesses), schools, clinics and electricity, fewer people would migrate, reducing pressure on city housing, transport and water; developing small towns spreads growth. Against: cities need direct investment anyway – mass transit (e.g. Lahore Orange Line, Karachi Green Line BRT), water schemes, sewerage and low-cost housing; rural development is slow and expensive and people may still be attracted by city wages; urban planning and regularisation of katchi abadis help those already in cities. Judgement: e.g. both are needed – rural development slows migration, but city infrastructure must still improve.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.
Q24Population distribution and density · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Approximate figures from the 2023 census:Punjab: population 127.7 million, area 205,344 km²Balochistan: population 14.9 million, area 347,190 km²

  1. (a) What is meant by population density? [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the population density of each province to the nearest whole number. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why Balochistan has a much lower population density than Punjab. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The average number of people living in each square kilometre of an area.
    B1.
  2. (b) Punjab: 127.7 million ÷ 205,344 km² ≈ 622 people per km²; Balochistan: 14.9 million ÷ 347,190 km² ≈ 43 people per km²
    B1 Punjab 622; B1 Balochistan 43.
  3. (c) Balochistan is mostly mountains, plateaus and desert, so there is little flat land for farming or building; rainfall is very low and there are few permanent rivers, so irrigation is limited and few crops can be grown; there are fewer industries and jobs, so people move away; poor roads and services make settlement difficult. Punjab has fertile alluvial plains watered by the Indus and its tributaries and a dense canal network.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: relief (mountains/plateau); arid climate / lack of water; little irrigation; few jobs / industry; poor communications; contrast with fertile, irrigated Punjab plains.
Q25Population distribution and density · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by population distribution? [1]
  2. (b) Describe the distribution of population in Pakistan. [3]
  3. (c) Explain two physical factors that encourage high population densities in parts of Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The way in which people are spread out across an area.
    B1.
  2. (b) Most people live in the Indus plain, especially in central and northern Punjab and along the Indus in Sindh; large concentrations in cities such as Karachi, Lahore, Faisalabad and Rawalpindi–Islamabad; the Peshawar valley is densely populated; the western plateaus of Balochistan, the deserts (Thar, Cholistan, Kharan) and the high northern mountains are thinly populated.
    1 mark per point up to 3; must include both dense and sparse areas for full marks.
  3. (c) Flat land in the Indus plain makes farming, building and transport easy; fertile alluvial soils deposited by rivers give high crop yields; reliable water from rivers and canals allows irrigation; a moderate climate with monsoon rain in northern Punjab supports farming.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: flat relief; fertile soil; water supply / irrigation; favourable climate; coastal access (Karachi).
Q26Population distribution and density · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Population and area of two districts (hypothetical figures):District A: population 2.3 million, area 3,410 km²District B: population 0.25 million, area 18,000 km²

  1. (a) Calculate the population density of each district to the nearest whole number. [2]
  2. (b) District A is in the canal-irrigated plain of central Punjab and District B is in the Cholistan Desert. Explain two reasons for the difference in density. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) District A: 2.3 million ÷ 3,410 km² ≈ 674 people per km²; District B: 0.25 million ÷ 18,000 km² ≈ 14 people per km²
    B1 A 674; B1 B 14.
  2. (b) District A has perennial canal water, so crops such as wheat, cotton and sugar cane can be grown all year and support many people, while District B has very low rainfall and almost no surface water, so only nomadic herding is possible; District A has towns, roads, industries and services offering jobs; District B has sandy soils and extreme temperatures, making settlement difficult.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: irrigation vs no water; fertile soils vs sand; jobs / industry / towns; transport; climate extremes in desert.
Q27Population distribution and density · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A mountain valley in Swat has an area of 340 km² of usable land and a population of 156,400. Calculate its population density. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why some valleys in the northern mountains have high population densities, even though mountain areas overall are sparsely populated. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) Suggest one problem caused by high densities in such valleys. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 156,400 ÷ 340 = 460 people per km²
    B1 460.
  2. (b) Valley floors are flatter and have fertile alluvial soil, so terraced and irrigated farming is possible; rivers and glacier-fed streams give a reliable water supply; roads and settlements follow the valleys, so trade, services and tourism are concentrated there; the surrounding steep slopes are unsuitable, so people crowd into the small usable area.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: flat valley floors; fertile soils; water supply; routes / roads along valleys; tourism and services; people concentrated in limited usable land.
  3. (c) Building on floodplains increases flood risk; pressure on forests for fuel and timber; small farm sizes.
    B1.
Q28Literacy, health and development · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) In a district, 4.6 million of the 7.2 million people aged 10 and over can read and write. Calculate the literacy rate. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why female literacy is lower than male literacy in many rural areas of Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.6 ÷ 7.2 × 100 = 63.9%
    M1 4.6/7.2 × 100; A1 63.9%.
  2. (b) There are fewer girls' schools in villages and they may be far away, so parents do not allow girls to travel for safety reasons; many families value boys' education more because sons are expected to earn; girls are kept at home to help with housework, fetching water or looking after siblings; early marriage ends girls' education; there is a shortage of female teachers.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: few or distant girls' schools; safety and travel; family preference for boys; domestic work; early marriage; lack of female teachers; poverty.
Q29Literacy, health and development · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Health data for two districts (hypothetical):Rural district: population 1,250,000, doctors 410City district: population 3,600,000, doctors 3,900

  1. (a) Calculate the number of people per doctor in each district, to the nearest whole number. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways in which health care in rural areas of Pakistan has been improved. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rural district: 3,049; city district: 923
    B1 3,049; B1 923.
  2. (b) Lady Health Workers visit homes to give advice on family planning, mother and child care and vaccination, reaching women who cannot travel; Basic Health Units and Rural Health Centres provide first-level treatment closer to villages; the Expanded Programme on Immunisation and polio campaigns vaccinate children door to door; health insurance schemes (e.g. Sehat Card) help poor families pay for hospital treatment.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: Lady Health Workers; BHUs / RHCs; vaccination campaigns; health insurance; mobile clinics; clean water schemes.
Q30Literacy, health and development · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Explain two ways in which improving literacy helps a country such as Pakistan to develop. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'Spending on health care is more important than spending on education for Pakistan's development.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Literate workers can learn new skills and use technology, so productivity in farming, industry and IT rises and incomes increase; educated mothers understand nutrition, hygiene and vaccination, so child deaths fall; literate women usually have fewer children, slowing population growth; literate citizens can understand their rights and take part in decisions.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: skills and productivity; health awareness; lower birth rates; better jobs and incomes; participation / awareness of rights.
  2. (b) Health: a healthy workforce misses fewer days and is more productive; reducing child and maternal deaths and diseases such as hepatitis, TB and polio saves lives now; disease outbreaks after floods show the need for health services. Education: literacy is still only about 60%, and education raises skills, incomes and awareness; educated mothers improve family health, so education also improves health; schooling for girls reduces birth rates. Judgement: e.g. education has wider long-term effects, including on health, but basic health care is needed for children to attend and learn.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.
