Q1Fishing industry and sustainability · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Name the two coastal provinces of Pakistan. [2]
  2. (b) State two differences between marine fishing and inland fishing. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why fishing is important to people living on the Makran coast. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sindh and Balochistan.
    1 each.
  2. (b) Marine fishing takes place in the sea (Arabian Sea) and inland fishing in rivers, lakes, reservoirs and fish ponds; marine catches are larger and include shrimp, tuna and pomfret, while inland catches are mainly freshwater species such as rohu and other carp.
    1 per difference, max 2.
  3. (c) There is little farmland or industry in this dry area, so fishing is the main source of jobs, income and food for coastal villages.
    1 + 1 development.
Q2Fishing industry and sustainability · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define 'overfishing'. [1]
  2. (b) Explain two causes of overfishing in Pakistan's coastal waters. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Catching fish faster than the stock can reproduce, so numbers fall.
    1 mark.
  2. (b) Many more boats and modern trawlers now fish the same area, so the total catch has grown too large. Fine-mesh nets catch young fish before they breed, and regulations on mesh size and closed seasons are poorly enforced.
    1 + 1 development each: too many boats/trawlers; fine-mesh nets / juvenile catch; weak enforcement; rising demand for export and fishmeal.

Q8Fishing industry and sustainability · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why mangrove forests in the Indus Delta are important for fishing. [4]
  2. (b) State two reasons why the area of mangroves has decreased. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mangrove roots give shelter and food to young shrimp, crabs and fish, acting as nurseries before they move out to sea. Their loss therefore reduces future catches; mangroves also protect the coast from storms and erosion, protecting fishing villages.
    1 + 1 development each: breeding/nursery grounds; food supply; coastal protection; filter pollutants.
  2. (b) Any two: less fresh water and silt reaching the delta because of dams and barrages; cutting for fuelwood and fodder; camel grazing; pollution from Karachi; land reclamation for ports and housing.
    1 each.
Q9Fishing industry and sustainability · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A fishing cooperative in Keti Bandar recorded an average catch of 640 kg per boat per trip ten years ago and 480 kg per boat per trip this year (invented figures).

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage fall in the catch per trip. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons for this fall. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 640 − 480 = 160; 160 ÷ 640 × 100 = 25%.
    M1; A1 25%.
  2. (b) Overfishing and the use of fine-mesh nets have reduced the number of fish reaching breeding age. Less fresh water and silt reaching the delta, together with mangrove loss and pollution, have damaged breeding grounds.
    1 + 1 development each.

Q17Fishing industry and sustainability · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain why many small-scale fishermen in Pakistan remain poor. [4]
  2. (b) 'The fishing industry could make a much bigger contribution to Pakistan's economy.' Do you agree? Give reasons to support your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) They often borrow money from middlemen to buy boats, nets and fuel and must then sell their catch to them at low prices. Small boats without ice or cold storage cannot travel far or keep fish fresh, so they catch less and sell lower-quality fish.
    1 + 1 development each: debt to middlemen; low prices; small boats/poor equipment; lack of ice/cold storage; falling stocks; little education/alternative work.
  2. (b) For: long coastline and large exclusive economic zone with unused deep-sea stocks; exports could grow with better hygiene and processing; aquaculture can expand inland; new harbours (Gwadar). Against: stocks already overfished near the coast; poor infrastructure; export markets demand strict standards; pollution and mangrove loss; limited investment. Judgement required.
    Levels: L1 (1–2); L2 (3–4) both sides developed with judgement.
Q18Fishing industry and sustainability · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the building of dams and barrages on the Indus has affected inland and delta fisheries. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest two ways to protect river fish stocks. [2]

    1 + 1 development each. Credit reservoir fisheries (Tarbela, Mangla) as a positive point.
  2. (b) Any two: fish ladders/passes at barrages; closed seasons in rivers; ban on poisoning and dynamite fishing; stocking rivers from hatcheries; reducing pollution.
    1 each.

Q20Fishing industry and sustainability · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate the extent to which fish farming (aquaculture) can reduce the pressure on wild fish stocks in Pakistan. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: ponds and cages produce fish all year with controlled feeding and disease control, so less fish is taken from the sea and rivers; it creates jobs in inland areas where there is no sea fishing; it can produce fish close to markets. Against: farmed fish often need feed made from fish meal, which is caught from wild stocks; ponds need water and land and can pollute rivers; disease can spread to wild fish; starting a farm needs capital that small fishermen lack. Judgement: aquaculture can reduce pressure on wild fish if feed is sustainable and farms are well managed.
    Level 1 (1–2): simple statements about fish farming or wild stocks. Level 2 (3–4): developed points for and against. Level 3 (5–6): balanced evaluation of feed, pollution and livelihoods with a supported judgement.

Q60Fish types and catches · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate the view that the best way to increase Pakistan's fish catch is to buy more trawlers. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: more boats can fish further offshore and land more fish, creating jobs and income. Against: more trawlers increase overfishing and catch young fish (by-catch), use more fuel and damage the seabed, so catches may fall; better management such as mesh-size rules, closed seasons, harbours, cold storage and aquaculture may give a more sustainable increase. Judgement: more trawlers may add catch in the short term, but management and infrastructure are likely to give a lasting increase.
