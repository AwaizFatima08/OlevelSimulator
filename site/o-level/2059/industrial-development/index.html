Q1Tourism · Core · Paper 2 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) Name two mountain peaks in northern Pakistan that attract climbers and trekkers. [2]
  2. (b) State three attractions, other than mountain peaks, that bring tourists to Gilgit-Baltistan and northern Khyber Pakhtunkhwa. [3]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why it is difficult to develop tourism in the northern mountains. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two of: K2; Nanga Parbat; Gasherbrum I or II; Broad Peak; Rakaposhi; Tirich Mir.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any three: lakes (e.g. Attabad, Saif-ul-Malook); glaciers (e.g. Baltoro, Passu); valleys such as Hunza, Swat, Naran-Kaghan; the Karakoram Highway; Deosai Plains / national parks and wildlife; forts such as Baltit and Altit; cool summer climate; local culture and festivals.
    1 mark each, named or clearly described.
  3. (c) Roads are narrow and often blocked by landslides or snow, so journeys are long and unsafe, which puts tourists off; the tourist season is short (mainly summer), so hotels earn little for much of the year and investors are reluctant; few modern hotels and medical facilities in remote valleys; electricity and internet supply are unreliable.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').

  2. (b) State four types of job created directly by tourism in Pakistan. [4]

Q4Tourism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 10 marks

A hill resort in the Kaghan valley recorded these numbers of visitors (thousands) in one year (hypothetical data):Jan 2, Feb 3, Mar 6, Apr 12, May 25, Jun 48, Jul 55, Aug 41, Sep 18, Oct 10, Nov 4, Dec 3

  1. (a) Calculate the total number of visitors to the resort in the year. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the year's visitors who came in June, July and August. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Describe the pattern of visitors shown in the table. [3]
  4. (d) Explain two problems that this pattern causes for people who work in tourism in the valley. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 227 thousand (227,000 visitors)
    Sum of the twelve months = 227 thousand.
  2. (b) 63.4%
    June–August = 48 + 55 + 41 = 144 thousand (M1); 144 ÷ 227 × 100 = 63.4% (A1).
  3. (c) Very few visitors in winter (Nov–Feb, 2–4 thousand a month); numbers rise steadily from March; peak in July (55 thousand); fall sharply from September; the range is 53 thousand.
    1 mark per point, including at least one figure from the table, up to 3.
  4. (d) Work is seasonal, so guides, porters and hotel staff are unemployed for much of the year and must migrate or find other work; hotels are overcrowded in July, which causes high prices, water shortages and litter; hotels earn little income in winter, so it is hard to repay loans or pay staff all year.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').

Q7Tourism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A tour company recorded the countries of origin of its foreign visitors in one year (hypothetical data):China 4,500; United Kingdom 3,600; USA 2,700; Germany 1,200; Other countries 6,000

  1. (a) Calculate the number of degrees needed to show each group on a pie chart. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of visitors who came from China. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest two reasons why the number of visitors from China to Pakistan has increased in recent years. [2]

    Total = 18,000. Each value ÷ 18,000 × 360. China 90°, UK 72°, USA 54°, Germany 24°, Other 120°. 3 marks all correct; 2 marks three correct; 1 mark method only.
  2. (b) 25%
    4,500 ÷ 18,000 × 100 = 25%.
  3. (c) Business travel linked to CPEC projects; the Karakoram Highway and Khunjerab Pass give a direct land route; easier visa arrangements; good political relations between the two countries.
    1 mark each.
Q8Tourism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks

  2. (b) Read these two views:View A: 'Tourism in the northern areas should be expanded as fast as possible because it brings jobs and income.'View B: 'Tourism in the northern areas must be limited because the damage it causes outweighs the benefits.'Which view do you agree with more? Give reasons for your answer, referring to examples you have studied. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Litter and plastic left at lakes and campsites pollute water and harm wildlife; trees are cut for hotel building and fuelwood, causing soil erosion and landslides; untreated sewage from hotels flows into rivers; traffic jams on narrow roads cause air and noise pollution.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').

A balanced judgement such as controlled (sustainable) growth.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points, one side or generic. L2 (3–4): developed points on one side, or simple points on both sides. L3 (5–6): developed points on both sides with a named Pakistani example and a reasoned judgement.
Q9Tourism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A valley in Swat district had 640 hotel beds in 2015 and 1,040 hotel beds in 2024 (hypothetical data).

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in the number of hotel beds between 2015 and 2024. [2]
  2. (b) In 2024 the average occupancy of these beds over the whole year was 45%. Calculate the average number of beds filled each night. [1]
  3. (c) Explain two ways in which the growth of tourism may have affected the local people of the valley. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 62.5%
    (1,040 − 640) ÷ 640 × 100 (M1) = 62.5% (A1).
  2. (b) 468 beds
    1,040 × 0.45 = 468.
  3. (c) Positive: new jobs in hotels, transport and handicraft shops, raising incomes so fewer young people migrate to cities; better roads and electricity built for tourists also serve villages. Negative: land and food prices rise, so locals cannot afford them; traditional culture and dress may change; pressure on water supply and forests.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Credit positive or negative effects.
Q10Tourism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A provincial tourism department earned Rs 4.8 billion from tourism in 2022 and Rs 6.3 billion in 2024 (hypothetical data).

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in tourism revenue from 2022 to 2024. [2]
  2. (b) Evaluate the extent to which government action can increase the number of foreign tourists visiting Pakistan. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 31.25%
    (6.3 − 4.8) ÷ 4.8 × 100 (M1) = 31.25% (A1); accept 31.3%.
  2. (b) Government can help: the online e-visa system and visa on arrival for some countries make entry easier; building and repairing roads (motorways, Karakoram Highway) and airports improves access; promotion abroad improves Pakistan's image; better security. Limits: foreign travel advice and perceptions of insecurity are hard to change quickly; natural hazards such as floods and landslides; private investment in hotels is needed; global events affect travel. Judgement: government action is necessary but not enough on its own.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points, one side or generic. L2 (3–4): developed points on one side, or simple points on both sides. L3 (5–6): developed points on both sides with a named Pakistani example and a reasoned judgement.

Q12Large-scale manufacturing and location · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Q13Large-scale manufacturing and location · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A sugar mill in central Sindh crushes 4,500 tonnes of sugar cane a day. On average 10.2% of the mass of the cane is recovered as sugar (hypothetical data).

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of sugar the mill produces in one day. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of sugar produced in a crushing season of 120 days. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage of the mass of cane that is lost (not turned into sugar). [2]
  4. (d) Use your answers to explain why sugar mills are located close to the sugar cane fields. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 459 tonnes
    4,500 × 0.102 = 459 t.
  2. (b) 55,080 tonnes
    459 × 120 = 55,080 t.
  3. (c) 89.8%
    4,500 − 459 = 4041 t lost (M1); 4041 ÷ 4,500 × 100 = 89.8% (A1).
  4. (d) Cane loses about nine-tenths of its weight in processing, so it is much cheaper to transport the sugar than the cane; cane is perishable and its sugar content falls quickly after cutting, so it must be crushed within a day or two; cane is bulky and heavy, and farmers deliver it by tractor-trolley over short distances.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
Q14Large-scale manufacturing and location · Standard · Paper 2 style · 9 marks

A cement plant near the Salt Range produces 7,500 tonnes of cement a day. It needs 1.6 tonnes of raw materials to make 1 tonne of cement (hypothetical data).

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of raw materials the plant uses each day. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the mass of raw materials lost during manufacture. [2]
  3. (c) Name the main raw material for cement and one area of Pakistan where cement plants are found. [2]
  4. (d) Explain two environmental problems caused by cement factories. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12,000 tonnes
    7,500 × 1.6 = 12,000 t.
  2. (b) 37.5%
    Loss per tonne = 0.6 t (M1); 0.6 ÷ 1.6 × 100 = 37.5% (A1).

  4. (d) Dust from quarrying and kilns pollutes the air, causing breathing problems for nearby villagers and covering crops; quarrying limestone destroys hillsides and vegetation; kilns burn coal and give off carbon dioxide, adding to climate change; heavy trucks damage roads.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').

  1. (a) Explain two ways in which government policy can influence where industries are located in Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]

Show answer
  1. Q26Cottage and small-scale industry · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
