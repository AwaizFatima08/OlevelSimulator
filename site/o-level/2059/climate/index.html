Q1Climate extremes and hazards · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by the term drought? [2]
  2. (b) Explain two effects of drought on people living in the Thar Desert. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A long period with much less rainfall than normal, so that there is a serious shortage of water.
    B1 long period / prolonged; B1 below-normal rainfall / water shortage.
  2. (b) Wells and ponds dry up, so women and children walk long distances for water, which may be unsafe to drink and spreads disease; grazing and fodder fail, so cattle, goats and sheep die and families lose their main source of food and income; food shortages lead to malnutrition, especially among children; many families migrate to barrage areas or towns to find work.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: water shortage; livestock deaths; crop failure and hunger / malnutrition; disease; migration; debt.
Q2Climate extremes and hazards · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe the causes of the severe floods in Pakistan in 2022. [3]
  2. (b) Explain two impacts of floods such as those of 2022 on farming in Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Monsoon rainfall from June to August was several times the normal amount in Sindh and Balochistan; very high temperatures earlier in the year increased glacier melt in the north; much of the lower Indus plain is very flat, so water could not drain away; embankments were breached and hill torrents flooded towns.
    1 mark per point up to 3: exceptional monsoon rain (Sindh, Balochistan); heatwave / glacier melt; flat land, poor drainage; breaches / hill torrents; deforestation or climate change accepted with explanation.
  2. (b) Standing crops such as cotton, rice and dates were destroyed, so farmers lost income and the country lost export earnings; livestock drowned, removing a source of milk and income; floodwater stood for months, so wheat could not be sown on time for the next rabi season; irrigation canals and roads were damaged, increasing costs.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: crop losses; livestock deaths; delayed sowing; damaged canals / roads; food prices rise; soil damage or silt.

Q11Climate extremes and hazards · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Explain how heavy rain or rapid snowmelt can trigger landslides on the slopes beside the Karakoram Highway. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest two ways of reducing the danger of landslides to traffic on mountain roads. [2]
  3. (c) State one economic effect of the highway being blocked by a landslide for several days. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rain or meltwater soaks into loose rock and soil, making it heavier and reducing the friction holding it on the steep slope, so it slides; water flowing over the surface washes away material at the foot of slopes that were already cut back for the road, so the slope above collapses.

  2. (b) Build retaining walls and nets on the most dangerous slopes; close the road and warn travellers during very heavy rain.

Q12Climate extremes and hazards · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 11 marks

Air quality index (AQI) readings at one monitoring station in Lahore on six days in November:180, 245, 310, 420, 390, 260

  1. (a) State the number of readings that were above 300 ('hazardous'). [1]
  2. (b) Explain why smog is worst in Lahore between November and January. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) 'Smog in Lahore can only be reduced by controlling crop burning.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3
    B1 3.
  2. (b) In winter cold, calm air near the ground is trapped under a layer of warmer air (temperature inversion), so pollutants cannot rise and spread; winds are light, so smoke builds up; at this time farmers burn rice straw after the harvest and smoke drifts over the city; emissions from vehicles, brick kilns and factories add to the pollution, and fog mixes with the smoke.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: temperature inversion; calm winds; crop residue burning; vehicles, kilns, industry; fog.
  3. (c) For: crop residue burning in Punjab after the rice harvest produces large amounts of smoke exactly when smog peaks; offering farmers machines such as happy seeders or buying straw could cut this quickly. Against: vehicles are a major year-round source, so better fuel, emission checks and public transport (e.g. Lahore Metro Bus and Orange Line) are needed; brick kilns can switch to zig-zag technology; factories and burning of rubbish also contribute; planting trees and banning open burning in the city help. Judgement: e.g. controlling crop burning is important but not enough on its own, because traffic and industry pollute every day.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.
Q13Climate extremes and hazards · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Pakistan produces less than 1% of the world's greenhouse gas emissions but is one of the countries most affected by climate change. Explain two reasons why Pakistan is very vulnerable to climate change. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'Pakistan should spend more on adapting to climate change than on reducing its own emissions.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Its rivers depend on glaciers in the Himalaya, Karakoram and Hindu Kush, so faster melting first raises flood and GLOF risk and later threatens water supply; most of the country is arid or semi-arid with high temperatures, so heatwaves and droughts become more extreme; farming, which employs a large share of the workforce, depends on irrigation and monsoon rain; many people are poor and live in flood-prone areas, so they cannot easily recover.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: glacier dependence; heat and aridity; agriculture-dependent economy; flood-prone plains / coast; poverty and weak infrastructure.
  2. (b) For adaptation: Pakistan's emissions are small, so cutting them will hardly change world temperatures; its people are already suffering floods, heatwaves and droughts, so money for flood defences, early warning, heat action plans, drought-resistant crops and water storage saves lives now. For reducing emissions: cleaner energy such as solar, wind and hydel reduces costly oil imports and air pollution (smog); reforestation such as large tree-planting programmes both stores carbon and reduces floods and soil erosion; showing action helps Pakistan gain international climate finance. Judgement: e.g. adaptation should come first, but many actions (solar power, trees) do both.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.
