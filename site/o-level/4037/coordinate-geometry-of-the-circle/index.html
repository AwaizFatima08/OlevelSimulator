Q1Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find the coordinates of the points where the line y = x + 1 meets the circle x² + y² = 25. [4]
  2. (b) Find the exact length of the chord joining these points. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (3, 4) and (−4, −3)
    x² + (x + 1)² = 25 M1; x² + x − 12 = 0 A1; x = 3, −4 A1; points A1
  2. (b) 7√2
    √(7² + 7²) M1; = 7√2 A1
Q2Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) The point (3, 4) lies on the circle x² + y² = 25. Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at this point. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3x + 4y = 25
    gradient of radius = 4/3 B1; tangent gradient −3/4 M1; y − 4 = −¾(x − 3) → 3x + 4y = 25 A1
Q3Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 13 at the point (4, 2). Give your answer in the form ax + by = c. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2x + 3y = 14
    radius gradient (2 + 1)/(4 − 2) = 3/2 B1; tangent gradient −2/3 M1; y − 2 = −⅔(x − 4) → 2x + 3y = 14 A1
Q4Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the coordinates of the points where the circle (x − 3)² + (y + 4)² = 25 meets the coordinate axes. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (0, 0), (6, 0), (0, −8)
    y = 0: (x − 3)² = 9 → x = 0, 6 M1 A1; x = 0: (y + 4)² = 16 → y = 0, −8 M1 A1
Q5Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Determine whether the line y = 2x meets the circle x² + y² − 10x + 16 = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) No: the discriminant is −220 < 0
    x² + 4x² − 10x + 16 = 0 → 5x² − 10x + 16 = 0 M1; 100 − 320 = −220 M1; < 0 so the line does not meet the circle A1
Q6Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Show that the line y = 2x + 10 is a tangent to the circle (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 20, and find the coordinates of the point of contact. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown; point of contact (−3, 4)
    (x − 1)² + (2x + 8)² = 20 M1; 5x² + 30x + 45 = 0 → x² + 6x + 9 = 0 A1; discriminant 36 − 36 = 0 so tangent A1; x = −3 M1; y = 4 A1
Q7Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the values of c for which the line y = x + c is a tangent to the circle x² + y² = 8. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) c = 4 or c = −4
    x² + (x + c)² = 8 → 2x² + 2cx + c² − 8 = 0 M1; 4c² − 8(c² − 8) = 0 M1; c² = 16 A1; c = ±4 A1
Q8Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The circle C has equation (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 25. The line y = 14 − 2x meets C at the points A and B, where A has the smaller x-coordinate.

  1. (a) Find the coordinates of A and B. [4]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB and show that it passes through the centre of C. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A(4, 6), B(6, 2)
    (x − 1)² + (12 − 2x)² = 25 M1; 5x² − 50x + 120 = 0 → x² − 10x + 24 = 0 A1; x = 4, 6 A1; y = 6, 2 A1
  2. (b) y = ½x + 3/2; at x = 1, y = 2 (the centre)
    midpoint (5, 4), gradient ½ M1; y − 4 = ½(x − 5) A1; substitute (1, 2) A1
Q9Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Show that the line 3x − 4y = 30 does not meet the circle x² + y² = 25. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown: discriminant −17600 < 0
    y = (3x − 30)/4 M1; 16x² + (3x − 30)² = 400 → 25x² − 180x + 500 = 0 A1; 180² − 4 × 25 × 500 = −17600 M1; < 0 so no intersection A1
Q10Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A circle has centre (1, 1) and radius 4. The point P has coordinates (9, 7).

  1. (a) Find the shortest and the longest distances from P to points on the circle. [3]
  2. (b) A tangent from P touches the circle at T. Find the exact length PT. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) shortest 6, longest 14
    distance from P to centre = √(64 + 36) = 10 M1 A1; 10 − 4 = 6, 10 + 4 = 14 A1
  2. (b) PT = 2√21
    PT² = 10² − 4² = 84 (radius ⟂ tangent) M1; PT = √84 = 2√21 A1
Q11Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

The circle x² + y² − 8x − 2y + 7 = 0 meets the line y = x − 1 at the points A and B.

  1. (a) Find the coordinates of A and B. [4]
  2. (b) Find the exact length of AB and the distance from the centre of the circle to the midpoint of AB. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (1, 0) and (5, 4)
    x² + (x − 1)² − 8x − 2(x − 1) + 7 = 0 M1; 2x² − 12x + 10 = 0 → x² − 6x + 5 = 0 A1; x = 1, 5 A1; y = 0, 4 A1
  2. (b) AB = 4√2; distance √2
    AB = √(16 + 16) = 4√2 B1; centre (4, 1), midpoint (3, 2) M1; √2 A1
Q12Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

The point P(4, 3) lies on the circle (x + 1)² + (y − 2)² = 26.

  1. (a) Find the equation of the normal to the circle at P. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x − 5y + 11 = 0
    the normal passes through the centre (−1, 2): gradient ⅕ M1; A1
  2. (b) 5x + y = 23
    gradient −5 M1; y − 3 = −5(x − 4) A1
Q13Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

On a map of a park in Islamabad, with 1 unit representing 10 m, the edge of a circular pond has equation x² + y² − 10x − 6y + 9 = 0. A straight path follows the line y = 2x − 12.

  1. (a) Find the coordinates of the points where the path meets the edge of the pond. [4]
  2. (b) Find the length of path that lies inside the pond, in metres, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (5, −2) and (9, 6)
    substitute: 5x² − 70x + 225 = 0 M1 A1; x² − 14x + 45 = 0 → x = 5, 9 A1; y = −2, 6 A1
  2. (b) 89.4 m
    √(16 + 64) = √80 units M1; × 10 = 89.4 m A1
Q14Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the gradients of the two tangents from the origin to the circle (x − 5)² + y² = 9. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) m = ¾ and m = −¾
    line y = mx: (x − 5)² + m²x² = 9 M1; (1 + m²)x² − 10x + 16 = 0 A1; 100 − 64(1 + m²) = 0 M1; m² = 9/16 A1; m = ±¾ A1
Q15Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The circle x² + y² − 4x + 6y − 12 = 0 passes through the point P(5, 1).

  1. (a) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P. [4]
  2. (b) The tangent meets the x-axis at Q and the y-axis at R. Find the exact area of triangle OQR, where O is the origin. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3x + 4y = 19
    centre (2, −3) B1; radius gradient 4/3 M1; tangent gradient −3/4 M1; 3x + 4y = 19 A1
  2. (b) 361/24
    Q(19/3, 0), R(0, 19/4) B1 B1; ½ × 19/3 × 19/4 = 361/24 A1
Q16Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the set of values of k for which the line y = kx + 5 meets the circle x² + y² = 5 at two distinct points. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) k < −2 or k > 2
    x² + (kx + 5)² = 5 M1; (1 + k²)x² + 10kx + 20 = 0 A1; 100k² − 80(1 + k²) > 0 M1; k² > 4 A1; k < −2 or k > 2 A1
Q17Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

The points A(4, 2) and B(2, −4) lie on the circle x² + y² = 20, whose centre is O.

  1. (a) Find the equations of the tangents to the circle at A and at B. [4]
  2. (b) The tangents meet at T. Find the coordinates of T. [2]
  3. (c) Find the area of the quadrilateral OATB. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) at A: 2x + y = 10; at B: x − 2y = 10
    radius OA gradient ½ → tangent gradient −2 M1; 2x + y = 10 A1; radius OB gradient −2 → tangent gradient ½ M1; x − 2y = 10 A1
  2. (b) T(6, −2)
    solve simultaneously M1 A1
  3. (c) 20
    two right-angled triangles, each ½ × √20 × √20 = 10 M1; total 20 A1
Q18Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The line y = x + k is a tangent to the circle (x − 3)² + (y − 1)² = 8. Find the possible values of k and the coordinates of the point of contact in each case. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 2, contact (1, 3); k = −6, contact (5, −1)
    (x − 3)² + (x + k − 1)² = 8 → 2x² + (2k − 8)x + (k − 1)² + 1 = 0 M1 A1; (2k − 8)² − 8((k − 1)² + 1) = 0 → k² + 4k − 12 = 0 M1 A1; k = 2: x = 1, y = 3 A1; k = −6: x = 5, y = −1 A1
Q19Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A circle has centre (2, −1) and touches the line 3x + 4y = 12. By finding the foot of the perpendicular from the centre to the line, find the radius and the equation of the circle. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) radius 2; (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 4
    perpendicular gradient 4/3: y + 1 = (4/3)(x − 2) M1 A1; solve with 3x + 4y = 12: x = 16/5, y = 3/5 M1 A1; radius² = (6/5)² + (8/5)² = 4 M1; equation A1
Q20Circle and line (tangents, intersections) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Circle C₁ has equation x² + y² = 25 and circle C₂ has equation x² + y² − 8x − 6y + 15 = 0.

  1. (a) Show that the points of intersection of C₁ and C₂ lie on the line 4x + 3y = 20. [2]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the points of intersection of the two circles. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    subtract the equations: 8x + 6y − 15 = 25 M1; 4x + 3y = 20 A1
  2. (b) (5, 0) and (1.4, 4.8)
    y = (20 − 4x)/3 into C₁ M1; 25x² − 160x + 175 = 0 → 5x² − 32x + 35 = 0 A1; x = 5, 7/5 A1; y = 0, 24/5 A1

Q23Equation of a circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A circle has centre (−4, 3) and touches the x-axis. Write down its equation, and explain why it does not touch the y-axis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x + 4)² + (y − 3)² = 9; the distance from the centre to the y-axis is 4, which is greater than the radius 3
    radius = 3 (distance to the x-axis) B1; equation B1; reason B1

Q26Equation of a circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The centre of a circle lies on the line y = 2x. The circle passes through the points (5, 0) and (1, 4). Find the equation of the circle. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x + 1)² + (y + 2)² = 40
    perpendicular bisector of the chord: midpoint (3, 2), chord gradient −1 so bisector y = x − 1 M1 A1; meet y = 2x: x = −1, centre (−1, −2) M1 A1; r² = 36 + 4 = 40 A1
Q27Equation of a circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the centre and radius of the circle x² + y² − 2x − 4y − 20 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Determine whether the point P(4, 7) lies inside, on or outside the circle. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) centre (1, 2), radius 5
    (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 25 M1 A1
  2. (b) outside, since the distance from the centre is √34 > 5
    distance² = 9 + 25 = 34 M1; 34 > 25 so outside A1
Q28Equation of a circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

A circle passes through the points A(−2, 3), B(4, 5) and C(6, −1).

  1. (a) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB. [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of BC. [2]
  3. (c) Hence find the equation of the circle. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −3x + 7
    midpoint (1, 4) B1; gradient AB = ⅓ so perpendicular gradient −3 M1; y = −3x + 7 A1
  2. (b) y = ⅓x + ⅓ (or x − 3y + 1 = 0)
    midpoint (5, 2), gradient BC = −3 so perpendicular gradient ⅓ M1; A1
  3. (c) (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 20
    solve: centre (2, 1) M1 A1; r² = 16 + 4 = 20 A1
Q29Equation of a circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The circle x² + y² − 4x + ky + 4 = 0 has radius 3. Find the possible values of the constant k, and state the centre in each case. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 6, centre (2, −3); k = −6, centre (2, 3)
    (x − 2)² + (y + k/2)² = 4 + k²/4 − 4 = k²/4 M1 A1; k²/4 = 9 → k = ±6 A1; centres (2, −k/2) A1
Q30Equation of a circle · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Circle C₁ has equation (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 9 and circle C₂ has equation (x − 7)² + (y − 10)² = 49.

  1. (a) Show that the circles touch each other. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the point where they touch. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown: distance between centres = 10 = 3 + 7
    centres (1, 2), (7, 10); radii 3, 7 B1; distance √(36 + 64) = 10 M1; = sum of radii, so they touch (externally) A1
  2. (b) (2.8, 4.4)
    point is 3/10 of the way from (1, 2) to (7, 10) M1; (1 + 1.8, 2 + 2.4) A1
