Q15Permutations · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Given that ⁿP₂ = 56, find the value of n. [3]
  2. (b) Show that ⁿP₃ ÷ ⁿP₂ = n − 2 for n ≥ 3. [2]
Show answer

Q26Permutations · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Numbers are formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 and 6, with no digit repeated.

  1. (a) Find how many 4-digit even numbers greater than 4000 can be formed from these digits. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 84
    Thousands digit must be 4, 5 or 6 so the number exceeds 4000; last digit is 2, 4 or 6, not the same as the thousands digit. Thousands digit 4: last digit 2 or 6 (2 choices), middle two digits from the remaining 4 digits in 4 × 3 = 12 ways, giving 24 M1; thousands digit 5: last digit 2, 4 or 6 (3 choices), giving 3 × 12 = 36 M1; thousands digit 6: last digit 2 or 4 (2 choices), giving 24; total 24 + 36 + 24 = 84 A1
