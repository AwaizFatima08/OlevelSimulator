Q1Area under a curve · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the coordinates of the points where the curve y = 6x − x² meets the x-axis. [1]
  2. (b) Find the area of the region enclosed by the curve and the x-axis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (0, 0) and (6, 0)
    B1
  2. (b) 36
    M1 integrate: 3x² − x³/3; M1 limits 0 and 6; A1 108 − 72 = 36

Q5Area under a curve · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The line y = x + 2 meets the curve y = 4 − x² at the points A and B. Find the x-coordinates of A and B. [2]
  2. (b) Find the area of the region enclosed between the line and the curve. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = −2 and x = 1
    M1 x² + x − 2 = 0; A1
  2. (b) 9/2
    M1 ∫(4 − x² − x − 2)dx; M1 2x − x²/2 − x³/3; M1 limits −2 to 1; A1 7/6 + 10/3 = 9/2
Q6Area under a curve · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = x³ − 4x meets the x-axis at x = −2, x = 0 and x = 2. Explain why ∫ from −2 to 2 of (x³ − 4x) dx = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Find the total area of the two regions enclosed by the curve and the x-axis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The region above the axis (−2 to 0) and the region below (0 to 2) have equal area, and areas below the axis give a negative integral, so they cancel.
    B1 equal areas above (−2 to 0) and below (0 to 2) the axis, by symmetry (x³ − 4x is an odd function: f(−x) = −f(x)); B1 the part below the axis gives a negative integral, so the two contributions cancel
  2. (b) 8
    M1 ∫ from 0 to 2 of (x³ − 4x) dx = −4; M1 use of modulus / symmetry; A1 8

Q8Area under a curve · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

Q24Finding a curve from its gradient · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) A curve is such that d²y/dx² = 6x − 4. The curve has a stationary point at (2, 1). Find dy/dx in terms of x. [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the curve. [3]
  3. (c) Find the coordinates of the other stationary point and determine the nature of both stationary points. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = 3x² − 4x − 4
    M1 3x² − 4x + c; M1 0 = 12 − 8 + c; A1 c = −4
  2. (b) y = x³ − 2x² − 4x + 9
    M1 integrate again; M1 1 = 8 − 8 − 8 + k; A1 k = 9
  3. (c) (−2/3, 283/27) ≈ (−0.667, 10.5) maximum; (2, 1) minimum
    M1 (3x + 2)(x − 2) = 0; A1 y = 283/27; M1 d²y/dx² at each; A1 −8 < 0 max, 8 > 0 min

Q27Integration as reverse of differentiation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

  2. (b) Hence find ∫ x e^(2x) dx. [3]
  3. (c) Evaluate ∫ from 0 to 1 of x e^(2x) dx, giving your answer in exact form and correct to 3 significant figures. [2]

  2. (b) ½x e^(2x) − ¼e^(2x) + c

  3. (c) (e² + 1)/4 ≈ 2.10
    M1; A1
