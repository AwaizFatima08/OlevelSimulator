Show answer
  1. (a) x = 90°, 330°
    cos(x − 30°) = ½ M1; x − 30° = 60°, 300° (range −30° to 330°) M1; x = 90°, 330° A1

Q3Trigonometric equations · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Show answer

Q4Trigonometric equations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Show that the equation 3 sec²θ − 5 tan θ − 5 = 0 can be written as 3 tan²θ − 5 tan θ − 2 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Hence solve 3 sec²θ − 5 tan θ − 5 = 0 for 0° ≤ θ ≤ 360°. Give your answers correct to 1 decimal place. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    sec²θ = 1 + tan²θ M1; 3 + 3tan²θ − 5tanθ − 5 = 0 → 3tan²θ − 5tanθ − 2 = 0 A1
  2. (b) θ = 63.4°, 161.6°, 243.4°, 341.6°
    (3tanθ + 1)(tanθ − 2) = 0 M1; tanθ = 2 → 63.4°, 243.4° A1; tanθ = −⅓ → 161.6°, 341.6° M1 A1
Q5Trigonometric equations · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Solve the equation 2 cos²x + 3 sin x = 3 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π radians, giving your answers as exact multiples of π. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = π/6, π/2, 5π/6
    Use cos²x = 1 − sin²x M1; 2sin²x − 3sin x + 1 = 0 A1; (2sin x − 1)(sin x − 1) = 0 M1; sin x = ½ → π/6, 5π/6 A1; sin x = 1 → π/2 A1
Q6Trigonometric equations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Solve cot(2y + π/4) = 0.5 for −π ≤ y ≤ π radians. Give your answers correct to 3 significant figures. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −2.98, −1.41, 0.161, 1.73
    tan(2y + π/4) = 2 M1; 2y + π/4 = 1.1071 + nπ M1; range for 2y + π/4 is −7π/4 to 9π/4 M1; y = ((1.1071 + nπ) − π/4)/2 for n = −2, −1, 0, 1 A2 (A1 for any two correct)
Q7Trigonometric equations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Solve 4 cosec²x + cot x − 7 = 0 for 0° < x < 360°. Give your answers correct to 1 decimal place where necessary. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 53.1°, 135°, 233.1°, 315°
    cosec²x = 1 + cot²x M1; 4cot²x + cot x − 3 = 0 A1; (4cot x − 3)(cot x + 1) = 0 M1; cot x = ¾ → tan x = 4/3 → 53.1°, 233.1° A1 A1; cot x = −1 → 135°, 315° A1

Q10Trigonometric equations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Solve cosec(x + 1) = −3 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π, where x is in radians. Give your answers correct to 3 significant figures. [4]
Show answer

    sin(x + 1) = −⅓ M1; x + 1 lies between 1 and 2π + 1; x + 1 = π + 0.3398 = 3.4814 or 2π − 0.3398 = 5.9434 M1 A1; x = 2.48, 4.94 A1

Q12Trigonometric equations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Show that sin²A/(1 − cos A) ≡ 1 + cos A. [2]
  2. (b) Hence solve 5 sin²(2x) = 3(1 − cos 2x) for 0° < x < 180°. Give your answers correct to 1 decimal place. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    sin²A = 1 − cos²A = (1 − cos A)(1 + cos A) M1; cancel (1 − cos A) A1
  2. (b) x = 56.8°, 123.2°
    sin²2x/(1 − cos 2x) = 3/5 so 1 + cos 2x = 0.6 M1; cos 2x = −0.4 A1; 2x = 113.58°, 246.42° M1; x = 56.8°, 123.2° A1 (cos 2x = 1 gives x = 0° or 180°, outside the range)
Q13Trigonometric equations · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Factorise 2s³ − s² − 2s + 1 completely. [2]
  2. (b) Hence solve 2 sin³x − sin²x − 2 sin x + 1 = 0 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2s − 1)(s − 1)(s + 1)
    s²(2s − 1) − (2s − 1) M1; (2s − 1)(s² − 1) = (2s − 1)(s − 1)(s + 1) A1
  2. (b) x = 30°, 90°, 150°, 270°
    sin x = ½ → 30°, 150° B1 B1; sin x = 1 → 90° B1; sin x = −1 → 270° B1
Q14Trigonometric equations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Solve 3 cos²y − 2 = sin y for 0 ≤ y ≤ 2π radians. Give your answers correct to 3 significant figures. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 0.449, 2.69, 4.02, 5.41
    3(1 − sin²y) − 2 − sin y = 0 M1; 3sin²y + sin y − 1 = 0 A1; sin y = (−1 ± √13)/6 = 0.4343 or −0.7676 M1; y = 0.449, 2.69 A1; y = 4.02, 5.41 A1 A1

Q17Trigonometric graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = 2 cos 3x − 1 is drawn for 0° ≤ x ≤ 180°. State the period of the curve. [1]
  2. (b) State the coordinates of the maximum and minimum points of the curve in this interval. [3]
  3. (c) Find the x-coordinates of the points where the curve crosses the x-axis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 120°
    360° ÷ 3 B1
  2. (b) maxima (0°, 1) and (120°, 1); minima (60°, −3) and (180°, −3)
    B1 maxima, B1 minima values, B1 all four x-coordinates
  3. (c) x = 20°, 100°, 140°

Q18Trigonometric graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = a sin bx + c, where a, b and c are positive constants, has a maximum point at (30°, 7). The next minimum point to the right is at (90°, −1). Find a, b and c. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 4, b = 3, c = 3
    a = (7 − (−1))/2 = 4 B1; c = (7 + (−1))/2 = 3 B1; half period 60° so period 120°, b = 3 B1 (check: 3 × 30° = 90° gives sin = 1)
Q19Trigonometric graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) For the curve y = 4 tan(x/2), where x is in radians and −π < x < 3π, state the period and the equations of the asymptotes. [3]
  2. (b) Find the value of y when x = 2π/3, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [1]
  3. (c) Find the values of x in the interval for which y = −4. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) period 2π; asymptotes x = π (and only that one in this interval)
    period π ÷ ½ = 2π B1; asymptotes where x/2 = π/2 + nπ, i.e. x = π + 2nπ M1; in −π < x < 3π only x = π A1
  2. (b) 6.93
    4 tan(π/3) = 4√3 = 6.93 B1
  3. (c) x = −π/2, 3π/2
    tan(x/2) = −1 M1; x/2 = −π/4, 3π/4 → x = −π/2, 3π/2 A1
Q20Trigonometric graphs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = 5 cos 2x − 2 is drawn for 0 ≤ x ≤ π. State the coordinates of its end points and of its minimum point. [2]
  2. (b) Find the set of values of k for which the equation 5 cos 2x − 2 = k has exactly two roots for 0 ≤ x ≤ π. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (0, 3), (π, 3); minimum (π/2, −7)
    B1 end points; B1 minimum
  2. (b) −7 < k ≤ 3
    The line y = k cuts the curve twice when it lies between the minimum and the maximum M1; k = −7 gives one root (x = π/2) A1; k = 3 gives x = 0 and x = π, two roots, so −7 < k ≤ 3 A1
Q21Trigonometric graphs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe the curve y = |2 sin x − 1| for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°: state the points where it meets the x-axis, the y-intercept, and the greatest value of y and where it occurs. [4]
  2. (b) Hence state the number of solutions of |2 sin x − 1| = 1 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°, and find them. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) meets x-axis at (30°, 0) and (150°, 0); y-intercept (0°, 1); greatest value 3 at x = 270°
    B1 zeros (2 sin x = 1); B1 y-intercept |−1| = 1; B1 greatest value 3; B1 at 270° (sin x = −1)
  2. (b) 4 solutions: 0°, 90°, 180°, 360°
    2 sin x − 1 = 1 → sin x = 1 → 90° B1; 2 sin x − 1 = −1 → sin x = 0 → 0°, 180°, 360° B1; 4 solutions B1
Q22Identities and proofs · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Prove that (1 + cos x)(cosec x − cot x) ≡ sin x. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Proof
    cosec x − cot x = (1 − cos x)/sin x M1; (1 + cos x)(1 − cos x)/sin x = (1 − cos²x)/sin x M1; = sin²x/sin x = sin x A1
Q23Identities and proofs · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Prove that tan x + cot x ≡ sec x cosec x. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Proof
    sin x/cos x + cos x/sin x M1; = (sin²x + cos²x)/(sin x cos x) M1; = 1/(sin x cos x) = sec x cosec x A1
Q24Identities and proofs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Show that (sec x − cos x)/tan x ≡ sin x. [3]
  2. (b) Hence solve (sec x − cos x)/tan x = −0.6 for 0° < x < 360°. Give your answers correct to 1 decimal place. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    sec x − cos x = (1 − cos²x)/cos x = sin²x/cos x M1 A1; ÷ (sin x/cos x) gives sin x A1
  2. (b) x = 216.9°, 323.1°
    sin x = −0.6 M1; 216.9°, 323.1° A1
Q25Identities and proofs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Show that cosec²θ/cot θ − cot θ ≡ tan θ. [3]
  2. (b) Hence solve cosec²θ/cot θ − cot θ = 3 for 0° < θ < 360°. Give your answers correct to 1 decimal place. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    cosec²θ = 1 + cot²θ M1; (1 + cot²θ)/cot θ − cot θ = 1/cot θ M1; = tan θ A1
  2. (b) θ = 71.6°, 251.6°
    tan θ = 3 M1; 71.6°, 251.6° A1
Q26Identities and proofs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Prove that (1 − sin x)/(1 + sin x) ≡ (sec x − tan x)². [4]
  2. (b) Hence, or otherwise, solve (sec x − tan x)² = 1/9 for 0° < x < 180°, x ≠ 90°. Give your answers correct to 1 decimal place. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Proof
    (sec x − tan x)² = (1 − sin x)²/cos²x M1 A1; cos²x = 1 − sin²x = (1 − sin x)(1 + sin x) M1; cancel (1 − sin x) to get (1 − sin x)/(1 + sin x) A1
  2. (b) x = 53.1°, 126.9°
    (1 − sin x)/(1 + sin x) = 1/9 M1; 9 − 9 sin x = 1 + sin x → sin x = 0.8 A1; x = 53.1°, 126.9° A1
Q27Identities and proofs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Show that sin⁴x − cos⁴x ≡ 1 − 2cos²x. [3]
  2. (b) Do not use a calculator in this part.Hence solve sin⁴x − cos⁴x = ½ for 0 ≤ x ≤ π radians. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    (sin²x − cos²x)(sin²x + cos²x) M1; = sin²x − cos²x A1; = 1 − cos²x − cos²x = 1 − 2cos²x A1
  2. (b) x = π/3, 2π/3
    1 − 2cos²x = ½ → cos²x = ¼ M1; cos x = ±½ M1; x = π/3, 2π/3 A1
Q28Sine and cosine rules (exact values) · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Do not use a calculator in this question.In triangle ABC, AB = 4 cm, AC = 6 cm and angle BAC = 60°.

  1. (a) Find the exact length of BC, giving your answer in the form a√b where a and b are integers. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of triangle ABC. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) BC = 2√7 cm
    BC² = 16 + 36 − 2(4)(6)cos 60° M1; = 28 A1; BC = √28 = 2√7 A1
  2. (b) 6√3 cm²
    ½ × 4 × 6 × sin 60° M1; = 12 × √3/2 = 6√3 A1
Q29Sine and cosine rules (exact values) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Do not use a calculator in this question.In triangle PQR, angle PQR = 45°, angle QPR = 30° and QR = 6 cm.You may use sin 105° = (√6 + √2)/4.

  1. (a) Find the exact length of PR. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of triangle PQR, giving your answer in the form a + b√3. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) PR = 6√2 cm
    PR/sin 45° = 6/sin 30° M1; PR = 6 × (√2/2) ÷ ½ M1; = 6√2 A1
  2. (b) 9 + 9√3 cm²
    angle PRQ = 105° B1; ½ × 6 × 6√2 × (√6 + √2)/4 M1; = (9√2/2)(√6 + √2) = 9√3 + 9 A1
Q30Sine and cosine rules (exact values) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

Do not use a calculator in this question.A triangular plot of land in Murree has sides XY = 5 m, YZ = 7 m and XZ = 8 m.

  1. (a) Show that angle YXZ = 60°. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of the plot. [2]
  3. (c) Find the exact value of sin XZY. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    cos YXZ = (5² + 8² − 7²)/(2 × 5 × 8) M1; = 40/80 = ½ A1; so angle = 60° A1
  2. (b) 10√3 m²
    ½ × 5 × 8 × sin 60° M1; = 10√3 A1
  3. (c) sin XZY = 5√3/14
    sin XZY/5 = sin 60°/7 M1; sin XZY = 5√3/14 A1
