Q1Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A customer who owes Shazia $480 has left the country and the debt will not be paid. Which entries record writing off the debt?A Debit irrecoverable debts, credit the customer's accountB Debit the customer's account, credit irrecoverable debtsC Debit provision for doubtful debts, credit irrecoverable debtsD Debit irrecoverable debts, credit sales [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Debit irrecoverable debts, credit the customer's account
    The expense (irrecoverable debts) is debited and the asset (the customer's account) is reduced by a credit. B is the reverse entry, used only when a written-off debt is reinstated.
Q2Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between an irrecoverable debt and a doubtful debt. [2]
  2. (b) State two reasons why a business creates a provision for doubtful debts. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Irrecoverable debt: certain not to be paid, so written off. Doubtful debt: may not be paid, so a provision is made but the debt stays in the customer's account.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Any two: prudence (not overstating assets/profit); matching the expected loss with the revenue of the year it arose; showing a realistic value of trade receivables; spreading the effect of bad debts.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q3Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Rizwan Stores owed a supplier $2,000 and was declared bankrupt. The supplier received a final payment of 30 cents for every $1 owed. The rest is irrecoverable.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount to be written off as irrecoverable. [1]
  2. (b) State the entries in the supplier's books for the cash received and the amount written off. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $1,400
    2,000 − (2,000 × 0.30) = 1,400 (B1).
  2. (b) Debit bank $600, credit Rizwan Stores $600; debit irrecoverable debts $1,400, credit Rizwan Stores $1,400.
    B1 each.
Q4Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) At 31 December 2025 a business had trade receivables of $48,000. It keeps a provision for doubtful debts of 3% of trade receivables. The provision brought forward was $1,200. What is the effect on the income statement for 2025?A expense of $240B income of $240C expense of $1,440D expense of $2,640 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A expense of $240
    New provision 3% × 48,000 = 1,440; increase 1,440 − 1,200 = 240, charged as an expense. C wrongly charges the whole provision.
Q5Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Before adjustments, a furniture wholesaler had trade receivables of $62,500 on 30 June 2026. A debt of $2,500 is to be written off. The provision for doubtful debts is to be kept at 4% of the remaining trade receivables. The provision on 1 July 2025 was $2,100.

  1. (a) Calculate the provision for doubtful debts required at 30 June 2026. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the change in the provision and state whether it is income or an expense in the income statement. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the net trade receivables shown in the statement of financial position. [1]
  4. (d) Name the accounting principle applied when making the provision. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,400
    62,500 − 2,500 = 60,000 (M1); 4% = 2,400 (A1).
  2. (b) Increase of $300; expense
    2,400 − 2,100 = 300 (B1); increase = expense (B1).
  3. (c) $57,600
    60,000 − 2,400 (B1).
  4. (d) Prudence
    B1.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount charged to the income statement for the year ended 31 December 2024. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the provision required at 31 December 2025 and the amount shown in the income statement for 2025, stating whether it is income or an expense. [3]
  3. (c) State the balance on the provision for doubtful debts account brought down on 1 January 2026 and its side. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $1,500
    5% × 30,000 = 1,500 (B1).
  2. (b) Provision $1,300; decrease of $200 shown as income
    1,300 (B1); 1,500 − 1,300 = 200 (M1); income (A1).
  3. (c) $1,300 credit
    B1.
Q7Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In the year ended 31 March 2025 Kamila wrote off a debt of $650 owed by Wahid. In January 2026 Wahid paid the full $650 by cheque.

  1. (a) State the two entries needed to reinstate the debt. [2]
  2. (b) State the entries to record the receipt of the cheque. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the debt is reinstated in Wahid's account before the receipt is recorded. [2]
  4. (d) State the effect of the recovery on the profit for the year ended 31 March 2026. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Debit Wahid's account $650; credit irrecoverable debts recovered $650
    B1 debit customer. B1 credit irrecoverable debts recovered (income).
  2. (b) Debit bank $650; credit Wahid's account $650
    B1.
  3. (c) So that the customer's account shows a full record of his payment history, which helps when deciding whether to give him credit again.
    B1 complete record. B1 used for future credit decisions.
  4. (d) Profit increases by $650
    B1.
Q8Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Tahir's draft profit for the year ended 30 September 2026 was $41,800. Trade receivables at that date were $35,900, before writing off a debt of $900. The provision for doubtful debts, previously $1,050, is to be 2.5% of the remaining trade receivables.

  1. (a) Calculate the new provision for doubtful debts. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the corrected profit for the year. [3]

  1. (a) $875
    35,900 − 900 = 35,000 (M1); 2.5% = 875 (A1).
  2. (b) $41,075
    41,800 − 900 (M1) + decrease in provision 1,050 − 875 = 175 (M1) = 41,075 (A1).
Q9Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A wholesaler of solar panels in Lagos bases its provision for doubtful debts on the age of each debt. At 31 December 2025:Debts up to 30 days old: $18,000 (provision 1%)Debts 31–60 days old: $7,500 (provision 4%)Debts over 60 days old: $2,400 (provision 10%)

  1. (a) Calculate the provision for doubtful debts required at 31 December 2025. [3]
  2. (b) The provision on 1 January 2025 was $900. State the amount and effect on the profit for 2025. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest two ways the business could reduce its irrecoverable debts in future. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $720
    180 + 300 + 240 (M1 for method, B1 two correct) = 720 (A1).
  2. (b) Decrease of $180; profit increases by $180
    900 − 720 = 180 (B1).
  3. (c) Any two: check credit references before giving credit; set credit limits; offer cash discounts for early payment; send statements and reminders promptly; stop supplying customers who pay late.
    1 mark each for any two.

  1. (a) Calculate the depreciation of loose tools for the year. [2]

Q13Depreciation and disposal of non-current assets · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Rashid & Co sold a delivery vehicle for $5,900 by cheque. The vehicle had cost $18,000 and accumulated depreciation on it was $11,200.

  1. (a) Calculate the net book value of the vehicle at the date of sale. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit or loss on disposal. [2]
  3. (c) List the entries in the disposal account. [3]

Q14Depreciation and disposal of non-current assets · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

On 1 January 2025 a laboratory in Karachi had equipment at cost of $40,000. On 1 July 2025 it bought more equipment for $12,000. Depreciation is 10% per annum on cost, calculated monthly from the date of purchase.

  1. (a) Calculate the depreciation charge on equipment for the year ended 31 December 2025. [3]
  2. (b) State how the equipment is shown in the statement of financial position, if accumulated depreciation at 31 December 2025 is $19,600. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $4,600
    40,000 × 10% = 4,000 (B1); 12,000 × 10% × 6/12 = 600 (M1); total 4,600 (A1).

    B1 cost 52,000. B1 NBV 32,400.

Q16Depreciation and disposal of non-current assets · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A textile mill bought a machine on 1 January 2023 for $32,000. Depreciation is 25% per annum by the reducing balance method; the financial year ends on 31 December, and no depreciation is charged in the year of sale. The machine was sold on 1 January 2026 for $14,200.

  1. (a) Calculate the depreciation for each of the years 2023, 2024 and 2025. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit or loss on disposal. [2]
  3. (c) State the entry for the profit on disposal in the disposal account and in the income statement. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2023 $8,000; 2024 $6,000; 2025 $4,500
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Profit $700
    NBV 32,000 − 18,500 = 13,500 (M1); 14,200 − 13,500 = 700 profit (A1).
  3. (c) Debit disposal account; shown as other income (added to gross profit) in the income statement
    B1.
Q17Depreciation and disposal of non-current assets · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Danish bought a delivery van for $50,000. He is choosing between the straight-line method (useful life 6 years, residual value $8,000) and the reducing balance method at 30% per annum.

  1. (a) Calculate the depreciation for the first two years under each method. [4]
  2. (b) State the difference in total profit for the first two years if reducing balance is used. [1]
  3. (c) Advise Danish which method to use. Justify your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Straight line $7,000 each year ($14,000 in total); reducing balance year 1 $15,000, year 2 $10,500 ($25,500 in total)
    B1 7,000; B1 15,000; B1 10,500; B1 totals.
  2. (b) Profit $11,500 lower
    25,500 − 14,000 = 11,500 (B1).
  3. (c) Reducing balance matches higher charges with the years when the delivery van is most productive and repairs are low, but lowers early profits; straight line is simpler and gives steady profits. Recommendation with reason, e.g. reducing balance because vans lose most value early. Once chosen, the method should be applied consistently.
    B1 point for each method (max 2). B1 reasoned recommendation.

Q20Accruals and prepayments · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Q25Valuation of inventory · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A sports shop in Sialkot has the following inventory at 31 December 2025:Item A: cost $2,400; expected selling price $3,100Item B: cost $1,800; slightly damaged, expected to sell for $1,500 after repairs of $200Item C: cost $950; expected selling price $1,100

  1. (a) Calculate the value of each item and the total value of closing inventory. [4]
  2. (b) Name the accounting principle applied and explain why it is applied. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A $2,400; B $1,300; C $950; total $4,650

    B1 prudence. B1 explanation.
Q26Valuation of inventory · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A pharmacy's year ended on 31 December 2025, but its inventory count was not done until 4 January 2026, when inventory at cost was $18,600. Between 1 and 4 January:- goods were sold for $2,000 (selling price is cost plus 25%)- goods costing $900 were received from suppliers- a customer returned goods sold for $250

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of goods sold between 1 and 4 January. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the value of inventory at 31 December 2025. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $1,600
    2,000 ÷ 1.25 = 1,600 (B1).
  2. (b) $19,100
    18,600 (B1 starting point) + 1,600 (B1) − 900 (B1) − 200 (cost of returns, 250 ÷ 1.25) = 19,100 (A1).
Q27Capital and revenue expenditure and receipts · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which of the following is capital expenditure for a printing company?A repairs to a printing pressB installation cost of a new printing pressC ink used during the yearD insurance of the factory [1]

  1. (a) B installation cost of a new printing press
    Installation is part of the cost of getting a new non-current asset ready for use, so it is capitalised. Repairs (A) only maintain an existing asset and are revenue expenditure.

Q30Capital and revenue expenditure and receipts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

On 1 January 2025 Farah bought a computer for her office for $3,600 and debited it to office expenses. Office equipment is depreciated at 25% per annum on cost.

  1. (a) Calculate the effect of the error on the profit for the year ended 31 December 2025. [3]
  2. (b) State the effect on non-current assets in the statement of financial position at 31 December 2025. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why it is important to distinguish between capital and revenue expenditure. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Profit understated by $2,700
    Expense overstated by 3,600 (M1); depreciation not charged 25% × 3,600 = 900 (M1); net understatement 2,700 (A1).
  2. (b) Understated by $2,700
    3,600 − 900 = 2,700 (B1).
  3. (c) So that profit is not misstated (revenue expenditure is charged to the income statement) and the statement of financial position shows a correct value of non-current assets.
    B1 profit. B1 asset values.
