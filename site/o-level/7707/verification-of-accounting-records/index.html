Q3Correction of errors and suspense accounts · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State two types of error that are not revealed by a trial balance. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a suspense account may be opened when a trial balance is prepared. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two of: omission, commission, principle, original entry, complete reversal, compensating errors.

Q4Correction of errors and suspense accounts · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) State whether an error of principle will cause the trial balance totals to disagree. Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) An amount is entered in the correct amount and on the correct side, but in the wrong class of account, e.g. capital expenditure entered in an expense account.

    1 mark: no. 1 mark: reason (equal debit and credit).

Show answer
  1. (a) C discount allowed of $50 was entered in the discount allowed account but not in the customer's account

Q7Correction of errors and suspense accounts · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The purchase of an office printer costing $900 was debited to the purchases account. Depreciation is ignored. What is the effect of this error? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A gross profit and profit for the year are both understated by $900
    Purchases are overstated by $900, so cost of sales is $900 too high, and gross profit and therefore profit are both $900 too low. B is wrong because a lower gross profit carries through to profit. Non-current assets are understated, not overstated.

Q14Correction of errors and suspense accounts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Kofi's trial balance on 31 March did not agree: the debit side totalled $86,420 and the credit side $85,950. The difference was entered in a suspense account and a draft profit for the year of $18,600 was calculated. The following errors were then found:1 The sales journal had been undercast by $300.2 Wages of $1,240 had been entered in the wages account as $1,420.3 Rent received of $250 had been entered in the bank account but omitted from the rent received account.4 Discount allowed of $85 had not been posted to the discount allowed account.5 A payment of $175 to Chen, a supplier, had been entered correctly in the cash book but not posted to Chen's account.

  1. (a) Prepare the suspense account, starting with the balance arising from the trial balance. [4]
  2. (b) Prepare a statement showing the corrected profit for the year. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Suspense: Cr balance b/d $470. Dr Sales $300, Dr Wages $180, Dr Rent received $250. Cr Discount allowed $85, Cr Chen $175. Totals $730 each side.
    Opening: debits exceed credits by 86420 − 85950 = 470, so a credit balance of 470 (1). Debit entries 300, 180 (1,420 − 1,240), 250 (1). Credit entries 85 and 175 (1). Both sides total 730.
  2. (b) $19,245
    Draft 18,600 + sales 300 (1) + wages 180 (1) + rent received 250 (1) − discount allowed 85 (1) = 19245. Error 5 affects only Chen's account and the suspense account, so there is no effect on profit.
Q15Correction of errors and suspense accounts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Bilal's draft financial statements for the year ended 31 December showed a profit of $27,400 and total current assets of $18,900. The following matters had not been dealt with:1 Insurance of $360 had been paid in advance for next year.2 A trade receivable of $520 was known to be irrecoverable and should be written off.3 Closing inventory included goods that cost $900 but could only be sold for $650.4 Commission of $430 was receivable but had not been received or recorded.5 Cash drawings of $600 had been debited to the wages account.

  1. (a) Calculate the corrected profit for the year. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the corrected total current assets. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $28,020
    27400 + 360 − 520 − 250 (900 − 650) + 430 + 600 = 28020. 1 mark for each two correct adjustments (max 3), 1 for the final figure.
  2. (b) $18,920
    18900 + 360 prepayment − 520 receivable − 250 inventory + 430 commission receivable = 18920. Item 5 does not affect current assets. 2 marks for adjustments, 1 for the final figure.
Q16Correction of errors and suspense accounts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

  1. (a) Prepare the journal entries to correct these errors. Narratives are not required. [6]
  2. (b) Calculate the balance in the suspense account before the corrections were made and state whether it was a debit or a credit balance. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 Dr Suspense $230; Cr Discount received $2302 Dr Sales returns $820; Cr Suspense $8203 Dr Suspense $450; Cr Fatima $450
    2 marks each. Error 2: the amount was entered on the wrong side, so twice the amount (2 × 410 = 820) is needed. Error 3: 1,500 − 1,050 = 450.

Q21Bank reconciliation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

For each item on the bank statement that is not yet in the cash book, state whether the cash book balance must be increased (debit the cash book) or decreased (credit the cash book).

  1. (a) Bank charges of $40 shown on the bank statement. [1]
  2. (b) A customer's cheque for $560 that has been dishonoured (returned unpaid by the bank). [1]
  3. (c) Interest of $75 credited by the bank. [1]
  4. (d) A standing order of $35 paid to a magazine publisher. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Decrease: credit the cash book $40.
    Bank charges are paid by the bank, so the cash book balance falls.
  2. (b) Decrease: credit the cash book $560.
    The bank has reversed the receipt, so the cash book balance falls. The customer's account must be debited again.
  3. (c) Increase: debit the cash book $75.
    Interest received increases the bank balance.
  4. (d) Decrease: credit the cash book $35.
    The bank has paid the amount, so the cash book balance is reduced.

Q25Bank reconciliation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Miriam's cash book shows a debit balance of $4,310 on 31 March. Her bank statement shows a balance of $5,090 in her favour on the same date. The difference is explained by:• unpresented cheques totalling $1,120• an outstanding lodgement of $620 (paid in but not yet credited by the bank)• bank interest of $280 credited by the bank but not yet recorded in the cash book

  1. (a) Calculate the updated cash book balance on 31 March. [3]
  2. (b) State the effect of the unpresented cheques on the bank statement balance compared with the cash book balance. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $4,590 debit
    4,310 + 280 interest = 4,590. Check: bank statement 5,090 = 4,590 + 1,120 unpresented − 620 outstanding lodgement.
  2. (b) The bank statement balance is higher, because the bank has not yet paid the unpresented cheques while the cash book has already deducted them.
    The cheques are already deducted in the cash book but not yet by the bank.
Q26Bank reconciliation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain why an unpresented cheque and an outstanding lodgement are treated in opposite ways in a bank reconciliation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) An unpresented cheque has been entered as a payment in the cash book but not yet paid by the bank, so the bank balance is higher. An outstanding lodgement has been entered as a receipt in the cash book but not yet credited by the bank, so the bank balance is lower.
    1 mark for the effect of an unpresented cheque on the bank balance; 1 mark for the effect of an outstanding lodgement.
Q27Bank reconciliation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the bank reconciliation statement should be prepared by someone other than the cashier who records the cash book. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The cashier handles cash and records the cash book, so one person should not both record and check the cash. An independent person reduces the risk of fraud or hidden errors.
    1 mark for separation of duties (internal control); 1 mark for explaining that an independent check detects fraud or errors.
Q28Bank reconciliation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

On 31 October the bank column of Soo-jin's cash book showed an overdraft of $1,460. Comparison with the bank statement showed:1 A standing order of $300 for rent had been paid by the bank but not entered in the cash book.2 Bank interest of $85 had been charged.3 A customer had paid $540 by credit transfer.4 A cheque for $910 sent to a supplier had not been presented.5 Takings of $1,275 paid in on 31 October had not been credited by the bank.

  1. (a) Calculate the updated cash book balance on 31 October. State whether it is a debit or a credit balance. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the balance shown on the bank statement on 31 October, stating whether it is an overdraft. [3]
  3. (c) State where the updated cash book balance will appear in the statement of financial position. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $1,305 credit (overdraft)
    −1,460 − 300 − 85 + 540 = -1305 (2 marks for adjustments), so an overdraft of 1305, a credit balance (1).
  2. (b) Overdraft of $1,670
    -1305 + 910 unpresented (1) − 1,275 not credited (1) = -1670, an overdraft of 1670 (1).
  3. (c) Under current liabilities (bank overdraft).
    1 mark.
