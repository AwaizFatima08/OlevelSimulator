Q1Cumulative frequency & box plots · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

The times, in minutes, that 15 students took to walk to a sports ground are listed in order:12, 14, 15, 17, 18, 20, 21, 23, 24, 26, 27, 29, 31, 33, 40

  1. (a) Find the median time. [1]
  2. (b) Find the lower quartile and the upper quartile. [2]
  3. (c) Find the interquartile range and the range. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 23 minutes
    n = 15, median is the 8th value = 23. B1
  2. (b) LQ = 17, UQ = 29
    LQ = 4th value = 17 B1; UQ = 12th value = 29 B1
  3. (c) IQR = 12 minutes, range = 28 minutes
    29 − 17 = 12 B1; 40 − 12 = 28 B1
Q2Cumulative frequency & box plots · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks

Box plots show the midday temperatures, in °C, in two cities during one month.City A: minimum 12, lower quartile 18, median 23, upper quartile 27, maximum 35.City B: minimum 20, lower quartile 24, median 26, upper quartile 28, maximum 31.

  1. (a) Find the range and the interquartile range for each city. [2]
  2. (b) Make two comparisons between the temperatures in the two cities. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) City A: range 23, IQR 9. City B: range 11, IQR 4.
    A: 35 − 12 = 23, 27 − 18 = 9; B: 31 − 20 = 11, 28 − 24 = 4. B2 (B1 for two correct)
  2. (b) City B had a higher median (26 °C against 23 °C), so it was generally warmer; City B had a smaller IQR (and range), so its temperatures were more consistent.
    B1 compares medians in context; B1 compares IQR or range in context.
Q3Cumulative frequency & box plots · Standard · Paper 2 style · calculator · 7 marks

The masses, m kg, of 80 parcels are recorded.0 < m ≤ 2: 62 < m ≤ 4: 144 < m ≤ 6: 286 < m ≤ 8: 208 < m ≤ 10: 12

  1. (a) Complete a cumulative frequency table for m ≤ 2, m ≤ 4, m ≤ 6, m ≤ 8 and m ≤ 10. [2]
  2. (b) Assuming the parcels are spread evenly within each class, estimate the median mass. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) In the same way, estimate the interquartile range. [2]
  4. (d) Estimate the number of parcels with a mass greater than 7 kg. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6, 20, 48, 68, 80
    Running totals B2 (B1 for 3 correct)
  2. (b) 5.43 kg
    40th parcel lies in 4 < m ≤ 6: 4 + (40 − 20)/28 × 2 M1 = 5.43 A1
  3. (c) 3.2 kg
    LQ = 20th = 4; UQ = 60th = 6 + (60 − 48)/20 × 2 = 7.2 M1; IQR = 3.2 A1
  4. (d) 22
    20/2 + 12 = 22 B1
Q4Cumulative frequency & box plots · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A cricketer's scores in 12 innings are:4, 31, 17, 0, 52, 23, 9, 45, 12, 68, 27, 38

  1. (a) Find the median. [1]
  2. (b) The lower quartile is the median of the lowest six scores and the upper quartile is the median of the highest six. Find the interquartile range. [3]
  3. (c) In the next innings the cricketer scores 30. Does the median increase, decrease or stay the same? Give a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25
    Order the data; (23 + 27) ÷ 2 B1
  2. (b) LQ = 10.5, UQ = 41.5, IQR = 31
    LQ (9 + 12)/2 = 10.5 B1; UQ (38 + 45)/2 = 41.5 B1; 31 B1
  3. (c) It increases, from 25 to 27: there are now 13 scores and the middle (7th) one is 27.
    B1 (increases, with reason)

Q6Cumulative frequency & box plots · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 8 marks

200 students took a test. Points on their cumulative frequency curve are (20, 18), (30, 50), (40, 100), (50, 150), (60, 182) and (70, 200), where the first number is the mark and the second is the cumulative frequency. Assume the curve is a straight line between these points.

  1. (a) Find the median, the lower quartile and the interquartile range of the marks. [3]
  2. (b) The lowest 30% of students must retake the test. Estimate the highest mark of a student who must retake it. [2]
  3. (c) Estimate the percentage of students who scored more than 55 marks. [2]
  4. (d) For a second group of students, the median mark is 44 and the interquartile range is 14. Make two comparisons between the two groups. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Median 40, LQ 30, IQR 20
    100th → 40 B1; 50th → 30 B1; 150th → 50, so IQR = 20 B1
  2. (b) 32
    30% of 200 = 60 M1; 30 + (60 − 50)/50 × 10 = 32 A1
  3. (c) 17%
    CF at 55 = 150 + 32/2 = 166 M1; 34/200 = 17% A1
  4. (d) Second group did better on average (higher median) and their marks were less spread out (smaller IQR).
    Both comparisons in context B1

  2. (b) What fraction of the girls travel by bus? [1]
  3. (c) What fraction of all the students travel by car? Give your answer in its simplest form. [2]

Q26Charts & tables · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

A pie chart shows how people in a town travel to work. The four sector angles are x°, 2x°, (x + 30)° and 90°.

  1. (a) Find x. [2]
  2. (b) The pie chart represents 480 people. How many people are in the sector with angle 2x°? [2]
  3. (c) In a second pie chart for a different town, 72 people are represented by a sector of 54°. How many people does the whole of this second pie chart represent? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 60
    x + 2x + (x + 30) + 90 = 360 M1; 4x = 240, x = 60 A1
  2. (b) 160
    120/360 × 480 M1; 160 A1
  3. (c) 480
    72 ÷ 54 × 360 M1; 480 A1

    B1

  3. (c) 62.25

Q28Scatter diagrams · Standard · Paper 2 style · calculator · 7 marks

A café records the midday temperature (°C) and the number of cups of hot chocolate sold on 8 days.Temperature: 2, 5, 8, 10, 13, 15, 18, 20Cups sold: 88, 80, 71, 30, 58, 52, 44, 38

  1. (a) One day does not fit the pattern. Write down its temperature. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the correlation and what it means in context. [2]
  3. (c) A line of best fit passes through (0, 94) and (20, 38). Find its equation in the form y = mx + c. [2]
  4. (d) Use your line to estimate the number of cups sold when the temperature is 12 °C, and the temperature when 66 cups are sold. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10 °C
    (10, 30) is the outlier B1
  2. (b) Negative correlation: as temperature rises, fewer cups are sold.
    Negative B1; context B1
  3. (c) y = −2.8x + 94
    Gradient (38 − 94)/20 = −2.8 M1; A1
  4. (d) 60 cups; 10 °C
    94 − 33.6 = 60.4 ≈ 60 B1; (94 − 66)/2.8 = 10 B1

The table shows the practice time (x hours per week) and typing speed (y words per minute) of 6 people.x: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 10y: 14, 19, 22, 25, 29, 35

  1. (a) Calculate the mean practice time and the mean typing speed. [2]
  2. (b) The line of best fit passes through the mean point and the point (0, 9). Find its equation. [2]
  3. (c) Use the line to estimate the typing speed of a person who practises for 9 hours a week. [1]
  4. (d) Describe the strength and type of correlation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (6, 24)
    36 ÷ 6 = 6 B1; 144 ÷ 6 = 24 B1
  2. (b) y = 2.5x + 9
    Gradient (24 − 9)/6 = 2.5 M1; A1
  3. (c) 31.5 words per minute
    2.5 × 9 + 9 B1
  4. (d) Strong positive
    B1
Q30Scatter diagrams · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 8 marks

The table shows the engine size (litres) and fuel economy (km per litre) of 7 cars.Engine size: 1.0, 1.3, 1.5, 1.8, 2.0, 2.4, 3.0Fuel economy: 18.5, 16.8, 15.4, 13.9, 12.6, 11.0, 8.3

  1. (a) Describe the correlation and what it means in context. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean engine size and the mean fuel economy. Give answers correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) The line of best fit passes through the mean point and the point (1.0, 18.4). Find the gradient of the line, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  4. (d) Use the line to estimate the fuel economy of a car with a 2.2 litre engine, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  5. (e) Explain why the line should not be used for a car with a 5.0 litre engine. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Strong negative correlation: cars with larger engines travel fewer kilometres per litre.
    B1
  2. (b) 1.86 litres and 13.8 km per litre
    B1 B1
  3. (c) −5.38
    (13.79 − 18.4)/(1.857 − 1.0) M1 A1
  4. (d) 11.9 km per litre
    18.4 + 1.2 × gradient M1 A1
  5. (e) 5.0 litres is outside the range of the data (extrapolation), so the trend may not continue.
    B1
