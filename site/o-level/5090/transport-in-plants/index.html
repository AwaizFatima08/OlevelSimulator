Q1Transpiration & factors · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) In which set of conditions will a sunflower plant lose water vapour fastest?A cool, humid, still airB warm, humid, windyC warm, dry, windyD cool, dry, still air [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C warm, dry, windy
    High temperature increases evaporation, low humidity gives a steep water vapour concentration gradient and wind removes water vapour from around the leaf. B is humid, which reduces the gradient.
Q2Transpiration & factors · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the term transpiration. [2]
  2. (b) Name the pores in the leaf through which most water vapour leaves. [1]
  3. (c) State one way in which transpiration is useful to a plant. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Loss of water vapour from the leaves (by evaporation of water at the surfaces of the mesophyll cells) followed by diffusion of water vapour through the stomata.
    B1 loss of water vapour from leaves / aerial parts; B1 evaporation from mesophyll cell surfaces and diffusion out through stomata
  2. (b) Stomata
    B1
  3. (c) Pulls water and mineral ions up the xylem / cools the leaves / supplies water for photosynthesis and support.
    B1 any one

Q7Transpiration & factors · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the rate of transpiration increases when the temperature rises. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the rate of transpiration decreases on a humid day in Karachi. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water molecules gain kinetic energy, so more water evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces and water vapour diffuses out faster.
    B1 more evaporation / molecules move faster; B1 faster diffusion of water vapour out of stomata
  2. (b) The air outside the leaf already contains much water vapour, so the concentration gradient between the air spaces and the outside air is smaller and diffusion is slower.
    B1 high water vapour concentration outside; B1 smaller concentration gradient so slower diffusion
Q8Transpiration & factors · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of water lost in 6 hours. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the starting mass that was lost. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the mean rate of water loss in g per hour. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the pot and soil were covered with a plastic bag. [1]

    B1 412.0 − 397.6
  2. (b) 3.50 %

  3. (c) 2.4 g/h
    B1 14.4 ÷ 6
  4. (d) So that mass lost is due to transpiration from the plant only, not evaporation from the soil.
    B1

Q12Transpiration & factors · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

On a very hot afternoon in Multan in June, a farmer notices that his cotton plants have drooping leaves, even though the soil is slightly damp. By the next morning the plants look normal again.

  1. (a) Explain why the plants wilted. [3]
  2. (b) When wilting occurs, the stomata close. Give one advantage and one disadvantage to the plant of closing its stomata. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water was lost by transpiration faster than it was absorbed by the roots; cells lost water and became flaccid (lost turgor); so the leaves and stems were no longer supported.
    B1 transpiration rate greater than water uptake; B1 cells lose turgor / become flaccid; B1 loss of support
  2. (b) Advantage: reduces water loss. Disadvantage: less carbon dioxide enters, so less photosynthesis (also less cooling / less mineral transport).
    B1 advantage; B1 disadvantage

Q19Water uptake & root hairs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A farmer in Faisalabad added far too much fertiliser to a field of young wheat plants. A few days later the plants wilted, although the soil was moist.

  1. (a) Use the term water potential to explain why the plants wilted. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest what the farmer should do to help the plants recover. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The fertiliser made the soil solution very concentrated, with a lower water potential than the root cells; water moved out of the root cells into the soil by osmosis; so less water reached the leaves and cells lost turgor.
    B1 soil water potential lower than root cells; B1 water leaves root cells by osmosis / cannot enter; B1 cells / plant lose turgor
  2. (b) Water the field heavily to dilute the fertiliser.
    B1
Q20Water uptake & root hairs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how water moves from the root hair cell to the xylem. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how water moves up the xylem to the leaves. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) By osmosis from cell to cell across the root cortex, down a water potential gradient (each cell has a lower water potential than the one before).
    B1 through cortex cells; B1 osmosis / water potential gradient
  2. (b) Water evaporates from mesophyll cells and is lost by transpiration; this creates a pull (tension) on the water in the xylem; water molecules stick together (cohesion) so a continuous column is drawn up.
    B1 transpiration / evaporation at leaves; B1 creates pull / suction; B1 water molecules stick together / continuous column

Q22Xylem & phloem structure · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks

Q25Translocation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks

Q27Translocation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) In spring a stored potato tuber starts to sprout new shoots. Explain why the tuber acts as a source at this time. [2]
  2. (b) Later in summer the potato plant forms new tubers. Explain why the new tubers are sinks. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why translocation can occur in both directions in a plant, unlike water transport. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stored starch is broken down to sucrose; the sucrose is transported in phloem to the growing shoots (sinks), which cannot yet photosynthesise enough.
    B1 starch → sucrose released; B1 transported to growing shoots / sinks
  2. (b) Sucrose from the leaves is transported to them and converted to starch for storage.
    B1 receive sucrose from leaves; B1 stored as starch
  3. (c) Sources and sinks can be above or below each other (and change with season), so phloem carries substances up and down.
    B1
Q28Translocation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A ring of bark, including the phloem, was carefully removed all the way round the trunk of a young apple tree. After some weeks the bark just above the ring had swollen.

  1. (a) Explain why the bark above the ring swelled. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the leaves above the ring did not wilt for several weeks. [2]
  3. (c) Predict what would happen to the roots after a long time, and explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The ring removed the phloem (which is in the bark); sucrose moving down from the leaves could not pass the ring; sucrose built up above the ring, and the extra sugar caused water to enter / extra growth, so the bark swelled.
    B1 phloem removed; B1 sucrose transport (down from leaves) blocked; B1 accumulation above ring
  2. (b) The xylem, which is deeper in the stem (wood), was not removed, so water still reached the leaves.
    B1 xylem intact / inside the bark; B1 water still transported up
  3. (c) The roots would die; they receive no sucrose for respiration, so they cannot release energy (and eventually the whole tree dies).
    B1 roots die / stop growing; B1 no sugar for respiration
Q29Transpiration & factors (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A student hung a cut leafy shoot of a neem tree from a balance with its cut end in a sealed flask of water (the water surface was covered with oil). She recorded the mass every hour, first in still air and then with a fan blowing.

  1. (a) Describe how the student's results could be used to show the effect of the fan. [2]
  2. (b) Without the fan the shoot lost 1.8 g in 3 hours. With the fan it lost 3.3 g in 3 hours. Calculate the percentage increase in the rate of water loss caused by the fan. [2]
  3. (c) Explain fully how water moves from the xylem in a leaf to the air outside, and why moving air speeds this up. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Compare the mass lost per hour with and without the fan; the rate with the fan is higher.
    B1 compare rates / mass loss; B1 under the same other conditions
  2. (b) 83.3 %
    M1 (3.3 − 1.8) ÷ 1.8 × 100; A1 83.3 %
  3. (c) Water passes from xylem into mesophyll cells by osmosis; it evaporates from the surfaces of mesophyll cells into air spaces; water vapour diffuses out through stomata down a concentration gradient; moving air removes water vapour near the stomata, keeping the gradient steep.
    1 mark each for four: osmosis into mesophyll; evaporation from cell surfaces into air spaces; diffusion of water vapour through stomata; down a concentration gradient; wind removes water vapour / maintains gradient
Q30Transpiration & factors (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Measurements on a rice plant in a field in Punjab on a clear summer day showed that water loss rose from 8 am to a peak at 2 pm and then fell. Water uptake by the roots followed a similar pattern but reached its peak about an hour later.

  1. (a) Explain why water loss from the plant is greater at 2 pm than at 8 am. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the rate of water uptake by roots lags behind the rate of transpiration in the early afternoon. [2]
  3. (c) State two ways in which a hot, dry climate could be a problem for a crop such as rice, which needs a lot of water. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Higher light intensity, so more stomata open wider; higher temperature, so faster evaporation and diffusion; lower humidity at midday, so steeper concentration gradient.
    1 mark each for three of: more light so stomata open wider; higher temperature, more evaporation; molecules have more kinetic energy / diffuse faster; lower humidity / steeper gradient
  2. (b) Water is first lost from the leaves, creating the pull; it takes time for the pull to be transmitted down the xylem and for water to cross the root, so uptake reaches its peak later.
    B1 loss from leaves comes first / drives uptake; B1 time for pull to travel / delay in uptake
  3. (c) Plants may wilt / lose turgor; stomata close so less photosynthesis and lower yield; more irrigation water needed.
    1 mark each for any two
