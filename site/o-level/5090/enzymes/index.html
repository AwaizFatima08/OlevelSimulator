Q1Enzyme investigations · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student in Lahore placed 2 g of grated potato into each of four boiling tubes and added 10 cm³ of hydrogen peroxide solution of different concentrations. Potato contains the enzyme catalase. After 2 minutes she measured the height of the foam. She did three trials at each concentration.Concentration / %: 5 | 10 | 15 | 20Trial 1 foam height / mm: 14 | 27 | 41 | 52Trial 2 foam height / mm: 16 | 29 | 22 | 55Trial 3 foam height / mm: 15 | 31 | 43 | 55

  1. (a) Identify the anomalous result in the table. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean foam height for 15 % hydrogen peroxide, leaving out the anomalous result. [1]
  3. (c) Describe the relationship between the concentration of hydrogen peroxide and the height of the foam. [2]
  4. (d) Name the gas that forms the bubbles in the foam. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 22 mm (15 % hydrogen peroxide, trial 2)
    B1 22 mm at 15 % – far below the other two trials (41 and 43 mm).
  2. (b) 42.0 mm
    (41 + 43) ÷ 2 = 42.0 mm. B1
  3. (c) As the concentration increases, the foam height increases steadily (roughly linear: mean heights 15, 29, 42 and 54 mm, about 12–14 mm more for every 5 % increase).
    B1 higher concentration → taller foam; B1 quoting data, e.g. mean 15 mm at 5 % rising to 54 mm at 20 %, or 'steady/roughly linear increase'. Do not accept 'directly proportional' (the line would not pass through the origin).
  4. (d) oxygen
    B1 oxygen (catalase breaks hydrogen peroxide into water and oxygen).

Q3Enzyme investigations · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Three tubes were kept at 35 °C for 10 minutes and then tested with iodine solution.Tube A: 5 cm³ starch + 1 cm³ amylase → yellow-brownTube B: 5 cm³ starch + 1 cm³ boiled and cooled amylase → blue-blackTube C: 5 cm³ starch + 1 cm³ distilled water → blue-black

  1. (a) Explain the result in tube A. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why tube B stayed blue-black. [2]
  3. (c) State the purpose of tube C. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Amylase digested the starch (to maltose/reducing sugar), so no starch was left to turn iodine blue-black.
    B1 starch broken down by amylase; B1 so no starch present / iodine stays yellow-brown.
  2. (b) Boiling denatured the amylase, so its active site changed shape and it could not digest starch; starch remained.
    B1 boiled enzyme denatured; B1 starch not digested / still present.
  3. (c) A control to show that starch does not break down by itself (without enzyme).
    B1 control / starch does not break down without amylase.

  2. (b) State what is wrong with writing '45 s' in each cell of the table. [1]

Q8Enzyme investigations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

In Karachi a laundry tested a biological washing powder and a non-biological washing powder on cotton cloths stained with egg and with cooking oil, at 40 °C and at 90 °C.

  1. (a) Name the enzymes in the biological powder that digest (i) an egg stain and (ii) a cooking-oil stain. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the biological powder removed more of the stains at 40 °C than the non-biological powder. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the biological powder was no better than the non-biological powder at 90 °C. [2]

Show answer
  1. (a) (i) protease; (ii) lipase

Q25Effect of temperature · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe and explain how the rate of an enzyme-controlled reaction changes as temperature rises from 10 °C to 70 °C, for an enzyme with an optimum of 40 °C. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rate rises from 10 °C to 40 °C because molecules have more kinetic energy and collide more often, forming more enzyme–substrate complexes; maximum at the optimum (40 °C); above 40 °C rate falls quickly as the enzyme is denatured, active site changes shape, substrate no longer fits; at about 70 °C there is no activity.
    1 mark each, max 5: rate increases to 40 °C; more kinetic energy; more collisions / more enzyme–substrate complexes; fastest at optimum; rate falls above optimum; denaturation; active site shape changed / substrate cannot fit; no activity at high temperature / irreversible.
