Q3Conservation & sustainability · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement describes a sustainable resource? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A a resource that is produced as rapidly as it is removed from the environment, so it does not run out
    A sustainable resource is replaced as fast as it is used (e.g. managed forest, fish stocks with quotas). Coal (D) is non-renewable on a human time scale; B is wrong because any living resource can be over-used.

Q9Conservation & sustainability · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage germination in this test. [1]
  2. (b) Seeds in the bank are dried and stored at about −20 °C. Explain why. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the seed bank tests a sample of each type of seed every few years. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest two advantages of conserving plant species as stored seeds rather than as growing plants in a botanic garden. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 82 %
    B1 41 ÷ 50 × 100 = 82 %.
  2. (b) Low water content and low temperature slow the seed's respiration and enzyme activity (and stop mould growth), so the seeds stay alive for many years.

    B1.
  4. (d) Seeds take up very little space, so many species and varieties can be stored; they stay alive for many years with little care; they are safe from pests, diseases and bad weather.
    1 mark each for any two: little space / many samples; long storage life / low cost; protected from disease, pests or natural disasters.

Q14Conservation & sustainability · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Scientists estimate that the stock of one kind of fish in the Arabian Sea is 50 000 tonnes and that, without fishing, it would increase by 12 % each year.

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of fish that can be caught each year without reducing the stock. [1]
  2. (b) The fishing fleet actually catches 9000 tonnes each year. Calculate the stock at the end of the first year and at the end of the second year, assuming the growth rate stays at 12 %. [3]

Show answer
  1. (a) 6000 tonnes
    B1 12 % of 50 000 = 6000.
