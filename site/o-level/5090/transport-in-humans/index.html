Q1Blood components · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is the function of platelets? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C helping blood to clot
    Platelets are cell fragments involved in clotting, which seals wounds. Engulfing bacteria (D) is done by phagocytes, a type of white blood cell.
Q2Blood components · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which part of the blood carries most of the urea from the liver to the kidneys? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A plasma
    Urea is dissolved in the plasma and carried to the kidneys. Red blood cells (C) carry oxygen bound to haemoglobin, not urea.

Q4Blood components · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A blood test report from a laboratory in Karachi for a 16-year-old girl, Ayesha, shows a low haemoglobin level and fewer red blood cells than normal. A normal sample contains about 4 500 000 red blood cells and 7500 white blood cells in each mm³.

  1. (a) Calculate how many red blood cells there are for every white blood cell in a normal sample. [1]
  2. (b) Ayesha's haemoglobin level is low. Explain why this makes her tired and short of breath when she climbs stairs. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest one mineral that should be increased in her diet and name one food that contains it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 600
    A1 4 500 000 ÷ 7500 = 600.
  2. (b) Less haemoglobin means less oxygen carried to the muscles; less aerobic respiration, so less energy released; she breathes faster / deeper to try to get more oxygen.
    B1 less oxygen transported (as oxyhaemoglobin); B1 less aerobic respiration / energy in muscles; B1 breathing rate increases to compensate.
  3. (c) Iron; e.g. red meat, liver, spinach, lentils (daal), eggs.
    B1 iron; B1 suitable food.
Q5Blood components · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a phagocyte destroys a bacterium. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the appearance of a phagocyte and a lymphocyte differ when seen in a stained blood smear. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how lymphocytes help to protect the body against a pathogen. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It surrounds and engulfs the bacterium into a vacuole (phagocytosis); enzymes digest it.
    B1 engulfs; B1 digests with enzymes.
  2. (b) Phagocyte: lobed (irregular) nucleus with granular cytoplasm, larger. Lymphocyte: large round nucleus filling most of the cell, little cytoplasm.
    B1 phagocyte lobed nucleus; B1 lymphocyte large round nucleus / little cytoplasm.
  3. (c) They produce antibodies; antibodies bind to antigens on the pathogen, marking it for destruction or making it clump.
    B1 produce antibodies; B1 antibodies specific to antigen / lead to destruction of pathogen.

  3. (c) Suggest why it is important that fibrinogen is soluble while it is carried in the plasma. [1]

    B1.
Q7Blood components · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A long-distance runner trained for four weeks at about 2200 m above sea level in the mountains of Gilgit-Baltistan. Her red blood cell count rose from 5.0 million per mm³ to 5.6 million per mm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in red blood cell count. [2]
  2. (b) There is less oxygen in each breath of air at high altitude. Explain how the change in red blood cell count helps the athlete. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the athlete performs better in a race at sea level for a few weeks after returning. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12 %
    M1 (5.6 − 5.0) ÷ 5.0 × 100; A1 12.
  2. (b) More red blood cells means more haemoglobin, so more oxygen can be carried in the blood to the muscles.
    B1 more haemoglobin; B1 more oxygen carried / delivered for respiration.
  3. (c) The extra red blood cells remain for some time; muscles receive more oxygen, more aerobic respiration, less lactic acid / less fatigue.
    B1 red cell count still high; B1 more aerobic respiration / less anaerobic respiration.

Q9Coronary heart disease & exercise · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe what happens in the coronary arteries in coronary heart disease. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a blocked coronary artery can cause a heart attack. [3]
  3. (c) Give two risk factors for coronary heart disease that a person can change. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fatty deposits (cholesterol) build up in the walls of the coronary arteries, narrowing the lumen; a blood clot may form and block the artery.
    B1 fatty / cholesterol deposits; B1 lumen narrowed or blocked / clot forms.
  2. (b) Less blood reaches part of the heart muscle; so less oxygen and glucose; the muscle cannot respire aerobically, cannot release enough energy and stops contracting / dies.
    B1 reduced blood flow to heart muscle; B1 less oxygen (and glucose); B1 less aerobic respiration / energy so muscle stops / dies.
  3. (c) Any two of: smoking, diet high in saturated fat / salt, lack of exercise, stress, obesity, high alcohol intake.
    B1 each.
Q10Coronary heart disease & exercise · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A study followed 500 men aged 40–50 in Lahore for ten years. 300 were non-smokers and 200 smoked. Of the non-smokers, 12 developed coronary heart disease. Of the smokers, 22 developed it.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of non-smokers and of smokers who developed coronary heart disease. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate how many times more likely a smoker in this study was to develop the disease than a non-smoker. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest two reasons why the study cannot prove that smoking causes coronary heart disease. [2]
  4. (d) Name one substance in tobacco smoke that increases the risk of coronary heart disease. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) non-smokers 4.0 %; smokers 11.0 %
    A1 12 ÷ 300 × 100 = 4.0; A1 22 ÷ 200 × 100 = 11.0.
  2. (b) 2.75 times
    A1 11.0 ÷ 4.0 = 2.75.
  3. (c) Other factors were not controlled (diet, exercise, stress, family history, age); small sample; only men from one city; correlation does not show cause.
    B1 each for any two.
  4. (d) nicotine or carbon monoxide
    B1.
Q11Coronary heart disease & exercise · Standard · Paper 4 style · 9 marks

A student sat still for 5 minutes and counted his pulse for 15 seconds: 18 beats. He then did step-ups for 3 minutes and counted his pulse for 15 seconds at the start of each minute after stopping.time after exercise / min: 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5beats in 15 s: 32, 28, 24, 21, 19, 18

  1. (a) Convert each count to a pulse rate in beats per minute. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage increase in pulse rate immediately after exercise compared with rest. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) State how long it took for the pulse rate to return to the resting rate. [1]

  5. (e) Suggest one way to make the pulse measurement more accurate. [1]

  1. (a) rest 72; after exercise: 128, 112, 96, 84, 76, 72 beats per minute
    M1 multiply each count by 4; A1 all correct.
  2. (b) 77.8 %

  3. (c) 5 minutes
    B1.
  4. (d) Muscles respire faster and need more oxygen and glucose; a faster heart rate delivers more blood; and removes carbon dioxide (and lactic acid) faster.

  5. (e) Count for a full 60 seconds; repeat and take a mean; use a pulse sensor / heart-rate monitor.

Q13Heart structure & function · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In which order does blood pass through these structures, starting from the vena cava? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs

Q14Heart structure & function · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Name the four chambers of the heart. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle. [2]
  3. (c) State the function of the septum. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the walls of the atria are thinner than the walls of the ventricles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) left atrium, right atrium, left ventricle, right ventricle
    B2 all four (B1 for two or three).
  2. (b) The left ventricle pumps blood all round the body (over a longer distance) at higher pressure; the right ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs.

  3. (c) It separates oxygenated blood (left side) from deoxygenated blood (right side).
    B1.
  4. (d) Atria only pump blood a short distance into the ventricles.
    B1.
Q15Heart structure & function · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A doctor listening with a stethoscope hears two sounds in each heartbeat, often written as 'lub-dub'. Explain what causes each sound. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the function of the semilunar valves. [2]
  3. (c) A patient has a leaking valve between the left atrium and left ventricle. Suggest one effect on the patient and why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 'Lub': the atrioventricular valves closing as the ventricles contract. 'Dub': the semilunar valves in the aorta and pulmonary artery closing as the ventricles relax.
    B1 first sound AV valves close; B1 second sound semilunar valves close.
  2. (b) They stop blood flowing back from the arteries (aorta / pulmonary artery) into the ventricles when the ventricles relax.
    B1 prevent backflow; B1 from arteries into ventricles.
  3. (c) Some blood flows back into the atrium when the ventricle contracts, so less oxygenated blood reaches the body; patient gets tired / breathless easily.
    B1 backflow / less blood pumped to body; B1 linked effect (tiredness, breathlessness).

Q17Blood vessels · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which blood vessel contains valves along its length? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D vein in the leg
    Veins carry blood at low pressure and have valves to prevent backflow. The aorta (A) is an artery with high pressure and no valves along its length (only the semilunar valve at its base).
Q18Blood vessels · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give three differences between the structure of an artery and a vein. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how the thick, elastic wall of an artery is related to its function. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Artery: thick wall, vein: thin wall; artery: narrow lumen, vein: wide lumen; artery: more muscle and elastic tissue; vein: has valves, artery does not.

Q19Blood vessels · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Capillary walls are one cell thick. Explain why this is important. [2]
  2. (b) The lumen of a capillary is about 8 µm wide and a red blood cell is about 7.5 µm wide. Suggest how this helps oxygen reach the tissues. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why most cells in the body are close to a capillary. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Short diffusion distance, so substances such as oxygen and glucose diffuse quickly between blood and tissues.
    B1 short distance; B1 fast diffusion / exchange.
  2. (b) Red cells pass in single file, pressed close to the capillary wall, so oxygen travels a very short distance; they move slowly, giving more time for exchange.
    B1 red cells close to wall / short distance; B1 slow flow / more time / single file.
  3. (c) Cells need a constant supply of oxygen and glucose and removal of carbon dioxide and wastes; diffusion is only fast over short distances.
    B1 supply / removal; B1 diffusion only effective over short distance.

Q22Circulatory system & double circulation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a double circulation. [2]
  2. (b) A fish has a single circulation. Explain one advantage of a double circulation to a mammal. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why blood going to the lungs is kept at lower pressure than blood going to the body. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Blood passes through the heart twice in one complete circuit of the body: once through the pulmonary circulation (heart to lungs and back) and once through the systemic circulation (heart to body and back).
    B1 passes through heart twice per circuit; B1 pulmonary and systemic circuits named or described.
  2. (b) Blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again at high pressure to the body; so oxygen is delivered faster to respiring tissues (supporting a high metabolic rate / keeping warm).
    B1 blood re-pressurised after lungs / high pressure to body; B1 faster delivery of oxygen.
  3. (c) High pressure could damage the delicate capillaries of the lungs (and the lungs are close to the heart).
    B1.

  2. (b) In a double circulation, blood from the body returns to the right side of the heart and must go to the lungs before returning to the left side, which pumps to the body.
    B1.

Q25Blood components (option) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Q27Lymph & tissue fluid · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how tissue fluid is formed. [2]
  2. (b) State two functions of tissue fluid. [2]
  3. (c) Describe what happens to the tissue fluid that does not return to the capillaries. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) High blood pressure at the arterial end of a capillary forces plasma (water and small dissolved substances) out through the capillary wall; this fluid surrounds the cells.
    B1 pressure forces plasma out of capillaries; B1 fluid (without red cells / large proteins) bathes the cells.
  2. (b) It carries oxygen, glucose and other nutrients to cells; it carries carbon dioxide and other wastes away from cells.
    B1 each.
  3. (c) It drains into lymph vessels and becomes lymph; it is returned to the blood in veins near the heart.
    B1 enters lymphatic vessels as lymph; B1 returned to blood.
Q28Lymph & tissue fluid · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In an adult, about 20 dm³ of fluid leaves the blood capillaries each day to form tissue fluid. About 17 dm³ of this returns directly into the capillaries.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the fluid that returns to the blood through the lymphatic system each day. [2]
  2. (b) In a disease spread by mosquitoes, small worms block lymph vessels in the legs. The legs become very swollen. Explain why. [2]
  3. (c) Lymph passes through lymph nodes. State the function of the lymph nodes. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15 %
    M1 (20 − 17) ÷ 20 × 100; A1 15.
  2. (b) Tissue fluid cannot drain into the lymph vessels; fluid builds up in the tissues, causing swelling.
    B1 lymph cannot drain; B1 fluid accumulates in tissues.
  3. (c) They contain lymphocytes (and phagocytes) that destroy pathogens / produce antibodies, filtering the lymph.
    B1 contain lymphocytes; B1 destroy pathogens / produce antibodies.
Q29Blood vessels (option) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Mean blood pressure in different vessels (kPa): aorta 13.3; large artery 12.0; capillary 4.0; vein 1.3; vena cava 0.3.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage fall in pressure between the aorta and the capillaries. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why it is important that pressure in the capillaries is much lower than in the arteries. [2]
  3. (c) Name the blood vessel that carries:(i) blood rich in absorbed food from the small intestine to the liver(ii) blood to the kidney(iii) oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 70 %
    M1 (13.3 − 4.0) ÷ 13.3 × 100; A1 70.
  2. (b) Capillary walls are very thin and would be damaged by high pressure; low pressure / slow flow gives time for exchange of substances.
    B1 thin walls would burst; B1 slow flow allows time for exchange.
  3. (c) (i) hepatic portal vein (ii) renal artery (iii) pulmonary vein
    B1 each.
Q30Blood vessels (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Blood in the veins of the legs must travel upwards to the heart at low pressure. Explain how this happens. [4]
  2. (b) A soldier standing still on guard duty for a long time on a hot day may faint. Suggest why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Veins have valves that stop blood flowing backwards; contracting leg (skeletal) muscles squeeze the veins and push blood along; the valves make sure it moves only towards the heart; wide lumen gives low resistance to flow.
    B1 valves prevent backflow; B1 skeletal muscles squeeze veins; B1 one-way flow towards heart; B1 wide lumen / low resistance.
  2. (b) Without leg muscle movement, blood collects in the leg veins; less blood returns to the heart, so less is pumped to the brain; the brain receives less oxygen (and glucose), causing fainting.
    B1 leg muscles not squeezing veins / blood pools in legs; B1 less returned to heart / pumped to brain; B1 less oxygen to brain.
