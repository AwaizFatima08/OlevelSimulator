Practice questions

Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Classification & keys · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which characteristic of living organisms is defined as 'a permanent increase in size and dry mass'?A growthB nutritionC reproductionD excretion [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A growth
    This is the definition of growth. Nutrition (B) is taking in materials for energy, growth and development.
Q2Classification & keys · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which shows the correct way to write the scientific name of the house sparrow in the binomial system?A Passer DomesticusB passer domesticusC Passer domesticusD PASSER domesticus [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C Passer domesticus
    The genus has a capital first letter and the species name is all lower case (printed in italics or underlined when handwritten). A wrongly capitalises the species name.
Q3Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the seven characteristics of living organisms. [3]
  2. (b) A car moves, uses fuel and gives out waste gases, but it is not alive. State two characteristics of living organisms that a car does not show. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion, nutrition
    3 marks for all seven; 2 marks for five or six; 1 mark for three or four.
  2. (b) growth and reproduction
    B1 growth; B1 reproduction. Accept sensitivity (detecting and responding to stimuli) as one of the two.
Q4Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define excretion. [2]
  2. (b) Define sensitivity. [2]
  3. (c) A sunflower turns its head to face the sun. Name the characteristic of living organisms shown. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The removal of waste products of metabolism, toxic materials and substances in excess of requirements from the body.
    B1 removal of waste products of metabolism / toxic materials; B1 substances in excess.
  2. (b) The ability to detect and respond to changes in the internal or external environment.
    B1 detect changes / stimuli; B1 respond.
  3. (c) sensitivity
    B1
Q5Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The deodar tree is named Cedrus deodara. State its genus. [1]
  2. (b) The cedar of Lebanon is named Cedrus libani. What does the shared first name tell you about these two trees? [1]
  3. (c) Give two rules for writing a binomial name. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cedrus
    B1
  2. (b) They belong to the same genus, so they are closely related.
    B1 same genus / closely related.
  3. (c) Genus first with a capital letter; species second in lower case; printed in italics or underlined when handwritten.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q6Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name the vertebrate group with each set of features.(i) feathers, a beak and wings(ii) moist skin with no scales; larvae with gills(iii) fur and external ears (pinnae)(iv) dry scaly skin; lays eggs with rubbery shells on land(v) fins, wet scales and gills [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) birds; (ii) amphibians; (iii) mammals; (iv) reptiles; (v) fish
    B1 each.
Q7Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State three features that show a housefly is an insect. [3]
  2. (b) State one feature that all arthropods share. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Body in three parts (head, thorax, abdomen); three pairs of legs; one pair of antennae; wings (one or two pairs); compound eyes.
    1 mark each for any three.
  2. (b) Jointed legs / exoskeleton / segmented body.
    B1 any one.
Q8Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Maize is a monocotyledon and mustard is a dicotyledon. State how the leaf veins of the two plants differ. [1]
  2. (b) State how many cotyledons are found in the seed of each plant. [1]
  3. (c) A mustard flower has 4 petals. A lily flower has 6 petals. Which of these is a monocotyledon? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Maize has parallel veins; mustard has a branching network of veins.
    B1
  2. (b) maize one; mustard two
    B1
  3. (c) The lily – its flower parts are in multiples of three.
    B1 lily; B1 multiples of three.
Q9Classification & keys · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A student looked at four animals with a hand lens and recorded her observations.Animal | body parts | pairs of legs | pairs of antennae | wings1 | 3 | 3 | 1 | 2 pairs2 | 2 | 4 | 0 | none3 | 2 | 5 | 2 | none4 | many similar segments | 30 | 1 | none

  1. (a) Name the arthropod group of each animal 1 to 4. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the total number of legs of animal 4. [1]
  3. (c) State one feature, other than jointed legs, that all four animals share. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 insect; 2 arachnid; 3 crustacean; 4 myriapod
    B1 each.
  2. (b) 60
    30 pairs × 2 = 60. B1
  3. (c) exoskeleton / segmented body
    B1
Q10Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State three features of plant cells that are not found in animal cells. [3]
  2. (b) State how plants and animals differ in the way they obtain food. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) cellulose cell wall; chloroplasts; large permanent vacuole containing cell sap
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Plants make their own food by photosynthesis; animals feed on other organisms.
    B1
Q11Classification & keys · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Suggest two reasons why biologists classify living organisms into groups. [2]
  2. (b) State the basis on which organisms are placed into the same group. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Makes it easier to identify and study organisms; shows evolutionary relationships; allows features of a new organism to be predicted; helps scientists communicate with one shared system.
    1 mark each for any two.
  2. (b) They share features (characteristics).
    B1
Q12Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which features does a spider have?A 3 body parts, 3 pairs of legs, 1 pair of antennaeB 2 body parts, 4 pairs of legs, no antennaeC 2 body parts, 5 pairs of legs, 2 pairs of antennaeD many body segments, many pairs of legs, 1 pair of antennae [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 2 body parts, 4 pairs of legs, no antennae
    Spiders are arachnids: two body parts, four pairs of legs and no antennae. A describes an insect; C a crustacean; D a myriapod.
Q13Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) An animal has moist skin without scales. It lays eggs in water and its young have gills. To which group does it belong?A fishB reptilesC amphibiansD birds [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C amphibians
    Moist scaleless skin, eggs laid in water and larvae (tadpoles) with gills are amphibian features. Fish (A) have scales and keep their gills as adults.
Q14Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which feature is characteristic of organisms in the fungus kingdom?A cells contain chloroplastsB cells have no nucleusC they feed by secreting enzymes onto dead organic matter and absorbing the productsD cell walls are made of cellulose [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C they feed by secreting enzymes onto dead organic matter and absorbing the products
    Fungi feed saprotrophically by extracellular digestion. Cellulose walls (D) and chloroplasts (A) are plant features; fungal cells do have a nucleus.

    B1 each.
Q16Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Key:1 (a) fruit has wings … go to 21 (b) fruit has no wings … go to 32 (a) one wing … J2 (b) two wings … K3 (a) fruit is fleshy … go to 43 (b) fruit is dry with hooks … N4 (a) one large seed … L4 (b) many seeds … M

  1. (a) Use the key to give the letter for each fruit.(i) a mango: fleshy, with one large seed(ii) a guava: fleshy, with many small seeds(iii) a dry fruit covered in tiny hooks(iv) a dry fruit with two wings [4]
  2. (b) State why each step of the key has exactly two statements. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) L; (ii) M; (iii) N; (iv) K
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Each step is a pair of contrasting statements, so every fruit fits only one of them and is sent along one route to a single end-point (a dichotomous key).
    B1 two contrasting/opposite statements so each organism fits only one / dichotomous.
Q17Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A prawn is a crustacean and a locust is an insect. Give three ways in which a prawn differs from a locust. [3]
  2. (b) Both animals have an exoskeleton. State one other feature they share. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Prawn has two pairs of antennae (locust one pair); prawn has more than four pairs of legs (locust three pairs); prawn has no wings (locust has wings); prawn body is cephalothorax and abdomen (locust head, thorax, abdomen).
    1 mark each for any three comparisons.
  2. (b) jointed legs / segmented body
    B1
Q18Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Bacteria are single-celled organisms. Some live in the soil of a rice field in Punjab.

  1. (a) Name the kingdom to which bacteria belong. [1]
  2. (b) State two structures found in a plant cell that are not found in a bacterial cell. [2]
  3. (c) State two structures that are found in a bacterial cell. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) prokaryotes (prokaryote kingdom)
    B1
  2. (b) Any two: nucleus; mitochondria; chloroplasts; large permanent vacuole; cellulose cell wall.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) Any two: cell wall; cell membrane; cytoplasm; ribosomes; circular DNA; plasmids.
    1 mark each.
Q19Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Ferns grow on the shady, damp banks of streams in the Murree hills, while flowering plants such as wild roses grow nearby.

  1. (a) Ferns and flowering plants are both in the plant kingdom. State two features shared by all plants. [2]
  2. (b) State two features that distinguish ferns from flowering plants. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cells with cellulose cell walls; chloroplasts / photosynthesis; multicellular.
    1 mark each for any two.
  2. (b) Ferns have no flowers; they reproduce by spores not seeds; leaves called fronds.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q20Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Fruit bats roost in large trees in Lahore and fly out at dusk to feed.

  1. (a) A bat flies like a bird. State three features that show it is a mammal. [3]
  2. (b) State one feature a bird has that a bat does not. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fur/hair; external ears (pinnae); mammary glands / feeds young on milk; (whiskers).
    1 mark each for any three.
  2. (b) feathers / a beak / lays hard-shelled eggs
    B1
Q21Classification & keys · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Key to some animals of Pakistan:1 (a) breathes with gills as an adult … mahseer1 (b) breathes with lungs … go to 22 (a) has feathers … peacock2 (b) has no feathers … go to 33 (a) has mammary glands … go to 43 (b) has no mammary glands … go to 54 (a) lives in rivers, has flippers … Indus river dolphin4 (b) lives on mountains, has horns … markhor5 (a) dry skin with scales … mugger crocodile5 (b) moist skin without scales … bullfrog

  1. (a) Use the key to identify the animal that ends at statement 5 (b). [1]
  2. (b) State the number of the statement that separates the Indus river dolphin from the markhor. [1]
  3. (c) The Indus river dolphin lives in water and has fins, like a mahseer fish. Explain why it is classified as a mammal, not a fish. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) bullfrog
    B1
  2. (b) 4
    B1

Q22Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which feature is typical of a monocotyledon such as wheat?A two cotyledons in each seedB leaves with a branching network of veinsC flower parts in multiples of threeD flower parts in multiples of five [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C flower parts in multiples of three
    Monocots have one cotyledon, parallel leaf veins and flower parts in threes. D (multiples of four or five) and B are dicotyledon features.
Q23Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which structure is found in both prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells but is NOT found in a virus?A ribosomesB a nucleusC a cell wallD genetic material [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A ribosomes
    Prokaryotic and eukaryotic cells both have ribosomes (as well as cytoplasm and a cell membrane); a virus has only a protein coat and genetic material. B is wrong because prokaryotic cells have no nucleus; C is wrong because animal cells have no cell wall; D is wrong because viruses do contain DNA or RNA.
Q24Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Leaf A: simple, smooth edge, parallel veinsLeaf B: compound (divided into leaflets), leaflets with toothed edgesLeaf C: simple, toothed edge, branching veinsLeaf D: compound, leaflets with smooth edges

  1. (a) Construct a dichotomous key to identify leaves A, B, C and D. Use only features given in the descriptions. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 (a) leaf divided into leaflets (compound) … go to 2; (b) leaf not divided (simple) … go to 3. 2 (a) leaflets with toothed edges … B; (b) leaflets with smooth edges … D. 3 (a) parallel veins … A; (b) branching (net) veins … C.
    B1 first split uses a feature dividing the four into 2 + 2 (or 1 + 3); B1 each statement is a pair of contrasting statements; B1 correct observable features used throughout; B1 every leaf identified correctly with one end-point each.
Q25Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The base sequence of the same 300-base section of a gene was compared in four species of bird. Number of bases that differ from species W:X: 6Y: 27Z: 45

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of bases that differ between species W and each of X, Y and Z. [2]
  2. (b) State which species is most closely related to W. Explain your answer. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why scientists now use DNA base sequences as well as visible features to classify organisms. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) X 2.0 %; Y 9.0 %; Z 15.0 %
    M1 number different ÷ 300 × 100; A1 all three correct.
  2. (b) X – it has the smallest difference in base sequence, so it shared a common ancestor with W most recently.
    B1 X; B1 fewest differences / most recent common ancestor.
  3. (c) Visible features can be similar in unrelated organisms (e.g. due to similar habitats); DNA sequences show evolutionary relationships more accurately.
    B1 visible features can mislead; B1 DNA reflects evolutionary relationship / common ancestry.
Q26Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a virus. [2]
  2. (b) Viruses are not placed in any of the five kingdoms. Suggest why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Genetic material (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a protein coat.
    B1 genetic material / DNA or RNA; B1 protein coat.
  2. (b) They are not made of cells (no cytoplasm, cell membrane or ribosomes); they cannot carry out the characteristics of life (respiration, nutrition, excretion, growth) on their own; they can only reproduce inside a host cell.
    1 mark each, max 3: not cells; no cytoplasm/ribosomes/membrane; no respiration/nutrition/excretion; reproduce only inside living host cells.
Q27Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A student wrote this key for five beetles collected in a park:1 Is it large? yes … go to 2; no … go to 32 Is it pretty? yes … beetle A; no … beetle B3 Is it dark? yes … beetle C; no … go to 44 Is it fast? yes … beetle D; no … beetle E

  1. (a) Explain why the questions in this key are not suitable, and suggest a better question for step 1. [4]
  2. (b) State two other rules for a good dichotomous key. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 'Large' and 'pretty' are subjective / depend on the person; size varies with age; colours may change; better: 'Does it have wings?' or 'Does it have more than three pairs of legs?' (an observable, countable feature).
    B1 features are opinions / not measurable; B1 size varies with age or individual; B1 two contrasting statements needed at each step; B1 a suitable observable alternative question.
  2. (b) Each step has only two choices; each organism appears at only one end-point; use features that are present in all specimens / visible.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q28Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Birds and reptiles both lay eggs with shells on land. Give two features that can be used to tell a bird from a reptile. [2]
  2. (b) Birds have scales on their legs. Explain why this does not make them reptiles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Birds have feathers; a beak; front limbs as wings; hard (chalky) eggshells, whereas reptiles have dry scales over the whole body and rubbery/leathery eggshells.
    1 mark each for any two contrasts.
  2. (b) Classification uses several features; birds have feathers and a beak, which reptiles lack.
    B1
Q29Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A scientist found a new organism growing on dead leaves in a forest in Swat. It is multicellular and made of thread-like hyphae. Its cells have nuclei and cell walls that are not made of cellulose, but no chloroplasts. It releases enzymes onto the dead leaves and absorbs the products.

  1. (a) Name the kingdom to which this organism belongs. [1]
  2. (b) Give two reasons from the description why it is not a plant. [2]
  3. (c) Give one reason from the description why it is not an animal. [1]
  4. (d) Describe its type of nutrition. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) fungi (fungus kingdom)
    B1
  2. (b) No chloroplasts / cannot photosynthesise; cell walls not made of cellulose.
    B1 each.
  3. (c) It has cell walls / it is made of hyphae.
    B1
  4. (d) It secretes enzymes onto dead leaves (extracellular digestion) and absorbs the soluble products (saprotrophic).
    B1 enzymes secreted outside onto food; B1 products absorbed.
Q30Classification & keys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

housefly: 3 pairs of legs, 1 pair of antennae, 1 pair of wingsbutterfly: 3 pairs of legs, 1 pair of antennae, 2 pairs of wingsspider: 4 pairs of legs, no antennae, no wings, no tailscorpion: 4 pairs of legs, no antennae, no wings, a long tail with a stingcrab: 5 pairs of legs, 2 pairs of antennae, no wings

  1. (a) Construct a dichotomous key to identify these five arthropods. Use only the features given. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 (a) has wings … go to 2; (b) no wings … go to 3. 2 (a) one pair of wings … housefly; (b) two pairs of wings … butterfly. 3 (a) has antennae … crab; (b) no antennae … go to 4. 4 (a) has a tail with a sting … scorpion; (b) no tail … spider.
    B1 first split divides the group with a clear feature (e.g. wings); B1 pairs of contrasting statements at each step; B1 four steps for five animals; B1 only given observable features used; B1 every animal reaches one end-point correctly.
