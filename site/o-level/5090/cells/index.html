Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Microscopy & magnification · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A student views a cheek cell with a light microscope. The image of the cell is 36 mm wide and the magnification is ×400. What is the actual width of the cell?A 0.09 µmB 9 µmC 90 µmD 900 µm [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 90 µm
    Actual size = image size ÷ magnification = 36 mm ÷ 400 = 0.09 mm = 90 µm (× 1000). A (0.09 µm) forgets to convert mm to µm.
Q2Microscopy & magnification · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which equation is correct for an image seen through a microscope?A actual size = image size × magnificationB magnification = actual size ÷ image sizeC image size = actual size × magnificationD image size = magnification ÷ actual size [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C image size = actual size × magnification
    magnification = image size ÷ actual size, so image size = actual size × magnification. B is the formula upside down, which gives a value less than 1 for an enlargement.
Q3Microscopy & magnification · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A student peeled the thin inner layer from an onion scale leaf, stained it with iodine solution and drew one cell as seen with a light microscope.

  1. (a) Line XY across the drawing of one cell measures 96 mm. The drawing has a magnification of ×120. Calculate the actual length of the cell in mm. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Convert your answer to (a) into micrometres (µm). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.8 mm
    M1 actual = image ÷ magnification = 96 ÷ 120; A1 0.8 mm
  2. (b) 800 µm
    B1 × 1000 (1 mm = 1000 µm)
Q4Microscopy & magnification · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Paramecium is a single-celled organism found in pond water in Lahore. A specimen is 0.18 mm long. Its image in a field guide is 54 mm long.

  1. (a) Calculate the magnification of the image. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Express the actual length of the Paramecium in µm. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×300
    M1 54 ÷ 0.18 (both in mm); A1 ×300
  2. (b) 180 µm
    B1 0.18 × 1000
Q5Microscopy & magnification · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A student in Peshawar wants to look at onion epidermis cells with a light microscope.

  1. (a) Describe how the student should prepare a slide of onion epidermis so that cells can be seen clearly. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why iodine solution is added. [1]
  3. (c) State why the layer of tissue must be very thin. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Peel a thin layer; place flat in a drop of water or iodine on the slide; lower a coverslip at an angle with a mounted needle; remove excess liquid with paper towel.
    1 mark each for any four of: peel a thin (one cell thick) layer of epidermis with forceps; place it flat on a clean slide; add a drop of water / iodine solution; lower coverslip at an angle / from one side using a mounted needle; to avoid air bubbles; blot excess liquid
  2. (b) It stains structures (e.g. the nucleus and cytoplasm) so they are easier to see.
    B1 stains / increases contrast
  3. (c) So that light can pass through it / cells do not overlap.
    B1
Q6Microscopy & magnification · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State the formula that links magnification, image size and actual size. [1]
  2. (b) A chloroplast is 5 µm long. In a diagram it is drawn 20 mm long. Calculate the magnification of the diagram. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) magnification = image size ÷ actual size
    B1
  2. (b) ×4000
    M1 20 mm = 20000 µm; A1 20000 ÷ 5 = ×4000
Q7Microscopy & magnification · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A student draws a root hair cell from a radish seedling at a magnification of ×40. The drawing of the cell, including its hair, is 18 mm long.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual length of the root hair cell in mm. [2]
  2. (b) State the actual length in µm. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.45 mm
    M1 18 ÷ 40; A1 0.45 mm
  2. (b) 450 µm
    B1
Q8Microscopy & magnification · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A chloroplast-containing alga cell is 25 µm long. In a textbook photograph the same cell is 50 mm long. What is the magnification of the photograph?A ×2B ×200C ×2000D ×20 000 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C ×2000
    Convert to the same units: 50 mm = 50000 µm. Magnification = 50000 ÷ 25 = ×2000. B (×200) comes from a wrong conversion (1 mm = 100 µm).
Q9Microscopy & magnification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A photomicrograph shows a sunflower pollen grain at a magnification of ×850. The diameter of the pollen grain on the photograph is 34 mm.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual diameter of the pollen grain in µm. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) A student makes a large drawing of the same pollen grain with a diameter of 68 mm. Calculate the magnification of the student's drawing. [2]
  3. (c) State why a magnification has no units. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 40 µm
    M1 34 mm = 34000 µm, ÷ 850; A1 40 µm
  2. (b) ×1700
    M1 68000 µm ÷ 40 µm; A1 ×1700 (no units)
  3. (c) It is a ratio of two lengths measured in the same units, so the units cancel.
    B1 ratio / same units cancel
Q10Microscopy & magnification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A mosquito larva collected from a water tank is 12 mm long. A student makes a drawing of the larva that is 132 mm long.

  1. (a) Calculate the magnification of the drawing. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Another student got an answer of ×0.09. Explain how you know this answer must be wrong. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×11
    M1 132 ÷ 12; A1 ×11
  2. (b) The drawing is larger than the larva, so the magnification must be greater than 1 (the student divided actual size by drawing size).
    B1 enlargement must be > 1 / divided the wrong way round
Q11Microscopy & magnification · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

A highly magnified image of a rod-shaped bacterium from yoghurt is 60 mm long. The magnification of the image is ×30000.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual length of the bacterium in µm. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) How many of these bacteria, placed end to end, would fit across a line 1 mm long? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2 µm
    M1 60 mm = 60000 µm, ÷ 30000; A1 2 µm
  2. (b) 500
    B1 1000 µm ÷ 2 µm = 500
Q12Microscopy & magnification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A student measured the lengths of five cheek cells on a photograph taken at ×1000. The image lengths were: 58 mm, 62 mm, 55 mm, 61 mm, 64 mm.

  1. (a) Calculate the mean image length. [1]
  2. (b) Use your mean to calculate the actual length of a typical cheek cell in µm. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the student measured five cells rather than one. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 60 mm
    B1 (58+62+55+61+64) ÷ 5 = 60 mm
  2. (b) 60 µm
    M1 60 mm ÷ 1000; A1 60 µm
  3. (c) Cells vary in size, so a mean of several is more representative / reliable.
    B1
Q13Microscopy & magnification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A student wants to draw a fish egg that is 0.8 mm in diameter at a magnification of ×50.

  1. (a) Calculate how long the student's drawing of the egg should be. [2]
  2. (b) State one feature of a good biological drawing that the student should include. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 40 mm
    M1 image = actual × magnification = 0.8 × 50; A1 40 mm
  2. (b) Clear continuous outline lines / no shading / correct proportions / drawing large enough / labels with ruled lines.
    B1 any one
Q14Microscopy & magnification · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A red blood cell has a diameter of 7.5 µm. It is drawn in a revision poster at a magnification of ×6000. What is the diameter of the drawing?A 4.5 mmB 45 mmC 450 mmD 800 mm [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 45 mm
    Image size = actual size × magnification = 7.5 µm × 6000 = 45000 µm = 45 mm. A divides by 10 000 instead of 1000; D divides magnification by size.
Q15Microscopy & magnification · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student draws a section through a mango leaf. The actual thickness of the leaf is 0.25 mm. On the drawing the thickness is 75 mm.

  1. (a) Calculate the magnification of the drawing. [2]
  2. (b) On the same drawing one palisade mesophyll cell is 21 mm long. Use your answer to (a) to calculate the actual length of this cell in µm. [2]
  3. (c) The student's calculated value is larger than the value given in a textbook. Suggest one reason, other than a calculation error, for this difference. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×300
    M1 75 ÷ 0.25; A1 ×300
  2. (b) 70 µm
    M1 21 ÷ 300 = 0.07 mm; A1 × 1000 = 70 µm
  3. (c) The drawing was not drawn exactly to scale / the cells were drawn too large in proportion to the leaf thickness, or the leaf used was thicker / different cells vary in size.
    B1 any one sensible reason
Q16Microscopy & magnification · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Two cells were photographed. Cell P (a cheek cell) has an image 45 mm long at ×1500. Cell Q (a plant cell) has an image 30 mm long at ×600.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual length of each cell in µm. [3]
  2. (b) A student says that cell P is bigger because its image is bigger. Use your answers to explain why the student is wrong. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cell P: 30 µm; cell Q: 50 µm
    M1 convert mm to µm (× 1000); A1 P = 45000 ÷ 1500 = 30 µm; A1 Q = 30000 ÷ 600 = 50 µm
  2. (b) Cell Q is actually larger, by 20 µm; image size depends on magnification as well as actual size.
    B1 Q is larger (by 20 µm); B1 P was magnified more so its image is larger even though the cell is smaller
Q17Microscopy & magnification · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A human egg cell has a diameter of about 70 µm. A photograph shows an egg cell with an image diameter of 42 mm.

  1. (a) Calculate the magnification of the photograph. [2]
  2. (b) The same photograph is enlarged on a classroom screen so that the image of the cell becomes 210 mm long. Calculate the new magnification. [2]
  3. (c) Explain whether enlarging the photograph lets you see more detail of the cell. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×600
    M1 42 mm = 42000 µm; A1 42000 ÷ 70 = ×600
  2. (b) ×3000
    M1 210 000 µm ÷ 70 µm; A1 ×3000 (or 600 × 5)
  3. (c) No — enlarging the photograph only makes the same image bigger; no new detail is revealed (resolution does not change).
    B1
Q18Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which structure is found in both plant cells and animal cells?A cell wallB chloroplastC large permanent vacuoleD ribosome [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D ribosome
    Ribosomes (protein synthesis) occur in all plant and animal cells. Cell walls, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole are found in plant cells only.

Q20Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State the main substance from which a plant cell wall is made. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the difference between the cell wall and the cell membrane in how substances pass through. [2]
  3. (c) State one function of the cell wall. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cellulose
    B1
  2. (b) The cell wall is fully permeable (lets most substances through); the cell membrane is partially permeable (controls what passes).
    B1 wall fully / freely permeable; B1 membrane partially permeable
  3. (c) Gives the cell a fixed shape / support / stops the cell bursting when it takes in water.
    B1
Q21Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A cell from a leaf is treated so that its mitochondria stop working. Which process in the cell stops first?A aerobic respirationB photosynthesisC diffusion of carbon dioxideD protein synthesis on ribosomes [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A aerobic respiration
    Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration. Photosynthesis happens in chloroplasts, so B is wrong; diffusion needs no energy from respiration.
Q22Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Lactobacillus is a bacterium used to make yoghurt (dahi). Its cell has a cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, a loop of circular DNA and small plasmids.

  1. (a) State two structures that are found in this bacterial cell but are not found in a human cheek cell. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the genetic material of a bacterium differs from that of a plant cell. [2]
  3. (c) Name the structures in the bacterium where proteins are made. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cell wall; plasmids (also accept circular DNA free in cytoplasm).
    1 mark each for any two of: cell wall, plasmid(s), circular DNA
  2. (b) Bacterium: a single circular DNA loop free in the cytoplasm (plus plasmids), not inside a nucleus. Plant cell: DNA in chromosomes inside a nucleus.
    B1 no nucleus / DNA free in cytoplasm; B1 circular DNA / plasmids vs chromosomes in nucleus
  3. (c) Ribosomes
    B1
Q23Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State the function of the nucleus. [1]
  2. (b) State the function of ribosomes. [1]
  3. (c) Describe the function of the large permanent vacuole in a plant cell. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Contains genetic material (DNA / chromosomes) that controls the activities of the cell.
    B1
  2. (b) Protein synthesis.
    B1
  3. (c) Stores cell sap (water, sugars, salts); when full it presses the cytoplasm against the cell wall, keeping the cell turgid and supporting the plant.
    B1 stores cell sap; B1 maintains turgor / support
Q24Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Cell R was found in a water sample from a pond in Murree. It has a nucleus, a cell wall, a cell membrane, several chloroplasts and a large vacuole full of cell sap.

  1. (a) Identify whether cell R is a plant cell, an animal cell or a bacterial cell. Give two reasons from the description. [3]
  2. (b) Cell S has a nucleus, cell membrane and many mitochondria but no cell wall. Name the type of cell. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Plant cell: it has chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole, and it has a cell wall as well as a nucleus.
    B1 plant; 1 mark each for any two of: chloroplasts; large (permanent) vacuole containing cell sap; nucleus together with a cell wall (bacteria have a cell wall but no nucleus; animal cells have no cell wall)
  2. (b) Animal cell
    B1
Q25Cell structure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Suggest why sperm cells have many mitochondria in the middle region. [2]
  2. (b) Root hair cells do not have chloroplasts. Explain why. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why cells in the pancreas that make digestive enzymes have many ribosomes. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) To carry out a lot of aerobic respiration, releasing energy for the tail to move / swim to the egg.
    B1 aerobic respiration releases energy; B1 for tail movement / swimming
  2. (b) They are underground so receive no light; photosynthesis cannot occur, so chloroplasts would be of no use.
    B1 no light underground; B1 so no photosynthesis
  3. (c) Enzymes are proteins, and ribosomes are where proteins are made.
    B1
Q26Cell structure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State where in the cell the chemical reactions of aerobic respiration take place. [1]
  2. (b) Muscle cells in a sprinter's legs contain more mitochondria than skin cells. Explain why. [2]
  3. (c) Name the part of the cell where most chemical reactions of the cell take place, and state what it contains. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mitochondria
    B1
  2. (b) Muscle contraction needs a lot of energy; more mitochondria allow more aerobic respiration to release this energy.
    B1 more aerobic respiration; B1 more energy (released) for contraction
  3. (c) Cytoplasm; contains water, dissolved substances / enzymes and organelles.
    B1 cytoplasm; B1 any one valid content
Q27Specialised cells & levels of organisation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which list is in order of increasing level of organisation?A cell → organ → tissue → organ systemB cell → tissue → organ → organ systemC tissue → cell → organ → organismD organ → tissue → organ system → organism [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B cell → tissue → organ → organ system
    Cells form tissues, tissues form organs, organs form organ systems. A puts organ before tissue.
Q28Specialised cells & levels of organisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) For each of the following, state whether it is a cell, a tissue, an organ or an organ system: (i) the heart (ii) xylem (iii) a neurone (iv) the stomach, intestines and liver working together. [4]
  2. (b) Define the term organ. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) organ (ii) tissue (iii) cell (iv) organ system
    1 mark each
  2. (b) A group of tissues working together to perform specific functions.
    B1
Q29Specialised cells & levels of organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Copy and complete the table to show one feature of each cell and how it helps the cell's function.Cell | function | feature | how the feature helpsciliated cell | ? | ? | ?red blood cell | ? | ? | ? [4]
  2. (b) State the meaning of the term tissue. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ciliated cell: moves mucus (and trapped particles) in the trachea/bronchi; cilia beat to move mucus. Red blood cell: transports oxygen; contains haemoglobin which binds oxygen / no nucleus so more room / biconcave shape gives large surface area.
    Ciliated: B1 function (move mucus), B1 feature + explanation (cilia beat / sweep). RBC: B1 function (carry oxygen), B1 feature + explanation (haemoglobin / no nucleus / biconcave)
  2. (b) A group of cells with similar structures, working together to perform a shared function.
    B1
Q30Specialised cells & levels of organisation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the shape of a root hair cell helps it to absorb water and mineral ions. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how a palisade mesophyll cell is adapted for photosynthesis. [2]
  3. (c) A motor neurone can be over 1 m long. Suggest how this length is an advantage. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The long hair-like extension gives a large surface area, so more water and ions can be absorbed at a time.
    B1 long extension / hair; B1 large surface area for absorption
  2. (b) It contains many chloroplasts to absorb light; it is long/column-shaped and near the upper surface so it receives more light.
    1 mark each for any two: many chloroplasts; near upper surface; elongated shape packed closely; large vacuole pushes chloroplasts to edge
  3. (c) It carries impulses over long distances (from the spinal cord to muscles) quickly without needing synapses along the way.
    B1
