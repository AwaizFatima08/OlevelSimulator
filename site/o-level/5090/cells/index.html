Q15Microscopy & magnification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A photograph of a liver cell taken with a microscope has a scale bar beside it. The scale bar line is 25 mm long and is labelled 10 µm. On the photograph the nucleus of the cell is 15 mm across and the whole cell is 95 mm long.

  1. (a) Use the scale bar to calculate the magnification of the photograph. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the actual diameter of the nucleus, in µm. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the actual length of the whole cell, in µm. [1]
  4. (d) The photograph is later printed at twice the size in a revision book. Explain why the scale bar still gives correct results but a printed '×2500' label would not. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×2500
    M1 convert 25 mm to 25 000 µm; A1 25 000 ÷ 10 = ×2500 (no units).
  2. (b) 6 µm
    M1 15 mm = 15 000 µm; A1 15 000 ÷ 2500 = 6 µm. Allow error carried forward from (a).
  3. (c) 38 µm
    B1 95 000 ÷ 2500 = 38 µm.
  4. (d) The scale bar is enlarged by the same amount as the cell, so the ratio stays correct; the written magnification no longer matches the new image size.
    1 mark each: scale bar and image are enlarged by the same factor / ratio unchanged; the printed magnification would now be ×5000 so the label would be wrong.

Q19Microscopy & magnification · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Q39Cell structure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
