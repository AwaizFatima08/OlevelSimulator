Show answer

Q12Fertilisation, seeds & germination · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

  2. (b) Calculate the mean radicle length for the seeds soaked for 8 hours. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [1]

Q21Flower structure & pollination · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) State one advantage and one disadvantage of an IUD compared with the contraceptive pill. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Copper is toxic to sperm / reduces sperm movement, so fewer reach the egg; it prevents an embryo from implanting in the uterus lining.
    1 mark each.

  2. (b) Explain why a person with AIDS may die from a disease such as tuberculosis that a healthy person can fight off. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) HIV infects and destroys a type of lymphocyte; over time the number of these lymphocytes falls; the immune system can no longer fight other infections (and some cancers), so the person develops AIDS.
    1 mark each: HIV attacks lymphocytes; number of lymphocytes falls; weakened immune system → other infections.
  2. (b) Too few lymphocytes to make antibodies / destroy the bacteria; so the pathogen multiplies unchecked.
    1 mark each.

  2. (b) Suggest one reason why some couples choose this method. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ovulation date is hard to predict because cycles vary in length; sperm can survive in the female body for several days; so intercourse days before ovulation can still lead to fertilisation (and the egg survives about a day after).

  2. (b) No cost / no hormones or devices / religious or personal beliefs.
    1 mark.

A regional blood bank tested 50 000 donations of blood in one year. 0.04 % of them tested positive for HIV.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of donations that tested positive. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why all donated blood is tested for HIV before it is used. [2]
  3. (c) For a few weeks after infection, a person's blood may test negative even though it contains HIV. Suggest why blood services ask donors questions about recent risks as well as testing. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20
    A1 50000 × 0.04 ÷ 100 = 20.
  2. (b) HIV is carried in blood; a patient receiving infected blood would become infected; so positive units are destroyed.
    1 mark each.

Q105Contraception & STIs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Strains of gonorrhoea bacteria that are resistant to several antibiotics are spreading. Explain how a population of bacteria becomes resistant to an antibiotic. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two ways to slow the spread of resistant gonorrhoea. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A random mutation makes some bacteria resistant; when the antibiotic is used, non-resistant bacteria are killed but resistant ones survive; the survivors reproduce and pass on the resistance allele, so the resistant strain becomes common (natural selection).
    1 mark each: mutation gives resistance; resistant bacteria survive antibiotic; they reproduce / pass on resistance.
  2. (b) Any two: use condoms; complete the full course of antibiotics; only use antibiotics when needed; test and treat partners; education about safe sex.
    1 mark each for any two.
