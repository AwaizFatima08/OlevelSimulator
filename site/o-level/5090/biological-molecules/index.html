Q3Food tests · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) State the colour of the reagent before the test and the colour seen if starch is present. [2]
  3. (c) A potato slice gives a positive result. Suggest why a slice of ripe mango gives only a weak result. [1]
Show answer

Show answer

  2. (b) ethanol is flammable
    B1 ethanol is flammable

  2. (b) Explain how the time taken links to the reducing sugar concentration. [1]

    B1 any one

  2. (b) State why fats form a cloudy emulsion in this test. [1]

Q18Food tests · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A doctor suspects that a patient has diabetes. Explain how a Benedict's test on the patient's urine could support this diagnosis. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why the Benedict's test is not used to measure exact blood glucose concentration in a hospital. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) glucose is a reducing sugar; urine of a person with diabetes may contain glucose, so the test turns green/yellow/orange/red instead of staying blue
    B1 glucose present in urine of diabetic (not normally present); B1 positive (colour change from blue) result
  2. (b) it is only semi-quantitative: the colours are judged by eye and only show a rough range; it does not give a number
    1 mark each for any two of: colour judged subjectively; only rough/semi-quantitative; colours overlap; slow/needs heating; other reducing sugars also react
Q19Food tests · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A seed is cut in half and one half is crushed with water, then filtered. Explain why the seed must be crushed before testing it for starch and protein. [2]
  2. (b) The filtrate gives a blue-black colour with iodine but the solid residue on the filter paper gives an even darker colour. Suggest why. [2]
  3. (c) State the result you would expect with Benedict's solution if the seed has just started to germinate, and explain your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) crushing breaks open cells (cell walls) so the starch/protein is released and can mix with the reagent
    B1 breaks cells/cell walls; B1 releases contents / larger surface area for reagent to reach
  2. (b) starch is insoluble (large molecules), so most stayed in the residue; only a little passed through into the filtrate
    B1 starch insoluble/large molecules; B1 most starch remains in the residue
  3. (c) orange/red (positive) because amylase breaks down starch into maltose, a reducing sugar
    B1 colour change from blue (green to red); B1 starch digested to maltose/reducing sugar by amylase

Q21Food tests · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Starch solution and amylase were mixed at 35 °C. Every 2 minutes a drop of the mixture was added to a drop of iodine solution on a spotting tile.0 min: blue-black2 min: blue-black4 min: dark brown6 min: orange-brown8 min: orange-brown
