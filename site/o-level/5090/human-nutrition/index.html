Practice questions

Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Physical & chemical digestion · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which enzyme digests protein in the stomach?A amylaseB lipaseC maltaseD pepsin [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D pepsin
    Pepsin is the protease of the stomach and works in acid. Amylase digests starch, so A is wrong.
Q2Physical & chemical digestion · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by chemical digestion. [2]
  2. (b) Give two examples of physical (mechanical) digestion in the alimentary canal. [2]
  3. (c) State how physical digestion helps chemical digestion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) the breakdown of large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules by enzymes
    B1 large insoluble → small soluble; B1 by enzymes
  2. (b) chewing by teeth; churning by the stomach muscles (also emulsification by bile)
    1 mark each for any two
  3. (c) it increases the surface area for enzymes to act on
    B1
Q3Physical & chemical digestion · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Complete the following for each enzyme: the food it digests and the product(s).(i) amylase(ii) protease(iii) lipase [3]
  2. (b) Name the enzyme on the lining of the small intestine that completes the digestion of starch, and its product. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) starch → maltose; (ii) protein → amino acids; (iii) fat → fatty acids and glycerol
    B1 each (substrate and product both needed)
  2. (b) maltase; glucose
    B1 maltase; B1 glucose
Q4Physical & chemical digestion · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the function of bile in digestion?A It breaks fats into fatty acids and glycerol.B It emulsifies fats into small droplets.C It digests starch to maltose.D It makes the small intestine acidic. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B It emulsifies fats into small droplets.
    Bile emulsifies fat (physical digestion), increasing surface area for lipase. Bile contains no enzyme, so A (the action of lipase) is wrong.
Q5Physical & chemical digestion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain the roles of hydrochloric acid in the stomach. [2]
  2. (b) Trypsin is a protease made by the pancreas. Suggest why trypsin does not work well in the stomach. [2]
  3. (c) State how the contents of the duodenum are made slightly alkaline. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) kills bacteria/microorganisms in food; gives a low (acidic) pH which is the optimum for pepsin
    B1 kills pathogens/bacteria; B1 optimum pH for pepsin/enzymes in stomach
  2. (b) its optimum pH is about 8 (alkaline); the acid of the stomach denatures it / it works slowly at low pH
    B1 optimum pH alkaline/neutral; B1 acid denatures / changes active site shape
  3. (c) bile (and pancreatic juice) neutralise the acid from the stomach
    B1
Q6Physical & chemical digestion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the type of tooth used for:(i) biting off pieces of naan(ii) grinding food into smaller pieces [2]
  2. (b) Explain why food that is chewed well is digested faster. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) incisors; (ii) molars (and premolars)
    B1 each
  2. (b) chewing breaks food into smaller pieces with a larger surface area; enzymes (such as salivary amylase) can act on more of the food at once
    B1 larger surface area; B1 more contact with enzymes/faster enzyme action
Q7Physical & chemical digestion · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Three tubes contained 5 cm³ milk, sodium carbonate solution and a pH indicator (pink in alkali, colourless at about pH 7).Tube A: + 1 cm³ lipase + 1 cm³ bile saltsTube B: + 1 cm³ lipase + 1 cm³ waterTube C: + 1 cm³ boiled lipase + 1 cm³ bile saltsTime for the pink colour to disappear (three repeats):A: 12, 14, 13 minB: 25, 29, 27 minC: no change after 60 min

  1. (a) Calculate the mean time for tube A and for tube B. Give your answers to the nearest minute. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the difference between the two results. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the pH falls as the fat is digested. [1]
  4. (d) State the purpose of tube C. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: (12 + 14 + 13) ÷ 3 = 13 min; B: (25 + 29 + 27) ÷ 3 = 27 min
    B1 13; B1 27
  2. (b) in A the fat was emulsified by bile into small droplets, giving a larger surface area for lipase, so it was digested faster
    B1 bile emulsifies; B1 larger surface area for lipase
  3. (c) fatty acids are produced
    B1
  4. (d) control: shows that the pH fall is caused by lipase
    B1
Q8Physical & chemical digestion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A piece of boiled egg white is placed in a tube with pepsin and dilute hydrochloric acid at 37 °C. After 2 hours the cloudy egg white has become clear. Explain this observation. [2]
  2. (b) Predict and explain the result if the tube had been kept at 70 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the egg white was cut into small cubes of equal size. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) pepsin digested the protein into smaller soluble molecules (peptides/amino acids), which dissolve so the liquid clears
    B1 protein digested by pepsin; B1 products soluble
  2. (b) egg white stays cloudy: pepsin is denatured (active site changes shape), so no digestion
    B1 no change; B1 denatured / active site shape lost
  3. (c) equal surface area so the comparison is fair / larger surface area for faster digestion
    B1
Q9Physical & chemical digestion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the products of chemical digestion must be small, soluble molecules. [2]
  2. (b) People who have had their gall bladder removed are advised to eat less fat at each meal. Suggest why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) so they can pass through the wall of the small intestine (be absorbed) into the blood/lymph
    B1 absorbed through intestine wall/membranes; B1 into blood/transported
  2. (b) bile is not stored, so less bile is released at a meal; fat is less emulsified and is digested more slowly / poorly
    B1 less bile available (released) at once; B1 less emulsification, fat digested less
Q10Balanced diet & deficiencies · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which mineral is needed to make haemoglobin?A calciumB iodineC ironD nitrogen [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C iron
    Iron is part of haemoglobin; lack causes anaemia. Calcium is needed for bones and teeth, so A is wrong.
Q11Balanced diet & deficiencies · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by a balanced diet. [2]
  2. (b) Name the deficiency disease caused by a lack of vitamin C and state one symptom. [2]
  3. (c) Name one food rich in vitamin C. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) a diet with all the nutrients (carbohydrates, proteins, fats, vitamins, minerals, fibre, water) in the correct amounts/proportions to meet the body's needs
    B1 all nutrients/food groups; B1 correct proportions/amounts
  2. (b) scurvy; bleeding gums (or slow wound healing)
    B1 scurvy; B1 symptom
  3. (c) citrus fruit such as oranges, lemons; guava; tomatoes
    B1
Q12Balanced diet & deficiencies · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A child living indoors for long periods develops soft, bent leg bones. Which deficiency is most likely?A vitamin CB vitamin DC ironD fibre [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B vitamin D
    Lack of vitamin D (made in skin in sunlight) reduces calcium absorption and causes rickets. Vitamin C deficiency causes scurvy, not bent bones.
Q13Balanced diet & deficiencies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A lunch of rice and chicken korma contains 60 g carbohydrate, 20 g protein and 15 g fat. Energy values: carbohydrate 17 kJ/g, protein 17 kJ/g, fat 39 kJ/g.

  1. (a) Calculate the total energy in the meal in kJ. [2]
  2. (b) State which nutrient in the meal provides the most energy per gram. [1]
  3. (c) Fibre provides no usable energy. Explain why fibre is still needed in the diet. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 60 × 17 + 20 × 17 + 15 × 39 = 1945 kJ
    M1 multiply each mass by its energy value; A1 1945
  2. (b) fat
    B1
  3. (c) it adds bulk, helping peristalsis (movement of food through the gut); prevents constipation
    B1 bulk/peristalsis; B1 prevents constipation
Q14Balanced diet & deficiencies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A woman in Karachi needs 18 mg of iron each day. A breakfast cereal contains 2.4 mg of iron per 100 g.

  1. (a) Calculate how many grams of the cereal she would need to eat to get her daily iron requirement from cereal alone. Give your answer to the nearest gram. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why women of child-bearing age need more iron than men of the same age. [2]
  3. (c) Name the condition caused by lack of iron. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18 ÷ 2.4 × 100 = 750 g
    M1 18 ÷ 2.4 (= 7.5 portions of 100 g); A1 750 g
  2. (b) iron is lost in blood during menstruation; needed to make haemoglobin for new red blood cells (also for the fetus in pregnancy)
    B1 blood loss in menstruation (or pregnancy); B1 iron to make haemoglobin/red cells
  3. (c) anaemia
    B1
Q15Balanced diet & deficiencies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Daily energy needs of two men of the same age and mass:office worker: 9 500 kJconstruction worker: 12 000 kJ

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in daily energy needed by the construction worker compared with the office worker. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the construction worker needs more energy. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two health risks if the office worker regularly eats 12 000 kJ a day. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (12 000 − 9 500) ÷ 9 500 × 100 = 26.3 %
    M1 difference ÷ 9 500 × 100; A1 26.3 %
  2. (b) more physical activity; more muscle contraction needs more respiration to release energy
    B1 more activity; B1 more respiration/energy for muscles
  3. (c) obesity (weight gain, fat stored); coronary heart disease / type 2 diabetes / high blood pressure
    1 mark each for two
Q16Balanced diet & deficiencies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe and explain the effects of a diet with too much saturated fat over many years. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two changes to a typical festive meal of fried pakoras, mutton karahi and sweet halwa that would reduce this risk. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) fat (cholesterol) deposits build up in artery walls, especially coronary arteries; arteries narrow / blood clots form; leads to coronary heart disease / heart attack; also obesity
    1 mark each for any three of: cholesterol/fat deposits in arteries; narrowing of lumen; reduced blood flow/clot; coronary heart disease/heart attack; obesity
  2. (b) bake or grill instead of frying; use less ghee/oil; leaner meat or lentils/fish; more vegetables/fruit; less sugar
    1 mark each for two sensible changes
Q17Digestive system & organs · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) In which organ is most water absorbed from digested food?A stomachB small intestineC large intestineD oesophagus [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B small intestine
    Most water is absorbed in the small intestine; the colon absorbs a smaller amount. The stomach absorbs little water, so A is wrong.
Q18Digestive system & organs · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Put these parts in the order that food passes through them: small intestine, mouth, large intestine, stomach, oesophagus. [2]
  2. (b) State the function of the oesophagus. [1]
  3. (c) Name the organ that makes bile. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) mouth → oesophagus → stomach → small intestine → large intestine
    B2 all correct; B1 one adjacent pair swapped
  2. (b) carries food from the mouth to the stomach by peristalsis
    B1
  3. (c) liver
    B1
Q19Digestive system & organs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how peristalsis moves food along the alimentary canal. [2]
  2. (b) State the function of the anus and the name of the process that removes undigested food. [2]
  3. (c) Explain the difference between egestion and excretion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) circular and longitudinal muscles in the wall contract and relax alternately; a wave of contraction behind the food pushes it along
    B1 muscle contraction (circular/longitudinal antagonistic); B1 wave squeezes/pushes food
  2. (b) faeces leave the body through the anus; egestion
    B1 removes faeces; B1 egestion
  3. (c) egestion removes undigested food that was never absorbed; excretion removes metabolic waste made by cells
    B1
Q20Digestive system & organs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The pancreas releases its juice into the duodenum. Name three enzymes in pancreatic juice. [3]
  2. (b) A patient's pancreatic duct is blocked. Suggest and explain two effects on the patient's faeces and body mass. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) amylase, trypsin (protease), lipase
    B1 each
  2. (b) faeces contain undigested fat (pale, fatty); weight loss because less food is digested and absorbed
    B1 more undigested food/fat in faeces; B1 weight loss / poor absorption
Q21Absorption & villi · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by absorption. [1]
  2. (b) Describe two features of a villus that help absorption. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) movement of small digested food molecules (and ions) from the small intestine into the blood/lymph
    B1
  2. (b) thin wall (one cell thick); microvilli give large surface area; rich capillary network; lacteal
    1 mark each for any two
Q22Absorption & villi · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Into which structure in a villus are the products of fat digestion mainly absorbed?A capillaryB lactealC microvillusD vein [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B lacteal
    Fatty acids and glycerol pass into the lacteal (lymph vessel). Glucose and amino acids enter the capillaries, so A is a distractor.
Q23Absorption & villi · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the capillary network in each villus helps to keep absorption of glucose fast. [2]
  2. (b) Some glucose is absorbed by active transport. State why active transport is needed. [1]
  3. (c) Name the blood vessel that carries absorbed food from the small intestine to the liver. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) blood flow carries absorbed glucose away; keeps a steep concentration gradient between the intestine and the blood
    B1 carries glucose away; B1 maintains concentration gradient (for diffusion)
  2. (b) to absorb glucose against a concentration gradient (when concentration in the lumen is lower than in the cells/blood)
    B1
  3. (c) hepatic portal vein
    B1
Q24Absorption & villi · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

In the small intestine, folds of the lining increase surface area by a factor of 3, villi by a further factor of 10, and microvilli by a further factor of 20.

  1. (a) Calculate the factor by which the surface area is increased by the folds, villi and microvilli together. [1]
  2. (b) Use your answer to calculate the absorbing surface area of a small intestine with a smooth inner surface area of 0.5 m². [1]
  3. (c) A disease in children flattens the villi. Suggest and explain two effects. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3 × 10 × 20 = 600 times
    B1 600
  2. (b) 0.5 × 600 = 300 m²
    B1 300 m²
  3. (c) smaller surface area so less absorption of nutrients; leads to weight loss / poor growth / deficiency (e.g. anaemia)
    B1 reduced absorption due to smaller surface area; B1 named consequence
Q25Assimilation & liver · Core · Paper 2 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by assimilation. [1]
  2. (b) State one way the body uses absorbed amino acids. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) movement of digested food molecules into cells, where they are used and become part of the cells
    B1
  2. (b) to make proteins (e.g. enzymes, antibodies, new cells for growth/repair)
    B1
Q26Assimilation & liver · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) After a meal of rice, the glucose concentration of blood in the hepatic portal vein is higher than in the hepatic vein. Explain why. [2]
  2. (b) Name the hormone that stimulates the liver to do this. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) the liver removes excess glucose from the blood and converts it to glycogen for storage
    B1 liver takes up glucose; B1 converted to/stored as glycogen
  2. (b) insulin
    B1
Q27Assimilation & liver · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the role of the liver in dealing with excess amino acids absorbed after a high-protein meal. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the hepatic portal vein carries blood to the liver before it reaches the rest of the body. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) deamination: amino group removed from excess amino acids; converted to urea; remaining part used for respiration / converted to glycogen or fat (stored)
    B1 deamination/removal of nitrogen-containing part; B1 urea formed (transported to kidneys); B1 rest used for energy/stored
  2. (b) so the liver can process absorbed food first: store excess glucose, break down harmful substances (e.g. alcohol), deaminate excess amino acids
    1 mark each for any two

Q29Balanced diet & deficiencies (option) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A teenager needs 40 mg of vitamin C a day. Fresh kinnow juice contains 36 mg of vitamin C per 100 cm³. One glass holds 200 cm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the daily vitamin C requirement given by one glass of the juice. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [2]
  2. (b) The juice was left in an open jug in the sun for a day. Suggest why its vitamin C content fell. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why vitamin C must be eaten every day, while vitamin D can be stored. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 200 ÷ 100 × 36 = 72 mg; 72 ÷ 40 × 100 = 180 %
    M1 72 mg; A1 180 %
  2. (b) vitamin C is destroyed by heat/light/oxidation by air
    B1
  3. (c) vitamin C is water-soluble and excess is lost in urine (not stored); vitamin D is fat-soluble and stored in the liver/fat
    B1 vitamin C not stored/lost in urine; B1 vitamin D stored in liver/fat
Q30Balanced diet & deficiencies (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe and explain the differences in daily dietary needs between a pregnant woman and a woman of the same age who is not pregnant. Consider energy, protein, calcium and iron. [5]
  2. (b) Suggest why advice on diet during pregnancy should consider the woman's usual diet and activity. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) more energy (for the growing fetus and extra body mass); more protein for growth of fetal tissues and placenta; more calcium for fetal bones and teeth; more iron for haemoglobin in fetal blood and extra maternal blood volume; also vitamin D for calcium absorption
    1 mark each for up to five of: extra energy + reason; extra protein + growth of fetus; extra calcium + bones/teeth; extra iron + haemoglobin/red cells; extra blood volume; vitamin D for calcium absorption
  2. (b) needs depend on body size, activity and existing nutrient stores/deficiencies
    B1
