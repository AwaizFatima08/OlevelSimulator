Q10Physical & chemical digestion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A 1 cm × 1 cm × 1 cm cube of cooked potato is cut into 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm cubes, all the same size.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of 1 mm × 1 mm × 1 mm cubes that can be cut from the 1 cm cube. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the total surface area of all the 1 mm cubes, in cm². Each 1 mm cube has a surface area of 6 mm². [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the smaller pieces are digested more quickly by enzymes. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1000 cubes
    1 cm = 10 mm, so 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 cubes.
  2. (b) 60 cm²
    1000 × 6 mm² = 6000 mm²; 1 cm² = 100 mm², so 6000 ÷ 100 = 60 cm². 1 mark for method, 1 for answer.
  3. (c) Larger total surface area, so more enzyme molecules can contact the food at the same time, giving more enzyme-substrate collisions per second.
    1 mark: larger surface area; 1 mark: more enzyme-food contact or collisions per unit time.

Show answer
  1. (a) A: (12 + 14 + 13) ÷ 3 = 13 min; B: (25 + 29 + 27) ÷ 3 = 27 min
    B1 13; B1 27
  2. (b) in A the fat was emulsified by bile into small droplets, giving a larger surface area for lipase, so it was digested faster
    B1 bile emulsifies; B1 larger surface area for lipase
  3. (c) fatty acids are produced
    B1
  4. (d) control: shows that the pH fall is caused by lipase

Q14Physical & chemical digestion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the chemical digestion of starch from the mouth to the end of the small intestine. Name each enzyme, where it is made, and the final product. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mouth: salivary amylase (made in the salivary glands) changes starch to maltose. In the stomach, the acid stops the amylase. In the duodenum, pancreatic amylase (made in the pancreas) changes the remaining starch to maltose. Maltase on the small intestine wall changes maltose to glucose, the final product.
    Any six: salivary amylase from salivary glands acts in the mouth; starch to maltose; acid in the stomach stops the amylase; pancreatic amylase from the pancreas acts in the duodenum; maltase on the intestine wall; maltose to glucose; glucose is the final product.
Q15Physical & chemical digestion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A student tests the digestion of boiled egg white by pepsin. She cuts the egg white into cubes of two sizes, 2 mm and 8 mm, using the same total mass (2 g) in each tube. Each tube has 5 cm³ of pepsin solution and 5 cm³ of dilute hydrochloric acid at 37 °C, and she records the time for the egg white to clear.

  1. (a) State the independent variable and the dependent variable. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the same total mass of egg white was used in each tube. [2]
  3. (c) The 2 mm pieces cleared in 40 minutes and the 8 mm pieces cleared in 160 minutes. Calculate how many times faster the 2 mm pieces were digested. [2]
  4. (d) Explain the difference in the times using the idea of surface area. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Independent: size (or surface area) of the egg white pieces. Dependent: time taken for the egg white to clear (or rate of digestion).
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) To keep the amount of substrate the same, so that the size of the pieces is the only variable that changes (a fair test).
    1 mark: keeps the amount of substrate constant; 1 mark: makes the size of the pieces the only variable.
  3. (c) 4 times faster (160 ÷ 40 = 4)
    1 mark for the method (160 ÷ 40); 1 mark for the answer.
  4. (d) The 2 mm pieces have a larger total surface area, so more pepsin molecules contact the protein per unit time, so digestion is faster.
    1 mark: larger surface area; 1 mark: more enzyme-substrate contact per unit time.

  1. (a) Iodine is needed to make thyroxine; without enough iodine less thyroxine is made, so the thyroid gland enlarges (goitre) as it tries to produce more; the metabolic rate falls, causing tiredness and weight gain.
    1 mark: iodine needed to make thyroxine; 1 mark: thyroid enlarges (goitre); 1 mark: metabolic rate falls.
Q90Balanced diet & deficiencies (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A nutrition label gives these values per 100 g of a biscuit: protein 12 g, carbohydrate 60 g, fat 8 g. Use these energy values: protein 17 kJ/g, carbohydrate 16 kJ/g, fat 37 kJ/g.

  1. (a) Calculate the energy in 100 g of the biscuit, in kJ. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the energy that comes from fat. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1460 kJ
    (12 × 17) + (60 × 16) + (8 × 37) = 204 + 960 + 296 = 1460 kJ. 1 mark method, 1 mark answer.
  2. (b) 20.3 %
    296 ÷ 1460 × 100 = 20.27 %, so 20.3 %. 1 mark method, 1 mark answer.
