Q2Light: reflection & refraction · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Sana stands 1.5 m in front of a large vertical plane mirror in a tailor's shop in Faisalabad.

  1. (a) State three properties of the image formed by a plane mirror. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the distance between Sana and her image. [1]
  3. (c) Sana walks 0.5 m towards the mirror. Calculate the new distance between Sana and her image. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Virtual; upright; same size as the object; laterally inverted; as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.
    1 mark each for any three.
  2. (b) 3.0 m
    B1 image 1.5 m behind mirror, so 1.5 + 1.5 = 3.0 m.
  3. (c) 2.0 m
    B1 she is now 1.0 m from the mirror, so 2 × 1.0 = 2.0 m.
Q3Light: reflection & refraction · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A ray of light in air strikes the side of a rectangular glass block. The angle of incidence is 40° and the angle of refraction in the glass is 25°.

  1. (a) Describe what happens to the direction of the ray as it enters the glass, and explain why. [2]
  2. (b) Define refractive index in terms of the speeds of light. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the refractive index of the glass. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It bends towards the normal, because light travels more slowly in glass than in air.
    B1 bends towards normal; B1 slows down in glass.
  2. (b) Refractive index = speed of light in vacuum (air) ÷ speed of light in the material.
    B1
  3. (c) 1.52
    C1 n = sin i ÷ sin r = sin 40° ÷ sin 25°; A1 1.52.
Q4Light: reflection & refraction · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A ray of light in air hits the flat face of a perspex block at an angle of incidence of 55°. The refractive index of the perspex is 1.50.

  1. (a) Calculate the angle of refraction in the perspex. [3]
  2. (b) The speed of light in air is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. Calculate the speed of light in the perspex. [2]
  3. (c) The ray leaves the opposite parallel face of the block. State the angle between the emerging ray and the normal and describe its direction compared with the incident ray. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 33.1°
    C1 sin r = sin i ÷ n; C1 sin r = sin 55° ÷ 1.50 = 0.546; A1 r = 33.1°.
  2. (b) 2.0 × 10⁸ m/s
    C1 v = c ÷ n; A1 2.0 × 10⁸ m/s.
  3. (c) 55°; it is parallel to the incident ray (but displaced sideways).
    B1 55°; B1 parallel to the original ray / laterally displaced.
Q5Light: reflection & refraction · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

An underwater lamp in a swimming pool in Lahore sends light upwards towards the water surface. The refractive index of water is 1.33.

  1. (a) Define critical angle. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the critical angle for light going from water into air. [2]
  3. (c) Light from the lamp hits the water surface at an angle of incidence of 60°. State and explain what happens to this light. [2]
  4. (d) State the two conditions needed for total internal reflection. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the angle of refraction is 90°.
    B1 angle of incidence in the denser (optically) medium; B1 refracted ray at 90° / along the boundary.
  2. (b) 48.8°
    C1 sin c = 1 ÷ n = 1 ÷ 1.33; A1 48.8°.
  3. (c) It is totally internally reflected back into the water, because 60° is greater than the critical angle (48.8°).
    B1 total internal reflection; B1 angle of incidence greater than critical angle (light going from water to air).
  4. (d) Light must travel from a denser medium towards a less dense one (e.g. water to air); the angle of incidence must be greater than the critical angle.
    B1 each.
Q6Light: reflection & refraction · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The refractive index of diamond is 2.42. The speed of light in air is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. What is the speed of light in diamond? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 1.2 × 10⁸ m/s
    v = c ÷ n = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.42 = 1.24 × 10⁸ m/s. D comes from multiplying by n; light can never travel faster than in air.
Q7Light: reflection & refraction · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Fibre-optic cables carry internet data between cities in Pakistan as pulses of light. Consider a simple glass fibre of refractive index 1.48 surrounded by air.

  1. (a) Calculate the critical angle for light inside the glass fibre. [2]
  2. (b) A ray inside the fibre hits the side wall at an angle of incidence of 60°. A second ray hits the wall at 35°. Describe what happens to each ray. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why a signal can travel along the fibre even where the cable bends gently. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why a very sharp bend in the cable can cause the signal to be lost. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 42.5°
    C1 sin c = 1 ÷ 1.48; A1 42.5°.
  2. (b) 60°: totally internally reflected and stays inside the fibre. 35°: mostly refracts out of the fibre (with a weak partial reflection) because 35° is less than 42.5°.
    B1 60° → TIR; B1 35° → refracts out / partially reflected, less than critical angle.
  3. (c) Light keeps striking the inner wall at angles greater than the critical angle, so it is totally internally reflected again and again along the fibre with very little loss.
    B1 angle of incidence stays above critical angle; B1 repeated total internal reflections.
  4. (d) At a sharp bend the light meets the wall at an angle smaller than the critical angle, so it refracts out.
    B1
Q8Light: reflection & refraction · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A student traces rays through a rectangular glass block on paper using optical pins. She measures the angle of refraction r for five angles of incidence i:i / °: 20, 30, 40, 50, 60r / °: 13, 20, 25, 31, 35

  1. (a) Calculate sin i ÷ sin r for each pair of readings. Give each value to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean value of the refractive index. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the value for i = 30° differs most from the mean. [1]
  4. (d) Describe two techniques the student should use to locate the rays accurately with the pins. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.52, 1.46, 1.52, 1.49, 1.51
    B3 all five correct (1.52, 1.46, 1.52, 1.49, 1.51); B2 four; B1 two or three.
  2. (b) 1.50
    B1 1.50 (ecf).
  3. (c) For i = 30° the angle of refraction is small (20°), so a reading error of about 1° (e.g. a pin slightly out of line, or the protractor misread) is a large fraction of it and changes sin r a lot.
    B1
  4. (d) Place the pins far apart (at least 5 cm); view the bases of the pins (not the tops) and line them up so they appear in a straight line; use a sharp pencil and draw the normal accurately with a protractor.
    1 mark each for any two techniques tied to the pin method.

Q10General wave properties · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A boy on the shore of Kinjhar Lake hits the water with a stick 2.5 times every second. The waves produced have a wavelength of 0.4 m.

  1. (a) State what is meant by the wavelength of a wave. [1]
  2. (b) State what is meant by the frequency of a wave. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the water waves. [2]
  4. (d) The boy hits the water harder but at the same rate. State what happens to the amplitude, the frequency and the speed of the waves. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The distance between two neighbouring crests (or the same point on adjacent waves).
    B1
  2. (b) The number of waves passing a point (or produced) per second.
    B1
  3. (c) 1.0 m/s
    C1 v = fλ = 2.5 × 0.40; A1 1.0 m/s.
  4. (d) Amplitude increases; frequency stays the same; speed stays the same.
    B2 all three; B1 two.
Q11General wave properties · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A student lays a long slinky spring on a smooth bench and fixes one end.

  1. (a) Describe how the student can use the spring to send a longitudinal wave along it. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how the student can use the same spring to send a transverse wave along it. [1]
  3. (c) For the longitudinal wave, explain the terms compression and rarefaction. [2]
  4. (d) State what is transferred by a wave and what is not transferred. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Push and pull one end backwards and forwards along the length of the spring.
    B1
  2. (b) Move one end from side to side, at right angles to the length of the spring.
    B1
  3. (c) Compression: a region where the coils are closer together than normal; rarefaction: a region where the coils are further apart than normal.
    B1 each.
  4. (d) Energy is transferred; the material (the coils) is not transferred – the coils only oscillate about fixed positions.
    B1 energy; B1 not matter / particles only oscillate.
Q12General wave properties · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A dipper vibrates in a ripple tank. A student counts 15 waves passing a mark in 6.0 s. She measures the distance between the first and the sixth of 6 consecutive crests as 0.5 m.

  1. (a) Calculate the frequency of the waves. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the wavelength of the waves. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the waves. [1]
  4. (d) The waves move into a region where the water is shallower. State what happens to their speed, frequency and wavelength. [3]
  5. (e) The boundary between the deep and shallow water is at an angle to the wavefronts. State the name of the effect on the direction of the waves. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.5 Hz
    B1 f = 15 ÷ 6.0 = 2.5 Hz.
  2. (b) 0.10 m
    C1 six crests span five wavelengths; A1 λ = 0.50 ÷ 5 = 0.10 m.
  3. (c) 0.25 m/s
    B1 v = fλ = 0.25 m/s (ecf).
  4. (d) Speed decreases; frequency stays the same; wavelength decreases.
    B1 each.
  5. (e) Refraction (the wavefronts change direction).
    B1
Q13General wave properties · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Straight water waves of wavelength 2.0 cm pass through a gap in a barrier. For which gap width is the spreading of the waves (diffraction) greatest? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 2.0 cm
    Diffraction is greatest when the gap is about the same size as the wavelength; the 2.0 cm gap equals the wavelength, so the waves spread out most (almost semicircular wavefronts). With the 20 cm gap the waves pass through almost straight, with spreading only at the edges.
Q14General wave properties · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

In a valley in Gilgit-Baltistan surrounded by high ridges, a family can receive a long-wave radio station of frequency 200 kHz, but mobile-phone signals of frequency 900 MHz from a mast beyond the ridge are very weak.

  1. (a) Calculate the wavelength of each signal. The speed of radio waves is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. [2]
  2. (b) Use your answers to explain why the long-wave signal is still received in the valley but the mobile signal is weak. [3]
  3. (c) Describe, in words, the pattern of wavefronts produced when plane waves pass through a gap much wider than their wavelength. Mention the wavelength. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Long-wave: 1500 m; mobile: 0.33 m
    C1 λ = v ÷ f; A1 both: 1500 m and 0.33 m.
  2. (b) Diffraction is significant when the wavelength is comparable with the size of the obstacle/gap; the 1500 m waves diffract (bend) round the mountain ridges into the valley; the 0.33 m waves are far smaller than the hills, diffract very little, so the valley is in a 'shadow'.
    B1 long wavelength diffracts more; B1 comparable to size of hills/ridges so bends round into the valley; B1 short wavelength hardly diffracts – shadow region.
  3. (c) Mostly straight wavefronts continue through the gap with only slight curving at the edges; the wavelength is unchanged.
    B1 mainly straight with slight bending at edges; B1 same wavelength (spacing) after the gap.
Q15General wave properties · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A tsunami crosses the deep Indian Ocean at a speed of 200 m/s. The time between one wave crest and the next passing a buoy is 600 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the frequency of the tsunami. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the wavelength of the tsunami in the deep ocean. Give your answer in km. [2]
  3. (c) Near the coast the speed falls to 20 m/s. Calculate the new wavelength. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the frequency of the wave does not change when it moves into shallow water. [1]
  5. (e) Suggest why the height of the wave increases as it approaches the coast. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.00167 Hz (1.7 × 10⁻³ Hz)
    B1 f = 1 ÷ T = 1 ÷ 600.
  2. (b) 120 km
    C1 λ = v × T (= v ÷ f); A1 120 km.
  3. (c) 12 km
    C1 frequency/period unchanged; A1 12 km.
  4. (d) The frequency is set by the source; the number of waves arriving per second must equal the number leaving per second.
    B1
  5. (e) The waves slow down and bunch up (shorter wavelength), so the same energy is concentrated into a shorter length of water, giving a larger amplitude.
    B1

  1. (a) D violet

Q17Lenses & dispersion · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A boy in Bahawalpur holds a converging lens above a sheet of paper on a sunny day. He moves the lens until a small bright image of the Sun appears. The distance from the lens to the paper is then 12 cm.

  1. (a) State what is meant by the principal focus of a converging lens. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the image of the Sun forms at a distance equal to the focal length. [1]
  3. (c) The boy finds that the paper becomes very hot at the bright spot. Explain why. [1]
  4. (d) State one safety precaution he should take. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The point on the principal axis through which rays parallel to the axis pass after refraction by the lens.
    B1 rays parallel to principal axis; B1 converge to / pass through a point on the axis.
  2. (b) The Sun is so far away that its rays arriving at the lens are parallel, so they meet at the principal focus.
    B1
  3. (c) The lens concentrates the light (and infrared) energy from a large area into a small spot.
    B1
  4. (d) Never look at the Sun through the lens; do it on a non-flammable surface / keep away from dry grass.
    B1
Q18Lenses & dispersion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A converging lens has a focal length of 10 cm. An object is placed on the principal axis.

  1. (a) Describe the image formed when the object is 25 cm from the lens. State where it forms. [3]
  2. (b) Describe, in words, the two rays you would draw to locate this image on a ray diagram. [2]
  3. (c) The object is moved to 6 cm from the lens. Describe the image now and state one use of the lens in this arrangement. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Real, inverted, diminished; on the opposite side of the lens, between 10 cm and 20 cm from it (between F and 2F).
    B1 real and inverted; B1 diminished (smaller); B1 position between F and 2F on the other side.
  2. (b) A ray from the top of the object parallel to the principal axis, which refracts through the principal focus on the far side; a ray from the top of the object straight through the optical centre, undeviated.
    B1 each (accept a ray through the near focus emerging parallel).
  3. (c) Virtual, upright, magnified, on the same side as the object; used as a magnifying glass.
    B1 virtual and upright; B1 magnified; B1 magnifying glass.
Q19Lenses & dispersion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Ayesha and her grandfather both visit an optician in Rawalpindi.

  1. (a) Ayesha is short-sighted. Describe what she sees and state where the image of a distant object forms in her eye. [2]
  2. (b) State the type of lens used to correct short sight and explain how it works. [2]
  3. (c) Her grandfather is long-sighted. State the type of lens he needs and where the image would form without it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Distant objects look blurred (near objects are clear); the image forms in front of the retina.
    B1 distant objects blurred; B1 image in front of retina.
  2. (b) A diverging (concave) lens; it spreads the rays out before they enter the eye so the eye lens focuses them further back, on the retina.
    B1 diverging lens; B1 rays diverged so image moves back onto retina.
  3. (c) Converging (convex) lens; without it the image of a near object forms behind the retina.
    B1 converging; B1 behind the retina.
Q20Lenses & dispersion · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

On graph paper, a thin converging lens lies along the y-axis with its optical centre at (0, 0); the principal axis is the x-axis and 1 unit = 1 cm. The focal length is 4 cm, so a principal focus is at (4, 0). An object is a vertical arrow from (−12, 0) to (−12, 3).Ray 1 leaves the top of the arrow parallel to the axis, reaches the lens at (0, 3) and is refracted through (4, 0).Ray 2 leaves the top of the arrow and passes straight through the optical centre.

  1. (a) Write down the equation of the line followed by ray 1 after it passes through the lens. [1]
  2. (b) Write down the equation of the line followed by ray 2. [1]
  3. (c) Find the coordinates where the rays meet. Hence state the position and height of the image. [3]
  4. (d) Describe the image fully (three properties). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It passes through (0, 3) and (4, 0): y = 3 − 0.75x.
    B1
  2. (b) It passes through (−12, 3) and (0, 0): y = −0.25x.
    B1
  3. (c) (6, −1.5); the image is 6 cm from the lens on the opposite side and 1.5 cm tall.
    M1 3 − 0.75x = −0.25x; A1 x = 6, y = −1.5; B1 image 6 cm from lens, height 1.5 cm.
  4. (d) Real, inverted, diminished (half the size of the object).
    B2 all three; B1 two.
Q21Lenses & dispersion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A narrow beam of white light enters a glass prism at an angle of incidence of 50°. The refractive index of the glass is 1.51 for red light and 1.53 for violet light.

  1. (a) Calculate the angle of refraction in the glass for the red light and for the violet light. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the angle between the red and violet rays inside the glass. [1]
  3. (c) Explain, in terms of speed, why the colours separate. [2]
  4. (d) State what is meant by monochromatic light. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Red: 30.5°; violet: 30.0°
    C1 sin r = sin 50° ÷ n; A1 red 30.5°; A1 violet 30.0°.
  2. (b) 0.44° (about 0.4°)
    B1 30.49° − 30.05° = 0.44° (accept 0.4–0.5°, ecf from rounded angles).
  3. (c) Different colours (frequencies) travel at different speeds in glass, so they have different refractive indices; violet slows most and is refracted most, red least.
    B1 different speeds / refractive indices for different colours; B1 violet refracted more than red.
  4. (d) Light of a single frequency (single colour / wavelength).
    B1

Q23Sound · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

An electric bell is hung inside a sealed glass jar. The bell is switched on and air is then slowly pumped out of the jar.

  1. (a) Describe what happens to the sound heard as the air is pumped out, and explain why. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how sound travels through air, using the words compression and rarefaction. [2]
  3. (c) List solids, liquids and gases in order of the speed of sound in them, fastest first. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The sound becomes quieter and then cannot be heard (though the bell is still seen vibrating), because sound needs a medium and cannot travel through a vacuum.
    B1 sound fades / disappears; B1 sound needs a medium (cannot travel in vacuum).
  2. (b) The vibrating source pushes air particles back and forth along the direction of travel, producing regions of high pressure (compressions) and low pressure (rarefactions) that travel outwards.
    B1 particles vibrate parallel to direction of travel (longitudinal); B1 compressions and rarefactions passed on.
  3. (c) Solids, liquids, gases.
    B1
Q24Sound · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Two students stand 85 m from a high wall of their school in Hyderabad. One claps two wooden blocks and the other measures the time until the echo is heard as 0.50 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the speed of sound given by this measurement. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why timing one echo with a hand-held stopwatch is not reliable. [1]
  3. (c) Describe how the students could improve the method. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 340 m/s
    C1 total distance = 2 × 85 = 170 m; A1 v = 170 ÷ 0.50 = 340 m/s.
  2. (b) 0.50 s is short compared with human reaction time (about 0.2 s), so the percentage error is large.
    B1
  3. (c) Clap repeatedly so each clap coincides with the echo of the previous one; time 20 (or more) claps and divide; repeat and average.
    B1 time many intervals/claps and divide; B1 repeat and average / use a larger distance.
Q25Sound · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A fishing ship near Gwadar uses an echo sounder. A pulse of ultrasound is sent straight down and the echo from the sea bed returns 0.8 s later. The speed of sound in sea water is 1500 m/s.

  1. (a) State what is meant by ultrasound. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the depth of the sea below the ship. [2]
  3. (c) A deeper part of the sea gives an echo that is quieter. State the property of the sound wave that has changed and how it has changed. [1]
  4. (d) State how the pitch of a sound is related to its frequency. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sound with a frequency above 20 kHz (above the human hearing range).
    B1
  2. (b) 600 m
    C1 distance = speed × time ÷ 2; A1 600 m.
  3. (c) The amplitude is smaller.
    B1
  4. (d) Higher frequency gives a higher pitch.
    B1
Q26Sound · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

During a storm over Lahore, Bilal sees a flash of lightning and hears the thunder 4.5 s later. The speed of sound in air is 340 m/s.

  1. (a) Calculate the distance to the lightning. [2]
  2. (b) Show that the time for the light to reach Bilal is negligible. The speed of light is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s. [1]
  3. (c) A tuning fork of frequency 256 Hz is struck. Calculate the wavelength of the sound in air and in water. The speed of sound in water is 1480 m/s. [3]
  4. (d) The tuning fork is struck harder. State and explain how the sound heard changes, and whether its wavelength changes. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1530 m
    C1 d = v × t = 340 × 4.5; A1 1530 m.
  2. (b) t = 1530 ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 5.1 µs, far less than 4.5 s (and reaction time).
    B1
  3. (c) Air: 1.33 m; water: 5.78 m
    C1 λ = v ÷ f; A1 1.33 m; A1 5.78 m.
  4. (d) It sounds louder because the amplitude is larger; the pitch and wavelength do not change (frequency and speed unchanged).
    B1 louder – larger amplitude; B1 wavelength unchanged.

Q30Electromagnetic spectrum · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A Pakistani television channel broadcasts live cricket using a communications satellite in a geostationary orbit about 36 000 km above the Equator.

  1. (a) Suggest why microwaves, rather than visible light or long-wave radio, are used to communicate with satellites. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the time for a signal to travel from a ground station up to the satellite and back down to a receiver directly below. Assume the satellite is 36 000 km above both. [3]
  3. (c) A television's remote control uses a different region of the spectrum. Name it and state one reason why it is suitable. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Microwaves pass through the atmosphere (and clouds) without much absorption, and their short wavelength means little diffraction, so they can be sent as a narrow beam to the satellite.
    B1 pass through atmosphere/ionosphere; B1 little diffraction / narrow beam (or high data rate).
  2. (b) 0.24 s
    C1 distance = 2 × 3.6 × 10⁷ m; C1 t = d ÷ v; A1 0.24 s.
  3. (c) Infrared; it is short range, does not pass through walls (no interference with neighbours), and is harmless at low power.
    B1 infrared; B1 any suitable reason.
