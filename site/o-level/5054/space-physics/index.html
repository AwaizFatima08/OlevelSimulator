Q1Stars & the Universe · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is a light-year?A the time light takes to reach the Earth from the SunB the distance travelled by light in a vacuum in one yearC the time for the Earth to orbit the SunD the brightness of a star seen from one year away [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B the distance travelled by light in a vacuum in one year
    A light-year is a unit of distance: the distance light travels through a vacuum in one year (about 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m). A confuses it with a time.
Q2Stars & the Universe · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which two elements make up most of the Sun?A hydrogen and heliumB hydrogen and oxygenC helium and carbonD iron and nickel [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A hydrogen and helium
    The Sun is mostly hydrogen with helium (made by fusion). Iron and nickel make up much of the Earth's core, not the Sun.

Q6Stars & the Universe · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A star with a mass about twenty times that of the Sun reaches the end of its stable life. Which sequence is correct?A red giant → planetary nebula → white dwarfB red supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black holeC white dwarf → red giant → supernovaD protostar → red supergiant → white dwarf [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B red supergiant → supernova → neutron star or black hole
    Very massive stars expand into red supergiants and explode as supernovae, leaving a neutron star or (for the most massive) a black hole. A is the path for a star like the Sun.
Q7Stars & the Universe · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A stable star stays the same size for billions of years. Explain why, in terms of the forces acting. [2]
  2. (b) Describe what happens to a star like the Sun when most of the hydrogen in its core has been used. [2]
  3. (c) Describe what remains at the end of the life of a star like the Sun. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The inward force of gravity is balanced by the outward force (pressure) due to the high temperature from fusion in the core.
    B1 gravity pulls inwards; B1 balanced by outward pressure from fusion energy / high temperature.
  2. (b) Fusion in the core slows; the outer layers expand and cool, forming a red giant.
    B1 expands; B1 becomes a red giant (outer layers cooler, red).
  3. (c) The outer layers drift away as a planetary nebula, leaving a small hot dense core called a white dwarf.
    B1 planetary nebula; B1 white dwarf.

Q10Stars & the Universe · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

New stars are forming in the Orion Nebula, a huge cloud of dust and gas.

  1. (a) Describe how a protostar forms from an interstellar cloud. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the protostar becomes hotter as it contracts. [2]
  3. (c) State what must happen in the protostar for it to become a stable star. [1]
  4. (d) Some interstellar clouds contain elements such as iron and carbon as well as hydrogen. Suggest where these heavier elements came from. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gravity pulls the dust and gas together; the cloud collapses into a dense, hot ball (protostar).
    B1 gravitational attraction pulls particles together; B1 cloud contracts into a dense clump.
  2. (b) Gravitational potential energy of the falling material is converted into kinetic energy of the particles, so the temperature rises.
    B1 loss of gravitational potential energy; B1 converted to kinetic energy (thermal energy) of particles.
  3. (c) Its core must become hot enough for hydrogen nuclei to fuse into helium.
    B1
  4. (d) They were made by fusion in earlier stars and scattered by supernovae (and planetary nebulae).
    B1

  1. (a) The Hubble constant is 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second. A galaxy is 1.0 × 10²⁴ m from the Earth. What is the speed at which it is moving away from the Earth?A 2.2 × 10⁶ m/sB 4.5 × 10⁴¹ m/sC 4.5 × 10⁻⁷ m/sD 2.2 × 10⁻⁴² m/s [1]

  1. (a) A 2.2 × 10⁶ m/s
    v = H₀d = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ × 1.0 × 10²⁴ = 2.2 × 10⁶ m/s. B comes from dividing d by H₀ instead of multiplying (that gives a time-like quantity, not a speed).
Q13Stars & the Universe · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Astronomers measure the redshift of a distant galaxy and find it is moving away from the Earth at 1.5 × 10⁷ m/s. The Hubble constant H₀ = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second.

  1. (a) Calculate the distance to the galaxy in metres. [2]
  2. (b) Convert this distance into light-years. One light-year = 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m. [1]
  3. (c) The quantity 1/H₀ gives an estimate of the age of the Universe. Calculate this age in years. [3]
  4. (d) Describe how astronomers can find the distance to a far galaxy without using its speed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.82 × 10²⁴ m
    C1 d = v ÷ H₀ = 1.5 × 10⁷ ÷ 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸; A1 6.82 × 10²⁴ m.
  2. (b) 7.2 × 10⁸ light-years
    B1 7.2 × 10⁸ ly (ecf).
  3. (c) 4.5 × 10¹⁷ s ≈ 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years (about 14 billion years)
    C1 1 ÷ 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 4.5 × 10¹⁷ s; C1 one year = 3.16 × 10⁷ s; A1 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years.
  4. (d) By measuring the brightness of a supernova in it (supernovae of a particular type have a known brightness, so the dimmer it looks the further away it is).
    B1
Q14Stars & the Universe · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Galaxy A is 3.0 × 10²⁴ m from the Earth and galaxy B is 1.2 × 10²⁵ m from the Earth.

  1. (a) Calculate the speed at which each galaxy is moving away from the Earth. H₀ = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second. [2]
  2. (b) State which galaxy shows the greater redshift. Explain your answer. [2]
  3. (c) A student says: 'All galaxies are moving away from the Earth, so the Earth must be at the centre of the Universe.' Explain why this conclusion is not correct. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: 6.6 × 10⁶ m/s; B: 2.64 × 10⁷ m/s
    C1 v = H₀d; A1 both: 6.6 × 10⁶ m/s and 2.64 × 10⁷ m/s.
  2. (b) Galaxy B – it is moving away faster, and a greater recession speed produces a greater increase in observed wavelength.
    B1 B; B1 greater speed of recession → greater redshift.
  3. (c) Space itself is expanding, so an observer in any galaxy would see all other galaxies moving away from them; there is no centre.
    B1 the Universe/space is expanding uniformly; B1 observers anywhere see the same – no special centre.
Q15Stars & the Universe · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe what happens in the core of a very massive star near the end of its life, leading to a supernova. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why scientists say 'the iron in your blood was made inside stars'. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the Sun will not become a supernova. [1]
  4. (d) State why a black hole cannot be seen directly. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fusion makes heavier and heavier elements up to iron; when fusion can no longer release energy, the core collapses under gravity and the outer layers are blown off in a huge explosion (supernova).
    B1 heavier elements formed by fusion (up to iron) in a red supergiant; B1 core collapses / star explodes.
  2. (b) Elements heavier than helium (such as iron) are made by fusion inside stars and released by supernovae into nebulae; the Solar System formed from such a nebula, so its material includes these elements.
    B1 heavy elements made in stars / supernovae; B1 scattered into a nebula from which the Sun, Earth (and we) formed.
  3. (c) Its mass is too small; it will become a red giant then a white dwarf.
    B1
  4. (d) Its gravitational field is so strong that not even light can escape from it.
    B1
Q16Stars & the Universe · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In the 1960s, radio engineers found a faint signal that their antenna picked up whichever direction it pointed.

  1. (a) State what is meant by the cosmic microwave background radiation (CMBR). [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the CMBR was produced and why it is now detected as microwaves. [3]
  3. (c) Explain how the CMBR supports the Big Bang theory. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Microwave radiation of a particular frequency that is detected coming from all directions in space (very evenly).
    B1 microwave radiation; B1 coming from all directions / everywhere in space.
  2. (b) It was emitted shortly after the Universe formed, when the Universe was very hot (short-wavelength radiation); as the Universe expanded, the wavelength of the radiation was stretched (increased) so it is now in the microwave region.
    B1 produced shortly after the Universe began / when the Universe was very hot; B1 Universe has expanded since; B1 wavelength stretched / increased into the microwave region.
  3. (c) It is the leftover radiation predicted from a hot, dense early Universe that has since expanded and cooled.
    B1

Q19Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why there is day and night in Lahore. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the Sun appears to rise in the east and set in the west. [2]
  3. (c) In June the Sun is higher in the sky at midday in Lahore than in December. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Earth rotates on its axis once in about 24 hours; Lahore faces the Sun for part of the rotation (day) and faces away for the rest (night).

    B1 tilt of the Earth's axis; B1 Northern Hemisphere tilted towards the Sun in June.

Q25Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Data for five planets:planet: Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars, Jupiterdistance from Sun / million km: 58, 108, 150, 228, 778orbital period / Earth years: 0.24, 0.62, 1.00, 1.88, 11.9mean surface temperature / °C: 167, 464, 15, −65, −110 (cloud tops)mean density / g/cm³: 5.4, 5.2, 5.5, 3.9, 1.3

  1. (a) Describe the relationship between distance from the Sun and orbital period. [1]
  2. (b) Use the data to suggest why the inner four planets are rocky and Jupiter is a gas giant. Refer to how the Solar System formed. [3]
  3. (c) Venus is further from the Sun than Mercury but is hotter. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The further a planet is from the Sun, the longer its orbital period (increasing more than proportionally).
    B1
  2. (b) The Solar System formed from a rotating cloud of gas and dust by accretion; near the Sun it was too hot for light, volatile substances (hydrogen, helium, ices) to condense, so only rock and metal clumped together – high-density planets; further out it was cold enough for gases and ices to collect, so Jupiter is large with low density.
    B1 accretion of gas and dust (cloud/nebula); B1 high temperature near the Sun – only rocky/metal material condensed (high density); B1 colder further out – gases collected → low density gas giant.
  3. (c) Venus has a thick atmosphere of carbon dioxide that traps infrared radiation (strong greenhouse effect); Mercury has almost no atmosphere, so it loses energy easily.
    B1 thick CO₂ atmosphere / greenhouse effect on Venus; B1 traps infrared emitted by the surface (Mercury has no atmosphere).

  2. (b) 43 minutes
    C1 7.78 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 2590 s; A1 43 minutes.
  3. (c) The field becomes weaker with increasing distance from the Sun, so planets further out have lower orbital speeds.
    B1 weaker further away; B1 lower orbital speed.
Q27Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A comet orbits the Sun in a very elongated (elliptical) orbit. At its closest it passes inside the orbit of Venus; at its furthest it is beyond Neptune.

  1. (a) State where in its orbit the comet moves fastest. [1]
  2. (b) Explain, in terms of energy, why the comet's speed changes around its orbit. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the gravitational force on the comet is greatest when it is nearest the Sun. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest why the comet develops a bright tail only when it is close to the Sun. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Closest to the Sun.
    B1
  2. (b) As the comet falls towards the Sun, gravitational potential energy is converted to kinetic energy, so it speeds up; as it moves away, kinetic energy is converted back to gravitational potential energy and it slows down; total energy is constant (conserved).
    B1 GPE → KE as it approaches the Sun; B1 KE → GPE moving away; B1 total energy conserved.
  3. (c) The gravitational force decreases with distance, so it is largest at the smallest distance.
    B1
  4. (d) Near the Sun it is heated, so ice turns to gas and releases dust, which reflects sunlight (and is pushed away from the Sun).
    B1
Q28Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Io is a moon of Jupiter. It orbits at a radius of 4.22 × 10⁸ m with a period of 1.77 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the average orbital speed of Io in m/s. [3]
  2. (b) The Moon orbits the Earth at a similar radius of 3.84 × 10⁸ m with a speed of about 1.0 × 10³ m/s. Calculate how many times faster Io moves. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why Io moves much faster than the Moon even though their orbital radii are similar. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.73 × 10⁴ m/s
    C1 T = 1.77 × 86 400 = 1.53 × 10⁵ s; C1 v = 2π × 4.22 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.53 × 10⁵; A1 1.73 × 10⁴ m/s.
  2. (b) About 17 times
    B1 17 (ecf).
  3. (c) Jupiter has a much greater mass than the Earth, so its gravitational field (pull) at that distance is much stronger; a faster speed is needed to stay in orbit.
    B1 Jupiter much more massive; B1 stronger gravitational field/force at the same distance → greater orbital speed.
Q29Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the Solar System formed according to the accretion model. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the Sun contains more than 99 % of the mass of the Solar System. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why all the planets orbit the Sun in the same direction and in nearly the same plane. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A large cloud of gas and dust (containing hydrogen, helium and heavier elements from earlier stars) began to collapse under gravity; it formed a rotating disc; most material collected at the centre to form the Sun; in the disc, dust and gas particles collided and stuck together (accretion), growing into planetesimals and then planets.
    B1 cloud of gas and dust collapses due to gravity; B1 rotating disc with Sun forming at the centre; B1 particles in the disc collide and clump together into planets.
  2. (b) Gravity pulled most of the cloud's material into the centre, where the Sun formed.
    B1
  3. (c) They formed from the same rotating, flattened disc of material, so they kept its direction of rotation and its plane.
    B1 formed from a (flattened) disc; B1 the disc was rotating in one direction.
Q30Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A robotic rover of mass 950 kg is sent to Mars.

  1. (a) Calculate the weight of the rover on Earth and on Mars. Gravitational field strength: Earth 9.8 N/kg; Mars 3.7 N/kg. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why the gravitational field strength at the surface of Mars is less than at the surface of the Earth. [1]
  3. (c) Mars orbits the Sun at a radius of 2.28 × 10¹¹ m with a period of 687 Earth days. Calculate its orbital speed in km/s. [3]
  4. (d) The Earth's orbital speed is about 30 km/s. Explain why Mars moves more slowly. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Earth: 9310 N; Mars: 3515 N
    C1 W = mg; A1 both values.
  2. (b) Mars has a much smaller mass than the Earth (field strength at the surface depends on the planet's mass and radius).
    B1
  3. (c) 24.1 km/s
    C1 T = 687 × 86 400 = 5.94 × 10⁷ s; C1 v = 2πr ÷ T; A1 24.1 km/s.
  4. (d) Mars is further from the Sun, where the Sun's gravitational field is weaker.
    B1
