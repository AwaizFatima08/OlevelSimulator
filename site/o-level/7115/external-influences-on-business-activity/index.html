Q1Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Margalla Cement operates a large cement plant near a village in northern Punjab.

  1. (a) Define 'external cost'. [2]
  2. (b) Identify two external costs that Margalla Cement's plant might create for the village. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A cost of a business's activity that is paid by people or groups outside the business (society), not by the business itself.
    1 mark: a cost resulting from the business's activity. 1 mark: borne by third parties/society, not the business.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. dust/air pollution causing breathing problems; noise from machinery; heavy lorries damaging local roads and causing congestion; damage to farmland or water supplies; loss of natural landscape from quarrying.
    1 mark each.
Q2Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Pine View is a hotel in Murree that attracts many tourists every summer.

  1. (a) Define 'sustainable development'. [2]
  2. (b) Identify two ways Pine View could reduce its impact on the environment. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Development that meets the needs of people today without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
    1 mark: meeting present needs. 1 mark: without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. install solar panels / energy-saving lighting; reuse towels to save water and detergent; recycle waste; stop using single-use plastic; buy food from local suppliers; harvest rainwater.
    1 mark each.
Q3Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A pressure group is campaigning against a drinks company that sells water in single-use plastic bottles in Pakistan.

  1. (a) Define 'pressure group'. [2]
  2. (b) State two methods the pressure group could use to influence the drinks company. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) An organisation of people with a common interest who try to influence the decisions of businesses or the government.
    1 mark: group of people with a shared interest/cause. 1 mark: tries to influence business or government decisions.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. organising a consumer boycott; protests or demonstrations; social media campaigns and bad publicity; petitions; lobbying the government to pass laws.
    1 mark each.
Q4Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

The government has built a new metro bus line through the centre of Lahore.

  1. (a) Define 'external benefit'. [2]
  2. (b) Identify two external benefits of the new metro bus line. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A gain from an activity that is received by people or groups outside the organisation carrying out the activity.
    1 mark: a benefit/gain from an activity. 1 mark: received by third parties/society.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. less road congestion as people leave their cars; less air pollution; shorter journey times so workers reach jobs more easily; more customers for shops near stations; new jobs.
    1 mark each.
Q5Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Several leather tanneries near a river in Punjab release chemical waste into the water. The provincial government wants them to change.

  1. (a) Identify two ways a government could reduce pollution caused by businesses such as the tanneries. [2]
  2. (b) State two reasons why a tannery might choose to act in an environmentally friendly way. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: laws/regulations with fines; pollution taxes; permits/licences that limit waste; subsidies or grants for cleaner equipment; inspections.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any two: avoid fines or closure; improve reputation; attract customers/foreign buyers who demand clean production; reduce waste and costs; avoid pressure group action.
    1 mark each.

Consumers are paying more attention to how businesses behave.

  1. (a) State two examples of unethical business behaviour. [2]
  2. (b) Outline one way consumers can influence a business to behave more ethically. [2]

Q8Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Evergreen Oils produces palm oil on plantations in Malaysia. An environmental pressure group says the company is cutting down rainforest to plant more palm trees.

  1. (a) Identify two ways the government could control the environmental impact of Evergreen Oils. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways the pressure group's campaign could affect Evergreen Oils. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two, e.g. laws banning or limiting forest clearance; fines for illegal clearing; taxes on environmental damage; permits/licences for land use; subsidies for sustainable farming.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Boycott: food manufacturers and consumers may stop buying its palm oil, so sales and profit fall. 2) Bad publicity: the company's reputation is damaged, making it harder to attract investors or win contracts, and it may need to spend on public relations. 3) The company may change its methods, e.g. replanting forest, which raises costs but may later improve its image.
    For each of the two points: 1 mark for identifying the point, 1 mark for applying it to the business, 1 mark for developing the consequence (max 3 per point).
Q9Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

The provincial government has introduced a pollution tax of PKR 1.50 per brick on brick kilns that still use old, smoky technology. Shah Bricks produces 600,000 bricks a month using the old technology.

  1. (a) Calculate the monthly pollution tax Shah Bricks must pay. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways the pollution tax could affect Shah Bricks. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) PKR 900,000
    M1: 600,000 × 1.50. A1: PKR 900,000.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Higher costs: the PKR 900,000 a month reduces profit unless it raises brick prices, which may lose customers to cleaner kilns. 2) Incentive to change: the owner may invest in cleaner (zig-zag) kiln technology to avoid the tax; this needs finance now but saves tax and improves its image. Other: may cut output or workers.
    For each of the two points: 1 mark for identifying the point, 1 mark for applying it to the business, 1 mark for developing the consequence (max 3 per point).
Q10Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Northway Fashion is a clothing retailer in the UK. A newspaper reports that one of its suppliers uses child labour. Another supplier, which follows strict labour standards, charges more.

  1. (a) Define 'ethical decision'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why Northway Fashion might choose the more expensive supplier. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) A decision based on what is morally right, rather than only on what makes the most profit.
    1 mark: based on moral principles/doing what is right. 1 mark: may not maximise profit.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Reputation: continuing with a supplier using child labour could lead to boycotts and lost sales, so paying more protects long-term revenue. 2) Marketing advantage: it can advertise ethically made clothes, attracting customers willing to pay higher prices. Other: avoids legal action; attracts staff and investors who value ethics.
    For each of the two points: 1 mark for identifying the point, 1 mark for applying it to the business, 1 mark for developing the consequence (max 3 per point).
Q11Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A government is deciding whether to build a new international airport outside a city. The following effects have been identified:1 Construction cost paid by the airport company2 Noise for families living nearby3 New jobs for local people in hotels and taxis4 Wages paid by the airport company to its own staff5 Loss of farmland and wildlife habitat6 Shorter journey times for business travellers from the whole region

  1. (a) Classify each of the six effects as a private cost, an external cost, or an external benefit. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the government might approve the airport even though it creates external costs. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Private costs: 1 and 4. External costs: 2 and 5. External benefits: 3 and 6.
    4 marks for all six correct; 3 marks for five; 2 marks for four; 1 mark for two or three.
  2. (b) The government considers the total social costs and benefits; if the external benefits (jobs, faster travel, growth) plus private benefits are greater than all the costs, society as a whole gains.
    1 mark: compare social benefits with social costs. 1 mark: development, e.g. benefits outweigh costs so society gains.
Q12Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

A consumer group has started a social-media campaign against Trendy Threads, a clothing brand, claiming that its factories pay workers below the minimum wage. Many young customers have shared the posts.

  1. (a) Identify two reasons why pressure groups can be successful. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways Trendy Threads could respond to the pressure group's campaign. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: strong public support; media/social-media coverage; can organise boycotts that reduce sales; well-funded or expert; the issue affects many people; government may act on their concerns.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) (1) Investigate and raise wages to at least the minimum, then publish the results – this costs more but can restore its reputation and sales. (2) Use independent inspections/ethical certification and promote this in advertising, so customers trust the brand again. Also: meet the pressure group; public statement/apology; change suppliers.
    For each point: 1 mark knowledge, 1 mark application to this business, 1 mark developed consequence (analysis).
Q13Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Gwadar Fisheries Ltd catches fish off the coast of Balochistan. Its annual catch has fallen from 12,000 tonnes to 8,400 tonnes in five years because of overfishing in the area.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage fall in the annual catch. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why sustainable development is important to Gwadar Fisheries Ltd. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 30%
    (12,000 − 8,400) ÷ 12,000 × 100 (M1) = 30% (A1).
  2. (b) (1) Its whole business depends on fish stocks – if overfishing continues, catches and revenue will keep falling until the business cannot survive. (2) Customers and export buyers increasingly want sustainably caught fish, and the government may impose quotas or bans, so acting now protects sales and avoids penalties.
    For each point: 1 mark knowledge, 1 mark application to this business, 1 mark developed consequence (analysis).
Q14Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Oasis Hotels owns six hotels in Pakistan. The directors think that becoming more environmentally friendly will only increase costs.

  1. (a) Explain two ways becoming more environmentally friendly could increase Oasis Hotels' profits. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) (1) Lower costs: solar panels, LED lighting and water-saving showers reduce electricity and water bills, increasing profit after the initial investment. (2) Higher revenue: many tourists and companies prefer 'green' hotels, so Oasis can attract more guests or charge higher prices; it may also gain good publicity and government support. Also: avoid fines.
    For each of two points: 1 mark knowledge, 1 mark application to the case, 2 marks developed analysis.
Q15Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Green Leaf Tea Company buys tea from farmers in Sri Lanka. It pays farmers above the market price and uses only recyclable packaging. Its shareholders want higher profits.

  1. (a) Outline one way ethical behaviour could increase Green Leaf's sales. [2]
  2. (b) Do you think acting ethically will reduce Green Leaf's profits? Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) e.g. Customers who care about fair treatment of farmers may choose Green Leaf over rival brands, increasing sales and allowing a higher price.
    1 mark for the point, 1 mark for development in context.
  2. (b) Reduce: paying above market prices and recyclable packaging raise costs; if prices cannot rise, profit margins fall; price-sensitive customers may buy cheaper tea. Not reduce: stronger brand image and loyalty; can charge a premium price; reliable supply from loyal farmers; avoids bad publicity. Judgement, e.g. in the short run profit may fall, but in the long run it may rise if customers value ethical tea; it depends on how many customers will pay more.
    Up to 2 marks for knowledge/analysis of points for, up to 2 for points against, 2 marks for a justified judgement that explains why one side outweighs the other in this context.
Q16Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

Bean There is a chain of coffee shops in Karachi. It buys 20,000 kg of coffee beans a year from Supplier X at $4.50 per kg. Supplier X has been accused of paying workers very low wages. Supplier Y is a certified fair-trade supplier that charges 12% more per kg. Bean There's customers are mostly young professionals.

  1. (a) Should Bean There switch to Supplier Y? Justify your answer using suitable calculations. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Calculations: $10,800. Switch: protects reputation; fair-trade logo can be used in promotion and may attract young professionals who care about ethics; may justify slightly higher prices; avoids risk of boycott. Stay: extra $10,800 a year raises costs and reduces profit; customers may not notice or pay more; Supplier X may improve. Judgement, e.g. switch, because the extra cost is small compared with the risk to the brand and could be covered by a very small price rise per cup.
    Calculations: 20,000 × 4.50 = $90,000. 12% × 90,000 = $10,800. Content: extra cost 12% × 20,000 × 4.50 = $10,800 a year; fair-trade logo in promotion, appeal to ethically minded young professionals, reputation and boycott risk, possible small price rise per cup; higher costs and lower profit, customers may not notice or pay more. Mark bands (12; knowledge 2, application 2, analysis 4, evaluation 4): Level 3 (9–12): developed analysis of both sides / all options applied to Bean There, using the data, and a justified decision that explains why the rejected option(s) are weaker. Level 2 (5–8): advantages and disadvantages analysed and applied to Bean There, with a decision that is only partly justified or one-sided. Level 1 (1–4): relevant knowledge with little application or analysis; a decision with no reasoning earns no evaluation marks. Correct calculations count towards application and analysis; follow through an earlier calculation error.
Q17Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

Kohinoor Spinning Mills pays PKR 2.1 million a month for electricity from the national grid. It could install solar panels on its roof for PKR 18 million. The panels would reduce its electricity bill by PKR 450,000 a month. The mills would need a bank loan to buy the panels.

  1. (a) Should Kohinoor Spinning Mills install the solar panels? Justify your answer using suitable calculations. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Calculations: 40 months. For: saves PKR 5.4 m a year; cost recovered in 40 months (about 3 years 4 months) and panels last much longer; less pollution so better image with buyers; protects against rising grid prices and power cuts. Against: PKR 18 m loan means interest costs; savings may be less on cloudy days; money cannot be used for new machinery (opportunity cost). Judgement, e.g. install, because savings repay the cost in just over 3 years while panels last far longer, as long as loan interest is affordable.
    Calculations: 18,000,000 ÷ 450,000. 40 months. Content: payback 18,000,000 ÷ 450,000 = 40 months; saving PKR 5.4 m a year; panels last much longer than the payback; protection from grid price rises and power cuts; image with buyers; loan interest; lower output on cloudy days; opportunity cost (machinery). Mark bands (12; knowledge 2, application 2, analysis 4, evaluation 4): Level 3 (9–12): developed analysis of both sides / all options applied to Kohinoor Spinning Mills, using the data, and a justified decision that explains why the rejected option(s) are weaker. Level 2 (5–8): advantages and disadvantages analysed and applied to Kohinoor Spinning Mills, with a decision that is only partly justified or one-sided. Level 1 (1–4): relevant knowledge with little application or analysis; a decision with no reasoning earns no evaluation marks. Correct calculations count towards application and analysis; follow through an earlier calculation error.
Q18Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

AutoPak assembles cars in Karachi. The government and customers are increasingly concerned about the environment.

  1. (a) Identify two ways AutoPak could make its cars more environmentally friendly. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two environmental issues AutoPak faces. Which is likely to be the most important to AutoPak? Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two, e.g. produce hybrid or electric cars; improve fuel efficiency; use recycled materials; reduce weight; fit cleaner engines meeting emission standards.

Waste and resource use: disposal of old parts and use of steel and plastics raises costs and attracts criticism. Other: energy use in the factory; pressure groups. Q19Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

A government plans to introduce a carbon tax of $5 per tonne of carbon dioxide (CO₂). Rock Cement's plant emits 200,000 tonnes of CO₂ a year.

  1. (a) Calculate the carbon tax Rock Cement would pay each year. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Do you think taxes are better than laws (regulations) for reducing pollution from businesses such as Rock Cement? Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) $1,000,000
    200,000 × 5 (M1) = $1,000,000 (A1).
  2. (b) Taxes: give businesses a reason to cut emissions in the cheapest way and raise government revenue; but firms may just pay and pass the cost to customers, so pollution may not fall much. Laws: set strict limits and can stop dangerous pollution quickly; but need inspection and may be costly for firms to meet all at once. Judgement e.g.: laws for very harmful pollution, taxes for gradual reduction of CO₂.
    Marks: knowledge 2, analysis 2, evaluation 2 – evaluation needs a justified judgement that weighs both sides; a bare yes/no or a summary earns no evaluation mark.
Q20Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

Ravi Dyeing Ltd releases untreated waste water into a canal used by farmers. The government has started fining it. The directors are considering three options.Option 1: build its own water-treatment plant – cost Rs 45 million, then running costs Rs 3 million a year.Option 2: carry on and pay fines of about Rs 8 million a year. Officials warn that the factory could be closed if pollution continues.Option 3: move to an industrial estate with a shared treatment plant – moving cost Rs 20 million, then a fee of Rs 5 million a year. The estate is 40 km away and some workers may not move.

  1. (a) Which option should Ravi Dyeing Ltd choose? Justify your answer using suitable calculations and ethical considerations. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Five-year cost: Option 1 = 45 + 5 × 3 = Rs 60 million; Option 2 = 5 × 8 = Rs 40 million plus risk of closure, damaged reputation and harm to farmers – unethical and probably not allowed to continue; Option 3 = 20 + 5 × 5 = Rs 45 million, cheapest legal option but loses some skilled workers and adds transport costs. Over 10 years: Option 1 = Rs 75 million, Option 3 = Rs 70 million. Judgement e.g.: Option 3 on cost, or Option 1 if keeping the workforce and site matters more; Option 2 should be rejected.
    Calculations: 45 + 15 = 60; 8 × 5 = 40; 20 + 25 = 45; 10-year: 45 + 30 = 75, 20 + 50 = 70. Levels: L3 (9–12) a justified recommendation that weighs the options against each other, uses the case data (and calculations where asked) and explains why the rejected option is weaker; L2 (5–8) developed analysis of the options applied to the business, decision only partly supported; L1 (1–4) relevant knowledge with limited application, or a bare decision.

Q24Business and the international economy · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Sialkot Sports makes footballs that it exports to the USA. It charges PKR 3,360 per football. The exchange rate is $1 = PKR 280.

  1. (a) Calculate the price of one football in US dollars at this exchange rate. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) The exchange rate changes to $1 = PKR 260 and Sialkot Sports keeps its price at PKR 3,360. Calculate the new price in US dollars, correct to 2 decimal places, and state whether the rupee has appreciated or depreciated. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $12.00
    M1: 3,360 ÷ 280. A1: $12.
  2. (b) $12.92; the rupee has appreciated
    B1: 3,360 ÷ 260 = $12.92. B1: appreciated (fewer rupees are needed to buy $1).

  2. (b) A rise in the value of a currency compared with another currency, so it buys more of the other currency.
    1 mark: rise in value; 1 mark: against another currency / buys more foreign currency.

Q27Business and the international economy · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Textile Tech in Faisalabad is buying a knitting machine from Germany for €20,000. When it ordered, the exchange rate was €1 = Rs 300. When it pays, the rate is €1 = Rs 320.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of the machine in rupees at each exchange rate and the increase in cost. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) State whether the rupee has appreciated or depreciated against the euro, and explain your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 6,000,000 and Rs 6,400,000; increase Rs 400,000
    20,000 × 300 = 6,000,000 and 20,000 × 320 = 6,400,000 (1); increase 400,000 (1).
  2. (b) Depreciated – more rupees are now needed to buy €1.
    1 mark depreciated; 1 mark reason.
Q28Business and the international economy · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Pak Auto Assemblers in Karachi imports steel at $650 per tonne. The government has put a 15% tariff on imported steel.

  1. (a) Calculate the tariff paid on each tonne of imported steel. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways the tariff could affect Pak Auto Assemblers. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) $97.50 per tonne

  2. (b) e.g. 1) Higher costs: steel costs $747.50 a tonne, raising the cost of each car; either price rises (fewer sales) or profit per car falls. 2) Change of supplier: it may buy steel from local mills instead, which could be cheaper now but may be lower quality or slower to deliver. Other: may need more working capital; competitiveness against imported cars may fall.

In Country Q the labour force is 72 million people, and 5.4 million of them are unemployed. Fresh Pack Foods needs to hire 200 new factory workers.

  1. (a) Calculate the unemployment rate. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Do you think high unemployment is good for Fresh Pack Foods? Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.5%
    5.4 ÷ 72 × 100 (M1) = 7.5% (A1).
  2. (b) Good: many applicants, so it is easy to recruit 200 workers; wage demands are lower; workers value their jobs and may work harder. Bad: unemployed people have less income, so demand for packaged food may fall (or switch to cheaper brands); government may raise taxes to pay benefits. Judgement depends on whether Fresh Pack sells basic foods (demand stable) or luxury snacks (demand falls).

Q58Economic issues · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Prices in a country rose by 12% last year. Workers at Kohat Garments earn Rs 50,000 a month. The trade union wants a 12% pay rise; the managers offer 8%.

  1. (a) Calculate a worker's monthly pay after an 8% rise and after a 12% rise. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Do you think Kohat Garments should give its workers a 12% pay rise? Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 54,000 and Rs 56,000
    50,000 × 1.08 = 54,000 (1); 50,000 × 1.12 = 56,000 (1).
  2. (b) For: with only 8%, workers' real income falls (prices +12%), lowering motivation and possibly causing strikes or labour turnover. Against: higher wage costs reduce profit and competitiveness, especially for exports; the firm's own costs are already rising with inflation; it may lead to further price rises. Judgement e.g.: a rise close to inflation if it can raise prices or productivity; otherwise 8% plus non-financial benefits.
    Marks: knowledge 2, analysis 2, evaluation 2 – evaluation needs a justified judgement that weighs both sides; a bare yes/no or a summary earns no evaluation mark.
Q59Economic issues · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

Coastal Cold Storage wants to build a new warehouse costing Rs 50 million, financed by a bank loan. The interest rate is now 20% a year, but economists expect it to fall to 15% next year. Inflation is high, so building costs are expected to rise by 10% if it waits a year. The new warehouse is forecast to add Rs 14 million a year to profit before interest.

  1. (a) Should Coastal Cold Storage build the warehouse now or wait one year? Justify your answer using suitable calculations. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Build now: annual interest 20% × 50m = Rs 10 million; profit after interest Rs 4 million a year, starting a year earlier. Wait: cost 50m × 1.1 = Rs 55 million; interest 15% × 55m = Rs 8.25 million; profit after interest Rs 5.75 million a year – but one year's profit (Rs 4 million) is lost, and the interest-rate fall is only a forecast. Waiting gives Rs 1.75 million more a year from year 2, so it takes over two years to make up the lost first year. Judgement e.g.: build now (and refinance if rates fall) unless the business is uncertain about demand.
    Calculations: 0.2 × 50 = 10; 14 − 10 = 4; 50 × 1.1 = 55; 0.15 × 55 = 8.25; 14 − 8.25 = 5.75; 5.75 − 4 = 1.75; 4 ÷ 1.75 ≈ 2.3 years. Levels: L3 (9–12) a justified recommendation that weighs the options against each other, uses the case data (and calculations where asked) and explains why the rejected option is weaker; L2 (5–8) developed analysis of the options applied to the business, decision only partly supported; L1 (1–4) relevant knowledge with limited application, or a bare decision.
Q60Economic issues · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

AutoParts Pakistan makes car parts and employs 200 workers, each paid Rs 45,000 a month. A recession has cut its orders by 25%. Managers are considering three options.Option 1: make 50 workers redundant, paying each Rs 150,000 in redundancy pay.Option 2: put all 200 workers on a four-day week, cutting the wage bill by 20%.Option 3: keep all workers and spend Rs 6 million on marketing to win export orders in the Middle East; no orders are guaranteed.

  1. (a) Which option should AutoParts Pakistan choose? Justify your answer using suitable calculations. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Option 1: one-off cost 50 × 150,000 = Rs 7.5 million; saves 50 × 45,000 × 12 = Rs 27 million a year, so net Rs 19.5 million in year 1 (less than Option 2's Rs 21.6 million) but more after that; it loses skilled workers who are needed when the recession ends, and morale falls. Option 2: saves 20% × 200 × 45,000 × 12 = Rs 21.6 million a year with no redundancy cost; keeps skills and can return to five days quickly. Option 3: costs Rs 6 million with uncertain return, but tackles the cause (lack of orders) and spreads risk across markets. Judgement e.g.: Option 2, perhaps with some of Option 3, because recessions are temporary.
    Calculations: 50 × 150,000 = 7,500,000; 50 × 45,000 × 12 = 27,000,000; 200 × 45,000 × 12 = 108,000,000; 20% of that = 21,600,000. Levels: L3 (9–12) a justified recommendation that weighs the options against each other, uses the case data (and calculations where asked) and explains why the rejected option is weaker; L2 (5–8) developed analysis of the options applied to the business, decision only partly supported; L1 (1–4) relevant knowledge with limited application, or a bare decision.
