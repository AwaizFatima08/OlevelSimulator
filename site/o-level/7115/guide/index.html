O Level Business Studies (7115)

Exam guide

Paper structure, topic weights, banker topics, trends, common mistakes and grade thresholds, from our analysis of the May/June 2022 to May/June 2026 papers.

Figures are estimates from one variant of each paper per session. Always check the current syllabus on cambridgeinternational.org.

Paper structure

Business Studies has two written papers. Each is worth half of the final grade (80 + 80 = 160 marks), and you answer every question on both.

Component Duration Marks Format
Paper 1 Short Answer and Data Response 1 hour 30 minutes 80 4 compulsory questions of 20 marks, each built around a short business scenario (sometimes with a small table or chart)
Paper 2 Case Study 1 hour 30 minutes 80 4 compulsory questions of 20 marks, all about one business described in a separate insert with appendices

Paper 1 pattern (identical in every session). Each question has five parts:

  • (a) 2 marks and (b) 2 marks: define a term, identify two points, or do a short calculation.
  • (c) 4 marks: outline two points, usually linked to the business in the stem. Occasionally it is pure knowledge, such as "identify four" or "state four".
  • (d) 6 marks: explain two points (or one advantage and one disadvantage) in the context of the business.
  • (e) 6 marks: a judgement question. It is either "Do you think …? Justify your answer" or "Explain two … Which is likely to be the most important? Justify your answer".

Paper 2 pattern (identical in every session). Each question has an (a) worth 8 marks and a (b) worth 12 marks. The (a) parts ask you to explain two or four points, and one of them is often generic. The (b) parts give you two or three options (for example, three ways to promote, two locations or two sources of finance) and ask which the business should choose. Usually two or three (b) parts point you to an appendix, and about one per paper asks you to justify your answer "using suitable calculations".

Changes 2022–2026. The mark structure did not change at all. The only shift is in Paper 1 part (e). In 2022 and November 2023 every (e) was a "Do you think …?" question. By June 2026, three of the four were "Explain two … which is most important?" questions. The June 2026 grade thresholds also list a separate route, Component 50 (90 marks). None of its papers are in this set, so check the current syllabus if it applies to you.

Bankers

These came up in every session (or all but one):

  • Marketing mix. This is the largest single area, and it appears on both papers every time. Expect promotion and advertising methods, pricing methods, packaging, the product life cycle and extension strategies, new product development, channels of distribution (retailers or wholesalers) and ecommerce. On Paper 2 it is the most common 12-mark decision: which way to promote, which brand to drop, which distribution option or which marketing-mix element matters most.
  • Recruitment, selection and training. Expect types of training (induction, on-the-job, off-the-job), internal versus external recruitment, part-time versus full-time workers, redundancy versus dismissal, legal controls over employment, and choosing between job applicants from an appendix.
  • Costs and break-even. You will meet a break-even calculation or chart, margin of safety, fixed, variable and average costs, and economies or diseconomies of scale in almost every session. Paper 2 often asks you to choose between two products using break-even or profit calculations.
  • Environmental and ethical issues. Expect external costs and benefits, pressure groups, sustainable development, and whether being ethical helps or hurts profit. A 12-mark Paper 2 question on ethics or the environment appeared in four of the nine sessions.
  • Production methods. Job, batch and flow production, lean production, efficiency and inventory all recur. Questions often ask whether one method is "the best" for a type of business.
  • International economy. Expect globalisation, multinational companies (good or bad for a country), import tariffs and quotas, and exchange rate changes.
  • Organisation and management. Expect leadership styles (democratic and laissez-faire especially), delegation, span of control and chain of command, functions of management and trade unions.
  • Enterprise and growth. Expect the characteristics of an entrepreneur, the business plan, why new businesses fail, why businesses stay small, ways to measure size, and methods of growth (including takeovers).
  • Economic issues. Expect unemployment, recession and the business cycle, interest rates, taxation and government spending.

Rarely tested

  • Marketing strategy, beyond the marketing mix. This covers licensing, joint ventures, the problems of entering foreign markets and legal controls over marketing. It appeared in only two sessions (November 2024 and June 2025), but both times it was worth more than a single small part.
  • Statement of financial position. Defining current or non-current assets and liabilities, and classifying items, appeared in only four sessions, and not at all since November 2024. These are quick marks if they come up.
  • Classification of businesses. Sectors of the economy and public versus private sector are mostly short 2-mark definitions.
  • Achieving quality. Quality control versus quality assurance appeared only once before 2025 (November 2023), but it has been in every session since (see Trends).
  • Cash-flow forecasting and business objectives each appeared in only four sessions, but they are core ideas and can easily return.

Trends

  • Paper 1 (e) is changing. "Explain two … which is most important?" questions grew from none in 2022 to three of four in June 2026. Practise explaining two points fully and then choosing between them with a reason.
  • Quality, location and motivation have become more frequent in 2025–2026. Quality appeared in all three of the latest sessions, and location questions (relocation factors, choosing a country or a site from an appendix) appeared in four of the last five sessions.
  • Entrepreneurship and growth stay strong. Their share rose from about 5% of marks in 2022–2023 to over 8% in 2025–2026.
  • International trade topics were heaviest in 2022–2023 (tariffs, exchange rates and globalisation, about 8% of marks) and lighter recently (about 4%). They are still examined every session, mostly through multinational companies.
  • Ratio and accounts analysis is a Paper 2 favourite. The current ratio, ROCE and profit margins, or comparing two companies' accounts, carried a 12-mark Paper 2 question in four sessions. Paper 1 also includes one short calculation in every paper.

Question styles

  • Define (2 marks): a precise textbook definition. One mark is often lost for being vague (for example, "short-term" instead of "repayable within 12 months").
  • Identify / State (2 or 4 marks): brief points with no explanation needed.
  • Calculate … Show your working (2 marks on Paper 1): break-even output, profit margin, ROCE, current ratio, average cost or total variable cost. On Paper 2, calculations support a 12-mark decision.
  • Outline (4 marks): a point plus a link to the business. Analysis earns no extra marks here.
  • Explain (6 marks on Paper 1, 8 on Paper 2): point, application to the business, and development of the consequences, done separately for each point.
  • Do you think …? / Which is most important? / Which should the business choose? (6 marks on Paper 1, 12 on Paper 2): you must weigh both sides and give a supported judgement. On Paper 2 you should also say why the rejected options are weaker.
  • Using Appendix X and other information: interpret tables of accounts, market research charts, location data or applicant profiles, and comment on them rather than copying figures.

Common mistakes

Based on the 2022 and 2023 examiner reports (none were in the set for later sessions):

  • Imprecise definitions. Examples include market share without saying "of total market sales", current liabilities without the 12-month time limit, and confusing profit with revenue.
  • Missing application. Answers that are not linked to the business in the stem (its product, size or sector) lose the application marks. Use a different piece of context for each point.
  • Repeating the same analysis for both points in a two-part explain question.
  • Putting the wrong skill in the wrong part. Analysis in an "outline" (c) part and application in a Paper 1 (e) part earn nothing. Part (c) tests knowledge (sometimes with application). Part (e) tests knowledge, analysis and evaluation.
  • Weak evaluation. A bare decision or a summary of earlier points is not evaluation. Make a choice, give the reason, and explain why it beats the alternative.
  • Confusing related terms. Examiners noted mix-ups between globalisation and multinationals, democratic leadership and delegation, quality control and quality assurance, primary and secondary research, the private sector and a private limited company, and fixed and variable costs.
  • Answering from the wrong viewpoint. Examples include writing about benefits to the franchisee when asked about the franchisor, or about employees when the question asks about the business.
  • Paper 2 calculations. Candidates made errors in break-even workings or simply copied appendix figures without comment. The weakest areas named included economies of scale, lean production, the statement of financial position, ecommerce and the stages of the product life cycle.
  • Listing instead of developing. On Paper 2, a few well-developed points score higher than a long list.

Revision priorities for Oct/Nov 2026 and 2027

  1. Marketing mix in full. Cover promotion methods, pricing methods, packaging, the product life cycle and extension strategies, distribution channels and ecommerce (both opportunities and threats). It is worth about a ninth of all marks.
  2. Break-even and costs. Practise the formula, reading a chart, margin of safety, average cost and economies or diseconomies of scale until calculations are automatic.
  3. Ratios. Learn the current ratio, ROCE and gross and profit margins, and practise comparing two businesses or two years and judging from them.
  4. People. Cover recruitment and training types, redundancy and dismissal, leadership styles, motivation methods (financial and non-financial, Herzberg, job rotation, teamworking) and communication methods.
  5. External influences. Cover ethics and the environment, pressure groups, multinationals, globalisation, exchange rates and tariffs, and the business cycle and interest rates.
  6. Production and quality. Know job, batch and flow production, lean production, inventory and quality control versus assurance. Quality and location are on a rising trend.
  7. Enterprise and growth. Know entrepreneur characteristics, business plans, failure risk, staying small, measuring size and methods of growth.
  8. Quieter areas worth a quick review. These are the statement of financial position (absent since November 2024), marketing strategy for foreign markets, business objectives, and cash-flow forecasts.
  9. Technique. For every Paper 1 (e) and Paper 2 (b), practise a two-sided argument followed by a justified decision that explains why the other option is weaker. Use a different piece of case context for every point.
