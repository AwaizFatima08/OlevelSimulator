Q1Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the events of the first revelation to the Prophet (pbuh) and what happened immediately afterwards. [5]
  2. (b) What can Muslim husbands and wives learn from Khadija's response? Explain your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) At about the age of 40 (610 CE) the Prophet (pbuh) was meditating in the cave of Hira when the angel Jibril came and told him to read; he said he could not read; the angel pressed him three times and then revealed the first verses of Sura 96 (Iqra); he returned home shaken and asked Khadija to cover him; she comforted him, reminding him of his good character, and took him to her cousin Waraqa bin Naufal, who said this was the same angel who had come to Moses and warned that his people would drive him out.
    1 mark each for any five of: age about 40 / month of Ramadan; retreat in the cave of Hira on Mount Nur; Jibril appeared and commanded 'Read'; the Prophet (pbuh) replied that he could not read; the angel embraced / squeezed him three times; the first verses of Sura 96.1–5 were revealed; he returned trembling and asked to be wrapped up; Khadija reassured him (he is kind to relatives, helps the weak, honours guests); visit to Waraqa bin Naufal; Waraqa confirmed it was the angel who came to Moses and predicted opposition.
  2. (b) Spouses should support each other in hard times, listen calmly, encourage each other's good qualities and seek wise advice together.
    Up to 3 marks for developed points: she comforted rather than doubted him (1); she reminded him of his good deeds — encouragement builds confidence (1); she sought advice from a knowledgeable person — couples should seek good counsel when facing problems, with example (1).
Q2Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the persecution suffered by early Muslims in Makka. Refer to named individuals. [6]
  2. (b) Why do you think these early Muslims remained firm in their faith? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bilal was tortured by his master Umayya bin Khalaf, laid on hot sand with a rock on his chest, yet kept saying God is One, until Abu Bakr bought and freed him; Yasir and his wife Sumayya were tortured and Sumayya was killed by Abu Jahl, the first martyr of Islam; their son 'Ammar was also tortured; Khabbab bin al-Aratt was made to lie on burning coals; the Prophet (pbuh) himself was insulted, had filth thrown on him while praying and thorns placed in his path.
    1 mark per accurate developed point, up to 6, e.g.: Bilal — tortured by Umayya bin Khalaf on hot sand with a stone; repeated 'Ahad, Ahad'; freed by Abu Bakr; Sumayya — killed by Abu Jahl, first martyr; Yasir also died under torture; 'Ammar tortured; Khabbab — burned on coals; slaves and poor Muslims suffered most; the Prophet (pbuh) was mocked and had dirt / entrails thrown on him; Abu Lahab's wife put thorns in his path.
  2. (b) Because their faith in God and the Hereafter was stronger than fear of pain, and they were inspired by the Prophet's (pbuh) example and the promise of reward.
    1 mark for a reason, 1 for developing it (e.g. certainty of reward, support of the community, conviction that idols were false).
Q3Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the events of the Night Journey and Ascension (al-Isra' wal-Mi'raj). [5]
  2. (b) Explain why the gift of the five daily prayers during this event is important for Muslims. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) One night in Makka, before the Hijra, the angel Jibril took the Prophet (pbuh) from the Sacred Mosque in Makka to al-Aqsa in Jerusalem on al-Buraq; there he led the earlier prophets in prayer; he then ascended through the heavens, meeting prophets such as Adam, Jesus, Moses and Abraham, and reached the Lote Tree of the Furthest Boundary; the five daily prayers were made obligatory (first fifty, reduced to five); in the morning the Quraysh mocked the account, but Abu Bakr believed it at once and was called al-Siddiq.
    1 mark each for any five of: journey by night from Masjid al-Haram to Masjid al-Aqsa (mentioned in Sura 17.1); accompanied by Jibril; riding al-Buraq; led the prophets in prayer at al-Aqsa; ascension through the seven heavens; met earlier prophets (any named correctly); reached Sidrat al-Muntaha; gift of salat — fifty reduced to five on Moses's advice; Quraysh mocked / asked him to describe Jerusalem; Abu Bakr's immediate belief earned the title al-Siddiq.
  2. (b) Prayer was given directly in the heavens, showing its high status; it is a daily meeting with God that keeps the believer close to Him and disciplined.
    Up to 3 marks: unique way it was commanded shows importance (1); prayer as a regular link to God / spiritual ascent for the believer (1); practical effect, e.g. punctuality and calm amid busy work or school (1).
Q4Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the Prophet (pbuh) began to preach Islam openly in Makka. [5]
  2. (b) Explain what Muslims today can learn from the way the Prophet (pbuh) presented his message. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) After about three years of private preaching, God commanded him to warn his nearest relatives; he invited the Banu Hashim to a meal and called them to Islam, and only 'Ali responded; he climbed Mount Safa and called the clans of Quraysh, asking if they would believe him if he warned of an army behind the hill; they said yes as he had always been truthful; he then warned them of a severe punishment; Abu Lahab cursed him and Sura 111 was revealed about Abu Lahab.
    1 mark each for any five of: private / secret preaching for about three years; command to warn his close relatives; invitation of the Banu Hashim to a meal; few responded, 'Ali offered support; call from Mount Safa to the Quraysh; question about an army behind the hill; people agreed he was truthful (al-Amin); warning about punishment / need to worship one God; Abu Lahab's angry reply; revelation of Sura 111; public preaching then began at the Ka'ba and to pilgrims.
  2. (b) He built trust first through honesty, started with those close to him and spoke clearly and kindly, so Muslims should invite others through good character, family first, and with wisdom rather than force.
    Up to 3 marks: reputation for truthfulness made people listen — character is the best invitation (1); began with family — start at home (1); clear, gentle and patient presentation, e.g. explaining Islam to colleagues or classmates respectfully (1).
Q5Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the ways in which the leaders of the Quraysh tried to stop the Prophet (pbuh) from preaching. [6]
  2. (b) What does the Prophet's (pbuh) response to these pressures teach Muslims about steadfastness? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) They mocked and called him a poet, sorcerer or madman; they pressured his uncle Abu Talib to withdraw protection; they offered him wealth, leadership or kingship through 'Utba bin Rabi'a if he stopped; they tortured weak Muslims; they tried to stop people listening to him; they imposed a social and economic boycott on the Banu Hashim; finally they plotted to kill him.
    1 mark each for any six of: insults / labels (poet, magician, madman, liar); asking Abu Talib to hand him over or silence him; the Prophet's (pbuh) reply that he would not stop even if given the sun and moon (paraphrase); offers of wealth, leadership, marriage or kingship (via 'Utba); demands for miracles; persecution of followers; warning pilgrims not to listen; boycott in the valley of Abu Talib; plot to assassinate him before the Hijra.
  2. (b) That a believer should not give up truth for money, power or comfort, and should remain patient under pressure.
    1 mark for the lesson, 1 for a modern example (e.g. refusing a bribe at work despite pressure).
Q6Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Give an account of the migrations of Muslims to Abyssinia. [6]
  2. (b) What can Muslims learn from this event about relations with people of other faiths? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In the fifth year of prophethood (about 615 CE), because of persecution, the Prophet (pbuh) allowed a small group, including 'Uthman and his wife Ruqayya, to go to Abyssinia, ruled by a just Christian king, the Negus; a larger group followed under Ja'far bin Abi Talib; the Quraysh sent 'Amr bin al-'As and 'Abdullah bin Abi Rabi'a with gifts to have them returned; the Negus called the Muslims; Ja'far explained Islam and recited from Sura Maryam; the Negus was moved, refused to hand them over and allowed them to live in safety.
    1 mark each for any six of: date (fifth year of prophethood / 615 CE); reason — persecution in Makka; the Prophet (pbuh) described the Negus as a just king; first small group included 'Uthman and Ruqayya; second, larger group led by Ja'far; Quraysh envoys 'Amr bin al-'As and 'Abdullah bin Abi Rabi'a with gifts; they accused the Muslims of insulting Jesus / abandoning religion; Ja'far's speech; recitation from Sura Maryam; the Negus wept and protected them; envoys returned empty-handed.
  2. (b) Just and kind people of other faiths can be trusted allies, and Muslims should respect and co-operate with them.
    1 mark for the lesson, 1 for development (e.g. Muslims living in non-Muslim countries should be law-abiding and grateful citizens).
Q7Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Give an account of the Prophet's (pbuh) visit to Ta'if. [5]
  2. (b) What lessons can Muslims learn from the Prophet's (pbuh) response to the people of Ta'if? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) After the Year of Sorrow he went to Ta'if with Zayd bin Haritha to invite the Thaqif tribe to Islam; its leaders rejected him rudely and set street boys and slaves on him, who threw stones until he was bleeding; he sheltered in a garden and prayed to God, complaining of his weakness but seeking only God's pleasure; Addas, a Christian servant, brought him grapes and accepted his message; Jibril came with the angel of the mountains, offering to crush the people between two mountains, but he refused, hoping their descendants would worship God.
    1 mark each for any five of: went with Zayd bin Haritha; to the Thaqif tribe / three leaders; about the tenth year of prophethood; leaders mocked and refused; youths and slaves pelted him with stones; his feet bled / Zayd was injured protecting him; rested in a garden belonging to 'Utba and Shayba; his prayer of complaint to God; Addas offered grapes and became Muslim; angel of the mountains offered to destroy the town; he refused and prayed for future generations.
  2. (b) Forgiveness rather than revenge, patience under rejection and hope for people's future guidance.
    Up to 3 marks: he forgave even when he could have had revenge (1); he turned to God in prayer during hardship (1); example applied today, e.g. replying to online abuse with patience and dignity (1).
Q8Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe what Ja'far bin Abi Talib told the Negus about the Muslims' life before and after Islam. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why Ja'far's approach was effective, and what Muslims living as minorities can learn from it. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Before Islam they worshipped idols, ate dead animals, committed immoral acts, broke family ties, mistreated neighbours and the strong oppressed the weak; then God sent a messenger of known honesty who called them to worship one God, to speak the truth, keep trusts, be kind to relatives and neighbours, avoid bloodshed and indecency, not consume orphans' wealth, and to pray, give charity and fast; because they followed him their people persecuted them.
    1 mark each for any five accurate points: idol worship; eating carrion / immoral acts; cutting family ties; bad treatment of neighbours / strong oppressing weak; a messenger known for honesty and purity; call to worship one God and abandon idols; truthfulness and keeping trusts; kindness to relatives and neighbours; avoiding crime, false witness and taking orphans' property; prayer, charity and fasting; persecution as a result; request for protection; recitation from Sura Maryam about Mary and Jesus.
  2. (b) He spoke truthfully and calmly, focused on shared moral values and recited about Mary and Jesus, which the Christian king respected; Muslims today should explain their faith honestly, find common ground and behave with dignity.
    Up to 3 marks: honesty, even on sensitive points about Jesus (1); appeal to shared values and respect for the listener's faith (1); application to minorities today, e.g. interfaith dialogue in Europe (1).
Q9Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the boycott of the Banu Hashim and the 'Year of Sorrow'. [6]
  2. (b) In your opinion, what was the greater loss for the Prophet (pbuh): Abu Talib or Khadija? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Quraysh agreed a written boycott hung in the Ka'ba: no trade, marriage or social contact with the Banu Hashim (and Banu Muttalib) unless they handed over the Prophet (pbuh); the clan was confined to the valley of Abu Talib for about three years, suffering hunger; some sympathetic Makkans secretly sent food and later campaigned to end it; the boycott ended about the tenth year of prophethood; soon after, Abu Talib and Khadija died, so that year was called the Year of Sorrow; the Prophet (pbuh) lost his protector and his supporting wife, and persecution increased.
    1 mark each for any six of: reason — Banu Hashim refused to hand him over; written agreement placed in the Ka'ba; ban on trade, marriage and contact; confined to the valley (Shi'b) of Abu Talib; about three years; hunger, children crying, eating leaves; some relatives smuggled food; some Makkan leaders worked to end it / the document was found destroyed except God's name; death of Abu Talib (protector); death of Khadija (support and comfort); name 'Year of Sorrow'; increased persecution afterwards.
  2. (b) A reasoned judgement, e.g. Abu Talib's death because it removed tribal protection and led to more open persecution; or Khadija's because she was his first believer and closest emotional support.
    1 mark for a clear judgement, 1 for a developed reason.
Q10Makkan period: preaching and opposition · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain the reasons why the leaders of Makka opposed the message of the Prophet (pbuh). [6]
  2. (b) Which of these reasons do you think was the most important? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Religious: it attacked the idols of their ancestors; economic: Makka earned from pilgrims visiting the idols at the Ka'ba; social: Islam made rich and poor, masters and slaves equal; political: they feared losing leadership to the Banu Hashim; belief: they rejected resurrection and accountability, which would limit their way of life.
    1 mark per reason, +1 for development, up to 6, e.g.: loyalty to the religion of their forefathers (1+1); fear of losing income from pilgrims and trade fairs connected with the idols (1+1); equality of all believers threatened the class system / slaves (1+1); clan rivalry — accepting a prophet from Banu Hashim would raise that clan (1+1); belief in the Day of Judgement threatened their freedom to oppress and act immorally (1+1).
  2. (b) A reasoned choice, e.g. economic, because the Ka'ba's idols attracted the pilgrims and trade on which Makka's wealth depended.
    1 mark for judgement, 1 for supporting reason.
Q11Madina period: community, treaties and diplomacy · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the Prophet (pbuh) established the Muslim community in Madina through the building of the mosque and the brotherhood (mu'akhat). [6]
  2. (b) Explain how mosques today can follow the example of the Prophet's (pbuh) mosque. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) He bought land from two orphans and built the mosque with simple materials, working himself carrying bricks; it served as a place of prayer, teaching, government and shelter for poor Companions (the people of the Suffa); he paired each Emigrant with a Helper as brothers; the Ansar shared homes, property and work; for example Sa'd bin al-Rabi' offered half his wealth to 'Abdur Rahman bin 'Awf, who asked only to be shown the market.
    1 mark each for any six of: land bought from two orphan boys; the Prophet (pbuh) helped build it himself; simple structure of mud bricks, palm trunks and leaves; uses — prayer, teaching, meetings, court, welcoming delegations; Suffa platform for poor students; the Prophet's (pbuh) rooms built beside it; brotherhood between Muhajirun and Ansar; Ansar shared homes, wealth and farms; example of Sa'd bin al-Rabi' and 'Abdur Rahman bin 'Awf; brotherhood replaced tribal loyalty with faith.
  2. (b) By being community centres that teach, help the poor and welcome everyone, not only places of prayer.
    1 mark for the point, 1 for a specific example (e.g. a mosque in Birmingham running a food bank, or a Lahore mosque offering free tuition).
Q12Madina period: community, treaties and diplomacy · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the main terms of the Constitution (Charter) of Madina. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how the principles of this charter could help people of different faiths live together in a country such as Pakistan today. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Muslims (Emigrants and Helpers) formed one community; the Jewish tribes and other groups living in Madina were part of the alliance; each group had freedom of religion; all would defend Madina together against attack and share the costs of war; no one would ally with the Quraysh; disputes would be referred to God and the Prophet (pbuh); Madina was declared a sacred, protected place; blood money and ransom customs would continue fairly within each group.
    1 mark each for any five of: Muslims as one ummah; Jews of Madina recognised as a community with the believers; freedom of religion for each group; joint defence of Madina; shared war expenses; no help or protection to the Quraysh; disputes referred to the Prophet (pbuh) as final authority; Madina a sanctuary (haram); protection of allies and the weak; justice — wrongdoers punished whatever their tribe.
  2. (b) Equal citizenship, freedom of worship and shared duty to protect the country create trust and unity among Muslims, Christians, Hindus and others.
    Up to 3 marks: freedom of religion / protection of places of worship (1); equal rights and justice before the law (1); working together for the common good, e.g. disaster relief or defence (1).
Q13Madina period: community, treaties and diplomacy · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the terms of the Treaty of Hudaybiyya and what happened in the two years after it. [5]
  2. (b) The Qur'an describes this treaty as a clear victory, even though some Companions thought its terms were unfair. Explain why it was a victory. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Terms: no fighting for ten years; the Muslims would return to Madina that year and come for pilgrimage the next year for three days, carrying only sheathed swords; any Makkan who went to Madina without his guardian's permission would be returned, but a Muslim going to Makka would not be; tribes could ally with either side. Afterwards, peace allowed many people to learn about and accept Islam; the Muslims performed the lesser pilgrimage the following year; letters were sent to rulers; and in 8 AH the treaty was broken by allies of the Quraysh, leading to the conquest of Makka.
    1 mark each for any five, at least three terms: ten-year truce; return this year, pilgrimage next year for three days; only sheathed swords; Makkans coming to Madina without permission to be returned, not the reverse; tribes free to join either side; afterwards: many accepted Islam during peace (e.g. Khalid bin al-Walid and 'Amr bin al-'As); 'umra performed the next year; letters to rulers; breach by Banu Bakr against Banu Khuza'a led to the conquest.
  2. (b) It gave the Muslims recognition as an equal party, ended the threat of war and allowed Islam to spread peacefully, so the number of Muslims grew quickly and Makka was later taken almost without fighting.
    Up to 3 marks: Quraysh recognised the Muslims as a power by signing (1); peace allowed preaching and many conversions (1); it led to the peaceful conquest / long-term gain over short-term disappointment (1).
Q14Madina period: community, treaties and diplomacy · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the letters that the Prophet (pbuh) sent to rulers inviting them to Islam. [5]
  2. (b) What lessons can Muslims learn from these letters about spreading the message of Islam? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) After Hudaybiyya he sent letters sealed with a silver ring reading 'Muhammad, Messenger of God' to rulers including the Byzantine emperor Heraclius, the Persian king Khusrau, the Negus of Abyssinia and the ruler of Egypt (al-Muqawqis); Heraclius questioned Abu Sufyan about the Prophet (pbuh) and was impressed but did not accept for fear of his nobles; Khusrau tore the letter up; the Negus responded respectfully; al-Muqawqis replied politely and sent gifts.
    1 mark each for any five of: sent after the Treaty of Hudaybiyya (about 7 AH); seal with 'Muhammad Messenger of God'; letters invited to Islam and warned of responsibility for subjects; Heraclius (Byzantium) — questioned Abu Sufyan, respectful but did not convert; Khusrau (Persia) — tore the letter; the Prophet (pbuh) said his kingdom would be torn apart; Negus — accepted / respectful reply; al-Muqawqis (Egypt) — polite reply and gifts; envoys such as Dihya al-Kalbi sent to Heraclius.
  2. (b) The message is universal and should be shared respectfully and clearly with leaders and ordinary people, using the best means of communication available, while leaving the response to the listener.
    Up to 3 marks: Islam is for all humanity, not one nation (1); polite, clear, written communication — today through media and the internet (1); no compulsion — the Prophet (pbuh) did not force acceptance (1).
Q15Madina period: community, treaties and diplomacy · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the events at Hudaybiyya before the treaty was signed. [5]
  2. (b) Evaluate the leadership qualities shown by the Prophet (pbuh) during these events. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 6 AH the Prophet (pbuh) set out with about 1,400 Companions in the state of ihram to perform 'umra, with sacrificial animals; the Quraysh blocked them, so they camped at Hudaybiyya; 'Uthman was sent as envoy to Makka; a rumour spread that he had been killed; the Companions pledged under a tree to stand by the Prophet (pbuh) — the Pledge of Ridwan; 'Uthman returned safely and the Quraysh sent Suhayl bin 'Amr to negotiate; when the treaty was written Suhayl objected to 'Messenger of God' and the Prophet (pbuh) agreed to write his own name; Suhayl's son Abu Jandal arrived in chains and had to be returned.
    1 mark each for any five of: 6 AH; about 1,400 pilgrims; intention of 'umra, unarmed apart from travellers' swords / animals for sacrifice; Quraysh refused entry; camp at Hudaybiyya; 'Uthman sent as envoy; rumour of his death; Pledge of Ridwan under the tree; Suhayl bin 'Amr as Quraysh negotiator; dispute over writing 'In the name of God, the Most Merciful' and 'Messenger of God'; Abu Jandal incident; Companions upset; sacrifice and shaving of heads at Hudaybiyya.
  2. (b) He showed patience and flexibility over wording, firmness in keeping promises (returning Abu Jandal), and wisdom in choosing peace for long-term benefit; he also consulted Umm Salama when Companions hesitated.
    Up to 3 marks for a reasoned evaluation: patience / flexibility on non-essentials (1); keeping his word even when painful (1); vision for peace and wise use of advice, with judgement on which quality mattered most (1).
Q16Battles and expeditions · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Give an account of the Battle of Badr. [6]
  2. (b) Why do you think the victory at Badr was so important for the early Muslim community? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In Ramadan 2 AH (624 CE) about 313 Muslims set out to intercept a Quraysh caravan led by Abu Sufyan; the caravan escaped and a Makkan army of about 1,000 marched out; the Muslims camped by the wells of Badr on advice from a Companion; the Prophet (pbuh) prayed earnestly through the night; the battle began with single combats in which Hamza, 'Ali and 'Ubayda fought; God sent angels to help and rain to firm the ground; the Muslims won; about 70 Makkans, including Abu Jahl, were killed and about 70 captured; 14 Muslims were martyred.
    1 mark each for any six of: date (17 Ramadan 2 AH / 624); about 313 Muslims with few horses and camels; Quraysh about 1,000; cause — Abu Sufyan's caravan and Quraysh protection of it; camp near the wells (advice of al-Hubab); the Prophet's (pbuh) prayer for victory; duels — Hamza, 'Ali, 'Ubayda; help of angels / rain; Abu Jahl killed; about 70 Quraysh killed and 70 captured; 14 Muslims martyred; prisoners treated kindly, some freed for teaching ten children to read.
  2. (b) It strengthened the Muslims' faith that God supports believers, gave them status in Arabia and weakened the Quraysh leadership.
    1 mark for a reason, 1 for development (e.g. a small, poorly armed group defeated a large army, confirming trust in God).
Q17Battles and expeditions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the Battle of Uhud. [6]
  2. (b) What lesson did the Muslims learn about obedience at Uhud? Explain how it applies today. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 3 AH the Quraysh came with about 3,000 men to avenge Badr; the Prophet (pbuh) consulted and went out; 'Abdullah bin Ubayy withdrew with about 300, leaving about 700 Muslims; 50 archers under 'Abdullah bin Jubayr were placed on a hill and told not to leave; the Muslims were winning, but most archers left their post to collect spoils; Khalid bin al-Walid led cavalry around the hill and attacked from behind; the Muslims were thrown into confusion; Hamza was martyred by Wahshi; the Prophet (pbuh) was wounded and a rumour spread that he had been killed; about 70 Muslims were martyred.
    1 mark each for any six of: date (3 AH / 625); Quraysh about 3,000 seeking revenge for Badr; consultation, decision to fight outside the city; withdrawal of 'Abdullah bin Ubayy and about 300; about 700 Muslims; 50 archers on the hill under 'Abdullah bin Jubayr; instruction not to leave whatever happened; early Muslim success; archers left their post; Khalid bin al-Walid's attack from behind; Hamza martyred (by Wahshi); the Prophet (pbuh) wounded / rumour of his death; about 70 martyrs including Mus'ab bin 'Umayr.
  2. (b) Disobeying clear instructions for personal gain can harm the whole community, so Muslims should follow rightful leadership and rules even when tempted.
    1 mark for the lesson (archers' disobedience caused the setback), 1 for a modern application (e.g. following safety rules at work or a team plan).
Q18Battles and expeditions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the Battle of the Trench (Khandaq). [5]
  2. (b) Explain what Muslims can learn from the way the Prophet (pbuh) accepted Salman's suggestion. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 5 AH a confederate army of about 10,000 from the Quraysh and allied tribes marched on Madina; on Salman al-Farsi's advice the Muslims, about 3,000, dug a trench on the open northern side, with the Prophet (pbuh) digging alongside them; the enemy could not cross and besieged the city for several weeks; the Jewish tribe of Banu Qurayza broke their agreement, creating danger from inside; 'Ali killed the champion 'Amr bin 'Abd Wudd in single combat; disagreement was sown among the enemy and a fierce cold wind and storm destroyed their camp, so they withdrew.
    1 mark each for any five of: 5 AH / 627; confederates (al-Ahzab) about 10,000; Muslims about 3,000; trench suggested by Salman al-Farsi (a Persian method); the Prophet (pbuh) worked with the Companions in hunger and cold; siege of several weeks; Banu Qurayza broke their treaty; duel — 'Ali killed 'Amr bin 'Abd Wudd; division sown among the enemy (Nu'aym bin Mas'ud); violent wind / storm; enemy withdrew without taking Madina.
  2. (b) Good leaders consult others and accept good ideas from anyone, whatever their background, and work alongside their people.
    Up to 3 marks: consultation (shura) is part of Islamic leadership (1); merit not origin — Salman was a Persian convert (1); example today, e.g. a company manager or school head welcoming ideas from staff and students (1).
Q19Battles and expeditions · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the expedition to Tabuk. [5]
  2. (b) What does this expedition teach Muslims about sincerity in difficult times? Explain your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 9 AH, on news that the Byzantines were gathering forces in the north, the Prophet (pbuh) called for an expedition in intense summer heat at harvest time; Companions gave generously — 'Uthman equipped a large part of the army, Abu Bakr gave all his wealth and 'Umar half of his; about 30,000 marched; the hypocrites made excuses and stayed behind; at Tabuk no enemy appeared; the Prophet (pbuh) stayed some days and made agreements with local tribes; Ka'b bin Malik and two others who stayed behind without excuse were boycotted until God accepted their repentance.
    1 mark each for any five of: 9 AH / 630; Byzantine threat; extreme heat, long distance, harvest time; appeal for funds; 'Uthman's large contribution; Abu Bakr gave everything; 'Umar gave half; army about 30,000; hypocrites' excuses; no battle took place; treaties with local tribes / show of strength; Ka'b bin Malik and two others boycotted and later forgiven.
  2. (b) True faith shows when sacrifice is hard: the sincere gave wealth and effort despite heat and harvest, while hypocrites made excuses; Ka'b's honesty about his failure shows truthfulness brings forgiveness.
    Up to 3 marks: sacrifice reveals sincerity (1); contrast with hypocrites' excuses (1); Ka'b's honesty rather than lying — applied today, e.g. admitting a mistake at work (1).
Q20Battles and expeditions · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the Battle of Hunayn. [5]
  2. (b) Compare the lessons of Badr and Hunayn about numbers and victory. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 8 AH, soon after the conquest of Makka, the Hawazin and Thaqif tribes gathered to attack; the Muslims marched with about 12,000 men, and some felt sure of victory because of their numbers; the enemy ambushed them in a narrow valley at dawn and many Muslims fled; the Prophet (pbuh) stood firm with a few Companions and al-'Abbas called the others back; they regrouped and won; large amounts of spoils and captives were taken, and later the captives were returned when the Hawazin accepted Islam.
    1 mark each for any five of: 8 AH, after the conquest of Makka; enemies — Hawazin and Thaqif; Muslims about 12,000, including new Muslims from Makka; overconfidence in numbers (mentioned in the Qur'an, Sura 9); ambush in the valley of Hunayn; Muslims fled in confusion; the Prophet (pbuh) stood firm; al-'Abbas called the Companions back; victory; much booty; generosity to new Muslims / captives returned.
  2. (b) At Badr a small, faithful army won; at Hunayn a large army nearly lost through overconfidence; together they teach that victory comes from God and reliance on Him, not from size or resources.
    Up to 3 marks: Badr — few but trusting in God (1); Hunayn — many but proud, near defeat (1); conclusion applied today, e.g. not relying only on wealth or connections but on effort and trust in God (1).
Q21Conquest of Makka and final years · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Give an account of the conquest of Makka. [6]
  2. (b) What does the Prophet's (pbuh) treatment of the people of Makka teach Muslims today? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 8 AH, after allies of the Quraysh (Banu Bakr) attacked the Banu Khuza'a, allies of the Muslims, the Treaty of Hudaybiyya was broken; the Prophet (pbuh) marched with about 10,000 men; at camp outside Makka Abu Sufyan came and accepted Islam; the Prophet (pbuh) announced that anyone who stayed in Abu Sufyan's house, in their own home or in the Sacred Mosque was safe; the army entered Makka with almost no fighting; the Prophet (pbuh) entered humbly; he cleared the Ka'ba of about 360 idols, reciting that truth had come and falsehood vanished; Bilal called the adhan from the Ka'ba; he pardoned the people of Makka, saying there was no blame on them that day.
    1 mark each for any six of: breach of Hudaybiyya (Banu Bakr against Banu Khuza'a); Abu Sufyan's failed attempt to renew the treaty; 10,000 Muslims; Ramadan 8 AH / 630; camp fires at Marr al-Zahran; Abu Sufyan's acceptance of Islam; amnesty conditions (his house, own homes, the Haram); entry from several directions, little resistance; the Prophet (pbuh) entered with head bowed in humility; idols destroyed (Sura 17.81 recited); Bilal's adhan on the Ka'ba; general pardon, even of former enemies.
  2. (b) To forgive enemies when in a position of power, rather than taking revenge.
    1 mark for the lesson, 1 for a modern application (e.g. forgiving a relative who wronged you, or reconciliation after conflict).
Q22Conquest of Makka and final years · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the main teachings of the Prophet's (pbuh) Farewell Sermon. [6]
  2. (b) Which teaching of the sermon do you think is most needed in the world today? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Delivered during the Farewell Pilgrimage in 10 AH at 'Arafat: the lives, property and honour of Muslims are sacred; interest (usury) is abolished; blood feuds from the time of ignorance are ended; husbands and wives have rights over each other and women must be treated kindly; all humans descend from Adam, and no Arab is better than a non-Arab, nor white than black, except by piety; Muslims are brothers; perform prayer, fasting, zakat and hajj; he left the Qur'an (and his guidance) to hold on to; those present should pass on the message.
    1 mark each for any six of: setting — Farewell Hajj, 10 AH, plain of 'Arafat; sanctity of life, property and honour; usury abolished; blood revenge of pre-Islamic times ended; rights of women and kindness to wives; rights of husbands; equality of all races — superiority only by piety; brotherhood of Muslims; do not wrong others / return trusts; carry out the pillars; hold to the Qur'an and the Sunna; convey the message to those absent; he asked whether he had conveyed the message and called God to witness.
  2. (b) A reasoned choice, e.g. racial equality, because discrimination still divides societies.
    1 mark for judgement, 1 for a developed reason with a modern example.
Q23Conquest of Makka and final years · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the last illness and death of the Prophet (pbuh) and how the Muslims reacted. [5]
  2. (b) Why do you think Abu Bakr's words after the Prophet's (pbuh) death were important? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 11 AH (632 CE), soon after the Farewell Pilgrimage, he fell ill with fever and headache; with his wives' agreement he stayed in 'A'isha's room; he continued to advise the Muslims, especially about prayer and kind treatment of those under their care; he died at about the age of 63; many Companions were shocked and 'Umar at first refused to believe it; Abu Bakr reminded the people that whoever worshipped Muhammad should know he had died, but God is alive and never dies, and recited the verse that Muhammad is only a messenger (Sura 3.144); his family, including 'Ali and al-'Abbas, washed his body, and he was buried in the room where he died.
    1 mark each for any five of: date 11 AH / 632; illness after the Farewell Hajj; stayed in 'A'isha's room; final advice about prayer and those in one's care; died aged about 63; shock and grief of the Companions; 'Umar's initial disbelief; Abu Bakr's speech and recitation of Sura 3.144; washing by his family including 'Ali and al-'Abbas; burial in the room / beside his mosque in Madina.
  2. (b) They calmed the community, reminded people that the Prophet (pbuh) was a human messenger and that worship belongs to God alone, and kept Muslims united at a dangerous moment.
    Up to 3 marks: protected tawhid — worship only for God (1); brought calm and acceptance during grief (1); allowed the community to stay united and move on to choosing a leader (1).
Q24Conquest of Makka and final years · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the Year of Delegations (9 AH) and how the Prophet (pbuh) dealt with the delegations that came to Madina. [5]
  2. (b) Why do you think so many tribes accepted Islam in the years after the conquest of Makka? Give reasons. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) After the conquest of Makka, the victory at Hunayn and the expedition to Tabuk, tribes from all over Arabia sent delegations to Madina, so 9 AH became known as the Year of Delegations; the Prophet (pbuh) received them in his mosque, treated them as honoured guests and gave them gifts; the Thaqif of Ta'if accepted Islam, but their requests to keep their idol al-Lat for a time and to be excused from prayer were refused; the Christians of Najran discussed their beliefs with him, were allowed to pray in the mosque and made a treaty that let them keep their faith; teachers and officials were sent back with many tribes; Sura 110 describes people entering God's religion in crowds; in the same year Abu Bakr led the Hajj.
    1 mark each for any five of: 9 AH called the Year of Delegations ('Am al-Wufud); followed the conquest of Makka, Hunayn and Tabuk; delegations from across Arabia; received in the mosque with honour and gifts; Thaqif accepted Islam; their requests to keep al-Lat / be excused from prayer refused; Christians of Najran — discussion, allowed to pray in the mosque, treaty letting them keep their religion; teachers / officials sent with tribes; Sura 110 (people entering Islam in crowds); Abu Bakr led the Hajj of 9 AH.
  2. (b) The Quraysh, leaders of Arabia, had accepted Islam, so other tribes no longer feared them; the mercy shown at Makka and the Prophet's (pbuh) generous treatment of delegations won hearts; and Islam was now clearly the strongest power, offering peace and unity instead of tribal war.
    Up to 3 marks for developed reasons: the fall of the Quraysh removed the main obstacle (1); mercy and generosity — general pardon at Makka, honour shown to delegations (1); strength and security of the Muslim state / end of tribal wars, with judgement on the main reason (1).
Q25Migration and the move to Madina · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the two Pledges of 'Aqaba. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why these pledges were important for the future of Islam. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In 621 CE twelve people from Yathrib met the Prophet (pbuh) at 'Aqaba near Makka during pilgrimage and pledged to worship one God, not steal, not commit adultery, not kill their children, not slander and to obey him in good; he sent Mus'ab bin 'Umayr with them to teach Islam; the next year, 622, about 73 men and 2 women came and pledged to protect him as they protected their own families, and invited him to Yathrib; this opened the way for the Hijra.
    1 mark each for any five of: first pledge 621 / twelve people (Aws and Khazraj); terms — worship one God, no theft, no adultery, no killing children, no slander, obedience in good; Mus'ab bin 'Umayr sent as teacher; Islam spread in Yathrib; second pledge 622 / about 73 men and 2 women; promise to defend the Prophet (pbuh) like their own families; invitation to migrate; leaders (naqibs) appointed; Muslims began to migrate to Yathrib.
  2. (b) They gave the Muslims a safe base and loyal supporters at a time of severe persecution in Makka, making the Hijra and the first Muslim community possible.
    Up to 3 marks: safe place and protection after the loss of Abu Talib (1); a community willing to follow Islamic teaching (1); led to the Hijra, the Madinan state and the spread of Islam (1).
Q26Migration and the move to Madina · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Give an account of the Prophet's (pbuh) migration (Hijra) from Makka. [6]
  2. (b) What does the Hijra teach Muslims about trust in God and planning? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Quraysh met and decided that a young man from each clan would strike him together so that no single clan could be blamed; God informed him of the plot; 'Ali slept in his bed and stayed to return items people had left in trust; the Prophet (pbuh) left with Abu Bakr and hid in the cave of Thawr for three nights; when searchers reached the cave Abu Bakr was worried and the Prophet (pbuh) reassured him that God was with them; Asma bint Abi Bakr brought food and 'Abdullah bin Abi Bakr brought news; with a guide they took an unusual route; Suraqa bin Malik chased them for a reward, but his horse stumbled repeatedly and he asked for protection.
    1 mark each for any six of: meeting at Dar al-Nadwa; plan for men of all clans to kill him together; permission to migrate revealed; 'Ali in his bed; 'Ali returned the trusts; left with Abu Bakr; cave of Thawr for three nights; reassurance 'God is with us' (Sura 9.40); Asma's food / Dhat al-Nitaqayn; 'Abdullah bin Abi Bakr's news; shepherd 'Amir bin Fuhayra covered tracks; guide 'Abdullah bin Urayqit; Suraqa's chase for 100 camels and his horse stumbling; arrival at Quba.
  2. (b) Muslims should plan carefully and take precautions, and then put their trust in God for the outcome.
    1 mark for each half: careful planning (secret route, guide, hiding) and complete trust in God (reassurance in the cave).
Q27Migration and the move to Madina · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the Prophet's (pbuh) arrival in Quba and Madina. [5]
  2. (b) What can Muslims learn from the way the people of Madina welcomed the Prophet (pbuh) and the Emigrants? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) He stayed some days in Quba, where he built the first mosque; 'Ali joined him there; on the way to Madina he led the first Friday prayer; the people of Madina came out joyfully to welcome him; many asked him to stay with them, but he said his camel would choose; it knelt on land belonging to two orphans, which became the site of the mosque; he stayed in the house of Abu Ayyub al-Ansari until his own rooms were built; Yathrib became known as Madinat al-Nabi, the city of the Prophet.
    1 mark each for any five of: stay at Quba; building of the Quba mosque, the first mosque; 'Ali joined him; first Friday prayer on the journey; joyful welcome in Madina; tribes offered hospitality; he let his camel (al-Qaswa') decide; it knelt on orphans' land; land bought for the mosque; stayed with Abu Ayyub al-Ansari; Yathrib renamed Madina.
  2. (b) Muslims should welcome newcomers, refugees and guests generously and with joy, as the Ansar did.
    Up to 3 marks: hospitality as a sign of faith (1); generosity shared even when resources are limited (1); modern example, e.g. families in Pakistan hosting flood victims or communities welcoming refugees (1).
Q28Migration and the move to Madina · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the Prophet (pbuh) and the Muslims migrated from Makka to Yathrib. [5]
  2. (b) 'The Hijra was the most important turning point in the life of the Prophet (pbuh).' To what extent do you agree? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Persecution in Makka had increased after the deaths of Abu Talib and Khadija; Ta'if had rejected him; the people of Yathrib had accepted Islam at 'Aqaba and promised protection; Yathrib's tribes, the Aws and Khazraj, needed a leader to end their feuds; there was a plot to kill him; God gave permission to migrate; Yathrib offered freedom to practise and spread Islam.
    1 mark per reason, +1 for development, up to 5: persecution intensified / loss of protectors (1+1); invitation and protection promised at the Pledges of 'Aqaba (1+1); Yathrib needed a peacemaker between Aws and Khazraj (1); assassination plot (1); divine permission (1); opportunity to build a community (1).
  2. (b) Largely agree: it turned a persecuted group into a community and state, which is why the Islamic calendar begins with it; but some may argue the first revelation or the conquest of Makka was equally important.
    Up to 3 marks: judgement (1); support — new community, state, calendar starts from the Hijra (1); counter-argument with another event (1).
Q29Early life before prophethood · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the childhood of the Prophet (pbuh) up to the age of eight. [6]
  2. (b) What can Muslims learn from the way the Prophet (pbuh) was cared for as an orphan? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) He was born in Makka around 570 CE, the Year of the Elephant; his father 'Abdullah had died before his birth; following Makkan custom he was given to a foster-mother, Halima al-Sa'diyya of the Banu Sa'd, in the desert, where her family was blessed with plenty; he returned to his mother Amina; when he was about six she died at al-Abwa' on the way back from Yathrib, and Umm Ayman brought him home; his grandfather 'Abd al-Muttalib cared for him lovingly until he died when the boy was about eight; then his uncle Abu Talib took him in.
    1 mark each for any six of: born in Makka, about 570 CE, Year of the Elephant; clan Banu Hashim of Quraysh; father 'Abdullah died before his birth; nursed by Halima al-Sa'diyya; desert life for health and pure Arabic; blessings for Halima's household; returned to Amina; Amina died at al-Abwa' when he was six; Umm Ayman brought him back; grandfather 'Abd al-Muttalib's care; 'Abd al-Muttalib died when he was eight; uncle Abu Talib became guardian.
  2. (b) Families and society have a duty to care for orphans with love and support, as his relatives did.
    1 mark for the lesson, 1 for a practical example (e.g. supporting orphan sponsorship or caring for a relative's orphaned child).
Q30Early life before prophethood · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the life and character of the Prophet (pbuh) between the ages of twelve and forty, before prophethood. [6]
  2. (b) Explain why the Prophet's (pbuh) good character before prophethood was important for his later mission. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) At about twelve he went with Abu Talib on a trade journey to Syria, where the monk Bahira saw signs of prophethood in him; he worked as a shepherd and then as a trader; he joined the Hilf al-Fudul, a pact to protect the oppressed; he was known as al-Amin (the trustworthy) and al-Sadiq (the truthful); he traded for Khadija, who was impressed by his honesty and proposed marriage — he married her at 25; at about 35 he settled the dispute over replacing the Black Stone by placing it on a cloak that each clan leader lifted; he avoided idol worship and often retreated to the cave of Hira to reflect.
    1 mark each for any six of: trade journey to Syria at about twelve; monk Bahira; shepherd; trader; Hilf al-Fudul; titles al-Amin / al-Sadiq; employed by Khadija; her servant Maysara reported his honesty; marriage at 25; rebuilding of the Ka'ba and the Black Stone dispute at 35; solution with the cloak; never worshipped idols; retreats to Hira.
  2. (b) Because people already trusted him, it was harder for them to call him a liar, and his reputation gave weight to his message.
    1 mark for the point, 1 for development (e.g. the Quraysh admitted at Mount Safa that he had never lied).
