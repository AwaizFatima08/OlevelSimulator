  2. (b) Describe the arrangement and the motion of the particles in a gas. [2]
  3. (c) Use your answers to explain why a gas can be compressed but a solid cannot. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Particles are closely packed in a regular arrangement (lattice); they vibrate about fixed positions.
    Close together / regular arrangement B1; vibrate in fixed positions (do not move from place to place) B1
  2. (b) Particles are far apart and randomly arranged; they move quickly in random directions.
    Far apart / random arrangement B1; move rapidly and randomly in all directions B1
  3. (c) In a gas there is a lot of space between the particles so they can be pushed closer; in a solid the particles are already touching.
    Idea of large spaces between gas particles (none to close up in a solid) B1

  1. (a) A sample of a substance is heated from −50 °C to 150 °C. It melts at −20 °C and boils at 80 °C. Describe the changes to the arrangement, movement and energy of the particles during the whole process. [4]

  2. (b) Faster: hydrogen (Mr 2) is lighter than helium (Mr 4), so its particles move faster and diffuse out more quickly.
    B1 faster. B1 lower Mr, 2 compared with 4.
Q58Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A porous pot (unglazed clay, which lets gases pass through) contains air and is closed with a bung. A glass tube from the bung dips into a beaker of coloured water, with the water level marked. A large beaker filled with carbon dioxide is placed over the pot.

  1. (a) Predict what happens to the level of the coloured water in the glass tube. Explain your answer. [3]
  2. (b) After some time, the water level returns to its starting position. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The water level rises up the tube (towards the pot). Air particles inside the pot (Mr about 29) diffuse out faster than the heavier carbon dioxide particles (Mr 44) diffuse in, so there are fewer gas particles inside and the pressure inside falls.
    B1 water rises in the tube. B1 air diffuses out faster than CO₂ diffuses in (CO₂ heavier). B1 pressure inside the pot decreases.
  2. (b) Eventually carbon dioxide and air are evenly mixed inside and outside the pot, so the numbers of particles (and pressures) inside and outside become equal again.
    B1 gases become evenly mixed / same concentration both sides. B1 pressure inside equals pressure outside.
Q59Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

A mixture of equal volumes of hydrogen and carbon dioxide is pumped slowly along one side of a porous barrier. Some gas diffuses through the barrier and is collected on the other side.

  1. (a) Explain why the gas that has passed through the barrier contains a higher proportion of hydrogen than the original mixture. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why the gas must be passed through many barriers, one after another, to obtain almost pure hydrogen. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hydrogen molecules (Mr 2) move faster than carbon dioxide molecules (Mr 44) at the same temperature, so they reach and pass through the pores more often.
    B1 hydrogen lighter / lower Mr. B1 moves faster so diffuses through the barrier faster.
  2. (b) Each barrier only increases the proportion of hydrogen a little, because some carbon dioxide also diffuses through.
    B1 separation at each stage is incomplete; CO₂ also passes through.
Q60Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student writes: 'When the purple colour from a potassium manganate(VII) crystal is evenly spread through the water, diffusion stops because the particles stop moving.' Explain what is wrong with this statement. [2]
  2. (b) The student repeats the experiment with a crystal of a substance whose particles have a much larger relative mass. Predict how the time for the colour to spread evenly would change. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The particles never stop moving; they keep moving randomly. What stops is the net movement, because equal numbers of particles now move in each direction.
    B1 particles are still moving (random motion continues). B1 there is no net movement / no concentration difference.
  2. (b) It would take longer, because heavier particles move more slowly at the same temperature, so they diffuse more slowly.
    B1 takes longer. B1 heavier particles move more slowly.
