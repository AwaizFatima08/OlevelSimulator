  2. (b) Describe the arrangement and the motion of the particles in a gas. [2]
  3. (c) Use your answers to explain why a gas can be compressed but a solid cannot. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Particles are closely packed in a regular arrangement (lattice); they vibrate about fixed positions.
    Close together / regular arrangement B1; vibrate in fixed positions (do not move from place to place) B1
  2. (b) Particles are far apart and randomly arranged; they move quickly in random directions.
    Far apart / random arrangement B1; move rapidly and randomly in all directions B1
  3. (c) In a gas there is a lot of space between the particles so they can be pushed closer; in a solid the particles are already touching.
    Idea of large spaces between gas particles (none to close up in a solid) B1

Q6Kinetic particle theory & changes of state · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Q19Kinetic particle theory & changes of state · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

  1. (a) The bromine in air; gas particles move much faster and are much further apart than liquid particles, so the bromine particles collide with other particles less often and travel further between collisions.
    Bromine in air B1; gas particles move faster B1; more space between gas particles / fewer collisions slowing them B1
  2. (b) Use warmer water.

    9 ÷ 5 = 1.8 B1

Q29Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The experiment with a long tube and cotton wool plugs is repeated. This time the plug at one end is soaked in concentrated ammonia and the plug at the other end in concentrated hydrobromic acid, which gives off hydrogen bromide gas, HBr. A white solid, ammonium bromide, forms where the gases meet.[Ar: H, 1; N, 14; Br, 80]

  1. (a) Calculate the relative molecular masses of NH₃ and HBr. [1]
  2. (b) Predict where the white solid forms, compared with the ring formed with ammonia and hydrogen chloride (Mr 36.5) in a tube of the same length. Explain your answer. [2]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the formation of ammonium bromide. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) NH₃ 17; HBr 81
    Both correct B1
  2. (b) Even closer to the acid end than with HCl, because HBr is heavier than HCl and diffuses even more slowly than HCl, while the ammonia diffuses at the same rate.
    Closer to the HBr end than the HCl ring was B1; HBr has higher Mr so diffuses more slowly B1

Q30Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A porous pot (unglazed clay, through which gases can pass) is filled with air and closed with a bung. A glass tube from the bung dips into a beaker of coloured water. A large beaker filled with hydrogen is lowered over the pot. Bubbles come out of the bottom of the tube into the water.

  1. (a) Explain why bubbles come out of the tube. [3]
  2. (b) The hydrogen beaker is removed. Predict what happens to the coloured water in the tube. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hydrogen molecules are lighter (Mr 2) than the molecules in air (mainly N₂, 28, and O₂, 32), so hydrogen diffuses into the pot faster than air diffuses out; the number of gas particles in the pot increases, so the pressure inside rises and gas is pushed out of the tube.
    Hydrogen diffuses in faster than air diffuses out B1; because H₂ has lower Mr / moves faster B1; more particles inside so pressure increases B1
  2. (b) Water rises up the tube; hydrogen now diffuses out of the pot faster than air diffuses in, so the pressure inside falls below atmospheric pressure.
    Water rises up the tube B1; hydrogen diffuses out faster than air in, lowering pressure inside B1
