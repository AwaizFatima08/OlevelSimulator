Q1Tests for ions, gases & water · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A solution of a salt is tested. With aqueous sodium hydroxide a white precipitate forms which dissolves in excess to give a colourless solution. With aqueous ammonia a white precipitate forms which does not dissolve in excess. Which cation is present?A Al³⁺B Zn²⁺C Ca²⁺D Cu²⁺ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Al³⁺
    Al³⁺: white ppt soluble in excess NaOH but insoluble in excess NH₃. Zn²⁺ (B) is wrong because its white ppt also dissolves in excess aqueous ammonia; Ca²⁺ ppt does not dissolve in excess NaOH; Cu²⁺ gives a light blue ppt.
Q2Tests for ions, gases & water · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A gas is bubbled into acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII). The solution changes from purple to colourless. Which gas is it?A carbon dioxideB sulfur dioxideC ammoniaD chlorine [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B sulfur dioxide
    Sulfur dioxide is a reducing agent and decolourises acidified manganate(VII). Carbon dioxide (A) turns limewater milky but has no effect on manganate(VII).

    B1
Q4Tests for ions, gases & water · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Aqueous ammonia is added a drop at a time to copper(II) sulfate solution until it is in excess. Describe what is seen. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the test for carbon dioxide and give the result. [2]
  3. (c) State the colour of the precipitate formed when aqueous sodium hydroxide is added to a solution containing iron(III) ions. [1]

    B1 limewater; B1 milky / cloudy
  3. (c) red-brown
    B1
Q5Tests for ions, gases & water · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which reagents are used to show that a solution contains sulfate ions?A dilute nitric acid, then aqueous silver nitrateB dilute nitric acid, then aqueous barium nitrateC aqueous sodium hydroxide, then aluminium foil and warmingD acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII) [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B dilute nitric acid, then aqueous barium nitrate
    Sulfate gives a white precipitate (barium sulfate) with aqueous barium nitrate in acid solution. A tests for halide ions; C tests for nitrate; D tests for sulfite.
Q6Tests for ions, gases & water · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

X is dissolved in distilled water and the solution is divided into three portions.Test 1: aqueous sodium hydroxide is added until in excess. Observation: green precipitate, insoluble in excess; on standing the surface of the precipitate turns brown.Test 2: aqueous ammonia is added until in excess. Observation: green precipitate, insoluble in excess.Test 3: the solution is acidified with dilute nitric acid and aqueous barium nitrate is added. Observation: white precipitate.

  1. (a) Identify the cation in X. Explain your answer using test 1. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why the precipitate in test 1 turns brown at the surface on standing. [1]
  3. (c) Identify the anion in X. [1]
  4. (d) Name salt X. [1]

    B1 oxidation by air / oxygen to iron(III)
  3. (c) sulfate ion, SO₄²⁻
    B1
  4. (d) iron(II) sulfate
    B1 (allow FeSO₄)
Q7Tests for ions, gases & water · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Each is one of: calcium carbonate, potassium bromide, copper(II) sulfate.P: lilac flame colour; its solution acidified with dilute nitric acid gives a cream precipitate with aqueous silver nitrate.Q: orange-red flame colour; with dilute hydrochloric acid it effervesces and the gas turns limewater milky.R: blue-green flame colour; its solution gives a light blue precipitate with aqueous sodium hydroxide, and a white precipitate with dilute nitric acid and aqueous barium nitrate.

  1. (a) Name salt P. [1]
  2. (b) Name salt Q. Name the gas given off with the acid. [2]
  3. (c) Name salt R. [1]
  4. (d) State the flame colour expected for a sodium salt. [1]
Show answer

    B1 (K⁺ from lilac flame, Br⁻ from cream ppt)
  2. (b) calcium carbonate; carbon dioxide
