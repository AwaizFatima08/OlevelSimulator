Q1Transition elements · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which property is typical of transition elements? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B they form coloured compounds
    Transition elements form coloured compounds (and have high densities and melting points). D describes Group I metals.
Q2Transition elements · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Compare the melting points and densities of iron and sodium. [2]
  2. (b) State one way in which iron or its compounds are used as a catalyst. [1]
  3. (c) State the colour of a solution of a typical copper(II) compound, such as copper(II) sulfate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Iron has a much higher melting point and a much higher density than sodium.
    B1 melting point; B1 density
  2. (b) Iron is the catalyst in the Haber process for making ammonia.
    B1
  3. (c) blue
    B1
Q3Transition elements · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give two properties of copper that make it suitable for electrical wiring in homes. [2]
  2. (b) Copper is a transition element. State one physical property, other than conductivity, that copper has in common with other transition elements. [1]
  3. (c) Copper roofs on old buildings turn green. Suggest what this shows about copper compounds. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) good electrical conductor; ductile (can be drawn into wires)
    B1 each (allow malleable / unreactive)
  2. (b) high density (or high melting point)
    B1
  3. (c) Copper forms coloured compounds.
    B1
Q4Transition elements · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Iron forms two chlorides, FeCl₂ and FeCl₃. Give the oxidation number of iron in each. [2]
  2. (b) Name FeCl₃ using an oxidation number. [1]
  3. (c) Describe a test with aqueous sodium hydroxide that distinguishes solutions of the two chlorides. Give both results. [2]
  4. (d) State the property of transition elements shown by iron forming both chlorides. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) +2 in FeCl₂; +3 in FeCl₃
    B1 each
  2. (b) iron(III) chloride
    B1
  3. (c) FeCl₂ gives a green precipitate; FeCl₃ gives a red-brown precipitate.
    B1 each
  4. (d) They have variable oxidation numbers.
    B1
Q5Transition elements · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the catalyst used in the Contact process and give the oxidation number of vanadium in it. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why transition elements and their compounds are useful in industry as catalysts. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) vanadium(V) oxide, V₂O₅; +5
    B1; B1
  2. (b) They speed up reactions (by providing a lower activation energy route) without being used up, so less energy/lower temperatures are needed and costs are reduced.
    B1 increase rate / lower activation energy; B1 not used up / saves energy or cost
Q6Transition elements · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The table gives some properties of four metals, J, K, L and M.J: melting point 98 °C; density 0.97 g/cm³; compounds whiteK: melting point 1538 °C; density 7.87 g/cm³; compounds green or red-brownL: melting point 650 °C; density 1.74 g/cm³; compounds whiteM: melting point 1085 °C; density 8.96 g/cm³; compounds blue or green

  1. (a) Which two elements are most likely to be transition elements? Give two pieces of evidence from the table. [3]
  2. (b) Element J reacts rapidly with cold water. Suggest which group J is in. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) K and M; they have high melting points, high densities and coloured compounds.
    B1 K and M; B2 any two pieces of evidence
  2. (b) Group I
    B1
Q7Transition elements · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give the oxidation number of manganese in MnO₂ and in KMnO₄. [2]
  2. (b) Give the oxidation number of chromium in Cr₂O₃ and in K₂Cr₂O₇. [2]
  3. (c) Use your answers to state a property that manganese and chromium share with other transition elements. [1]
  4. (d) Name manganese(IV) oxide's role when it is added to hydrogen peroxide. [1]
Show answer

    x + 2(−2) = 0 → +4 B1; 1 + x + 4(−2) = 0 → +7 B1
  2. (b) +3 and +6
    2x = 6 → +3 B1; 2 + 2x − 14 = 0 → +6 B1
  3. (c) variable oxidation numbers (form ions/compounds with different oxidation numbers)
    B1

    B1
Q8Transition elements · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Chromium is extracted by heating chromium(III) oxide with aluminium powder.Cr₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Cr [Ar: O, 16; Cr, 52]

  1. (a) Give the oxidation number of chromium in Cr₂O₃ and in Cr. [1]
  2. (b) Explain whether chromium(III) oxide is oxidised or reduced in this reaction. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of chromium formed from 76 g of chromium(III) oxide. [2]
  4. (d) Chromium is used to plate steel taps. Suggest two reasons. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) +3 and 0
    B1 both
  2. (b) Reduced: it loses oxygen / chromium's oxidation number decreases.
    B1
  3. (c) 52 g
    Mr Cr₂O₃ = 152; moles = 0.50 M1; Cr = 1.0 mol × 52 = 52 g A1
  4. (d) It resists corrosion; it is hard / shiny and attractive.
    B1 each
Q9Group VII · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which halogen is a red-brown liquid at room temperature? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C bromine
    Bromine is a red-brown liquid. Chlorine (B) is a pale yellow-green gas; iodine is a grey-black solid.
Q10Group VII · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the colour and state of the halogens change from chlorine to iodine. [2]
  2. (b) Chlorine is added to the water supply in cities such as Lahore. State why. [1]
  3. (c) State how the reactivity of the halogens changes down the group. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Colour gets darker (pale yellow-green → red-brown → grey-black); state changes from gas to liquid to solid.
    B1 colour darker; B1 gas → liquid → solid
  2. (b) To kill bacteria (sterilise the water).
    B1
  3. (c) It decreases.
    B1
Q11Group VII · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which mixture of aqueous solutions reacts to form a new halogen? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C chlorine + potassium bromide
    A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from its halide: chlorine displaces bromine. In A, B and D the halogen is less reactive than the halide's halogen, so no reaction.
Q12Group VII · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe what is seen when chlorine water is added to colourless aqueous potassium bromide. [1]
  2. (b) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why there is no reaction when bromine water is added to aqueous potassium chloride. [1]
  4. (d) State which species is oxidised in (b). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The solution turns orange (yellow-orange).
    B1 orange / yellow-orange
  2. (b) Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂
    B1 species; B1 balanced
  3. (c) Bromine is less reactive than chlorine, so it cannot displace chlorine from chloride ions.
    B1
  4. (d) bromide ions, Br⁻
    B1

Astatine, At, is below iodine in Group VII.

  3. (c) Write the formula of the compound formed between sodium and astatine. [1]

  1. (a) solid; black
    B1 solid; B1 black (dark)

  3. (c) NaAt
    B1

  1. (a) Give the result for each blank: (i) chlorine with potassium bromide; (ii) bromine with potassium iodide; (iii) iodine with potassium bromide. [3]
  2. (b) Use the results to put chlorine, bromine and iodine in order of reactivity, most reactive first. [1]
  3. (c) Write the ionic equation for reaction (ii). [2]

  1. (a) (i) orange/yellow-brown colour – bromine formed; (ii) brown colour – iodine formed; (iii) no reaction

  3. (c) Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂
    B1 species; B1 balanced
Q15Group VII · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Cl₂ + 2KI → 2KCl + I₂ [Ar: Cl, 35.5; I, 127]

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of iodine formed when 0.710 g of chlorine reacts with excess aqueous potassium iodide. [3]
  2. (b) Fluorine is at the top of Group VII. Predict how its reactivity compares with chlorine and suggest why it is not used in school experiments. [2]

  1. (a) 2.54 g

Q16Arrangement & periodic trends · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) An element is in Group III and Period 3 of the Periodic Table. What is its electronic configuration? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 2,8,3
    Period 3 = three occupied shells; Group III = three outer electrons: 2,8,3 (aluminium). A has only two shells (Period 2).

Q18Arrangement & periodic trends · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) How does the nature of the oxides of the elements change across Period 3 from sodium to chlorine? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B basic to acidic
    Metal oxides on the left (Na₂O, MgO) are basic; non-metal oxides on the right (SO₂, P₄O₁₀) are acidic. A reverses the trend.
Q19Arrangement & periodic trends · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) State how metallic character changes down Group IV, from carbon to lead. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) sodium metal; silicon non-metal with some metallic properties (semi-metal); chlorine non-metal; metallic character decreases from left to right.
    B1 classification; B1 silicon; B1 decreases across
  2. (b) It increases (carbon non-metal, tin and lead metals).
    B1
Q20Arrangement & periodic trends · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Melting points of the Period 3 elements (°C): Na 98, Mg 650, Al 660, Si 1410, P 44, S 115, Cl −101, Ar −189.

  1. (a) Name the element with the highest melting point and explain it in terms of structure. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why chlorine has a low melting point. [2]
  3. (c) Which elements in the table are solids at 100 °C? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Silicon; it has a giant covalent structure; many strong covalent bonds must be broken to melt it.
    B1 silicon; B1 giant covalent; B1 many strong bonds broken
  2. (b) It is made of simple molecules (Cl₂); only weak forces between molecules need to be overcome.
    B1 simple molecular; B1 weak intermolecular forces
  3. (c) magnesium, aluminium, silicon and sulfur
    B1 all four (melting point above 100 °C)

Q26Noble gases · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why helium is used instead of hydrogen in airships even though hydrogen is less dense. [2]
  2. (b) Argon is used as a shield gas when welding steel. Suggest why. [1]
  3. (c) Compare the reactivity of potassium (Group I) with that of argon in terms of their electron arrangements. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hydrogen is flammable/explosive with air; helium is unreactive and does not burn.
    B1; B1
  2. (b) It stops the hot metal reacting with oxygen in the air.
    B1
  3. (c) Potassium (2,8,8,1) easily loses its single outer electron, so it is very reactive; argon (2,8,8) has a full outer shell and does not lose, gain or share electrons, so it is unreactive.
    B1 K has one outer electron, lost easily; B1 Ar full outer shell; B1 conclusion K reactive, Ar unreactive

Q30Group I · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

0.46 g of sodium reacts completely with water.2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ [Ar: Na, 23; molar volume 24 dm³ at r.t.p.]

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of hydrogen formed at r.t.p. [2]
  2. (b) The solution is made up to 250 cm³. Calculate the concentration of sodium hydroxide in mol/dm³. [2]
  3. (c) Predict one observation that would be different if the same experiment were done with caesium. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 240 cm³
    moles Na = 0.46 ÷ 23 = 0.020 M1; H₂ = 0.010 mol × 24 000 = 240 cm³ A1
  2. (b) 0.0800 mol/dm³
    moles NaOH = 0.020 M1; 0.020 ÷ 0.250 = 0.0800 A1
  3. (c) The reaction would be much more violent / explosive, because reactivity increases down Group I (caesium loses its outer electron more easily).
    B1 observation; B1 reason
