Q63Electroplating & industrial electrolysis · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student tries to silver-plate a copper ring using aqueous sodium nitrate as the electrolyte and a silver anode. Explain why no useful silver coating forms at first, and name the gas seen at the cathode. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why aqueous silver nitrate is a better electrolyte. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why a very small current gives a better coating than a large current. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) There are no silver ions in the electrolyte at the start; hydrogen (from water) is discharged at the cathode instead of sodium.
    B1 no Ag⁺ initially, H⁺/water reduced; B1 hydrogen.
  2. (b) It provides Ag⁺ ions, which are discharged in preference to H⁺ (silver is less reactive than hydrogen).
    B1.
  3. (c) The metal deposits slowly and evenly, so it sticks well (a large current gives a rough, powdery coat).
    B1.
