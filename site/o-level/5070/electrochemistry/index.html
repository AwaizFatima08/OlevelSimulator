Q10Electrolysis: products & electrode equations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) Explain the difference. [1]
  3. (c) Name the gas formed at the cathode in both cases and explain why sodium is not formed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) concentrated: chlorine; very dilute: oxygen
    Each B1
  2. (b) In concentrated solution there are many chloride ions so Cl⁻ is discharged; in very dilute solution OH⁻ ions (from water) are discharged instead, giving oxygen.

  3. (c) Hydrogen; sodium is more reactive than hydrogen, so hydrogen ions (from water) are discharged instead.
    Hydrogen B1; Na more reactive than H B1

  2. (b) The ions gain electrons at the cathode.
    B1

    B1

  1. (a) No carbon dioxide (greenhouse gas) from the van; no carbon monoxide, oxides of nitrogen or particulates (less air pollution / smog).
    1 mark each for any two: no CO₂; no CO; no NOₓ/SO₂; no particulates; only water produced
  2. (b) The hydrogen is often made using fossil fuels (e.g. from methane) or electricity from fossil-fuel power stations.
    B1
  3. (c) Hydrogen is hard to store / few filling stations / vans more expensive.
    B1
Q27Cells & hydrogen fuel cells · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A fuel cell bus uses 0.40 kg of hydrogen on a short journey.[Ar: H, 1; O, 16. One mole of gas occupies 24 dm³ at r.t.p.]

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of hydrogen used. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of water produced, in kg. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the volume of oxygen used, at r.t.p., in dm³. [1]
  4. (d) In the fuel cell, hydrogen loses electrons at one electrode. State whether hydrogen is oxidised or reduced. Explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 200 mol
    400 g ÷ 2 = 200 B1
  2. (b) 3.6 kg
    moles H₂O = moles H₂ = 200 M1; 200 × 18 = 3600 g = 3.6 kg A1
  3. (c) 2400 dm³
    100 mol × 24 = 2400 B1
  4. (d) Oxidised, because it loses electrons.
    B1

Q29Electroplating & industrial electrolysis · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, dissolved in molten cryolite. The electrodes are made of carbon (graphite).

  1. (a) Explain why the aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite. [1]
  2. (b) Write the half-equations for the reactions at the cathode and at the anode. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbon anodes must be replaced regularly. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It lowers the operating temperature (aluminium oxide has a very high melting point), saving energy.
    B1
  2. (b) Cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → AlAnode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻
    Each B1
  3. (c) The oxygen formed reacts with the hot carbon anodes to form carbon dioxide, so the anodes burn away.
    Oxygen reacts with carbon B1; forms CO₂ so anode wears away B1
Q30Electroplating & industrial electrolysis · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Impure copper is purified by electrolysis using aqueous copper(II) sulfate. In one cell the impure copper anode loses 12.8 kg and the pure copper cathode gains 12.2 kg. Assume all the copper that leaves the anode is deposited on the cathode, and that the impurities do not dissolve but fall to the bottom of the cell.

  1. (a) State what the cathode is made of at the start. [1]
  2. (b) Write the half-equation for the reaction at the anode. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage of copper in the part of the anode that was used up. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate the mass of impurities (sludge) collected below the anode. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) a thin sheet of pure copper
    B1
  2. (b) Cu → Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻
    B1
  3. (c) 95.3 %
    12.2 ÷ 12.8 M1; × 100 = 95.3 % A1
  4. (d) 0.6 kg
    12.8 − 12.2 = 0.6 kg B1
