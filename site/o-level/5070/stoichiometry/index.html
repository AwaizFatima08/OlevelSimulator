  1. (a) How many moles of sodium hydroxide are in 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 0.00500
    moles = 0.200 × 25.0/1000 = 0.00500 mol. C forgets to convert cm³ to dm³.
Q2Concentration & titration calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

25.0 cm³ of aqueous sodium hydroxide is exactly neutralised by 20.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid used. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide in mol/dm³. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.00200 mol
    0.100 × 20.00/1000 = 0.00200 B1
  2. (b) 0.0800 mol/dm³
    moles NaOH = 0.00200 (1 : 1) M1; 0.00200 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0800 A1
Q3Concentration & titration calculations · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What volume of 0.250 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid contains 0.0100 mol of HCl? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 40.0 cm³
    volume = moles ÷ concentration = 0.0100 ÷ 0.250 = 0.0400 dm³ = 40.0 cm³. D (400 cm³) comes from converting dm³ to cm³ wrongly; A comes from multiplying 0.0100 × 0.250 × 1000.
Q4Concentration & titration calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A technician in a school laboratory in Multan dissolves 5.85 g of sodium chloride in water and makes the solution up to 250 cm³. Calculate the concentration of the solution in g/dm³. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of this solution in mol/dm³. [Mr: NaCl, 58.5] [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of anhydrous sodium carbonate, Na₂CO₃, needed to make 500 cm³ of a 0.200 mol/dm³ solution. [Mr: Na₂CO₃, 106] [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 23.4 g/dm³
    5.85 ÷ 0.250 = 23.4 g/dm³ (B1)
  2. (b) 0.400 mol/dm³
    moles = 5.85 ÷ 58.5 = 0.100 (M1); 0.100 ÷ 0.250 = 0.400 mol/dm³ (A1). Or 23.4 ÷ 58.5.
  3. (c) 10.6 g
    moles = 0.200 × 0.500 = 0.100 (M1); mass = 0.100 × 106 = 10.6 g (A1)
Q5Concentration & titration calculations · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student titrates 25.0 cm³ portions of aqueous potassium hydroxide with 0.150 mol/dm³ nitric acid, using methyl orange.KOH + HNO₃ → KNO₃ + H₂OTitration: 1 (rough), 2, 3, 4Titre / cm³: 21.40, 20.85, 20.95, 20.90

  1. (a) Which titres are concordant? Calculate the mean titre to use. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of moles of nitric acid in the mean titre. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration of the potassium hydroxide in mol/dm³. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Titrations 2, 3 and 4 (20.85, 20.95, 20.90 cm³); mean = 20.90 cm³
    B1 choosing the three results within 0.10 cm³ of each other (not the rough titre); B1 mean 20.90 cm³
  2. (b) 0.003135 mol (3.14 × 10⁻³ mol)
    0.150 × 20.90 ÷ 1000 = 0.003135 mol
  3. (c) 0.125 mol/dm³
    1 : 1 ratio so 0.003135 mol KOH (M1); 0.003135 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.1254 → 0.125 mol/dm³ (A1)

Q7Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

25.0 cm³ of 0.120 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide is neutralised by 18.75 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid.2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of sodium hydroxide in 25.0 cm³. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in mol/dm³. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in g/dm³. [Mr: H₂SO₄, 98] [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.00300 mol
    0.120 × 0.0250 = 0.00300 B1
  2. (b) 0.0800 mol/dm³

  3. (c) 7.84 g/dm³
    0.0800 × 98 = 7.84 B1
Q8Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A student dilutes 25.0 cm³ of a brand of vinegar to 250 cm³ with distilled water. 25.0 cm³ of the diluted vinegar needs 21.60 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide for neutralisation.CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the diluted vinegar. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the original vinegar in mol/dm³ and in g/dm³. [Mr: CH₃COOH, 60] [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0864 mol/dm³
    moles NaOH = 0.100 × 0.02160 = 0.00216 = moles acid M1; 0.00216 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0864 A1
  2. (b) 0.864 mol/dm³; 51.8 g/dm³
    × 10 = 0.864 mol/dm³ B1; 0.864 × 60 = 51.8 g/dm³ B1
Q9Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid exactly neutralises 25.0 cm³ of aqueous potassium hydroxide.2KOH + H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂OWhat is the concentration of the potassium hydroxide? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 0.160 mol/dm³
    moles H₂SO₄ = 0.100 × 0.0200 = 0.00200; moles KOH = 2 × 0.00200 = 0.00400; concentration = 0.00400 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.160 mol/dm³. A (0.0800) ignores the 2 : 1 mole ratio.
Q10Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A laboratory assistant in Karachi dilutes 50.0 cm³ of 2.00 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid with distilled water to a total volume of 400 cm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the concentration of the diluted acid. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of the diluted acid needed to react exactly with 1.06 g of anhydrous sodium carbonate.Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂ [Mr: Na₂CO₃, 106] [3]
  3. (c) Name the piece of apparatus that should be used to measure the 50.0 cm³ of the original acid accurately. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.250 mol/dm³
    moles = 2.00 × 0.0500 = 0.100; 0.100 ÷ 0.400 = 0.250 mol/dm³ (or 2.00 × 50/400)
  2. (b) 80.0 cm³
    moles Na₂CO₃ = 1.06 ÷ 106 = 0.0100 (M1); moles HCl = 0.0200 (M1); volume = 0.0200 ÷ 0.250 = 0.0800 dm³ = 80.0 cm³ (A1)
  3. (c) pipette
    B1 pipette (fixed volume, accurate); a measuring cylinder is not accurate enough
Q11Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Limewater is a solution of calcium hydroxide. 25.0 cm³ of limewater is titrated with 0.0500 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. 18.40 cm³ of acid is needed.Ca(OH)₂ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + 2H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid used. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of calcium hydroxide in the limewater in mol/dm³. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration in g/dm³. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [Mr: Ca(OH)₂, 74] [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.000920 mol
