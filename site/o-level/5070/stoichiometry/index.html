Q1Concentration & titration calculations · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) How many moles of sodium hydroxide are in 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 0.00500
    moles = 0.200 × 25.0/1000 = 0.00500 mol. C forgets to convert cm³ to dm³.
Q2Concentration & titration calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

25.0 cm³ of aqueous sodium hydroxide is exactly neutralised by 20.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid used. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide in mol/dm³. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.00200 mol
    0.100 × 20.00/1000 = 0.00200 B1
  2. (b) 0.0800 mol/dm³
    moles NaOH = 0.00200 (1 : 1) M1; 0.00200 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0800 A1
Q3Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is the concentration, in g/dm³, of 0.150 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide? [Mr: NaOH, 40] [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 6.00 g/dm³
    g/dm³ = mol/dm³ × Mr = 0.150 × 40 = 6.00. B divides by 40 instead of multiplying.
Q4Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

25.0 cm³ of 0.120 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide is neutralised by 18.75 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid.2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of sodium hydroxide in 25.0 cm³. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in mol/dm³. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in g/dm³. [Mr: H₂SO₄, 98] [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.00300 mol
    0.120 × 0.0250 = 0.00300 B1
  2. (b) 0.0800 mol/dm³
    moles H₂SO₄ = 0.00300 ÷ 2 = 0.00150 M1; 0.00150 ÷ 0.01875 = 0.0800 A1
  3. (c) 7.84 g/dm³
    0.0800 × 98 = 7.84 B1
Q5Concentration & titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A student dilutes 25.0 cm³ of a brand of vinegar to 250 cm³ with distilled water. 25.0 cm³ of the diluted vinegar needs 21.60 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide for neutralisation.CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the diluted vinegar. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the original vinegar in mol/dm³ and in g/dm³. [Mr: CH₃COOH, 60] [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0864 mol/dm³
    moles NaOH = 0.100 × 0.02160 = 0.00216 = moles acid M1; 0.00216 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0864 A1
  2. (b) 0.864 mol/dm³; 51.8 g/dm³
    × 10 = 0.864 mol/dm³ B1; 0.864 × 60 = 51.8 g/dm³ B1
Q6Concentration & titration calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

An antacid tablet of mass 0.500 g contains calcium carbonate. It is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid (an excess). When the reaction stops, the excess acid in the whole solution is titrated and needs 64.00 cm³ of 0.250 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide.CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂; NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O [Mr: CaCO₃, 100]

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid added at the start. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid left over (in excess). [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of calcium carbonate in the tablet. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate the percentage by mass of calcium carbonate in the tablet. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0250 mol
    0.500 × 0.0500 = 0.0250 B1
  2. (b) 0.0160 mol
    0.250 × 0.06400 = 0.0160 B1
  3. (c) 0.450 g
    acid reacted = 0.0090 mol M1; CaCO₃ = 0.0045 mol × 100 = 0.450 g A1
  4. (d) 90.0%
    0.450 ÷ 0.500 × 100 = 90.0% B1
Q7Concentration & titration calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

1.40 g of the hydroxide of a Group I metal, MOH, is dissolved in water and made up to 250 cm³ in a volumetric flask. 25.0 cm³ of this solution is neutralised by 25.00 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.MOH + HCl → MCl + H₂O [Ar: H, 1; O, 16]

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of MOH in 25.0 cm³ of the solution and in the 250 cm³ flask. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the relative formula mass of MOH. [1]
  3. (c) Deduce the relative atomic mass of M and identify M. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.00250 mol; 0.0250 mol
    0.100 × 0.02500 = 0.00250 B1; × 10 = 0.0250 B1
  2. (b) 56
    1.40 ÷ 0.0250 = 56 B1
  3. (c) 39; potassium
    56 − 17 = 39 M1; potassium A1
Q8Moles & reacting masses · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) How many moles of carbon dioxide molecules are in 4.4 g of carbon dioxide? [Mr: CO₂, 44] [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 0.10
    moles = mass ÷ Mr = 4.4 ÷ 44 = 0.10 mol. A is a factor of 10 too small.
Q9Moles & reacting masses · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

In a lime kiln in Sindh, limestone (calcium carbonate) is heated strongly.CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ [Ar: C, 12; O, 16; Ca, 40]

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of calcium carbonate in 25.0 g. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of calcium oxide formed when 25.0 g of calcium carbonate is completely decomposed. [2]
  3. (c) State one use of calcium oxide. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.250 mol
    25.0 ÷ 100 = 0.250 B1
  2. (b) 14.0 g
    moles CaO = 0.250 M1; 0.250 × 56 = 14.0 g A1
  3. (c) Neutralising acidic soil (or making cement / removing acidic gases)
    B1
Q10Moles & reacting masses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ [Ar: C, 12; O, 16; Fe, 56]

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum mass of iron, in tonnes, that can be made from 8.0 tonnes of iron(III) oxide. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of carbon monoxide needed to react with 8.0 tonnes of iron(III) oxide. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.6 tonnes
    Mr Fe₂O₃ = 160 M1; 160 t gives 2 × 56 = 112 t of Fe M1; 8.0 × 112/160 = 5.6 t A1
  2. (b) 4.2 tonnes
    160 t Fe₂O₃ needs 3 × 28 = 84 t CO M1; 8.0 × 84/160 = 4.2 t A1
Q11Moles & reacting masses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

2.4 g of magnesium is added to a solution containing 0.15 mol of hydrochloric acid.Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂ [Ar: Mg, 24; Cl, 35.5]

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of magnesium in 2.4 g. [1]
  2. (b) Show which reactant is in excess. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of magnesium chloride formed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.10 mol
    2.4 ÷ 24 = 0.10 B1
  2. (b) Magnesium is in excess: 0.10 mol Mg needs 0.20 mol HCl, but only 0.15 mol HCl is present.
    M1 compare 0.10 mol Mg needing 0.20 mol HCl (or 0.15 mol HCl needs 0.075 mol Mg); A1 Mg in excess
  3. (c) 7.13 g (allow 7.1 g)
    moles MgCl₂ = 0.15 ÷ 2 = 0.075 M1; Mr MgCl₂ = 95; 0.075 × 95 = 7.125 = 7.13 g A1
Q12Moles & reacting masses · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
