Q3Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A city council plans to ban single-use plastic shopping bags.

  1. (a) Explain why disposing of plastics in landfill sites causes problems. [2]
  2. (b) Describe one problem caused by plastic waste in rivers and seas. [1]
  3. (c) Burning plastic waste is sometimes suggested. Give one disadvantage. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest one way a city council could reduce plastic waste. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Most plastics are non-biodegradable, so they remain for a very long time and take up land.
    B1 non-biodegradable; B1 persist / use up space.
  2. (b) Harms or kills marine animals that swallow it or become trapped; microplastics enter food chains; blocks drains.
    B1.
  3. (c) Produces toxic gases (e.g. hydrogen chloride from PVC, carbon monoxide) / releases carbon dioxide.
    B1.
  4. (d) Ban or charge for single-use plastic bags; encourage recycling or reusable bags.
    B1.

  2. (b) PET is a polyester. Name the two types of monomer and the linkage. [2]
  3. (c) Name the small molecule formed when each linkage is made, and the type of polymerisation. [1]
Show answer

Q5Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Proteins are natural polymers. Name the monomers and the linkage in proteins. [2]
  2. (b) A protein chain is made from 50 amino acid molecules joined in a line. How many water molecules are eliminated? [1]
  3. (c) Give one similarity and one difference between the structures of a protein and nylon. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) amino acids; amide (peptide) linkage
    B1 each.
  2. (b) 49
    B1 one water molecule per linkage; 50 − 1 = 49 linkages.
  3. (c) Similarity: both contain amide linkages / both condensation polymers. Difference: a protein is made from many different amino acids (each monomer has both –NH₂ and –COOH groups), whereas nylon is made from two different monomers that alternate.
    B1 similarity; B1 difference.
Q6Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Write the repeat unit of poly(propene), made from propene CH₂=CHCH₃. [2]

  3. (c) The repeat unit of a non-stick coating is –CF₂–CF₂–. Give the structural formula and name of its monomer. [2]
Show answer

  2. (b) 210 000
    M1 Mr propene = 42; A1 5000 × 42 = 210 000.
  3. (c) CF₂=CF₂; tetrafluoroethene
    B1 formula; B1 name.

Q11Carboxylic acids & esters · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student titrates 25.0 cm³ of a vinegar sample from a shop in Multan with 1.00 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide. The mean titre is 20.8 cm³.CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of sodium hydroxide used. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the vinegar in mol/dm³. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration in g/dm³ to 3 significant figures. (Mr CH₃COOH = 60) [1]
  4. (d) Name a suitable indicator and give the colour change at the end-point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0208 mol
    B1 20.8 ÷ 1000 × 1.00.
  2. (b) 0.832 mol/dm³
    B1 0.0208 ÷ 0.0250.
  3. (c) 49.9 g/dm³
    B1 0.832 × 60 = 49.92.
  4. (d) Thymolphthalein: colourless to blue (accept phenolphthalein: colourless to pink).
    B1 indicator suitable for a weak acid–strong base titration (thymolphthalein or phenolphthalein; not methyl orange); B1 matching colour change at the end-point.
Q12Carboxylic acids & esters · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Write the equation for the reaction of ethanoic acid with ethanol. [1]
  2. (b) 6.0 g of ethanoic acid reacts with excess ethanol. Calculate the maximum mass of ethyl ethanoate. (Mr: CH₃COOH = 60, CH₃COOC₂H₅ = 88) [2]
  3. (c) The student obtains 5.72 g. Calculate the percentage yield. [1]
  4. (d) Give two reasons why the yield is less than 100 %. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
    B1.
  2. (b) 8.8 g
    M1 6.0 ÷ 60 = 0.10 mol; A1 0.10 × 88 = 8.8 g.
  3. (c) 65.0 %
    B1 5.72 ÷ 8.8 × 100.
  4. (d) The reaction is reversible / reaches equilibrium; some product lost during separation or purification; some ester evaporates (volatile).
    1 mark each for any two.
Q13Formulae, naming, homologous series & isomers · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which compound is in the same homologous series as ethanol? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A C₃H₇OH
    C₃H₇OH (propanol) has the same –OH functional group and general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH. CH₃COOH (B) is a carboxylic acid, a different series.
Q14Formulae, naming, homologous series & isomers · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give three characteristics of a homologous series. [3]
  2. (b) The general formula of the alkanes is CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Write the molecular formula of the alkane with 8 carbon atoms, found in petrol. [1]
  3. (c) Write the general formula of the alkenes. [1]

    1 mark each for any three.

    B1 2 × 8 + 2 = 18.
  3. (c) CₙH₂ₙ
    B1.

    B1 formulae; B1 balanced.
Q20Alcohols · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Compare making ethanol by fermentation with making it by the catalytic addition of steam to ethene. Give two advantages of each method. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why a country with a large sugar industry and little petroleum might choose fermentation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fermentation: uses renewable raw materials (sugar crops); low temperature and normal pressure so less energy. Hydration of ethene: fast reaction; continuous process; produces pure ethanol (no need for separation); high yield.
    B1 each of two fermentation advantages; B1 each of two hydration advantages.
  2. (b) Sugar (molasses) is readily available and cheap, while ethene comes from petroleum, which would have to be imported.
    B1.

Q30Alkanes · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Butane burns completely: 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O. Calculate the volume of oxygen needed to burn 30 cm³ of butane, and the volume of carbon dioxide formed. (All volumes at room temperature and pressure.) [3]
