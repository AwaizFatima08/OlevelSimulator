Q1Inventory and supply chain management · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Lahore Looms uses 40 cones of cotton yarn a day. The supplier takes 5 days to deliver. Lahore Looms keeps a buffer inventory of 100 cones and always orders 800 cones at a time.

  1. (a) Calculate the re-order level for cotton yarn at Lahore Looms. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum inventory level. [1]
  3. (c) Explain one cost to Lahore Looms of holding inventory. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 300 cones
    Re-order level = (daily usage × lead time) + buffer inventory (M1) = 40 × 5 + 100 = 300 cones (A1).
  2. (b) 900 cones
    Buffer + re-order quantity = 100 + 800 = 900 (B1).
  3. (c) Storage cost: yarn must be kept in a dry warehouse and insured; the space and insurance cost money and the cash tied up in 900 cones of yarn cannot be used elsewhere (opportunity cost). Also accept: damage/deterioration, theft, obsolescence.
    1 mark for knowledge of the term/concept; up to 2 marks for explaining it in the context of the business (application).
Q2Inventory and supply chain management · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term 'just-in-time (JIT) inventory management'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one disadvantage of JIT for a bakery that supplies bread to supermarkets every morning. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A system in which materials and components are delivered just when they are needed for production, and finished goods are produced to meet orders, so little or no inventory (and no buffer) is held.
    1 mark: supplies arrive exactly when needed / produce to order; 1 mark: minimal or zero inventory held.
  2. (b) If the flour supplier's delivery is late (e.g. because of a road closure or strike), the bakery has no buffer inventory and cannot bake, so it fails to deliver bread to supermarkets that morning, losing revenue and possibly the contract.
    1 mark for knowledge of the term/concept; up to 2 marks for explaining it in the context of the business (application). Accept: higher ordering/delivery costs, loss of bulk-buying discounts, cannot meet sudden rises in demand.

  2. (b) Explain one reason why a hospital pharmacy holds buffer inventory of insulin. [3]
Show answer

Q4Inventory and supply chain management · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

BuildRight Hardware sells bags of cement in Multan. Its inventory control data: maximum inventory 2,400 bags; buffer inventory 400 bags; average daily sales 150 bags; lead time 4 days. A delivery always brings inventory back up to the maximum level.

  1. (a) Calculate the re-order quantity. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the re-order level. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate how many days, to the nearest day, each delivery lasts if sales stay at the average rate. [2]
  4. (d) Explain one reason why BuildRight's actual inventory levels might not follow this pattern. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2,000 bags
    Max − buffer = 2,400 − 400 = 2,000 bags (B1).
  2. (b) 1,000 bags
    Buffer + (daily usage × lead time) = 400 + (150 × 4) M1 = 1,000 bags A1.
  3. (c) 13 days
    2,000 ÷ 150 M1 = 13.33 ≈ 13 days A1.
  4. (d) Demand for cement is seasonal/uneven (e.g. building slows in the monsoon), so daily usage varies and the re-order level is reached earlier or later than planned.
    1 mark: reason (variable demand, late or early deliveries, supplier shortages); 1 mark: applied to cement/building trade; 1 mark: consequence (stock-out or excess inventory).
Q5Inventory and supply chain management · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Speedy Spares distributes car spare parts to 300 garages in Peshawar. Garages expect parts to be delivered the same day they are ordered.

  1. (a) Identify one cost of holding too little inventory. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'stock-out'. [3]
  3. (c) Explain one reason why Speedy Spares might hold a high level of inventory even though this is expensive. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lost sales / lost customers (stock-out costs).
    1 mark for any valid cost: lost sales, loss of goodwill, cost of emergency orders, idle production.
  2. (b) A stock-out is when a business has no inventory of an item when a customer wants it, so the order cannot be met.
    1 mark: inventory runs out/zero; 1 mark: customer demand cannot be met; 1 mark: example or consequence (garage goes to a rival).
  3. (c) Garages need the part the same day; if Speedy Spares does not have it, the garage will order from a competitor and may switch permanently, so the lost sales and goodwill cost more than holding inventory.
    1 mark: reason; 1 mark: applied to same-day delivery/garages; 1 mark: developed consequence.
Q6Inventory and supply chain management · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Rehman Motors runs car repair workshops. It imports brake pads from Thailand. It holds 3,600 brake pads and uses 450 a week. Shipping times have varied between 3 and 9 weeks this year.

  1. (a) Calculate how many weeks Rehman Motors' current inventory of brake pads will last. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'supply chain management'. [3]
  3. (c) Explain one reason why Rehman Motors might choose to hold more inventory than usual. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8 weeks
    3,600 ÷ 450 = 8 weeks (B1).
  2. (b) Managing the flow of materials, components, information and finance from suppliers through production to the customer, coordinating all the businesses involved to minimise cost and delays. For Rehman Motors this includes managing the overseas brake-pad supplier, shipping and its workshops' needs.
    1 mark: flow of goods/information from suppliers to customer; 1 mark: coordination to improve efficiency/reduce cost or delay; 1 mark: application.
  3. (c) Its brake pads are imported and shipping times are uncertain (delays at ports); holding extra inventory means workshops do not run out and lose customers who need urgent repairs. Also accept: expected price rises/depreciation of the rupee, bulk discounts.
    1 mark for knowledge of the term/concept; up to 2 marks for explaining it in the context of the business (application).

Q8Inventory and supply chain management · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Rainbow Paints holds an average of Rs 6.0 million of raw materials. Its annual holding cost (storage, insurance, damage and the opportunity cost of capital) is 22 % of the average value of inventory. With a just-in-time (JIT) system, average inventory would fall to Rs 1.5 million, but more frequent deliveries would add Rs 600,000 a year to transport costs.

  1. (a) Calculate the net annual saving to Rainbow Paints from introducing JIT. [3]
  2. (b) Explain one non-financial factor Rainbow Paints should consider before introducing JIT. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 390,000
    Reduction in average inventory = 6.0 − 1.5 = Rs 4.5m M1; holding cost saved = 4.5 × 0.22 = Rs 990,000 M1; net saving = 990,000 − 600,000 = Rs 390,000 A1.
  2. (b) Reliability of suppliers: paint pigments may come from abroad, so a single late delivery could stop production because there is no buffer.
    1 mark: factor (supplier reliability, quality of deliveries, relationship with suppliers, staff training); 1 mark: application; 1 mark: development.

Q12Inventory and supply chain management · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 12 marks

SmartTel assembles mobile phones in Pakistan using components imported from China and South Korea. It holds about 10 weeks of components, worth Rs 1.2 billion. In 2025, delays at the port stopped production for 2 weeks. New phone models are launched every 9 months.

  1. (a) Evaluate whether SmartTel should change to a just-in-time (JIT) system for its phone components. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: SmartTel holds 10 weeks of components; JIT would release working capital (Rs 1.2 billion) and cut storage costs; phone components become obsolete quickly as models change, so less inventory means less write-off; encourages efficiency and quality. Against: components are imported from China and South Korea with long, unreliable lead times; the 2025 port delays stopped production for 2 weeks even with inventory; exchange-rate swings make frequent small orders more expensive; JIT needs reliable suppliers, close relationships and good transport, which SmartTel lacks. Judgement: a full JIT system is too risky given its international supply chain; SmartTel should reduce inventory gradually (e.g. lower buffer for components sourced locally, JIT for packaging) and develop local suppliers before adopting JIT more widely.
    Benefits vs risks of JIT in context of imported components and supply disruption; partial adoption; judgement. Mark scheme: AO1 knowledge 2, AO2 application 2, AO3 analysis 2, AO4 evaluation 6. Level 3 (9–12): developed analysis in context plus a supported judgement that answers the question for this business (e.g. 'it depends on…' weighed, with a clear conclusion). Level 2 (5–8): developed analysis with some, limited or unsupported evaluation. Level 1 (1–4): knowledge and some application; little or no analysis.
Q13Inventory and supply chain management · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 16 marks

HomeNest sells flat-pack furniture online in Pakistan. It buys 20,000 chair frames a year from a supplier in Vietnam at Rs 2,000 each; lead time is 8 weeks and HomeNest holds an average inventory of 5,000 frames. A local supplier in Sialkot offers frames at Rs 2,300 each with a lead time of 1 week, so average inventory could fall to 1,200 frames. HomeNest's annual holding cost is 20 % of the value of average inventory. Customer reviews often mention 'out of stock' messages, and the Vietnamese supplier was late three times last year.

  1. (a) Calculate the change in HomeNest's total annual cost (purchase cost plus holding cost) if it switches to the Sialkot supplier. [4]
  2. (b) Evaluate whether HomeNest should switch to the Sialkot supplier. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Total annual cost rises by Rs 4,552,000.
    Extra purchase cost = 20,000 × (2,300 − 2,000) = Rs 6,000,000 M1. Holding cost now = 5,000 × 2,000 × 0.2 = Rs 2,000,000; with Sialkot = 1,200 × 2,300 × 0.2 = Rs 552,000 M1; saving = Rs 1,448,000 M1. Net increase = 6,000,000 − 1,448,000 = Rs 4,552,000 A1.
  2. (b) Judgement depends on whether faster, more reliable supply wins enough extra sales to cover the Rs 4.552m higher cost.
    Arguments for/against: For: 1-week lead time gives flexibility and fewer stock-outs (reviews show lost sales); less capital tied up; lower risk of shipping delays and exchange-rate changes; 'made in Pakistan' may appeal. Against: annual cost rises by about Rs 4.55m; unknown quality/capacity of Sialkot supplier; Vietnamese supplier may lower price or improve reliability; could use both suppliers. Judgement: e.g. switch if extra contribution from avoided stock-outs exceeds Rs 4.55m a year, or trial a dual-sourcing arrangement first. Level 3 (9–12): a supported, contextual judgement that weighs the arguments (e.g. 'it depends on…'), built on developed analysis of both sides. Level 2 (5–8): developed analysis applied to the context, with at most a simple or unsupported judgement. Level 1 (1–4): knowledge and limited application; points listed, not developed. 0: no creditable content.

BrightTech (an invented company) designs and sells smart home devices (doorbells, cameras) in Pakistan and the Middle East.Timeline:2021: All devices assembled in BrightTech's own factory in Islamabad; capacity utilisation 55 %.2022: Outsources assembly of cameras to a Chinese contract manufacturer; closes half its factory.2023: Also outsources customer support to a call centre in the Philippines.2024: A batch of 20,000 cameras has a software fault; recall costs Rs 160 million. Customer satisfaction falls.2025: Brings customer support back in-house; considers outsourcing doorbell assembly too.Appendix 1: average cost per camera: 2021 Rs 7,800; 2025 Rs 5,900. Gross profit margin: 2021 22 %; 2025 31 %.Appendix 2: time from new design to product launch: 2021: 9 months; 2025: 14 months.Appendix 3: customer satisfaction score (out of 10): 2021: 8.1; 2023: 6.4; 2025: 7.2.

  1. (a) Evaluate the effectiveness of BrightTech's outsourcing strategy between 2021 and 2025. [20]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mixed: outsourcing cut costs (camera cost −24 %, margin up 9 points), but quality, speed to market and customer satisfaction suffered; support outsourcing failed and was reversed.
