Q1External environment (economic, political, competition) · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Basmati Gold exports rice from Pakistan to the USA. It prices its rice at Rs 250,000 per tonne. The exchange rate changed from US$1 = Rs 280 to US$1 = Rs 300.

  1. (a) Calculate the US dollar price of one tonne of Basmati Gold rice before and after the change in the exchange rate. Give your answers to the nearest cent. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how this change in the exchange rate affects the competitiveness of Basmati Gold in the USA. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Before: $892.86; after: $833.33 per tonne.
    Before: 250,000 ÷ 280 = $892.86 (M1 A1 for one correct). After: 250,000 ÷ 300 = $833.33 (A1).
  2. (b) The rupee has depreciated, so with the rupee price unchanged the dollar price falls by about $60 a tonne; Basmati Gold's rice becomes cheaper for US importers than rice from rival exporting countries, so demand and export volume should rise (or the firm can raise its rupee price and earn a higher margin).
    1 mark: rupee depreciation makes exports cheaper in dollars; 1 mark: more price competitive vs rival suppliers; 1 mark: consequence (more sales / option to raise margin).
Q2External environment (economic, political, competition) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Bake House is a bakery in Quetta. Its monthly sales revenue is Rs 2,000,000, ingredient costs are Rs 1,200,000 and other costs are Rs 500,000. Inflation causes ingredient costs to rise by 12 %. Bake House raises its prices by 5 %, and the number of items sold stays the same. Other costs do not change.

  1. (a) Calculate Bake House's monthly profit after the changes. [3]
  2. (b) Explain one other way inflation could affect Bake House. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 256,000
    New revenue = 2,000,000 × 1.05 = Rs 2,100,000 (M1). New material cost = 1,200,000 × 1.12 = Rs 1,344,000 (M1). Profit = 2,100,000 − 1,344,000 − 500,000 = Rs 256,000 (A1).
  2. (b) Employees will ask for higher wages to keep up with rising prices, increasing Bake House's costs further; if it cannot pass these on to price-sensitive customers, profit falls again. Also accept: customers' real incomes fall so they buy fewer cakes; uncertainty delays investment.
    1 mark for knowledge of the term/concept; up to 2 marks for explaining it in the context of the business (application).
Q3External environment (economic, political, competition) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Arcadia Generics (an invented company) makes medicines in Pakistan. It imports active ingredients from Germany. Each order costs €400,000. The exchange rate changed from €1 = Rs 300 to €1 = Rs 330.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost in rupees of one order of raw materials before and after the change in the exchange rate. [3]
  2. (b) Explain one way Arcadia Generics could respond to the rise in its import costs. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Before: Rs 120,000,000; after: Rs 132,000,000.
    Before: 400,000 × 300 = Rs 120 million (M1 A1 shared); after: 400,000 × 330 = Rs 132 million (A1).
  2. (b) It could find suppliers of active ingredients in Pakistan or a country whose currency has not risen, reducing exposure to the euro; or raise medicine prices, although government price controls on medicines may limit this.
    1 mark (knowledge): valid response (raise price, change supplier, hedge/forward contract, cut other costs); up to 2 marks (application/explanation): applied to a medicine maker.
Q4External environment (economic, political, competition) · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term 'recession'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one way a recession might affect a dealer selling luxury cars in Islamabad. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A period in which the economy's output (real GDP) falls, usually for at least two consecutive quarters, with falling incomes and rising unemployment.
    1 mark: fall in real GDP/output/economic activity; 1 mark: for two consecutive quarters (or sustained period) / associated with rising unemployment.
  2. (b) Luxury cars are income-elastic: as incomes and business profits fall, customers delay buying expensive new cars, so the dealer's sales and profit fall sharply; it may have to offer discounts to clear inventory.
    1 mark (knowledge): effect of recession (lower incomes/demand, harder credit); up to 2 marks (application/explanation): applied to luxury cars with high income elasticity.
Q5External environment (economic, political, competition) · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Indus Auto (an invented company) assembles cars in Karachi. Steel accounts for 18 % of its production costs and almost all of it is imported. The government has introduced a 30 % tariff on imported steel to protect local steel mills.

  1. (a) Identify one government policy, other than tariffs, that could affect Indus Auto. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'tariff'. [3]
  3. (c) Explain one effect on Indus Auto of the new tariff on imported steel. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Interest rate changes (monetary policy) – also accept tax changes, subsidies, competition policy, environmental laws, import quotas.
    1 mark for a valid policy.
  2. (b) A tax on imported goods, which raises their price in the domestic market; e.g. a 30 % tariff on imported steel raises the cost of steel sheets that Indus Auto buys from China.
    1 mark (knowledge): tax on imports; up to 2 marks (application/explanation): effect on prices/protection and/or applied to Indus Auto.
  3. (c) Steel makes up a large share of the cost of each car; the tariff raises costs, so Indus Auto must raise car prices (losing sales to imported cars) or accept lower profit margins.
    1 mark (knowledge): higher input costs; up to 2 marks (application/explanation): consequences for prices/profit/competitiveness of a car maker.
Q6External environment (economic, political, competition) · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) The central bank has raised its interest rate from 12 % to 17 %. Analyse two effects of this rise on AutoPak, a car manufacturer that sells most of its cars to customers who buy on credit (car loans). [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1. Lower demand: car loans become more expensive, so monthly repayments rise; many middle-income customers postpone buying, so AutoPak's sales and revenue fall, leaving spare capacity and higher unit fixed costs. 2. Higher costs of finance: AutoPak's own overdraft/loan interest rises, raising costs and reducing profit; it may delay investment in a new model, harming long-term competitiveness. (Also: rupee may appreciate, making imported parts cheaper.)
    Effects: consumer demand for credit-financed goods, cost of borrowing for the firm, investment decisions, exchange rate, inventory costs. Mark scheme: AO1 knowledge 2, AO2 application 2, AO3 analysis 4. Level 2 (5–8): two developed chains of analysis, each clearly applied to this business. Level 1 (1–4): knowledge and/or application with undeveloped or generic analysis. Evaluation earns no extra marks.
Q7External environment (economic, political, competition) · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) The government has raised the national minimum wage by 25 %. Analyse two effects of this change on BurgerHub, a fast-food chain with 1,200 employees, most of whom are paid the minimum wage. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1. Higher costs: wage costs rise by up to 25 %, a large share of a labour-intensive fast-food firm's costs; unless prices rise, profit margins fall, and higher menu prices may reduce demand from price-sensitive young customers. 2. Motivation and staff turnover: better pay may reduce labour turnover and absenteeism, lowering recruitment and training costs and improving service speed; BurgerHub may also respond by investing in self-order kiosks to reduce staff numbers. Also: higher incomes of low-paid workers may increase demand for fast food.
    Effects: wage costs/prices/profit; motivation/turnover; substitution of capital for labour; demand effect of higher incomes. Mark scheme: AO1 knowledge 2, AO2 application 2, AO3 analysis 4. Level 2 (5–8): two developed chains of analysis, each clearly applied to this business. Level 1 (1–4): knowledge and/or application with undeveloped or generic analysis. Evaluation earns no extra marks.
Q8External environment (economic, political, competition) · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Gulshan Furniture's sales revenue rose by 18 % in 2025 compared with 2024. During the same period, the rate of inflation was 24 %.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage change in Gulshan Furniture's real sales revenue in 2025. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why Gulshan Furniture's managers should not be satisfied with the 18 % rise in sales revenue. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) −4.84 %.
    Real change = (1.18 ÷ 1.24 − 1) × 100 (M1 M1) = −4.84 % (A1). Accept −6 % (18 − 24) for 1 mark only.
  2. (b) Prices in the economy rose by 24 %, so in real terms revenue fell by about 4.8 %; if Gulshan raised its own prices in line with inflation, it is actually selling fewer units, suggesting it is losing market share.
    1 mark (knowledge): nominal vs real – inflation exceeds growth; up to 2 marks (application/explanation): interpretation for Gulshan's sales volume/market share.
Q9External environment (economic, political, competition) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Capital University (an invented private university) in Islamabad charges annual fees of Rs 900,000. Unemployment in the city has risen from 6 % to 9 % in a year.

  1. (a) Analyse two effects of a rise in unemployment on Capital University. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lower demand from fee-paying students: families who lose income cannot afford fees of Rs 900,000 a year, so enrolments fall and revenue drops; the university may need to offer scholarships or instalment plans. Easier recruitment of staff: with more people looking for work, the university can recruit qualified administrative staff and lecturers more easily and at lower salaries, reducing costs. (Also accept: more mature students seeking retraining, increasing demand for short courses.)
    Indicative content: fall in demand/ability to pay; counter-cyclical demand for retraining; easier, cheaper recruitment; bad debts from unpaid fees. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 4. Analysis Level 2 (3–4): two developed chains of cause and effect applied to a private university charging high fees; Level 1 (1–2): undeveloped or generic chains. Exactly two points are credited; no credit for evaluation.
Q10External environment (economic, political, competition) · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Chai Junction (an invented company) runs 35 cafés in Karachi selling tea and snacks. BeanWorld, an international coffee chain with a strong brand and large marketing budget, plans to open 20 cafés in Karachi over the next year, many in the same shopping malls as Chai Junction.

  1. (a) Analyse two effects on Chai Junction of the entry of BeanWorld into its market. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) Loss of market share and pressure on prices: BeanWorld's strong international brand and modern cafés attract young customers, so Chai Junction's sales fall; to compete it may cut prices or run promotions, reducing profit margins. Pressure to improve: Chai Junction may need to refurbish branches, add loyalty apps and differentiate with local products (e.g. karak chai, desi snacks), raising costs in the short run but possibly strengthening its unique selling point. (Also accept: competition for staff and prime locations raising rents and wages.)
    Indicative content: loss of market share; price competition and lower margins; need to differentiate/innovate; competition for locations and staff. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 4. Analysis Level 2 (3–4): two developed chains of cause and effect applied to a local café chain facing a multinational rival; Level 1 (1–2): undeveloped or generic chains. Exactly two points are credited; no credit for evaluation.
Q11External environment (economic, political, competition) · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 12 marks

Kayu Craft makes mid-priced wooden furniture in Malaysia. 70 % of its sales are in Malaysia and 30 % are exported. Malaysia is joining a regional trade agreement that will remove tariffs on furniture traded between member countries over three years. Manufacturers in two other member countries have labour costs about 25 % lower than Kayu Craft.

  1. (a) Evaluate whether Kayu Craft will benefit from Malaysia joining the new regional trade agreement. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Benefits: removal of tariffs on furniture exports makes Kayu Craft's products cheaper in member countries, opening a larger market and economies of scale; cheaper imported fittings and varnish lower costs. Threats: tariffs also fall on imports, so lower-cost rivals from Vietnam and Indonesia gain access to Malaysia, where 70 % of sales are made; Kayu Craft must compete on price or differentiate; meeting common product standards may add costs. Judgement: depends on whether its cost and quality allow it to compete — as a mid-priced, design-led firm with only 30 % exports, the threat to its home market may outweigh export gains in the short term, unless it uses the agreement to expand exports aggressively and develop its design USP.
    Use data: share of home vs export sales, competitors' costs, tariff changes. Weigh opportunities (exports, cheaper inputs) against threats (imports, standards). Clear judgement. Mark scheme: AO1 knowledge 2, AO2 application 2, AO3 analysis 2, AO4 evaluation 6. Level 3 (9–12): developed analysis in context plus a supported judgement that answers the question for this business (e.g. 'it depends on…' weighed, with a clear conclusion). Level 2 (5–8): developed analysis with some, limited or unsupported evaluation. Level 1 (1–4): knowledge and some application; little or no analysis.
Q12External environment (economic, political, competition) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 20 marks

SkyBlue Airways (an invented Pakistani airline). Appendix 1: GDP is forecast to fall by 1.5 % next year. Appendix 2: jet fuel prices rose 30 % in 12 months; fuel is 38 % of SkyBlue's costs and is priced in US dollars. Appendix 3: the rupee fell 15 % against the US dollar. Appendix 4: domestic load factor (percentage of seats filled) fell from 81 % to 68 %; international load factor stayed at 79 %. Appendix 5: a foreign low-cost airline has started flights on SkyBlue's two most profitable routes.

  1. (a) Advise SkyBlue Airways on the strategy it should follow over the next two years in response to these changes in its external environment. [20]
Show answer
  1. (a) Options: (1) Cut costs — reduce routes with low load factors, delay delivery of new aircraft, renegotiate leases, hedge fuel; protects cash during recession. (2) Change marketing — cut fares on domestic routes to fill seats (demand is price-elastic in recession), focus on business travellers and the Hajj/Umrah and diaspora markets where demand is less affected. (3) Exploit the weak rupee: overseas customers find SkyBlue cheaper, so expand international routes priced in foreign currency, while noting that fuel and leases are priced in dollars and become more expensive. Recommendation: a combination — protect cash flow (fuel hedging, temporary route cuts), keep fleet investment only for fuel-efficient aircraft, and focus on routes with foreign-currency revenue; justify with appendix data (load factor fall, fuel share of costs, dollar-denominated costs). Conditions: depends on how long the recession lasts and competitors' actions.
    Must use appendices: recession/GDP, fuel price, exchange rate, load factors, competitor entry. Consider economic, political, competitive factors. Clear prioritised recommendation. Levels: Level 4 (16–20): accurate knowledge, strong use of the case data, three or four developed chains of analysis and a well-supported judgement/recommendation throughout, specific to this business and its situation. Level 3 (11–15): developed analysis in context with some supported evaluation. Level 2 (6–10): some analysis, mostly descriptive use of the case; evaluation weak or unsupported. Level 1 (1–5): knowledge with little application.
Q13External environment (economic, political, competition) · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 12 marks

CodeBridge (an invented company) provides software services from Lahore. 85 % of its revenue comes from clients in the USA and is paid in US dollars. Its costs are: salaries 60 % (paid in rupees); laptops, software licences and cloud services 25 % (priced in US dollars); office and other costs 15 % (in rupees). The exchange rate has changed from US$1 = Rs 280 to US$1 = Rs 310.

  1. (a) Evaluate whether CodeBridge will benefit overall from the depreciation of the Pakistani rupee. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Benefits: 85 % of revenue is earned in US dollars, so each dollar now brings more rupees (Rs 280 to Rs 310, +10.7 %); salaries (60 % of costs) are paid in rupees, so profit margins rise; it can cut its dollar prices to win more contracts. Costs: laptops and cloud subscriptions (25 % of costs) are priced in dollars and rise; depreciation often brings higher inflation in Pakistan, leading staff to demand pay rises and more engineers leaving for jobs abroad. Judgement: overall CodeBridge benefits, because its dollar revenue is much larger than its dollar costs, but the gain may shrink over time if inflation pushes up wages; it should use the gain to retain staff.
    Indicative content: effects on export revenue and price competitiveness; imported input costs; inflation and wage pressure; staff retention; time period. Evaluation: net judgement based on proportions of revenue and costs in each currency. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 2, Evaluation 6. Evaluation Level 3 (5–6): judgements weighed throughout and a supported conclusion specific to the business; Level 2 (3–4): a judgement with some support in context; Level 1 (1–2): an unsupported or generic judgement.
Q14External environment (economic, political, competition) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 12 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate whether changes in interest rates are the most important external influence on a house-building company. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Interest rates matter greatly: most buyers use mortgages, so higher rates raise monthly repayments and reduce demand; builders borrow to buy land, so higher rates raise costs. Other influences: incomes and employment (recession), government policy (planning permission, housing subsidies, taxes on property), raw material prices (cement, steel), exchange rates for imported materials, competition. Judgement: in economies where most buyers use mortgages, interest rates are often the most important; where few buyers use mortgages, incomes, inflation and building-material costs may matter more; the answer depends on how buyers finance homes and the company's own borrowing.
    Indicative content: effects of interest rates on demand and costs; alternative external influences; dependence on mortgage market and financing. Evaluation: a reasoned comparison and conclusion. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 2, Evaluation 6. Evaluation Level 3 (5–6): judgements weighed throughout and a supported conclusion specific to the business; Level 2 (3–4): a judgement with some support in context; Level 1 (1–2): an unsupported or generic judgement.
Q15External environment (economic, political, competition) · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 12 marks

Volta Bikes (an invented company) makes electric motorbikes in Lahore with capacity of 15,000 bikes a year. It sells them for Rs 320,000; a similar petrol motorbike costs Rs 250,000. The government has announced a subsidy of Rs 50,000 per electric motorbike for the next 3 years. Market research forecasts that Volta's demand will rise from 12,000 to 30,000 bikes a year. Doubling capacity would cost Rs 1.6 billion, borrowed at 17 % interest. Two Chinese manufacturers are considering entering the market.

  1. (a) Evaluate whether Volta Bikes should double its production capacity because of the new government subsidy. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: the subsidy of Rs 50,000 cuts the effective price of a Volta e-bike from Rs 320,000 to Rs 270,000, now close to a petrol motorbike (Rs 250,000); market research forecasts demand rising from 12,000 to 30,000 units a year, beyond current capacity of 15,000; first-mover advantage. Against: the subsidy lasts only 3 years and may be withdrawn earlier if the government faces budget problems; doubling capacity costs Rs 1.6 billion at a 17 % interest rate; rivals from China may enter and also receive the subsidy; charging infrastructure is limited. Judgement: expand, but in phases (e.g. +50 % now, more if demand is confirmed), reducing the risk of spare capacity if the subsidy ends.
    Indicative content: effect of subsidy on price and demand; political risk/time limit; cost of finance; competition; infrastructure; phased expansion. Evaluation: conditional decision using the data. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 2, Evaluation 6. Evaluation Level 3 (5–6): judgements weighed throughout and a supported conclusion specific to the business; Level 2 (3–4): a judgement with some support in context; Level 1 (1–2): an unsupported or generic judgement.

Q17Ethics, CSR and sustainability · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

ChocoDelight makes premium chocolate bars sold in supermarkets in the UAE. It buys only Fairtrade cocoa, which costs 15 % more than other cocoa but guarantees farmers a minimum price.

  1. (a) Identify one stakeholder group that benefits from ChocoDelight's use of Fairtrade cocoa. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'ethical decision'. [3]
  3. (c) Explain one reason why ChocoDelight might continue to use Fairtrade cocoa even though it costs 15 % more. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cocoa farmers (suppliers).
    1 mark: farmers/suppliers (accept customers who value ethics).
  2. (b) A decision based on what is morally right (honest, fair, responsible) rather than only on what is legal or most profitable. For ChocoDelight, paying farmers a fair price even though cheaper cocoa is available is an ethical decision.
    1 mark: morally right/fair; 1 mark: beyond legal requirements/may reduce profit; 1 mark: application.
  3. (c) Its target customers are willing to pay more for ethical products; the Fairtrade label differentiates its chocolate from cheaper brands, supporting a premium price and brand loyalty, so the higher cost is covered by higher revenue.
    1 mark for knowledge of the term/concept; up to 2 marks for explaining it in the context of the business (application). Also accept: protect reputation, secure reliable long-term supply, motivate staff, owners' values.

  2. (b) Explain one ethical issue a pharmaceutical company faces when setting the price of a new life-saving medicine. [3]

Q19Ethics, CSR and sustainability · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Rang Dyeing (an invented company) dyes cotton fabric near Faisalabad. Local farmers complain that the water table is falling. After installing water-recycling equipment, the plant's water use fell from 180 litres to 117 litres per kilogram of fabric dyed. An environmental audit will be published next month.

  1. (a) Identify one stakeholder group affected by Rang Dyeing's water use. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'environmental audit'. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage reduction in water used per kilogram of fabric. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Local community/farmers (also accept government, employees, customers, shareholders).
    1 mark for any valid group.
  2. (b) An independent check of a business's impact on the environment, e.g. measuring Rang Dyeing's water use, waste-water quality and energy use, and comparing them with targets or legal limits.
    1 mark (knowledge): independent assessment of environmental impact; up to 2 marks (application/explanation): what is measured and/or purpose, applied to the dyeing plant.
  3. (c) 35 %.
    Reduction = 180 − 117 = 63 litres (M1); 63 ÷ 180 × 100 (M1) = 35 % (A1).

  2. (b) Explain one way a hotel in Nathia Gali could become more environmentally sustainable. [3]

  1. (a) A way of measuring business performance in three areas: profit (economic), people (social impact) and planet (environmental impact).

Q21Ethics, CSR and sustainability · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) StyleMart is a clothing retailer that buys clothes from 40 factories in Bangladesh and Pakistan. It is considering paying independent auditors to inspect working conditions in all its suppliers' factories every year. Analyse two benefits to StyleMart of auditing its suppliers. [8]
