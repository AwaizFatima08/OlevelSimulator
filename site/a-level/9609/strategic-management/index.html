Q1Strategic implementation (culture, change, contingency, corporate plans) · Core · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) BrightIdeas is an advertising agency in Karachi. Staff work in small project teams formed for each client campaign; team members are chosen for their expertise rather than seniority. Analyse two features of a task culture that are likely to benefit BrightIdeas. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1. Focus on getting the job done through teams of experts: each campaign team brings together copywriters, designers and digital specialists, so creative problems are solved quickly and campaigns meet clients' briefs, helping BrightIdeas win repeat business. 2. Flexibility and power based on expertise rather than position: teams can be formed and disbanded as clients change, and junior staff with the best ideas are listened to; this encourages creativity and motivation, important in a competitive creative industry.
    Features of task culture (Handy): team-based, expertise-driven, flexible, results-focused; linked to an advertising agency. Mark scheme: AO1 knowledge 2, AO2 application 2, AO3 analysis 4. Q2Strategic implementation (culture, change, contingency, corporate plans) · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain the term 'role culture', using Pak Postal Services (a large state-owned organisation) as an example. [3]
  2. (b) Explain one problem of a role culture if Pak Postal Services wants to compete with private courier companies. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A culture in which people work within clearly defined roles, rules and procedures in a hierarchical structure; power comes from a person's position. At Pak Postal Services, staff follow set procedures for handling mail and decisions pass up the hierarchy.
    1 mark: clearly defined roles/rules/procedures; 1 mark: hierarchy/power from position (bureaucracy); 1 mark: application.
  2. (b) Role cultures are slow to change: new ideas such as same-day parcel delivery must pass through many levels and procedures, so private couriers launch new services first and win customers.
    1 mark for knowledge of the term/concept; up to 2 marks for explaining it in the context of the business (application).

Q16Strategic choice (Ansoff, decision trees, force field) · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Zaiqa Foods is choosing between two options. Launch a new frozen-meal range: cost Rs 5 million; probability 0.6 of high demand (return Rs 14 million) and 0.4 of low demand (return Rs 4 million). Upgrade its existing range: cost Rs 2 million; probability 0.7 of success (return Rs 6 million) and 0.3 of failure (return Rs 3 million).

  1. (a) Calculate the expected net value of each option and state which option Zaiqa Foods should choose on financial grounds. [4]
  2. (b) Explain one limitation of using a decision tree for this decision. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) New product: Rs 5.0 million; upgrade: Rs 3.1 million — choose the new product.
    New product: 0.6 × 14 + 0.4 × 4 = 10.0; − 5 = Rs 5.0m (M1 A1). Upgrade: 0.7 × 6 + 0.3 × 3 = 5.1; − 2 = Rs 3.1m (M1). Choose the new product (A1).
  2. (b) The probabilities and payoffs are estimates (e.g. the 0.6 chance of high demand); if they are wrong, the 'best' option may change. The method also ignores qualitative factors such as brand image.
    1 mark: limitation; 1 mark: explained.

    1 mark: driving > restraining → proceed; 1 mark: small margin/subjective; 1 mark: action to strengthen drivers or weaken restraints.

  1. (a) Rs 129 million (compared with Rs 110 million for selling the land).
    Expected return = 0.65 × 520 + 0.35 × 260 = 338 + 91 = 429 (M1 M1). Net = 429 − 300 = Rs 129m (M1 A1).
  2. (b) For: building gives a higher expected value (Rs 129m vs a certain Rs 110m); decision trees make the options, risks and probabilities explicit and comparable. Against: the advantage is small (Rs 19m) compared with the risk — if demand is weak the project loses Rs 40m (260 − 300), whereas selling is certain; the probabilities (0.65) are estimates based on a housing market affected by interest rates; costs may overrun; expected value is an average that would only occur over many decisions, not a one-off project; qualitative factors such as the firm's cash position, reputation, staff and risk attitude of owners are ignored. Judgement: the decision tree is useful but should not decide alone; given the small difference and the downside risk, Skyline should build only if it can afford a loss and has confidence in the demand forecast (e.g. pre-sales of apartments).
    Use the calculation; limitations of decision trees (estimates, averages, ignores qualitative factors, risk attitude); judgement. Q22Strategic choice (Ansoff, decision trees, force field) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 20 marks

Margalla Cement (an invented company). Appendix 1: domestic demand flat; capacity utilisation 68 %; gearing 52 %. Appendix 2: Option 1 — export cement to Central Asia: cost Rs 2.0 billion; probability of success 0.55 (return Rs 5.0 billion), failure 0.45 (return Rs 1.2 billion). Option 2 — set up a ready-mix concrete and construction services business in Pakistan: cost Rs 1.5 billion; probability of success 0.7 (return Rs 3.2 billion), failure 0.3 (return Rs 1.0 billion). Appendix 3: government plans large infrastructure spending over five years. Appendix 4: cement is heavy; road transport to Central Asia adds about 25 % to delivered cost; border closures occurred twice in the last three years.

  1. (a) Advise the board of Margalla Cement on which strategic option it should choose. [20]
Show answer
  1. (a) Decision tree: Option 1 (export to Central Asia — market development): 0.55 × 5.0 + 0.45 × 1.2 − 2.0 = Rs 1.29 billion. Option 2 (ready-mix concrete and construction services — related diversification/forward integration): 0.7 × 3.2 + 0.3 × 1.0 − 1.5 = Rs 1.04 billion. Option 1 has the higher expected value but more risk (worst case −0.8bn vs −0.5bn). Option 2 uses existing customers and synergies (own cement), steadier demand from government infrastructure projects, lower investment. Recommendation: justified choice, e.g. Option 2 because of lower risk, synergy and current high gearing, possibly piloting exports on a small scale; or Option 1 if the board accepts risk to use capacity. Q23Strategic choice (Ansoff, decision trees, force field) · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 16 marks

Atlas Pumps makes water pumps. Option A: expand its factory at a cost of Rs 40 million. If domestic demand grows (probability 0.6) the return is Rs 90 million. If it does not grow (0.4), Atlas will try to export the extra output: export success (0.5) returns Rs 60 million; export failure (0.5) returns Rs 20 million. Option B: subcontract extra production at a set-up cost of Rs 5 million; returns are Rs 45 million if demand grows (0.6) and Rs 15 million if it does not (0.4).

  1. (a) Calculate the expected net value of each option. [4]
  2. (b) Evaluate whether Atlas Pumps should expand its factory. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Expand: Rs 30 million; subcontract: Rs 28 million.
    Expand, no-growth branch: 0.5 × 60 + 0.5 × 20 = 40 (M1). Expand: 0.6 × 90 + 0.4 × 40 − 40 = 54 + 16 − 40 = Rs 30m (M1 A1). Subcontract: 0.6 × 45 + 0.4 × 15 − 5 = Rs 28m (A1).
  2. (b) Financial: expanding gives a slightly higher expected value (Rs 30m vs Rs 28m), but the difference is only Rs 2m and depends on several estimated probabilities, including the uncertain export-market outcome; the worst case for expansion is −Rs 20m (20 − 40) compared with +Rs 10m for subcontracting. Expansion requires Rs 40m investment, raising gearing or using scarce cash. Non-financial: expansion gives control over quality and capacity for long-term growth; subcontracting is flexible and low-risk but quality and delivery depend on another firm, and profit margins are lower. Judgement: because the expected values are nearly equal and expansion is far riskier, subcontracting is the better choice unless Atlas is confident about demand growth (e.g. firm orders) and can finance expansion easily; it could subcontract now and expand later when demand is proven.
    Use the two-stage decision tree; risk (worst outcomes), reliability of probabilities, finance, quality/control, flexibility; judgement. Q24Corporate strategy and competitive advantage · Core · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) FlyThrift is a new budget airline in Pakistan. Analyse two ways it could achieve a competitive advantage through cost leadership. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1. Standardised fleet and high aircraft utilisation: using one aircraft type reduces training, spare-parts and maintenance costs; quick turnarounds mean each aircraft flies more hours a day, spreading fixed costs over more passengers, so FlyThrift can offer the lowest fares and still make a profit. 2. Q30Strategic analysis (PEST, SWOT) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 20 marks

RideNow (an invented company) is a ride-hailing app in Pakistan with 20 % market share. Appendix 1: three rivals; fares fell 10 % last year in a price war. Appendix 2: 35 % of drivers stopped using RideNow last year; many drivers use two or three apps. Appendix 3: a large foreign ride-hailing company is planning to enter in 2027. Appendix 4: survey — 52 % of women passengers say safety is their main concern when choosing an app. Appendix 5: a new metro bus line has opened in two cities.

  1. (a) Using Porter's five forces and other strategic analysis, advise RideNow on its strategy for the next three years. [20]
Show answer
  1. (a) Five forces: rivalry is intense (three rivals, price wars — Appendix 1); buyer power is high (passengers switch apps easily, low switching costs); supplier power (drivers) is growing — drivers work for several apps and demand higher shares, and fuel costs rise; threat of substitutes (buses, metro, motorcycles, car-pooling) is moderate to high; threat of entry is moderate (capital needed for marketing, but technology is easy to copy; a foreign app is planning entry). The industry is structurally unattractive for pure price competition. Options: (1) differentiation — safety features for women passengers, reliable service, loyalty schemes, reducing buyer power; (2) driver loyalty programme (fuel cards, insurance) to reduce supplier power; (3) diversify into deliveries/payments to use its network (related diversification); (4) cost leadership — difficult against larger rivals. Recommendation: focus on differentiation and driver retention, expand into food/parcel deliveries in existing cities; justify using appendices (survey: safety concerns 52 %, driver churn 35 %), with limitations of five-forces analysis (static, industry-level).
    Apply all five forces with evidence; link to strategic options; justified recommendation; awareness of limitations.
