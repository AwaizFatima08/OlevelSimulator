Q1Management and leadership · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term 'delegation'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one benefit to the manager of a 120-room hotel in Murree of delegating tasks to department supervisors. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Passing authority (the power to make decisions or carry out a task) from a manager to a subordinate, while the manager keeps overall responsibility.
    1 mark: passing authority/power to complete a task down the hierarchy to a subordinate; 1 mark: manager still accountable/retains ultimate responsibility.
  2. (b) The manager is freed from routine work (e.g. drawing up housekeeping rotas), so can spend time on strategic issues such as winning conference bookings in the off-season. Also accept: supervisors know their areas better so decisions are quicker; supervisors are motivated and developed for promotion.
    1 mark for knowledge of the term/concept; up to 2 marks for explaining it in the context of the business (application). e.g. benefit identified (time freed / faster decisions / motivation) 1; linked to hotel departments such as housekeeping, front desk or restaurant 1; consequence for the hotel 1.
Q2Management and leadership · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term 'democratic leadership'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one difference between a leader and a manager, using the head teacher of a primary school in Abbottabad as an example. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A leadership style in which the leader involves employees in decision-making, discussing options and taking their views into account, although the leader may still make the final decision.
    1 mark: leader involves/consults subordinates in decisions; 1 mark: two-way communication / staff views influence the final decision.
  2. (b) A manager plans, organises and controls resources to meet set targets (e.g. timetables, budgets, staffing rotas), whereas a leader sets a vision and inspires others to follow it (e.g. persuading teachers to adopt a new reading programme). The head teacher must do both: run the school day smoothly and inspire staff and parents.
    1 mark (knowledge): leader = sets direction/inspires; manager = plans, organises, controls; up to 2 marks (application/explanation): difference applied to the school, e.g. drawing up timetables and budgets versus inspiring teachers to raise reading standards.
Q3Management and leadership · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Dr Kiran owns a dental clinic in Hyderabad with 4 dentists and 6 dental assistants. When deciding whether to open in the evenings, she held a staff meeting, listened to everyone's views and then took a vote. The senior dental assistant, who has worked there for 15 years, is respected by the other assistants, who often follow her advice. Last month the clinic's X-ray machine broke down and Dr Kiran had to rearrange 40 patient appointments.

  1. (a) Identify the leadership style Dr Kiran uses when deciding the clinic's opening hours. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'informal leader'. [3]
  3. (c) Explain one way Dr Kiran could use the senior dental assistant's informal leadership when the new evening opening hours are introduced. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Democratic leadership.
    1 mark: democratic (accept participative/consultative). Do not accept laissez-faire.
  2. (b) A person with no formal position of authority who influences the attitudes and behaviour of a group because of personality, experience or popularity, e.g. the senior dental assistant whom other assistants follow.
    1 mark (knowledge): person who influences others without a formal management role; up to 2 marks (application/explanation): why they have influence (experience, respect, personality) and/or an example from the clinic.
  3. (c) Dr Kiran could ask the senior dental assistant to explain the evening rota to the other assistants and to help plan the first weeks. Because the other assistants often follow her advice, they are more likely to accept the change and volunteer for evening shifts, so the new hours start smoothly.
    1 mark (knowledge): an informal leader influences colleagues without formal authority; up to 2 marks (application/explanation): the senior assistant's respect is used to win acceptance of the change (e.g. volunteers for evening shifts, smoother start).
Q4Management and leadership · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term 'paternalistic leadership'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one reason why a paternalistic leadership style might suit a family-owned bakery in Gujranwala. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A style in which the leader makes decisions on behalf of employees but does so in what they believe is the employees' best interests, looking after their welfare like a parent.
    1 mark: leader makes the decisions (may consult but decides); 1 mark: decisions are taken in the interests/welfare of employees.
  2. (b) Many staff have worked for the family for years and value security; the owner looking after their welfare (e.g. paying for medical bills, flexible leave at Eid) builds loyalty, so labour turnover stays low and experienced bakers are kept.
    1 mark (knowledge): feature of paternalistic leadership (care for staff welfare, leader decides); up to 2 marks (application/explanation): linked to the bakery, e.g. loyal long-serving bakers, small family business, effect on turnover/loyalty.
Q5Management and leadership · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Ahmed manages a garment factory in Faisalabad that makes T-shirts for a European retailer to very tight deadlines. He uses an autocratic leadership style. Analyse one advantage and one disadvantage of autocratic leadership for this factory. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Advantage: decisions are made quickly by Ahmed alone, so when the retailer changes an order at short notice the production lines can be re-planned at once and the tight delivery deadline is met, protecting the contract. Disadvantage: machinists have no say, so they may feel undervalued; labour turnover and absenteeism may rise, raising recruitment and training costs and threatening output quality.
    Advantage: quick decisions / clear instructions / control of quality under deadlines. Disadvantage: low motivation, no use of workers' ideas, dependence on one person, higher labour turnover. Marks: knowledge 1, application 1, analysis 3. Level 2 (3–5): a developed chain of analysis (cause → effect → impact) applied to this business. Level 1 (1–2): knowledge with limited application; no developed analysis.
Q6Management and leadership · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

CodeNest is a software house in Karachi with 35 programmers. The founder, Farah, lets each team decide how and when to complete client projects and rarely checks progress. Staff enjoy the freedom, but last year two client apps were delivered late.

  1. (a) Identify one leadership style that Farah appears to use. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'emotional intelligence'. [3]
  3. (c) Explain one role of a manager that Farah may be neglecting. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Laissez-faire.
    1 mark: laissez-faire (teams decide how and when to work and Farah rarely checks progress). Do not accept democratic: in a democratic style the leader consults but still makes or confirms the decision.
  2. (b) The ability of a leader to recognise, understand and manage their own emotions and those of others (Goleman: self-awareness, self-regulation, motivation, empathy, social skills). At CodeNest, Farah might notice a stressed programmer before a deadline and give support, keeping the team productive.
    1 mark: understanding and managing own emotions; 1 mark: understanding/responding to others' emotions (empathy, social skills); 1 mark: application to CodeNest.
  3. (c) Controlling/monitoring: setting targets and checking progress against them. Because nobody checks progress, two client apps were delivered late, damaging CodeNest's reputation. Also accept planning or coordinating.
    1 mark: a valid managerial role (Fayol: plan, organise, command, coordinate, control; or Mintzberg role); 2 marks: explained with reference to late projects at CodeNest.

Q8Management and leadership · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Al Noor Stadium in Doha seats 45,000 people and hosts football matches, concerts and exhibitions. It employs 120 permanent staff and up to 400 temporary staff on event days. Last year two events lost money because security and catering costs were higher than budgeted. Omar has just been appointed general manager.

  1. (a) Analyse two of Fayol's functions of management that Omar must carry out as the new general manager of Al Noor Stadium. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) Planning: Omar must set objectives (e.g. host 30 events a year) and plan staffing and security for each match; without plans, crowd safety and revenue targets would be at risk. Controlling: he must compare actual results (ticket sales, costs per event) with targets and take corrective action, e.g. cutting overtime if security costs exceed budget, which protects the stadium's profit. (Also accept organising, commanding, coordinating.)
    Indicative content: planning (objectives, event calendar, staffing); organising (allocating staff and resources, structure); commanding (giving instructions, motivating 400 match-day staff); coordinating (ticketing, security and catering working together); controlling (measuring performance against targets, corrective action). Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 4. Analysis Level 2 (3–4): two developed chains of cause and effect applied to a stadium hosting football matches and concerts in Doha; Level 1 (1–2): undeveloped or generic chains. Exactly two points are credited; no credit for evaluation.
Q9Management and leadership · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Hilal Insurance (an invented company) has a regional claims office in Faisalabad with 70 claims handlers. The previous manager checked every claim personally and believed staff would avoid work if not watched closely. Average claim approval took 19 days and labour turnover was 18 %. The new manager, Mehwish, believes most employees want responsibility and can be trusted to use their own judgement.

  1. (a) Analyse two ways in which Mehwish's view of employees, using McGregor's Theory X and Theory Y, could influence the leadership style at the regional office. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mehwish holds Theory Y views (staff enjoy work, seek responsibility). She is therefore likely to delegate authority and use a democratic style, e.g. letting claims handlers settle claims up to a limit, which speeds up decisions and improves customer satisfaction. Her predecessor held Theory X views (staff dislike work, need control), leading to an autocratic style with close supervision and checking of every claim; this slowed processing and demotivated staff, which may explain the 18 % turnover. Moving to Theory Y could raise motivation, but only if staff are trained to handle responsibility.
    Indicative content: Theory X → autocratic, close supervision, few delegated decisions, tight control; Theory Y → delegation, democratic/participative style, job enrichment, trust; effects on motivation, speed of decisions, turnover. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 4. Analysis Level 2 (3–4): two developed chains of cause and effect applied to the insurance claims office and its data (approval time, turnover); Level 1 (1–2): undeveloped or generic chains. Exactly two points are credited; no credit for evaluation.
Q10Management and leadership · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Rangoon Organics was started in Myanmar by Thura, who made every decision himself when it had 15 employees. It now has 260 employees and offices in Myanmar, Thailand and Singapore. Thura still approves all purchases above US$500 and all new customer contracts. Two experienced country managers resigned last year, saying they had 'no freedom to manage'.

  1. (a) Analyse two reasons why the autocratic leadership style used by the founder may be less effective now that Rangoon Organics has grown. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) With 260 staff in three countries, the founder cannot make every decision quickly: decisions bottleneck at the top, so country managers cannot respond to local supermarket buyers, losing orders. Experienced country managers are given no authority, so they become demotivated and may leave (two left last year), raising recruitment costs and losing market knowledge. (Also accept: lack of new ideas from staff; poor communication over long distances.)
    Indicative content: decision bottleneck/slower responses as the business grows; demotivation of skilled managers; loss of ideas/innovation; communication problems across countries. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 4. Analysis Level 2 (3–4): two developed chains of cause and effect applied to an organic food exporter that has grown from 15 to 260 staff in three countries; Level 1 (1–2): undeveloped or generic chains. Exactly two points are credited; no credit for evaluation.
Q11Management and leadership · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 12 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate whether emotional intelligence is the most important quality for the manager of a hospital emergency department. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: staff work under extreme stress; an emotionally intelligent manager notices burnout, calms conflict between doctors and nurses, and shows empathy to patients' families, which keeps the team effective and reduces staff turnover. Against: in emergencies, decisive (sometimes autocratic) decision-making, clinical knowledge, and organisational skills such as planning shifts and controlling resources may matter more; a caring manager who cannot prioritise could put lives at risk. Judgement: emotional intelligence is necessary but not sufficient; it is most important for long-term retention and teamwork, while technical competence and decisiveness matter most during a crisis. The best manager combines both.
    Analysis points: EI (self-awareness, empathy, social skills) and its effect on stress, teamwork, retention; alternative qualities: decisiveness, clinical expertise, planning/organising, communication. Evaluation: depends on situation (crisis vs routine), experience of team, hospital culture. Mark scheme: AO1 knowledge 2, AO2 application 2, AO3 analysis 2, AO4 evaluation 6. Level 3 (9–12): developed analysis in context plus a supported judgement that answers the question for this business (e.g. 'it depends on…' weighed, with a clear conclusion). Level 2 (5–8): developed analysis with some, limited or unsupported evaluation. Level 1 (1–4): knowledge and some application; little or no analysis.
Q12Management and leadership · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 20 marks

SunGrid installs solar panels across Punjab. Timeline:2021: Founder Imran Qureshi makes all decisions himself; demand rises as electricity prices increase. Revenue Rs 900 million.2022: Revenue Rs 1,450 million. Labour turnover reaches 28 %. Two senior engineers resign, saying they were 'never consulted'.2023: Large commercial projects run three months late. Customer complaints double.2024: Imran attends a leadership course, introduces weekly staff forums and delegates installation scheduling to four regional managers.2025: Labour turnover falls to 12 %; complaints fall by 40 %; revenue Rs 1,900 million but operating profit margin 9 % (14 % in 2021).

  1. (a) Evaluate the effectiveness of Imran Qureshi's leadership of SunGrid between 2021 and 2025. [20]
Show answer
  1. (a) Effective: in 2021–22 his autocratic, fast decisions let SunGrid exploit rising demand; revenue grew strongly. The switch to a more democratic style in 2024 (weekly forums, delegating installation scheduling to regional managers) coincided with labour turnover falling and customer complaints dropping. Less effective: the 2022–23 style caused high labour turnover (28 %) and the loss of two senior engineers, which delayed the commercial projects; the change of style came late and only after damage was done. Judgement: overall moderately effective — he adapted his style to the situation (Tannenbaum–Schmidt continuum), and later results improved, but revenue growth came partly from the market (energy prices) rather than leadership, and profit margin is still below 2021 levels. A full judgement needs data on profit and employee satisfaction after 2025.
    Use the timeline: autocratic phase and its results; turnover and resignations; change to democratic style; later turnover and complaint data. Consider external factors (energy prices, demand) versus leadership. Judgement on overall effectiveness with criteria (growth, staff, customers). Levels: Level 4 (16–20): accurate knowledge, strong use of the case data, three or four developed chains of analysis and a well-supported judgement/recommendation throughout, specific to this business and its situation. Level 3 (11–15): developed analysis in context with some supported evaluation. Level 2 (6–10): some analysis, mostly descriptive use of the case; evaluation weak or unsupported. Level 1 (1–5): knowledge with little application.
Q13Management and leadership · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 12 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate whether laissez-faire leadership is the most suitable style for the head of a university research laboratory developing new vaccines in Karachi. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Laissez-faire suits highly qualified, self-motivated scientists: freedom encourages creativity and new research ideas, and Theory Y staff may resent close control. However, vaccine research is tightly regulated: safety, ethics approvals and grant deadlines need clear direction and control, so a purely hands-off approach risks breaches and missed deadlines. Judgement: a mainly laissez-faire style for research design, combined with firm (more autocratic) control over safety and compliance, is likely best; the most suitable style depends on the task and the experience of each team member (contingency approach).
    Indicative content: advantages of laissez-faire for expert, motivated researchers (creativity, motivation, ownership); risks: lack of coordination, missed deadlines, safety/regulatory failures, junior researchers lacking guidance; alternatives such as democratic or situational leadership. Evaluation: depends on staff experience, nature of tasks (creative vs compliance), regulation, funding deadlines; a supported conclusion, e.g. a mixed or contingency style is most suitable. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 2, Evaluation 6. Evaluation Level 3 (5–6): judgements weighed throughout and a supported conclusion specific to the business; Level 2 (3–4): a judgement with some support in context; Level 1 (1–2): an unsupported or generic judgement.
Q14Management and leadership · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 12 marks

Margalla Cement (an invented company) employs 1,100 people at two plants. In 2023 it made a loss of Rs 2.1 billion. Faisal was appointed CEO and used an autocratic style: he cut costs, closed a kiln and approved every purchase himself. In 2025 the business made a profit of Rs 1.4 billion. However, absenteeism rose from 3 % to 7 % and two experienced plant managers resigned. The board now wants to start exporting cement to East Africa, which will need quick decisions by managers based in Mombasa.

  1. (a) Evaluate whether Faisal should change his leadership style to improve the performance of Margalla Cement. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Arguments for changing: an autocratic style may have suited the crisis (fast cost cutting) but data show falling morale – absenteeism has risen from 3 % to 7 % and two plant managers resigned – and the move into export markets needs ideas and quick local decisions, which a more democratic style with delegation would support. Arguments against: cement plants are safety-critical and capital-intensive, so central control over costs and safety may still be needed; profits have recovered under the current style; a sudden change may confuse staff. Judgement: Faisal should adapt rather than abandon his style – keep firm control over safety and capital spending but delegate decisions on export sales and plant improvements to managers, as the business is no longer in crisis.
    Indicative content: evidence of success (return to profit) versus warning signs (absenteeism up, resignations, need for innovation for exports); features of autocratic vs democratic/delegation; situational leadership – the right style depends on circumstances that have changed. Evaluation: whether the crisis is over; skills of managers; risks of change; a decision specific to Margalla Cement. Marks: Knowledge 2, Application 2, Analysis 2, Evaluation 6. Evaluation Level 3 (5–6): judgements weighed throughout and a supported conclusion specific to the business; Level 2 (3–4): a judgement with some support in context; Level 1 (1–2): an unsupported or generic judgement.
Q15Management and leadership · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 20 marks

Karakoram Couriers (an invented company) delivers parcels across northern Pakistan.Timeline:2015: Founded by Rashid Khan, who treats staff 'like family', pays for staff weddings and medical costs and makes all decisions himself.2020: 1,400 employees, 22 depots. Revenue Rs 6.2 billion.2024: Staff threaten a strike when a new rota is introduced without consultation; the rota is withdrawn.2025: Rashid retires. Sana Javed, a former logistics manager at a multinational, becomes CEO.2026 plan: introduce app-based parcel tracking, automate route planning for 30 % of routes and merge 22 depots into 15.Appendix 1: Revenue growth fell from 11 % (2022) to 2 % (2025).Appendix 2: Staff survey 2025 – 81 % say they are loyal to the business; 34 % trust senior management to manage change fairly.Appendix 3: A national rival has cut prices by 12 % and offers next-day delivery to all 22 towns Karakoram serves.

  1. (a) Advise Sana Javed on the leadership approach she should use at Karakoram Couriers over the next three years. [20]
Show answer
  1. (a) Options: continue the founder's paternalistic style; adopt a democratic/participative style; use a more autocratic style to drive the technology change; or a situational mix. Analysis: the 2026 plan (app-based tracking, 30 % of routes automated, merger of depots) is a major change that staff fear (Appendix 2 survey: only 34 % trust senior management); a purely autocratic style would speed implementation but could trigger the resistance seen in the 2024 strike threat; involving depot managers and informal leaders in planning (democratic) would win acceptance and use local knowledge but is slower when a rival is cutting prices (Appendix 3). Recommendation: a situational approach – consult and involve staff on how the change is implemented (depot teams, regular briefings), delegate route decisions to depot managers, but set clear non-negotiable deadlines and targets herself; keep paternalistic elements (job security pledge, retraining) that staff value. Success depends on her emotional intelligence and on communication.
    Indicative content: features of leadership styles; timeline evidence that the founder's paternalistic style built loyalty but slowed decisions; scale and urgency of change; staff trust data; competitor pressure; role of emotional intelligence and informal leaders. Evaluation: a clear recommendation that matches the style to the stage of change and the evidence, with conditions (e.g. depends on depot managers' ability, speed needed to respond to the rival). Marks: Knowledge 3, Application 3, Analysis 6, Evaluation 8. Analysis needs three or four developed chains using the timeline and appendices. Evaluation Level 3 (6–8): a clear, justified decision that weighs the evidence and is specific to the business and the time period; Level 2 (3–5): a judgement with partial support; Level 1 (1–2): assertion only.

Q17Recruitment, selection, training and dismissal · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

FreshMart runs 14 supermarkets in Lahore. It is installing self-service tills in all stores, so 40 checkout jobs will no longer be needed. It needs a new store manager and plans to promote one of its assistant managers. New shelf-stackers are chosen by a short interview and a one-day work trial.

  1. (a) Identify one method of selection used by FreshMart. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the term 'internal recruitment'. [3]
  3. (c) Explain one difference between dismissal and redundancy, using FreshMart as an example. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Interview (or work trial / aptitude test).
    1 mark: interview, test or work trial (shown in the data).
  2. (b) Filling a vacancy with a person who already works for the business, e.g. by promotion or transfer. FreshMart filling the store manager post with an existing assistant manager would be internal recruitment.
    1 mark: vacancy filled from existing employees; 1 mark: development (promotion/transfer/advertised internally); 1 mark: application to FreshMart.
  3. (c) Dismissal is ending employment because of the employee's fault (e.g. a cashier caught stealing or repeated lateness), whereas redundancy is ending employment because the job is no longer needed (e.g. checkout staff no longer needed after self-service tills are installed); redundant staff usually receive compensation.
    1 mark each: dismissal = fault of employee/misconduct/incompetence; redundancy = job no longer exists, not the worker's fault; 1 mark applied to FreshMart (self-service tills, store closure).
Q18Recruitment, selection, training and dismissal · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term 'off-the-job training'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one advantage to a bank in Dubai of using a specialist recruitment agency to recruit IT security specialists. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Training that takes place away from the employee's normal place of work/workstation, e.g. at a college, training centre or online course provider.
