Q2Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A negative temperature coefficient (NTC) thermistor and a 3.3 kΩ fixed resistor are connected in series across a 6.0 V supply of negligible internal resistance. The output voltage is taken across the fixed resistor. At 20 °C the resistance of the thermistor is 1.5 kΩ.

  1. (a) Calculate the output voltage at 20 °C. [2]
  2. (b) The temperature rises. State and explain the change in the output voltage. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.1 V
    C1 V = 6.0 × 3.3/(1.5 + 3.3); A1 4.1 V
  2. (b) The output voltage increases. The thermistor's resistance falls as temperature rises, so its share of the 6.0 V falls and the share across the fixed resistor rises.
    B1 increases; B1 thermistor resistance decreases so p.d. across it decreases
Q3Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A variable resistor of range 0 to 10 kΩ is connected in series with a 5.0 kΩ fixed resistor across a 6.0 V supply of negligible internal resistance. The output voltage is taken across the fixed resistor.

  1. (a) Calculate the largest output voltage. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the smallest output voltage. [2]
  3. (c) This circuit cannot make the output smaller than 2.0 V. Describe how the variable resistor alone could be connected as a potentiometer (potential divider) so that the p.d. across the 5.0 kΩ resistor could be varied all the way from 0 to 6.0 V. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.0 V
    Variable resistor at 0 Ω: all 6.0 V across the 5.0 kΩ resistor (A1)
  2. (b) 2.0 V
    C1 6.0 × 5.0/(5.0 + 10); A1 2.0 V
  3. (c) Connect the two ends of the variable resistor's track directly across the 6.0 V supply, and connect the 5.0 kΩ resistor between the sliding contact and one end of the track. Moving the slider varies the output from 0 V (slider at that end) to 6.0 V (slider at the other end).
    B1 whole track across the supply (all three terminals used); B1 output taken between slider and one end, giving 0 to 6.0 V
Q4Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

On a potentiometer wire, a cell X of e.m.f. 1.50 V gives a balance length of 72.0 cm. Without changing the driver circuit, cell X is replaced by cell Y, which balances at 55.2 cm.

  1. (a) Calculate the e.m.f. of cell Y. [2]
  2. (b) State why the driver cell must have a larger e.m.f. than either cell being tested. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.15 V
    C1 E ∝ balance length: E_Y = 1.50 × 55.2/72.0; A1 1.15 V
  2. (b) Otherwise the p.d. across the whole wire is less than the test e.m.f., and no balance point can be found.
    B1

Q6Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A gardener in Murree builds a frost alarm. An NTC thermistor is connected in series with a fixed resistor across a 5.0 V supply of negligible internal resistance. The output is taken across the thermistor and must be 2.0 V when the thermistor's resistance is 800 Ω.

  1. (a) Calculate the resistance of the fixed resistor needed. [2]
  2. (b) When the soil is colder, the thermistor's resistance is higher. State whether the output voltage is then above or below 2.0 V, and explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1200 Ω
    M1 same current: 2.0/800 = 3.0/R (or V_out = 5.0 × 800/(800 + R)); A1 R = 1200 Ω
  2. (b) Above 2.0 V: the thermistor has a larger share of the total resistance, so a larger share of the supply p.d.
    B1 above; B1 reason
Q7Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A potentiometer consists of a uniform resistance wire of length 100.0 cm connected across a driver cell of e.m.f. 2.00 V and negligible internal resistance. A test cell is connected, in series with a galvanometer, between one end of the wire and a sliding contact. The galvanometer reads zero when the sliding contact is 64.5 cm from that end.

  1. (a) Calculate the e.m.f. of the test cell. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why this method measures the e.m.f. of the test cell rather than its terminal p.d. on load. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why a longer potentiometer wire, with the same driver cell, would give a more precise result. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.29 V
    C1 p.d. per unit length = 2.00/100.0 V cm⁻¹; A1 E = 2.00 × 64.5/100.0 = 1.29 V
  2. (b) At balance the galvanometer shows zero, so no current flows through the test cell; with no current there is no p.d. across its internal resistance, so its terminal p.d. equals its e.m.f.
    B1 no current in test cell at balance; B1 no lost volts / terminal p.d. = e.m.f.
  3. (c) The balance length would be longer, so its percentage uncertainty would be smaller.
    B1
Q8Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A street-light controller uses a light-dependent resistor (LDR) in series with a 10 kΩ fixed resistor across a 9.0 V supply of negligible internal resistance. The output is taken across the LDR. The LDR's resistance is 500 kΩ in the dark and 2.0 kΩ in bright daylight.

  1. (a) Calculate the output voltage in the dark. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the output voltage in bright light. [1]
  3. (c) The circuit switches on a street light when the output voltage rises above 6.0 V. Calculate the LDR resistance at which this happens. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.82 V
    C1 9.0 × 500/(500 + 10); A1 8.82 V
  2. (b) 1.50 V
    9.0 × 2.0/12.0 = 1.50 V (A1)
  3. (c) 20 kΩ
    M1 6.0/R = 3.0/10 (same current); A1 R = 20 kΩ

Q10Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A potentiometer wire of length 1.000 m and resistance 5.0 Ω is connected in series with a resistor R and a driver cell of e.m.f. 4.0 V and negligible internal resistance. A standard cell of e.m.f. 1.018 V balances at 80.0 cm along the wire.

  1. (a) Calculate the p.d. across the whole potentiometer wire. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the current in the wire. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the resistance R. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why adding the series resistor R makes the potentiometer more suitable for measuring small e.m.f.s. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.27 V
    1.018 × 100/80.0 = 1.27 V (A1)
  2. (b) 0.255 A
    I = 1.27/5.0 = 0.255 A (A1)
  3. (c) 10.7 Ω
    M1 total resistance = 4.0/0.255 = 15.7 Ω; A1 R = 10.7 Ω
  4. (d) It reduces the p.d. per unit length of the wire, so a small e.m.f. balances at a longer, more precisely measured length.
    B1
Q11Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Two potential dividers are connected in parallel across a 12 V supply of negligible internal resistance. In the first, a 4.7 kΩ resistor is connected to the positive terminal and an NTC thermistor to the negative terminal; their junction is X. In the second, two 3.0 kΩ resistors are in series; their junction is Y. A high-resistance voltmeter is connected between X and Y.

  1. (a) Show that the voltmeter reads zero when the thermistor's resistance is 4.7 kΩ. [2]
  2. (b) At 40 °C the thermistor's resistance is 2.2 kΩ. Calculate the voltmeter reading and state which junction is at the higher potential. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest one advantage of this arrangement over a single potential divider for measuring small changes of temperature near the balance temperature. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both junctions are then at 6.0 V (half of 12 V), so there is no p.d. between them.
    B1 X at 12 × 4.7/9.4 = 6.0 V; B1 Y at 6.0 V, so p.d. zero
  2. (b) 2.17 V; Y is at the higher potential
    C1 V_X = 12 × 2.2/6.9 = 3.83 V; A1 2.17 V; B1 Y higher
  3. (c) The reading starts from zero, so a sensitive (small-range) voltmeter can be used and small changes are easily seen.
    B1
Q12Potential dividers & sensors (incl. potentiometer) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 15 marks
  1. (a) Plan an experiment to investigate how the output voltage V of a potential divider varies with the distance d between a light-dependent resistor (LDR) and a small lamp. The LDR is in series with a fixed resistor across a d.c. supply, and V is measured across the fixed resistor. It is suggested that V = V₀ d⁻ⁿ over a limited range, where V₀ and n are constants.Your account should include: the procedure, the measurements and how they are made, the control of variables, how the data are analysed to test the relationship and find n, and any safety precautions. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) A full plan: vary d, measure V, control lamp power and stray light, plot lg V against lg d; gradient = −n, intercept = lg V₀.
    Mark points (1 mark each, max 15): Defining the problem: d is the independent variable, V the dependent variable; keep lamp p.d./current (power), supply e.m.f. and fixed resistor constant. Method: circuit diagram described with LDR in series with fixed resistor and supply, voltmeter across fixed resistor; separate lamp circuit with ammeter/voltmeter to monitor lamp power; measure d from lamp filament to LDR face with a metre rule; align lamp and LDR at the same height facing each other (e.g. on an optical bench); range of at least 6 distances, e.g. 10 cm to 60 cm. Control: work in a darkened room or use a black tube/shield to exclude stray light; allow lamp to warm up and LDR to settle before reading; keep LDR temperature steady. Analysis: plot lg V against lg d; relationship valid if a straight line; gradient = −n; y-intercept = lg V₀.
