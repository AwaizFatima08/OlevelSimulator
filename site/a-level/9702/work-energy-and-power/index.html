Q3Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define work done. [1]
  2. (b) A shopkeeper in Faisalabad lifts a 25 kg sack of rice vertically through 1.4 m onto a cart at constant speed. Calculate the work done on the sack. [2]
  3. (c) State the form of energy that increases as the sack is lifted, and explain why the kinetic energy of the sack does not change. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Work done = force × displacement in the direction of the force.
    B1: force × distance moved in the direction of the force (not just 'force × distance').
  2. (b) 343 J

  3. (c) Gravitational potential energy increases; KE is constant because the speed is constant.

Q4Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A constant resultant force F acts on a body of mass m, initially at rest, and moves it a distance s in the direction of the force. Use the concept of work done to show that the kinetic energy of the body is ½mv², where v is its final speed. [3]
  2. (b) A car of mass 1200 kg travels along the motorway at 72 km h⁻¹. Calculate its kinetic energy. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) W = Fs; F = ma; v² = 2as so s = v²/2a; W = ma × v²/2a = ½mv², and the work done becomes kinetic energy.
    M1 W = Fs and F = ma; M1 v² = u² + 2as with u = 0 gives s = v²/(2a); A1 substitution giving W = ½mv² = KE
  2. (b) 2.40 × 10⁵ J
    72 km h⁻¹ = 20 m s⁻¹ (C1); KE = ½ × 1200 × 20² = 2.40 × 10⁵ J (A1)

A child of mass 32 kg starts from rest at the top of a water slide at a water park in Karachi. The bottom of the slide is 6.5 m vertically below the top.

  1. (a) Assuming there is no friction, calculate the speed of the child at the bottom of the slide. [2]
  2. (b) The actual speed at the bottom is 9.6 m s⁻¹. Calculate the energy transferred to thermal energy by friction during the descent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 11.3 m s⁻¹
    mgh = ½mv² so v = √(2gh) (C1) = √(2 × 9.81 × 6.5) = 11.3 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  2. (b) 566 J
    loss of GPE = 32 × 9.81 × 6.5 = 2040 J; gain in KE = ½ × 32 × 9.6² = 1475 J (C1); energy dissipated = 566 J (A1)

  2. (b) Calculate the height at which the ball has equal kinetic and gravitational potential energy. [2]

Q13Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A pendulum consists of a bob of mass 0.25 kg on a light string of length 1.20 m. The bob is pulled aside until the string makes an angle of 35° with the vertical, and is released from rest.

  1. (a) Show that the bob falls through a vertical height of about 0.22 m as it moves to the lowest point. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the bob at the lowest point. Assume air resistance is negligible. [2]
  3. (c) State and explain whether a bob of mass 0.50 kg released in the same way would have a different speed at the lowest point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) h = 1.2(1 − cos 35°) = 0.217 m
    M1 height of bob below pivot at release = 1.2 cos 35° = 0.983 m; A1 h = 1.20 − 0.983 = 0.217 m ≈ 0.22 m
  2. (b) 2.06 m s⁻¹
    ½mv² = mgh (C1); v = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.217) = 2.06 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) No: the speed is the same.
    B1 same speed; B1 m cancels in mgh = ½mv² (both GPE lost and KE gained are proportional to m).
Q14Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A gas in a cylinder is heated. It expands at a constant pressure of 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa, pushing a piston outwards. Its volume increases from 2.4 × 10⁻³ m³ to 3.1 × 10⁻³ m³.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done by the gas. [2]
  2. (b) The piston has a cross-sectional area of 0.012 m². Calculate the force exerted on the piston by the gas and the distance moved by the piston. [2]
  3. (c) Use your answers to (b) to confirm your answer to (a), and hence explain why W = pΔV is a form of 'work = force × displacement'. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 105 J
    W = pΔV (C1) = 1.5 × 10⁵ × (3.1 − 2.4) × 10⁻³ = 105 J (A1)
  2. (b) force = 1800 N; distance = 0.0583 m
    F = pA = 1.5 × 10⁵ × 0.012 = 1800 N (B1); distance = ΔV/A = 0.0007/0.012 = 0.0583 m (B1)
  3. (c) F × d = 1800 × 0.0583 = 105 J, the same; since F = pA and d = ΔV/A, Fd = pA × ΔV/A = pΔV.
    B1 numerical confirmation; B1 algebra F d = (pA)(ΔV/A) = pΔV
Q15Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A cricket ball of mass 0.16 kg is hit vertically upwards with an initial speed of 18 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum height the ball would reach if there were no air resistance. [2]
  2. (b) The ball actually rises to a height of 14.2 m. Calculate the average force of air resistance on the ball during its upward motion. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the ball takes longer to fall back from its highest point than it took to rise. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16.5 m
    ½mv² = mgh (C1); h = v²/2g = 18²/(2 × 9.81) = 16.5 m (A1)
  2. (b) 0.256 N
    initial KE = ½ × 0.16 × 18² = 25.9 J; GPE at top = 0.16 × 9.81 × 14.2 = 22.3 J (C1); work against air resistance = 3.63 J (C1); F = 3.63/14.2 = 0.256 N (A1)
  3. (c) Air resistance acts upwards on the way down, so the downward acceleration is less than g (and the speed at each height is lower).
    B1 air resistance opposes motion: on the way up it adds to the weight (deceleration > g), on the way down it reduces the acceleration below g.

  1. (a) Calculate v² for each result. [1]
  2. (b) It is suggested that v² is directly proportional to h. Use the data to test this suggestion. [2]
  3. (c) If all the gravitational potential energy were converted to kinetic energy, v² = 2gh. Determine the fraction of the GPE that is converted into kinetic energy of the trolley. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest one reason why not all the GPE becomes kinetic energy, and one improvement to the way v is measured. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) v²/m² s⁻²: 1.69, 3.39, 5.06, 6.76, 8.47
    B1 all five values correct to 3 s.f. (1.69, 3.39, 5.06, 6.76, 8.47)
  2. (b) v²/h = 16.9, 16.9, 16.9, 16.9, 16.9 m s⁻², constant within experimental error, so the suggestion is supported.
    M1 calculates v²/h (or plots v² against h: straight line through the origin) for all values; A1 valid conclusion: ratio constant (16.9) so proportional
  3. (c) about 0.86
    gradient of v² against h = 16.9 m s⁻² (C1); fraction = 16.9/(2 × 9.81) = 0.86 (A1)
  4. (d) Friction/air resistance (or energy to rotate the wheels); use a longer card or measure the card width with vernier calipers / use a data logger with two light gates.
    B1 friction at the axles or air resistance or rotational KE of wheels; B1 sensible improvement to the timing/measurement of v
Q17Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A car of mass 1500 kg travelling at 25 m s⁻¹ brakes and comes to rest in a distance of 48 m.

  1. (a) Calculate the average braking force. [2]
  2. (b) The car now travels at 50 m s⁻¹ and brakes with the same average force. Determine the braking distance and explain your reasoning. [2]
  3. (c) State the energy change that takes place in the brakes. [1]
Show answer

  2. (b) 192 m
    M1 KE ∝ v², so KE is 4 times greater; A1 work Fd = KE, so d = 4 × 48 = 192 m
  3. (c) Kinetic energy → thermal (internal) energy of the brakes and surroundings.
    B1 kinetic to thermal energy

The blades of a wind turbine near Jhimpir in Sindh sweep a circle of radius 40 m. Wind of speed 9.0 m s⁻¹ blows directly towards the blades. The density of air is 1.2 kg m⁻³.

  1. (a) Show that the mass of air passing through the circle swept by the blades per second is ρAv, where A is the area of the circle, and calculate its value. [2]
  2. (b) Hence show that the kinetic energy of the air arriving per second is ½ρAv³, and calculate it. [2]
  3. (c) The electrical output of the turbine is 0.80 MW. Calculate its efficiency, and explain why the efficiency can never be 100 %. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 54300 kg s⁻¹
    M1 volume per second = A × v (air moves v metres per second through area A), mass per second = ρAv; A1 1.2 × π × 40² × 9.0 = 54300 kg s⁻¹
  2. (b) 2.20 × 10⁶ W
    M1 KE per second = ½(mass per second)v² = ½(ρAv)v² = ½ρAv³; A1 ½ × 1.2 × π × 40² × 9.0³ = 2.20 × 10⁶ W
  3. (c) 36.4 %; the air must keep moving after the blades (it cannot be brought to rest), and there are friction and electrical losses.
    B1 0.80 × 10⁶/2.20 × 10⁶ × 100 = 36.4 %; B1 air still has KE after passing the blades / friction / heating in generator

  2. (b) On level ground the cyclist produces the same power. The resistive force is proportional to the square of the speed and is still 22 N at 5.0 m s⁻¹. Determine the constant speed of the cyclist on level ground. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 401 W
    component of weight down slope = 85 × 9.81 × sin 4.0° = 58.2 N (C1); total force = 58.2 + 22 = 80.2 N (C1); P = Fv = 80.2 × 5.0 = 401 W (A1)
  2. (b) 7.69 m s⁻¹
    F = kv² with k = 22/5.0² = 0.88 N s² m⁻² (C1); P = Fv = kv³ (C1); v = (401/0.88)^(1/3) = 7.69 m s⁻¹ (A1)
