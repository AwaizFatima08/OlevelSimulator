Q1Momentum, impulse & collisions · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

On a wet road in Lahore, a car of mass 1200 kg travelling at 15 m s⁻¹ runs into the back of a stationary car of mass 900 kg. The two cars lock together and move off along the same line.

  1. (a) Define linear momentum. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the two cars immediately after the collision. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the loss of kinetic energy in the collision. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Momentum is the product of mass and velocity.
    B1: mass × velocity (velocity, not speed).
  2. (b) 8.57 m s⁻¹
    M1: 1200 × 15 = (1200 + 900)v. A1: v = 18 000 / 2100 = 8.57 m s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 57.9 kJ
    C1: initial KE = ½ × 1200 × 15² = 135 kJ. C1: final KE = ½ × 2100 × 8.57² = 77.1 kJ. A1: loss = 57.9 kJ.
Q2Momentum, impulse & collisions · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which of the following is a correct unit for impulse?A kg m s⁻²B kg m s⁻¹C kg m² s⁻¹D N s⁻¹ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B kg m s⁻¹
    Impulse = force × time, so N s = (kg m s⁻²)(s) = kg m s⁻¹, the same unit as momentum. A is the unit of force, not impulse.
Q3Momentum, impulse & collisions · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A cricket ball of mass 0.16 kg is bowled at 32 m s⁻¹ towards a batter. It is hit straight back along the same line at 41 m s⁻¹. The bat is in contact with the ball for 1.2 ms.

  1. (a) State Newton's second law of motion in terms of momentum. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the magnitude of the change in momentum of the ball. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the average force exerted on the ball by the bat. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The resultant force on a body is proportional to (equal to) its rate of change of momentum.
    B1: resultant force ∝ / = rate of change of momentum (and in the same direction).
  2. (b) 11.7 N s
    C1: take towards the batter as positive: Δp = 0.16 × (−41) − 0.16 × 32. A1: |Δp| = 0.16 × 73 = 11.7 N s (kg m s⁻¹).
  3. (c) 9730 N
    C1: F = Δp/Δt = 11.7 / 1.2 × 10⁻³. A1: F = 9730 N (opposite to the ball's original direction).
Q4Momentum, impulse & collisions · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A football of mass 0.43 kg is at rest. During a kick, the force on the ball rises steadily from zero to 1800 N in 4.0 ms and then falls steadily back to zero in a further 6.0 ms. The force always acts in the same direction.

  1. (a) State what is represented by the area under a force–time graph. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the impulse given to the ball. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the ball as it leaves the foot. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The impulse of the force (the change in momentum).
    B1: impulse / change of momentum.
  2. (b) 9.00 N s
    C1: area of triangle = ½ × 1800 × 0.010. A1: 9.00 N s.
  3. (c) 20.9 m s⁻¹
    C1: impulse = mv − 0, v = 9.00 / 0.43. A1: 20.9 m s⁻¹.
Q5Momentum, impulse & collisions · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Two spheres collide head-on in a perfectly elastic collision. No external forces act. Which quantity is NOT necessarily the same before and after the collision?A the total momentum of the two spheresB the total kinetic energy of the two spheresC the relative speed of the two spheresD the momentum of each individual sphere [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D the momentum of each individual sphere
    Total momentum is always conserved; in an elastic collision total KE is conserved and the relative speed of approach equals the relative speed of separation. Momentum is transferred between the spheres, so each sphere's momentum changes. B is conserved here because the collision is elastic.
Q6Momentum, impulse & collisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

On a horizontal frictionless air track, trolley A of mass 0.50 kg moves at 0.60 m s⁻¹ towards trolley B of mass 0.30 kg, which is at rest. The trolleys collide head-on and the collision is perfectly elastic.

  1. (a) State what is meant by a perfectly elastic collision. [1]
  2. (b) Use conservation of momentum and the relative speeds of the trolleys to calculate the velocity of each trolley after the collision. [4]
  3. (c) Show that your answers to (b) are consistent with the collision being perfectly elastic. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A collision in which the total kinetic energy is conserved.
    B1: total kinetic energy is the same before and after (momentum is always conserved).
  2. (b) A: 0.150 m s⁻¹, B: 0.750 m s⁻¹, both in the original direction of A
    C1: 0.50 × 0.60 = 0.50 vA + 0.30 vB. C1: relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation: 0.60 = vB − vA. M1: solve: 0.30 = 0.50 vA + 0.30(vA + 0.60) → 0.80 vA = 0.12. A1: vA = 0.150 m s⁻¹, vB = 0.750 m s⁻¹ (same direction as A's initial motion).
  3. (c) KE before = 0.0900 J; KE after = 0.0900 J, so KE is conserved
    M1: before: ½ × 0.50 × 0.60² = 0.0900 J. A1: after: ½ × 0.50 × 0.15² + ½ × 0.30 × 0.75² = 0.0900 J, equal.
Q7Momentum, impulse & collisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A radon-222 nucleus, initially at rest, decays by emitting an α-particle of kinetic energy 5.49 MeV. The daughter nucleus is polonium-218. Take the mass of the α-particle as 4u and of the daughter nucleus as 218u, where u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

  1. (a) Explain why the daughter nucleus and the α-particle move off in exactly opposite directions. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the α-particle. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the daughter nucleus. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate the kinetic energy of the daughter nucleus, in MeV. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Total momentum before is zero, so the momenta after must be equal in magnitude and opposite in direction.
    B1: initial momentum is zero (nucleus at rest) and momentum is conserved. B1: so the two momenta must cancel: equal and opposite.
  2. (b) 1.63 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹
    C1: KE = 5.49 × 10⁶ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 8.78 × 10⁻¹³ J, m = 4 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. A1: v = √(2KE/m) = 1.63 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 2.98 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹
    C1: 4u × vα = 218u × vd. A1: vd = (4/218) × 1.63 × 10⁷ = 2.98 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹.
  4. (d) 0.101 MeV
    A1: KE ∝ p²/m with equal p, so KE_d = (4/218) × 5.49 = 0.101 MeV (or ½mv² with values from (c)).
Q8Momentum, impulse & collisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

On smooth ice, puck A of mass 0.17 kg moving at 6.0 m s⁻¹ strikes an identical puck B at rest. After the collision, A moves at 3.0 m s⁻¹ in a direction at 60° to its original direction. Friction is negligible.

  1. (a) Calculate the components of the velocity of puck B after the collision, parallel and perpendicular to the original direction of A. [3]
  2. (b) Hence find the speed of B and the angle its path makes with the original direction of A. [2]
  3. (c) Determine whether the collision is elastic. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.5 m s⁻¹ parallel; 2.6 m s⁻¹ perpendicular (on the opposite side of the line from A)
    C1: parallel: 0.17 × 6.0 = 0.17 × 3.0 cos 60° + 0.17 vx → vx = 4.5 m s⁻¹. C1: perpendicular: 0 = 0.17 × 3.0 sin 60° + 0.17 vy. A1: vy = −2.6 m s⁻¹ (opposite side).
  2. (b) 5.20 m s⁻¹ at 30° to the original direction
    A1: v = √(4.5² + 2.60²) = 5.20 m s⁻¹. A1: tan θ = 2.60/4.5, θ = 30° (on the other side of the line from A).
  3. (c) Yes: total KE before = 3.06 J = total KE after
    M1: KE before = ½ × 0.17 × 6.0² = 3.06 J; after = ½ × 0.17 × (3.0² + 5.20²) = 0.765 + 2.30 = 3.06 J. A1: KE conserved so elastic.
Q9Momentum, impulse & collisions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A ball of mass 0.20 kg falls vertically and hits the floor at 5.0 m s⁻¹. It rebounds vertically at 3.0 m s⁻¹. What is the magnitude of the change in momentum of the ball?A 0.40 N sB 1.0 N sC 1.6 N sD 3.4 N s [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 1.6 N s
    Velocity reverses, so Δp = 0.20 × (3.0 − (−5.0)) = 1.6 N s. A (0.40 N s) comes from subtracting speeds and ignoring the change of direction.
Q10Momentum, impulse & collisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A rifle of mass 4.2 kg fires a bullet of mass 12 g horizontally at 750 m s⁻¹. The rifle is initially at rest.

  1. (a) Calculate the recoil speed of the rifle, assuming it is free to move. [2]
  2. (b) The rifle is brought to rest over a distance of 0.15 m. Calculate the average force needed. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the bullet and rifle have equal magnitudes of momentum but very different kinetic energies. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.14 m s⁻¹
    C1: 0 = 0.012 × 750 − 4.2v. A1: v = 2.14 m s⁻¹.
  2. (b) 64.3 N
    C1: KE of rifle = ½ × 4.2 × 2.14² = 9.64 J. C1: work done = F × 0.15. A1: F = 64.3 N.
  3. (c) Momenta are equal and opposite (total zero); KE = p²/2m, so the much smaller mass of the bullet gives it far more KE (3380 J compared with 9.64 J).
    B1: momentum conserved from zero, so equal magnitudes. B1: KE = p²/2m, smaller mass → larger KE (bullet ≈ 3400 J).
Q11Momentum, impulse & collisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
