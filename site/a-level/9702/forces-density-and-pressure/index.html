Q1Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement defines the torque of a couple?A the sum of the two forces multiplied by the distance between themB one of the forces multiplied by the perpendicular distance between the lines of action of the forcesC one of the forces multiplied by the distance from either force to the centre of the objectD the resultant force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the pivot [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B one of the forces multiplied by the perpendicular distance between the lines of action of the forces
    Torque of a couple = one force × perpendicular separation of the two lines of action. A doubles the torque by adding both forces; D is wrong because a couple has zero resultant force.
Q2Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A rigid body is acted on by several coplanar forces. Which pair of conditions must both be true for the body to be in equilibrium?A resultant force is zero and resultant torque is zeroB resultant force is zero and the body is at restC resultant torque is zero and the forces all pass through the centre of gravityD the sum of clockwise forces equals the sum of anticlockwise forces [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A resultant force is zero and resultant torque is zero
    Equilibrium needs no resultant force (no linear acceleration) and no resultant torque about any point (no angular acceleration). B is wrong: a body moving at constant velocity can be in equilibrium.
Q3Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the moment of a force about a point. [1]
  2. (b) A mechanic in a Faisalabad workshop pushes on the end of a spanner 0.25 m from the centre of a nut with a force of 80 N at right angles to the spanner. Calculate the moment about the centre of the nut. [1]
  3. (c) He now pushes with the same 80 N force at 30° to the spanner, at the same point. Calculate the new moment and explain why it is smaller. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The force multiplied by the perpendicular distance from the point to the line of action of the force.
    B1
  2. (b) 20 N m
    80 × 0.25 = 20 N m (A1)
  3. (c) 10 N m; the perpendicular distance to the line of action is smaller (0.25 sin 30° = 0.125 m).
    M1 perpendicular distance 0.25 sin 30°; A1 10 N m
Q4Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State the principle of moments. [2]
  2. (b) On a see-saw in a park in Karachi, Ali (mass 32 kg) sits 1.5 m from the pivot. His sister (mass 24 kg) sits on the other side. The see-saw is uniform and pivoted at its centre. Calculate how far from the pivot she must sit for the see-saw to balance. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) For a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.
    B1 in equilibrium; B1 sum clockwise = sum anticlockwise about the same point
  2. (b) 2.0 m
    M1 32g × 1.5 = 24g × d; A1 d = 2.0 m (g cancels; the see-saw's weight acts at the pivot)
Q5Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A farmer lifts the handles of a wheelbarrow so that its legs are just off the ground. The wheel axle acts as the pivot. The load of soil has weight 600 N acting 0.40 m horizontally from the axle, the barrow's weight 150 N acts 0.60 m from the axle, and the farmer lifts vertically at the handles, 1.40 m from the axle. Calculate the vertical force the farmer applies. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the upward force of the ground on the wheel. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 236 N
    M1 moments about axle; M1 F × 1.40 = 600 × 0.40 + 150 × 0.60 = 330; A1 F = 236 N
  2. (b) 514 N
    750 − 236 = 514 N (B1)
Q6Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A uniform metre rule of mass 0.12 kg is balanced on a pivot at the 30.0 cm mark by hanging a mass from the 5.0 cm mark. What is the hung mass?A 0.072 kgB 0.096 kgC 0.144 kgD 0.48 kg [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 0.096 kg
    The rule's weight acts at the 50.0 cm mark, 20.0 cm from the pivot; the hung mass is 25.0 cm from it. m × 25.0 = 0.12 × 20.0, m = 0.096 kg. C comes from inverting the ratio of distances.
Q7Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A uniform wooden plank of length 4.0 m and weight 600 N rests horizontally across a small stream, supported at its ends A and B. A man of weight 750 N stands on the plank 1.2 m from A. Calculate the upward force on the plank at B. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the upward force at A. [1]
  3. (c) The man walks slowly from A to B. Describe how the force at A changes. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 525 N
    M1 moments about A: R_B × 4.0 = 600 × 2.0 + 750 × 1.2; A1 R_B = 525 N
  2. (b) 825 N
    R_A = 1350 − 525 = 825 N (A1)
  3. (c) It decreases steadily (linearly), from 1050 N when he is at A to 300 N when he is at B.
    B1 decreases (linearly); values not required
Q8Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A non-uniform log of length 5.0 m lies on level ground. A vertical force of 420 N is just enough to lift end A off the ground (end B stays on the ground). A vertical force of 280 N is just enough to lift end B off the ground (end A stays on the ground).

  1. (a) Define centre of gravity. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the weight of the log. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the distance of the centre of gravity of the log from end A. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The point through which the whole weight of the object may be considered to act.
    B1
  2. (b) 700 N
    When either end is just lifted the other end is the pivot; adding: W = 420 + 280 = 700 N (B1)
  3. (c) 2.0 m
    M1 lifting A, pivot at B: 420 × 5.0 = 700 × (5.0 − x); A1 x = 2.0 m (check: 280 × 5.0 = 700 × 2.0)
Q9Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a couple. [1]
  2. (b) To open a water tap, a girl applies two equal and opposite forces of 6.0 N with her finger and thumb on opposite sides of the tap handle. The lines of action of the forces are 3.0 cm apart. Calculate the torque applied. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the couple turns the tap handle but gives it no linear acceleration. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A pair of equal and opposite forces whose lines of action are parallel but do not coincide (separated).
    B1
  2. (b) 0.18 N m
    C1 torque = F × d; A1 6.0 × 0.030 = 0.18 N m
  3. (c) The forces are equal and opposite so the resultant force is zero, but they produce a resultant torque.
    B1
Q10Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 5 style · 8 marks

A student investigates a uniform metre rule of unknown mass M. A mass m is hung from the 10.0 cm mark and the rule is moved on a knife edge until it balances. The knife edge is then at the p cm mark. The centre of gravity of the rule is at the 50.0 cm mark.m / kg | p / cm0.050 | 37.10.080 | 32.70.120 | 28.70.160 | 25.80.200 | 23.8

  1. (a) Show that m = M(50.0 − p)/(p − 10.0), where p is in cm. Hence state what graph to plot to obtain M from its gradient. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate (50.0 − p)/(p − 10.0) for each row of the table. [2]
  3. (c) Use the first and last rows (or a best-fit line) to determine M. [2]
  4. (d) State one source of uncertainty in finding p and suggest an improvement. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Moments about the pivot: m(p − 10.0) = M(50.0 − p). Plot m against (50.0 − p)/(p − 10.0); gradient = M (line through origin).
    B1 moments equation; B1 graph and gradient = M
  2. (b) 0.476, 0.762, 1.139, 1.532, 1.899
    B1 all values correct; B1 consistent 3 s.f. or 3 d.p.
  3. (c) M ≈ 0.105 kg (accept 0.100–0.110 kg)
    M1 gradient = Δm/Δx using widely separated points; A1 M in range 0.100–0.110 kg
  4. (d) The balance point is hard to judge because the rule tips over a range of positions; mark the position with a pin/use a sharper knife edge and repeat to find the mean, or use larger masses so the balance is more sensitive.
    B1 source; B1 matching improvement
Q11Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A uniform rectangular cabinet of height 1.20 m, depth (front to back) 0.40 m and weight 500 N stands on a rough floor. A horizontal force F, perpendicular to the back face, is applied at the top of the back face, pushing the cabinet forwards. Calculate the value of F needed to just start tipping the cabinet about its bottom front edge (assume it does not slide). [3]
  2. (b) Explain why fitting heavy drawers low down makes the cabinet more stable. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 83.3 N
    B1 pivot at the bottom edge; weight acts 0.20 m from it; M1 F × 1.20 = 500 × 0.20; A1 F = 83.3 N
  2. (b) The centre of gravity is lowered, so the cabinet must be tilted through a larger angle before the line of action of the weight passes outside the base.
    B1 lower centre of gravity; B1 weight's line of action stays within base for larger tilt / needs larger toppling moment
Q12Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A light rod of length 0.50 m can turn about its centre. Two forces of 20 N act, one at each end, in opposite directions. Each force makes an angle of 60° with the rod. What is the torque of this couple?A 5.0 N mB 8.7 N mC 10 N mD 17 N m [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 8.7 N m
    Perpendicular distance between the lines of action = 0.50 sin 60° = 0.433 m. Torque = one force × separation = 20 × 0.433 = 8.7 N m. C ignores the angle (20 × 0.50); D adds both forces (2 × 20 × 0.433); A uses cos 60° instead of sin 60°.
Q13Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A uniform horizontal beam of length 2.40 m and weight 180 N is hinged to a wall at end H. Its other end E is held up by a straight cable attached to the wall above H; the cable makes an angle of 35° with the beam. A shop sign of weight 120 N hangs from the beam 2.00 m from H. The beam is in equilibrium.

  1. (a) Calculate the tension in the cable. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the force exerted by the hinge on the beam. [2]
  3. (c) Hence calculate the magnitude of the force exerted by the hinge. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 331 N
    M1 moments about hinge; M1 T sin 35° × 2.4 = 180 × 1.2 + 120 × 2.0 = 456; A1 T = 331 N
  2. (b) horizontal 271 N (away from the wall); vertical 110 N (upwards)
    B1 H = T cos 35° = 271 N; B1 V = 300 − T sin 35° = 110 N
  3. (c) 293 N
    √(271² + 110²) = 293 N (A1)
Q14Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A uniform ladder of length 5.0 m and weight 220 N leans against a smooth vertical wall, making an angle of 65° with the rough horizontal ground. A painter of weight 700 N stands on the ladder 3.5 m from its foot (measured along the ladder). The ladder is in equilibrium.

  1. (a) Explain why the force exerted by the wall on the ladder is horizontal. [1]
  2. (b) By taking moments about the foot of the ladder, calculate the force exerted by the wall. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the magnitude of the total force exerted by the ground on the ladder and its angle to the horizontal. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The wall is smooth, so there is no friction: the only force is the normal contact force, perpendicular to the wall.
    B1
  2. (b) 280 N
    M1 moments about foot; M1 R × 5.0 sin 65° = 220 × 2.5 cos 65° + 700 × 3.5 cos 65°; A1 R = 280 N
  3. (c) 962 N at 73.1° to the horizontal
    B1 vertical component = 220 + 700 = 920 N; B1 friction = 280 N; A1 √(920² + 280²) = 962 N at tan⁻¹(920/280) = 73.1°
Q15Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A decorative lamp of weight 36 N hangs at rest from two light strings fixed to a ceiling. One string makes an angle of 40° with the horizontal and the other makes an angle of 55° with the horizontal.

  1. (a) State the two conditions for the lamp to be in equilibrium. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the tension in each string. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Resultant force zero and resultant torque zero (the three forces are concurrent and form a closed triangle).
    B1
  2. (b) string at 40°: 20.7 N; string at 55°: 27.7 N
    M1 horizontal: T₁ cos 40° = T₂ cos 55°; M1 vertical: T₁ sin 40° + T₂ sin 55° = 36; A1 T₁ = 20.7 N; A1 T₂ = 27.7 N (or sine rule on the closed triangle: T₁ = 36 cos 55°/sin 95°)
Q16Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A tower crane on a building site in Islamabad has a horizontal jib. On one side of the tower a load of weight 12 kN hangs 25 m from the tower; the weight of the jib, 30 kN, acts 6.0 m from the tower on the same side as the load. A concrete counterweight is fixed 8.0 m from the tower on the other side.

  1. (a) Calculate the weight of counterweight needed so that the resultant moment about the tower is zero. [2]
  2. (b) The trolley now moves the 12 kN load in to 15 m from the tower, with the counterweight unchanged. Calculate the resultant moment about the tower and state its direction. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why tower cranes are designed so that the tower itself can withstand a resultant moment in either direction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 60000 N (60 kN)
    M1 C × 8.0 = 12 000 × 25 + 30 000 × 6.0 = 480 000; A1 C = 6.0 × 10⁴ N
  2. (b) 1.2 × 10⁵ N m, turning towards the counterweight side
    M1 60 000 × 8.0 − (12 000 × 15 + 30 000 × 6.0); A1 1.2 × 10⁵ N m towards the counterweight
  3. (c) The load and its position change during lifting (and the crane may be unloaded), so the moments cannot always balance.
    B1
Q17Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A uniform rod of length 1.6 m and weight 40 N is freely hinged at its lower end to the ground. It is held at rest at 60° to the horizontal by a horizontal force F applied at its upper end.

  1. (a) Calculate F. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the magnitude and direction of the force exerted on the rod by the hinge. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the lines of action of F, the weight and the hinge force must all pass through one point. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 11.5 N
    M1 moments about hinge; M1 F × 1.6 sin 60° = 40 × 0.8 cos 60°; A1 F = 11.5 N
  2. (b) 41.6 N at 73.9° above the horizontal
    B1 horizontal component 11.5 N (opposite to F); B1 vertical component 40 N; A1 41.6 N at 73.9° to horizontal
  3. (c) With only three forces in equilibrium, if they were not concurrent there would be a resultant moment about the meeting point of two of them.
    B1

[1]

  1. (a) A The water pressure on the bottom of the stone is greater than on its top.

  2. (b) Water in the reservoir behind a dam stands 120 m above an inspection gate. Calculate the pressure at the gate due to the water (density 1000 kg m⁻³). [1]
  3. (c) The gate has an area of 2.0 m². Calculate the force on it due to the water. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mass of column = ρAh; weight = ρAhg; pressure = weight/area = ρAhg/A = ρgh.

  2. (b) 1.18 × 10⁶ Pa
    1000 × 9.81 × 120 = 1.18 × 10⁶ Pa (A1)
  3. (c) 2.35 × 10⁶ N
    F = pA = 2.35 × 10⁶ N (A1)

Show answer

    C1 Δp = ρgh = 1000 × 9.81 × 0.145 = 1420 Pa; M1 gas pressure is greater than atmospheric (added); A1 1.024 × 10⁵ Pa

  1. (a) State Archimedes' principle. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the depth of the block below the water surface. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the smallest mass that must be placed on top of the block to make its top face level with the water surface. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The upthrust on a body in a fluid equals the weight of fluid displaced by the body.
    B1

Show answer
  1. (a) C 0.94

A weather balloon released from Karachi airport holds 2.4 m³ of helium of density 0.18 kg m⁻³. The rubber and string have a total mass of 0.35 kg. The density of the surrounding air is 1.20 kg m⁻³.

  1. (a) Calculate the upthrust on the balloon. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the total weight of the helium, rubber and string. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the greatest mass of instruments the balloon can lift at a steady speed, ignoring air resistance and the volume of the instruments. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the balloon would rise more slowly high in the atmosphere, even with no load. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 28.3 N
    1.20 × 2.4 × 9.81 = 28.3 N (A1)
  2. (b) 7.67 N

  3. (c) 2.10 kg

  4. (d) Air density decreases with height, so the upthrust (weight of air displaced) decreases.

Q30Density, pressure & upthrust · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A solid cylinder of length 10.0 cm, cross-sectional area 25 cm² and density 900 kg m⁻³ floats upright in a beaker containing a layer of oil (density 800 kg m⁻³) on top of water (density 1000 kg m⁻³). The cylinder is completely covered by liquid, with its lower part in the water and its upper part in the oil.

  1. (a) Calculate the length of the cylinder that is in the water. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the upthrust on the cylinder. [1]
  3. (c) Some of the oil is removed, but the cylinder remains completely covered by liquid. State and explain whether the length in the water changes. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.0 cm
    M1 upthrust = weight: (1000x + 800(0.10 − x))Ag = 900 × 0.10 × Ag; M1 200x = 10; A1 x = 0.050 m
  2. (b) 2.21 N
    Upthrust = weight = 900 × 0.10 × 0.0025 × 9.81 = 2.21 N (A1)
  3. (c) No change: the upthrust still has to equal the weight, and the balance between the two layers depends only on the densities, not on the depth of oil above.
    B1
