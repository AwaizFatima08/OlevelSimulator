Q1Centripetal acceleration & force · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A car of mass 1200 kg travels around a flat roundabout in Islamabad at a constant speed of 12 m s⁻¹ along a circular path of radius 25 m.

  1. (a) Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the car. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the resultant force on the car and state its direction. [2]
  3. (c) State what provides this force. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.76 m s⁻²
    a = v²/r (M1) = 144/25 = 5.76 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 6910 N towards the centre of the roundabout
    F = ma = 1200 × 5.76 = 6910 N (A1); towards the centre (B1).
  3. (c) Friction between the tyres and the road
    B1: (sideways) friction of road on tyres.
Q2Centripetal acceleration & force · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A ball on a string moves at constant speed in a horizontal circle on a smooth horizontal table, the other end of the string being fixed to a peg at the centre.

  1. (a) Explain why the ball is accelerating even though its speed is constant. [2]
  2. (b) State the direction of the resultant force on the ball and explain why this force does no work on the ball. [2]
  3. (c) The string breaks. Describe the subsequent path of the ball, as seen from above. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Its direction of motion (velocity) changes continuously; acceleration is rate of change of velocity.
    B1: velocity is a vector / direction changes; B1: change in velocity means acceleration.
  2. (b) Towards the centre of the circle; it is always perpendicular to the velocity, so there is no displacement in the direction of the force.
    B1: towards centre; B1: force perpendicular to motion → no work done, so kinetic energy (speed) constant.
  3. (c) A straight line along the tangent to the circle at the point of release.
    B1: tangential straight line (no resultant horizontal force).

Q11Centripetal acceleration & force · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A section of a racing track is a horizontal circular arc of radius 80 m, banked at 15° to the horizontal. A car of mass m travels round it. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) By considering the vertical and horizontal components of the normal contact force N, show that the speed at which no friction is needed is given by v² = rg tan θ. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate this speed. [1]
  3. (c) Explain what additional force is needed if the car travels faster than this speed, and state its direction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) N cos θ = mg and N sin θ = mv²/r; dividing gives tan θ = v²/(rg).

  2. (b) 14.5 m s⁻¹

    B1: N sin θ alone is too small to give mv²/r; B1: friction needed, acting down the banking (parallel to surface).
