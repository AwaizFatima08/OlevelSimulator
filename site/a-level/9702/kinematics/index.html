Q1Equations of motion & motion graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A car accelerates uniformly from 4.0 m s⁻¹ to 22 m s⁻¹ in a time of 6.0 s. What is the distance travelled in this time?A 54 mB 78 mC 108 mD 132 m [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 78 m
    s = ½(u + v)t = ½(4.0 + 22) × 6.0 = 78 m. 132 m wrongly uses the final speed for the whole time; 54 m uses only ½Δv × t and ignores the initial speed.
Q2Equations of motion & motion graphs · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A motorcycle starts from rest at a traffic signal in Lahore and accelerates uniformly at 2.5 m s⁻² along a straight road for 8.0 s.

  1. (a) State what is meant by acceleration. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the motorcycle at the end of the 8.0 s. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the distance travelled by the motorcycle in the 8.0 s. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rate of change of velocity.
    B1: change of velocity ÷ time taken (rate of change of velocity). 'Speed' instead of velocity scores 0.
  2. (b) 20 m s⁻¹
    v = u + at = 0 + 2.5 × 8.0 = 20 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 80 m
    s = ut + ½at² (M1) = ½ × 2.5 × 8.0² = 80 m (A1).
Q3Equations of motion & motion graphs · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A local train travels in a straight line between two stations. Its velocity–time graph is made of straight lines joining these points (time in s, velocity in m s⁻¹): (0, 0), (40, 16), (160, 16), (200, 0).

  1. (a) Calculate the acceleration of the train during the first 40 s. [2]
  2. (b) Determine the total distance travelled between the two stations. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the average speed of the train for the whole journey. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.40 m s⁻²
    a = gradient = Δv/Δt (M1) = 16/40 = 0.40 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 2560 m
    Distance = area under graph (M1): ½×40×16 + 120×16 + ½×40×16 = 320 + 1920 + 320 = 2560 m (A1).
  3. (c) 12.8 m s⁻¹
    2560/200 = 12.8 m s⁻¹ (A1).
Q4Equations of motion & motion graphs · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A lift in an office tower starts from rest and accelerates upwards uniformly at 1.2 m s⁻² for 2.5 s. It then moves at constant speed for 9.0 s and finally decelerates uniformly to rest in 2.0 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum speed of the lift. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the magnitude of the deceleration. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the total height risen by the lift. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.0 m s⁻¹
    v = at = 1.2 × 2.5 = 3.0 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 1.5 m s⁻²
    3.0/2.0 = 1.5 m s⁻² (A1).
  3. (c) 33.8 m
    Area under v–t graph (M1): ½×3.0×2.5 = 3.75 m; 3.0×9.0 = 27 m; ½×3.0×2.0 = 3.0 m (M1); total 33.8 m (A1).
Q5Equations of motion & motion graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A stone is dropped from rest from a high bridge and falls freely for 3.0 s. Air resistance is negligible and g = 9.81 m s⁻². How far does it fall?A 14.7 mB 29.4 mC 44.1 mD 88.3 m [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 44.1 m
    s = ½gt² = ½ × 9.81 × 9.0 = 44.1 m. 29.4 m is gt (the final speed in m s⁻¹), not a distance; 88.3 m omits the ½.
Q6Equations of motion & motion graphs · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A cyclist rides 4.2 km due north along a straight road in 10 min, then turns and rides 3.0 km due south in 5 min.

  1. (a) Distinguish between speed and velocity. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the average speed of the cyclist for the whole 15 min. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the average velocity of the cyclist for the whole 15 min. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Speed is a scalar (distance per unit time); velocity is a vector (displacement per unit time, with direction).
    B1: velocity has direction / is a vector (rate of change of displacement); speed is scalar.
  2. (b) 8.0 m s⁻¹
    Total distance 7200 m, total time 900 s (M1); 7200/900 = 8.0 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 1.33 m s⁻¹ due north
    Displacement = 4.2 − 3.0 = 1.2 km north (M1); 1200/900 = 1.33 m s⁻¹ north (A1; direction needed).
Q7Equations of motion & motion graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) An object moving in a straight line with uniform acceleration has a velocity of 5.0 m s⁻¹. After travelling a further 24 m its velocity is 11 m s⁻¹. What is its acceleration?A 0.25 m s⁻²B 2.0 m s⁻²C 2.5 m s⁻²D 4.0 m s⁻² [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 2.0 m s⁻²
    v² = u² + 2as → a = (121 − 25)/(2 × 24) = 2.0 m s⁻². 4.0 m s⁻² forgets the factor 2; 0.25 m s⁻² divides the change in speed by the distance.
Q8Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A ball is thrown vertically upwards and is caught again at the same height. Air resistance is negligible. Upwards is taken as positive. Which statement describes the acceleration–time graph of the ball while it is in the air?A a horizontal line at a negative valueB a sloping straight line from a positive value to a negative valueC a horizontal line at zero, because the ball returns to its starting pointD a horizontal line at a positive value until the top, then at a negative value [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A a horizontal line at a negative value
    The only force is weight, so acceleration is g downwards (−9.81 m s⁻²) throughout, including at the top. The sloping line describes the velocity–time graph, not acceleration.
Q9Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A driver on the M-2 motorway is travelling at 25 m s⁻¹ when he sees an obstruction. His reaction time is 0.65 s. The brakes then give a uniform deceleration of 6.5 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the thinking distance. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the braking distance. [2]
  3. (c) State the total stopping distance. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the stopping distance more than doubles when the initial speed is doubled. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16.3 m
    25 × 0.65 = 16.25 ≈ 16.3 m (B1; accept 16 m).
  2. (b) 48.1 m
    v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 25² − 2 × 6.5 × s (M1); s = 48.1 m (A1).
  3. (c) 64.3 m
    16.25 + 48.08 = 64.3 m (A1, allow ecf).
  4. (d) Thinking distance doubles (∝ u) but braking distance quadruples (∝ u²).
    B1: thinking distance ∝ u so doubles; B1: braking distance ∝ u² so becomes four times as large, so total more than doubles.
Q10Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A trolley is released from rest on a slope. Its displacement s along the slope is recorded at time t:t / s: 0, 0.50, 1.00, 1.50, 2.00s / m: 0, 0.10, 0.40, 0.90, 1.60

  1. (a) Show that the data are consistent with uniform acceleration from rest. [2]
  2. (b) Determine the acceleration of the trolley. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the velocity of the trolley at t = 2.0 s. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) s/t² is constant (0.40 m s⁻²) for every reading, so s ∝ t².
    M1: calculate s/t² for at least three readings (0.40, 0.40, 0.40, 0.40); A1: constant, so s = ½at² with constant a (starting from rest, s = 0 at t = 0).
  2. (b) 0.80 m s⁻²
    s = ½at² → a = 2s/t² (M1) = 2 × 1.60/2.0² = 0.80 m s⁻² (A1).
  3. (c) 1.6 m s⁻¹
    v = at = 0.80 × 2.0 = 1.6 m s⁻¹ (A1).
Q11Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A tennis ball is dropped from rest from a height of 1.8 m onto a hard floor. It leaves the floor with 70% of the speed it had just before impact. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the speed of the ball just before it hits the floor. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the height reached by the ball after the bounce. [2]
  3. (c) Taking downwards as positive, describe the velocity–time graph of the ball from release until it reaches the top of its first bounce. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.94 m s⁻¹
    v² = 2gh = 2 × 9.81 × 1.8 (M1); v = 5.94 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 0.882 m
    Rebound speed 0.70 × 5.94 = 4.16 m s⁻¹ (M1); h = v²/2g = 0.882 m (A1).
  3. (c) Straight line from 0 rising to +5.94 m s⁻¹; near-vertical drop to −4.16 m s⁻¹ at the bounce; straight line from −4.16 m s⁻¹ back to 0, parallel to the first line.
    B1: straight line through origin with positive gradient (9.81 m s⁻²) to +5.94; B1: sudden change to a negative velocity of smaller magnitude at impact; B1: second line has the same gradient as the first and ends at v = 0.
Q12Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A car decelerates uniformly from 30 m s⁻¹ to rest over a distance of 75 m. How long does it take to stop?A 2.5 sB 5.0 sC 6.0 sD 10 s [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 5.0 s
    s = ½(u + v)t → t = 2s/(u + v) = 150/30 = 5.0 s. 2.5 s comes from s/u, which assumes constant speed of 30 m s⁻¹ throughout.
Q13Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A student releases a trolley from rest at the top of a ramp and measures the time t for it to travel a distance d down the ramp to a light gate. The results are:d = 0.200 m, t = 0.86 sd = 0.400 m, t = 1.20 sd = 0.600 m, t = 1.48 sd = 0.800 m, t = 1.72 sd = 1.000 m, t = 1.90 s

  1. (a) Calculate t² for each reading, giving each value to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a graph of d against t² should be a straight line through the origin, and state what the gradient represents. [2]
  3. (c) The best-fit line through the origin has gradient 0.274 m s⁻². Calculate the acceleration of the trolley. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest one significant source of error in this experiment and one improvement to reduce it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) t² / s²: 0.740, 1.44, 2.19, 2.96, 3.61
    B1 all values correct; B1 all given to 3 s.f. as asked (the raw times are recorded to 0.01 s).
  2. (b) d = ½at² with u = 0, so d ∝ t²; gradient = a/2.
    B1: d = ½at² (from rest); B1: gradient = ½a.
  3. (c) 0.548 m s⁻²
    a = 2 × gradient = 2 × 0.274 = 0.548 m s⁻² (A1).
  4. (d) e.g. trolley not released exactly from rest / starting position unclear; use a release gate or electromagnet, or use light gates and data logger to measure t.
    B1 valid source (human reaction time starting the stopwatch, trolley pushed at release, friction varies along ramp); B1 matching improvement (electronic timing with light gates, release mechanism, repeat and average).
Q14Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A car starts from rest at t = 0. Its acceleration a varies with time as follows: a = 3.0 m s⁻² from t = 0 to t = 4.0 s; a = 0 from t = 4.0 s to t = 10.0 s; a = −2.0 m s⁻² from t = 10.0 s to t = 13.0 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the velocity of the car at t = 4.0 s and at t = 13.0 s. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the total distance travelled by the car in the 13.0 s. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12 m s⁻¹ and 6.0 m s⁻¹
    Velocity change = area under a–t graph (M1): 3.0 × 4.0 = 12 m s⁻¹; 12 − 2.0 × 3.0 = 6.0 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 123 m
    0–4 s: ½ × 3 × 16 = 24 m; 4–10 s: 12 × 6 = 72 m (M1); 10–13 s: 12×3 − ½×2×9 = 27 m (M1); total 123 m (A1).
Q15Equations of motion & motion graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A car decelerating uniformly in a straight line passes point P at 28 m s⁻¹ and point Q, 120 m further on, at 20 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the deceleration of the car. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the time taken to travel from P to Q. [2]
  3. (c) The car continues to decelerate at the same rate after Q. Calculate how much further it travels before it stops. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.6 m s⁻²
    v² = u² + 2as: 400 = 784 − 2a × 120 (M1); a = 1.6 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 5.0 s
    t = 2s/(u + v) = 240/48 (M1) = 5.0 s (A1).
  3. (c) 125 m
    0 = 20² − 2 × 1.6 × s (M1); s = 125 m (A1).
Q16Equations of motion & motion graphs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A student drops a small stone from rest at the top of a deep well in a village in Sindh. She hears the splash 2.40 s after release. Take g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Assume the sound reaches the student instantly. Calculate the depth of the water surface below the top of the well. [2]
  2. (b) The speed of sound in air is 340 m s⁻¹. Taking this into account, calculate the depth of the water surface. [3]
  3. (c) State one other assumption made in your calculations and say whether it makes your answer to (b) too large or too small. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 28.3 m
    s = ½gt² (M1) = ½ × 9.81 × 2.40² = 28.3 m (A1).
  2. (b) 26.5 m
    Let fall time = t. t + ½gt²/340 = 2.40 (M1). Solve the quadratic 0.014426t² + t − 2.40 = 0 (M1): t = 2.32 s, so depth = ½ × 9.81 × t² = 26.5 m (A1).
  3. (c) Air resistance on the stone is negligible; ignoring it makes the calculated depth too large.
    B1: air resistance ignored → stone actually falls more slowly, so true depth is less than calculated (calculated value too large). Accept also reaction time of student with a valid direction.
Q17Equations of motion & motion graphs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A bus moves along a straight road at a constant speed of 18 m s⁻¹. As it passes a stationary police car, the police car sets off from rest in the same direction with a constant acceleration of 2.4 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the time taken for the police car to draw level with the bus. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the distance travelled by each vehicle in this time. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the police car when it draws level, and comment on how it compares with the speed of the bus. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15 s
    Same displacement: 18t = ½ × 2.4 × t² (M1); t ≠ 0 so t = 2×18/2.4 (M1) = 15 s (A1).
  2. (b) 270 m
    18 × 15 = 270 m (A1).
  3. (c) 36 m s⁻¹, twice the speed of the bus
    v = at = 2.4 × 15 = 36 m s⁻¹ (A1); exactly twice 18 m s⁻¹ because the average speed of the car (½v) must equal 18 m s⁻¹ (B1).
Q18Equations of motion & motion graphs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 12 m s⁻¹ from a balcony 15 m above the ground. It misses the balcony on the way down and lands on the ground. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum height of the ball above the ground. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the time from release until the ball hits the ground. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the ball just before it hits the ground. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 22.3 m
    Rise above balcony = u²/2g = 144/19.62 = 7.34 m (M1); height = 15 + 7.34 = 22.3 m (A1).
  2. (b) 3.36 s
    Up positive: −15 = 12t − 4.905t² (M1); 4.905t² − 12t − 15 = 0 (M1); positive root t = 3.36 s (A1).
  3. (c) 20.9 m s⁻¹
    v² = u² + 2as = 12² + 2 × 9.81 × 15 (M1); v = 20.9 m s⁻¹ (A1).
Q19Equations of motion & motion graphs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A metro train runs between two stations 3.60 km apart on a straight track. It starts and ends at rest. Its maximum acceleration is 0.80 m s⁻², its maximum deceleration is 1.2 m s⁻² and its top speed is 25 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the distance travelled while the train accelerates to its top speed and the distance travelled while it decelerates. [2]
  2. (b) Hence calculate the minimum time for the journey. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 391 m and 260 m
    d = v²/2a: 625/1.6 = 391 m (A1); 625/2.4 = 260 m (A1).
  2. (b) 170 s
    Acceleration time 31.3 s, deceleration time 20.8 s (M1); cruising distance 3600 − 651 = 2949 m → 118.0 s (M1); total 170 s (A1).
Q20Equations of motion & motion graphs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A model rocket is launched vertically from rest at a school science fair. It accelerates uniformly upwards at 15 m s⁻² for 6.0 s, when its fuel runs out. It then moves freely under gravity. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the speed and the height of the rocket when the fuel runs out. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum height reached by the rocket. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the total time from launch until the rocket hits the ground. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 90 m s⁻¹ at 270 m
    v = 15 × 6.0 = 90 m s⁻¹ (A1); h = ½ × 15 × 36 = 270 m (A1).
  2. (b) 683 m
    Extra rise = 90²/(2 × 9.81) = 413 m (M1); total 683 m (A1).
  3. (c) 27.0 s
    Time to top after burn-out = 90/9.81 = 9.17 s (M1); fall from 683 m: t = √(2 × 683/9.81) = 11.8 s (M1); total 6.0 + 9.17 + 11.8 = 27.0 s (A1).
Q21Projectile motion · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A boy on a cliff at Gwadar throws a stone horizontally at 8.0 m s⁻¹ from a point 20 m above the sea. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the time taken for the stone to reach the sea. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the horizontal distance from the foot of the cliff to where the stone lands. [1]
  3. (c) State why the horizontal component of the stone's velocity stays constant. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.02 s
    Vertical: 20 = ½ × 9.81 × t² (M1); t = 2.02 s (A1).
  2. (b) 16.2 m
    8.0 × 2.02 = 16.2 m (A1).
  3. (c) There is no horizontal force (air resistance negligible), so no horizontal acceleration.
    B1: no horizontal force / gravity acts vertically only.
Q22Projectile motion · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A ball is projected at an angle above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible. Which quantity remains constant during its flight?A its kinetic energyB its speedC the horizontal component of its velocityD the vertical component of its velocity [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C the horizontal component of its velocity
    Gravity acts only vertically, so the horizontal velocity component does not change. The vertical component changes at 9.81 m s⁻² each second, so speed and kinetic energy also change.
Q23Projectile motion · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A footballer kicks a ball from the ground at 15 m s⁻¹ at 30° above the horizontal. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) State the magnitude and direction of the acceleration of the ball at its highest point. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the time taken for the ball to reach its highest point. [1]
  4. (d) State the speed of the ball at its highest point. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9.81 m s⁻² vertically downwards
    B1: 9.81 m s⁻² (g), downwards.
  2. (b) 13.0 m s⁻¹ and 7.5 m s⁻¹
    15 cos 30° = 13.0 m s⁻¹ (A1); 15 sin 30° = 7.5 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 0.765 s
    t = uᵧ/g = 7.5/9.81 = 0.765 s (A1).
  4. (d) 13.0 m s⁻¹
    Vertical component zero, so speed = horizontal component = 13.0 m s⁻¹ (B1).
Q24Projectile motion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

During a cricket match in Rawalpindi a batter hits the ball from ground level at 25 m s⁻¹ at 35° above the horizontal. Treat the ball as starting at ground level and ignore air resistance; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the horizontal and vertical components of the initial velocity. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum height reached by the ball. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the horizontal distance travelled before the ball returns to ground level. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20.5 m s⁻¹ and 14.3 m s⁻¹
    25 cos 35° = 20.5 m s⁻¹ (A1); 25 sin 35° = 14.3 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 10.5 m
    0 = 14.3² − 2 × 9.81 × h (M1); h = 10.5 m (A1).
  3. (c) 59.9 m
    Time of flight = 2 × 14.34/9.81 = 2.92 s (M1); range = 20.48 × 2.92 = 59.9 m (A1).
Q25Projectile motion · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Ball X is dropped from rest from the edge of a high table. At the same instant ball Y, of twice the mass, is projected horizontally at 3 m s⁻¹ from the same height. Air resistance is negligible. Which statement is correct?A X reaches the floor first because it travels a shorter distance.B Y reaches the floor first because it has a greater mass.C Both reach the floor at the same time, with the same speed.D Both reach the floor at the same time, but Y has the greater speed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D Both reach the floor at the same time, but Y has the greater speed.
    Both have zero initial vertical velocity and acceleration g, so equal fall times and equal vertical speeds on landing. Y also has 3 m s⁻¹ horizontally, so its speed is greater. C forgets the horizontal component.
Q26Projectile motion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A decorative fountain in a park in Islamabad has a nozzle that sends water out at 9.0 m s⁻¹ at 60° above the horizontal. Treat the water as a projectile with no air resistance; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum height of the water above the nozzle. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the horizontal distance from the nozzle to where the water returns to the level of the nozzle. [3]
  3. (c) The nozzle angle is changed to 30° with the same speed. State, with a reason, how the horizontal distance in (b) changes. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.10 m
    uᵧ = 9.0 sin 60° = 7.79 m s⁻¹ (M1); h = uᵧ²/2g = 3.10 m (A1).
  2. (b) 7.15 m
    Time = 2uᵧ/g = 1.59 s (M1); uₓ = 9.0 cos 60° = 4.50 m s⁻¹ (M1); distance 7.15 m (A1).
  3. (c) It stays the same, because sin(2 × 30°) = sin(2 × 60°).
    B1: range ∝ sin 2θ and sin 60° = sin 120°, so same range (but lower maximum height).
Q27Projectile motion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A marble rolls off the edge of a horizontal laboratory bench of height 0.90 m and lands on the floor 1.10 m horizontally from the edge of the bench. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the time taken for the marble to fall to the floor. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the marble as it leaves the table. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the marble just before it hits the floor. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.428 s
    0.90 = ½ × 9.81 × t² (M1); t = 0.428 s (A1).
  2. (b) 2.57 m s⁻¹
    1.10/0.428 = 2.57 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 4.92 m s⁻¹
    vᵧ = 9.81 × 0.428 = 4.20 m s⁻¹ (M1); √(2.57² + 4.20²) = 4.92 m s⁻¹ (A1).
Q28Projectile motion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

An athlete releases a javelin at a height of 1.8 m above level ground with a speed of 28 m s⁻¹ at 40° above the horizontal. Treat the javelin as a particle; ignore air resistance unless told otherwise; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the time for which the javelin is in the air. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the horizontal distance travelled. [2]
  3. (c) In reality air resistance acts on the javelin. State and explain two ways in which the actual path differs from the path you have calculated. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.77 s
    uᵧ = 28 sin 40° = 18.0 m s⁻¹ (B1); −1.8 = 18.0t − 4.905t² (M1); positive root t = 3.77 s (A1).
  2. (b) 80.8 m
    uₓ = 28 cos 40° = 21.4 m s⁻¹ (M1); 21.4 × 3.77 = 80.8 m (A1).
  3. (c) Lower maximum height and shorter range; the path is not symmetrical — it falls more steeply after the highest point.
    B1 each for any two: range less (horizontal velocity decreases); maximum height lower (extra downward force while rising); highest point reached sooner / path asymmetric, descending more steeply.
Q29Projectile motion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A basketball player releases a ball 2.00 m above the floor at 50° above the horizontal. The centre of the hoop is 3.05 m above the floor and 6.00 m horizontally from the release point. Ignore air resistance and the size of the ball; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Show that the equation of the path is y = x tan θ − gx²/(2u² cos² θ), where y is the height above the release point after a horizontal distance x. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the launch speed needed for the ball to pass through the centre of the hoop. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the time the ball takes to reach the hoop. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = (u cos θ)t, y = (u sin θ)t − ½gt²; eliminate t.
    M1: t = x/(u cos θ); A1: substitute into y = u sin θ t − ½gt² to give the result.
  2. (b) 8.37 m s⁻¹
    y = 1.05 m, x = 6.0 m, θ = 50° (M1); 1.05 = 7.151 − 9.81×36/(2u² × 0.4132) (M1); u = 8.37 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 1.12 s
    t = x/(u cos 50°) = 6.0/(8.37 × 0.643) = 1.12 s (A1).
Q30Projectile motion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

During flood relief work, a light aircraft flying horizontally at 60 m s⁻¹ at a height of 245 m releases a food package so that it lands on a marked target. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the horizontal distance before the target at which the package must be released. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the package just before it lands and the angle its velocity makes with the horizontal. [3]
  3. (c) Describe the position of the package relative to the aircraft as it falls, assuming the aircraft keeps the same velocity and air resistance is negligible. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 424 m
    t = √(2H/g) = √(490/9.81) = 7.07 s (M1, A1); distance = 60 × 7.07 = 424 m (A1).
  2. (b) 91.7 m s⁻¹ at 49.1° below the horizontal
    vᵧ = gt = 69.3 m s⁻¹ (M1); speed = √(60² + 69.3²) = 91.7 m s⁻¹ (A1); tan α = 69.3/60 → α = 49.1° (A1).
  3. (c) It stays vertically below the aircraft.
    B1: same horizontal velocity as the aircraft, so always directly below it.
