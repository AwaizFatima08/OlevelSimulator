Q1Photons & photoelectric effect · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by a photon. [1]
  2. (b) A green laser pointer emits light of wavelength 532 nm. Calculate the energy of one photon in joules. [2]
  3. (c) Express this energy in electronvolts. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A discrete quantum (packet) of electromagnetic energy.
    B1
  2. (b) 3.74 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
    C1 E = hc/λ; A1 3.74 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
  3. (c) 2.34 eV
    3.74 × 10⁻¹⁹/1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 2.34 eV (A1)
Q2Photons & photoelectric effect · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define work function. [1]
  2. (b) Define threshold frequency. [1]
  3. (c) The work function of zinc is 4.30 eV. Calculate its threshold frequency. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The minimum energy needed to remove an electron from the surface of the metal.
    B1
  2. (b) The minimum frequency of electromagnetic radiation that causes electrons to be emitted from the surface.
    B1
  3. (c) 1.04 × 10¹⁵ Hz
    C1 φ = hf₀ with φ = 4.30 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; A1 1.04 × 10¹⁵ Hz

  2. (b) Show that the energy E of a photon is related to its momentum p by E = pc, and use this to find the photon energy. [2]

    C1 p = h/λ; A1 1.02 × 10⁻²⁷ N s

    B1 derivation; B1 value
Q4Photons & photoelectric effect · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe three observations from photoelectric emission experiments that cannot be explained by a wave theory of light. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how the photon model accounts for the existence of a threshold frequency. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three: no emission below a threshold frequency however intense the light; emission is instantaneous even at low intensity; the maximum kinetic energy of the electrons depends on frequency, not intensity; increasing intensity increases the rate of emission only.
    B1 each, max 3
  2. (b) Each electron absorbs the energy of one photon, E = hf; if hf is less than the work function no electron can escape, however many photons arrive.
    B1 one photon to one electron; B1 hf < φ means no emission

Q7Photons & photoelectric effect · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Ultraviolet radiation of wavelength 250 nm falls on a clean potassium surface. The work function of potassium is 2.30 eV.

  1. (a) Calculate the energy of each photon in eV. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum kinetic energy of the emitted electrons in eV. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the maximum speed of the emitted electrons. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why most emitted electrons have less than this kinetic energy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.97 eV
    C1 E = hc/λ = 7.96 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; A1 4.97 eV
  2. (b) 2.67 eV
    KE_max = 4.97 − 2.30 = 2.67 eV (A1)
  3. (c) 9.69 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹
    C1 ½mv² = 4.28 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; A1 v = 9.69 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹
  4. (d) Electrons below the surface lose energy (in collisions) before escaping, or need more than the work function to escape.
    B1
Q8Photons & photoelectric effect · Standard · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

In a photoelectric experiment, light of different frequencies f falls on a metal surface, and the maximum kinetic energy KE_max of the emitted electrons is measured in electronvolts.f / 10¹⁴ Hz | KE_max / eV6.0 | 0.257.0 | 0.648.0 | 1.069.0 | 1.5010.0 | 1.88

  1. (a) Show that a graph of KE_max (in eV) against f should be a straight line, and state expressions for its gradient and its intercept on the KE_max axis. [2]
  2. (b) Using the first and last rows (or a best-fit line), determine a value for the Planck constant. [2]
  3. (c) Determine the work function of the metal in eV. [2]
  4. (d) Determine the threshold frequency. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) hf = φ + KE_max, so KE_max = (h/e)f − φ/e with KE in eV; gradient = h/e, intercept = −φ (in eV).
    B1 rearranged equation; B1 gradient h/e and intercept −φ/e
  2. (b) h ≈ 6.52 × 10⁻³⁴ J s (accept 6.4–6.9 × 10⁻³⁴)
    M1 gradient (in V s) × e; A1 h in range with unit
  3. (c) φ ≈ 2.19 eV (accept 2.1–2.4 eV)
    M1 φ = hf/e − KE_max for a point on the line (or −intercept); A1 φ
  4. (d) ≈ 5.39 × 10¹⁴ Hz (accept 5.3–5.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz)
    f₀ = φ/h, or the intercept on the f axis: 6.0 × 10¹⁴ − 0.25/(gradient 4.075 × 10⁻¹⁵ V s) (A1, consistent with (b) and (c))

Q12Photons & photoelectric effect · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Graphs of the maximum kinetic energy KE_max of photoelectrons against frequency f are plotted for two metals, P and Q. Both are straight lines. The line for P meets the frequency axis at 5.5 × 10¹⁴ Hz and the line for Q meets it at 8.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz.

  1. (a) Explain why the two lines are parallel. [1]

  3. (c) State the intercept that each line would make if extended to the KE_max axis. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The gradient of each line equals the Planck constant h (or h/e if KE_max is in eV), which is the same for all metals.
    B1
  2. (b) P: 2.28 eV; Q: 3.32 eV
    C1 φ = hf₀; A1 both
  3. (c) −2.28 eV for P and −3.32 eV for Q (minus the work function)
    B1

Q15Photons & photoelectric effect · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Photons carry momentum as well as energy. Solar sails use this to propel spacecraft.

  1. (a) Show that the force exerted on a surface that absorbs all the light falling on it is P/c, where P is the power of the light. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the force on a 1.0 m² black panel facing the Sun just outside the Earth's atmosphere, where the intensity is 1.36 kW m⁻². [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the force if the panel is replaced by a perfect mirror, and explain the difference. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each photon has momentum E/c; the photons arriving per second carry momentum P/c per second; when absorbed, rate of change of momentum = force = P/c.
    B1 p = E/c per photon; B1 rate of change of momentum = P/c
  2. (b) 4.53 × 10⁻⁶ N
    F = 1360/3.00 × 10⁸ (A1)
  3. (c) 9.07 × 10⁻⁶ N; each photon's momentum is reversed, so its change of momentum (and the force) is doubled.
    B1 value; B1 reason

  2. (b) White light passes through a cool gas. Explain why the spectrum of the transmitted light contains dark lines. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Electrons in the atoms are excited to higher energy levels; they fall to lower levels, emitting photons; each photon's energy equals the difference between two discrete levels, hf = E₁ − E₂, so only certain frequencies appear.
    B1 excitation; B1 de-excitation emits photon; B1 discrete levels give discrete frequencies/hf = ΔE
  2. (b) Atoms absorb photons whose energies exactly equal differences between their energy levels; these are re-emitted in all directions, so those wavelengths are missing (much weaker) in the forward beam.
    B1 absorb photons of energy = ΔE only; B1 re-emitted in all directions

Four energy levels of the hydrogen atom are −13.6 eV (ground state), −3.40 eV, −1.51 eV and −0.85 eV.

  1. (a) State the number of different photon energies that can be emitted as atoms in the −0.85 eV level return to the ground state by any route. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the shortest wavelength emitted. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the energy levels are given negative values. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6
    B1 (4→3, 4→2, 4→1, 3→2, 3→1, 2→1)
  2. (b) 9.75 × 10⁻⁸ m
    C1 ΔE = 13.6 − 0.85 = 12.75 eV; A1 9.75 × 10⁻⁸ m
  3. (c) Zero energy is defined for an electron that is free of the atom (at infinity); a bound electron has less energy than this.
    B1
Q30Energy levels & line spectra · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

An electron with kinetic energy 12.2 eV collides with a hydrogen atom in its ground state. Energy levels of hydrogen: −13.6 eV, −3.40 eV, −1.51 eV, −0.85 eV.

  1. (a) State which energy levels the hydrogen atom can be excited to by the collision. Explain your answer. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the kinetic energy of the electron after it excites an atom to the highest possible level. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the wavelengths of all the photons that could then be emitted. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) To −3.40 eV (needs 10.2 eV) or −1.51 eV (needs 12.09 eV), not −0.85 eV (needs 12.75 eV, more than 12.2 eV). Unlike photons, an electron can give up only part of its energy.
    B1 levels −3.40 and −1.51 eV; B1 12.75 eV > 12.2 eV / electron can transfer part of its energy
  2. (b) 0.11 eV
    12.2 − 12.09 = 0.11 eV (A1)
  3. (c) 1.03 × 10⁻⁷ m, 6.58 × 10⁻⁷ m and 1.22 × 10⁻⁷ m
    B1 12.09 eV → 1.03 × 10⁻⁷ m; B1 1.89 eV → 6.58 × 10⁻⁷ m; B1 10.2 eV → 1.22 × 10⁻⁷ m
