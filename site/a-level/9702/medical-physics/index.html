Q1X-rays, attenuation & CT · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how X-rays are produced in an X-ray tube. [3]
  2. (b) The accelerating potential difference is 80 kV. Calculate the minimum wavelength of the X-rays produced. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Electrons from a heated cathode are accelerated through a large p.d. to the anode; they strike a metal target and decelerate rapidly, emitting X-ray photons (braking radiation); characteristic lines come from inner-shell electron transitions in the target atoms.
    B1: electrons emitted by thermionic emission and accelerated through a high p.d. B1: electrons hit a (high-melting-point, e.g. tungsten) metal target. B1: rapid deceleration produces X-ray photons (continuous spectrum) / electron transitions in target atoms give characteristic lines.
  2. (b) 1.55 × 10⁻¹¹ m
    C1: eV = hc/λ_min (all KE of one electron → one photon). A1: λ_min = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ / (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 80 × 10³) = 1.55 × 10⁻¹¹ m.
Q2X-rays, attenuation & CT · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A parallel beam of X-rays passes through 4.0 cm of muscle. The linear attenuation coefficient of muscle for these X-rays is 0.20 cm⁻¹.

  1. (a) State what is meant by the linear attenuation coefficient µ. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the fraction of the incident intensity that emerges from the muscle. [2]
  3. (c) State one reason why a parallel beam is assumed in this calculation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The fraction (proportion) of the intensity of the beam absorbed/removed per unit thickness of the material (µ in I = I₀e^(−µx)).
    B1: fractional decrease in intensity per unit distance travelled (or the constant µ in I = I₀e^(−µx)).
  2. (b) 0.449
    C1: I/I₀ = e^(−0.20 × 4.0). A1: 0.449.
  3. (c) So that any decrease in intensity is due only to attenuation, not to the beam spreading out.
    B1: no reduction in intensity due to divergence of the beam.

Q6X-rays, attenuation & CT · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The shortest wavelength in the X-ray spectrum from a hospital X-ray tube is 2.5 × 10⁻¹¹ m. Use h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C.

Q10X-rays, attenuation & CT · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a CT scan produces a three-dimensional image of a section of the body. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest one advantage and one disadvantage of a CT scan compared with a single X-ray image. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Images of many thin slices are taken; for each slice, an X-ray tube rotates around the patient and detectors on the opposite side record the intensity from many directions; a computer calculates the attenuation for each voxel and builds an image of each slice; images of successive slices are combined to give a 3D image that can be viewed from any angle.
    B1: image of a thin slice (section) taken. B1: X-ray tube (and detectors) rotate around the patient, many directions in the plane of the slice. B1: computer deduces value for each voxel/pixel of the slice. B1: many slices combined into 3D image that can be rotated.
  2. (b) Advantage: 3D image / soft tissues and structures at different depths can be distinguished (no overlapping shadows). Disadvantage: much larger radiation dose (and higher cost, longer time).
    B1: advantage, e.g. 3D, can be viewed from any angle, better contrast for soft tissue, no overlap. B1: disadvantage, e.g. higher dose.

  3. (c) State why the gamma photons, rather than the positrons, are detected outside the body. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tumour cells are very active and absorb more glucose than normal tissue, so the tracer concentrates there.
    B1.
  2. (b) (i) so the patient does not stay radioactive for long (lower dose); (ii) so enough activity remains after production, transport and uptake to give an image.
    B1 each.
  3. (c) The positrons are annihilated within about a millimetre of the source; the gamma photons are penetrating and escape the body.
    B1.

PET scans and CT scans are often carried out together in one machine.

  1. (a) Explain the difference between the information given by a PET scan and by a CT scan. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest one advantage of combining the two scans. [1]
  3. (c) State why a β⁺ emitter is used for PET rather than an α emitter. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) PET shows where the tracer collects, i.e. the function/metabolic activity of tissue; CT shows the structure (anatomy) from differences in X-ray attenuation (density).

Q48PET scanning · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

In a PET scanner, two detectors A and B lie directly opposite each other on the ring, 0.80 m apart. Two gamma photons from one annihilation event are detected; the photon at A arrives 0.60 ns before the photon at B. Speed of gamma photons = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the distance of the annihilation point from the centre of the line joining the detectors, and state which detector it is nearer to. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why detectors on opposite sides of the ring record an event only if the two photons arrive within a few nanoseconds of each other. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9.00 cm from the centre, nearer detector A
    C1: if the event is a distance x from the centre, one photon travels x further than the other: path difference = 2x. C1: 2x = cΔt = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 0.60 × 10⁻⁹. A1: x = 9.00 cm, nearer A (which receives first).
  2. (b) Photons from the same annihilation arrive almost simultaneously; requiring coincidence rejects unrelated photons (from different events or scattered photons), so only genuine pairs define a line.
    B1: photons from one annihilation reach the detectors at nearly the same time (maximum difference ~ ring diameter/c). B1: rejects photons from separate events / scatter.

A patient is injected with a fluorine-18 glucose tracer of activity 370 MBq. The half-life of fluorine-18 is 110 minutes.

  1. (a) Calculate the activity of the tracer 75 minutes after injection, when the scan begins. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of fluorine-18 nuclei in the dose at the time of injection. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the patient is asked to rest quietly between injection and scan when imaging glucose uptake in the brain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 231 MBq
    C1: A = A₀(½)^(t/t½). A1: A = 370 × (½)^(75/110) = 231 MBq.

Q50PET scanning · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A positron is emitted with kinetic energy 0.25 MeV. Usually it slows to rest before annihilating, but suppose instead that it annihilates with a stationary electron while still moving with this kinetic energy. The rest energy of an electron and of a positron is 0.511 MeV.

  1. (a) Calculate the total energy of the gamma radiation produced. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why, in this case, the two photons are not emitted exactly in opposite directions. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest how this affects the image. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.27 MeV
    Total = 2 × 0.511 + 0.25 (M1) = 1.27 MeV (A1).
  2. (b) The positron has momentum before the annihilation; momentum is conserved, so the photons' momenta must add up to this non-zero value and cannot be exactly equal and opposite.
    B1: initial momentum not zero. B1: conservation of momentum → resultant photon momentum not zero.
  3. (c) The line joining the detected photons may not pass exactly through the point of annihilation, so the image is slightly blurred.
    B1.
Q51PET scanning · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

In a time-of-flight PET scanner, the difference in arrival times of the two photons at opposite detectors is used to locate the annihilation along the line joining them. The speed of the photons is 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that a point a distance x from the midpoint of the line gives a time difference Δt = 2x/c. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the timing precision needed to locate the point to within 5.0 mm along the line. [1]
  3. (c) A scanner has a timing precision of 400 ps. Calculate the uncertainty in position along the line and suggest how a sharp image is still produced. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) One photon travels (L/2 − x), the other (L/2 + x); path difference = 2x, so Δt = 2x/c.
    M1: path difference 2x. A1.
  2. (b) 3.33 × 10⁻¹¹ s
    Δt = 2 × 5.0 × 10⁻³/3.00 × 10⁸ = 3.33 × 10⁻¹¹ s (A1).
  3. (c) 0.060 m; many lines of response through each point are combined (by computer) and the point is where they intersect.
    x = cΔt/2 = 0.060 m (A1). B1: many lines from different directions intersect.

Q55PET scanning · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

In a PET scan the positron and electron are at rest when they annihilate. Mass of electron = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; speed of light = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the momentum of each gamma photon. [2]
  2. (b) Each decay produces one positron, which annihilates. Calculate the total power emitted as gamma radiation when the activity of the tracer in the body is 200 MBq. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why it is an advantage that gamma photons of this energy are produced, compared with lower-energy photons. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.73 × 10⁻²² N s
    C1: p = E/c = m_ec²/c = m_ec. A1: p = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 3.00 × 10⁸ = 2.73 × 10⁻²² N s.
  2. (b) 3.28 × 10⁻⁵ W

Q56PET scanning · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A PET tracer uses fluorine-18, half-life 110 minutes.

  1. (a) The tracer is produced in a cyclotron and takes 2.5 hours to reach the hospital and be injected. Calculate the activity it must have when it leaves the cyclotron so that 370 MBq is available at injection. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest one problem with using a positron emitter with a half-life of only 2 minutes, and one problem with a half-life of 2 days. [2]
  3. (c) State what is meant by annihilation in terms of mass and energy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 952 MBq
    C1: A = A₀(½)^(t/t½), so A₀ = A × 2^(t/t½). C1: t = 150 min, t/t½ = 1.36. A1: A₀ = 370 × 2^(150/110) = 952 MBq.
  2. (b) 2 minutes: decays before it can be transported and absorbed by the tissue (needs a cyclotron on site); 2 days: remains active in the body long after the scan, giving the patient an unnecessary dose.
    B1: very short: too little activity left by the time of the scan / needs on-site production. B1: long: prolonged exposure / higher dose to patient (and others).
  3. (c) A particle meets its antiparticle and their total mass (together with their kinetic energy) is converted into energy in the form of photons (two γ-ray photons).
    B1: particle and antiparticle; all mass converted to photon energy.
