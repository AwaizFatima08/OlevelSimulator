Q1Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by the internal energy of a system. [2]
  2. (b) State the first law of thermodynamics, explaining the meaning of each symbol in ΔU = q + W. [2]
  3. (c) State how the first law is related to the principle of conservation of energy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The sum of a random distribution of kinetic and potential energies of the molecules in the system.
    B1 sum of kinetic and potential energies of molecules; B1 random distribution.
  2. (b) The increase in internal energy ΔU of a system equals the thermal energy q supplied to the system plus the work W done on the system.
    B1 ΔU = increase in internal energy; B1 q = heating of system, W = work done on system.
  3. (c) It is a statement of conservation of energy: the energy of the system changes only by the energy transferred to it by heating and by work.
    B1.
Q2Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A gas in a cylinder absorbs 500 J of thermal energy and, at the same time, does 200 J of work on its surroundings as it expands. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas. [2]
  2. (b) The gas is ideal. State and explain what happens to its temperature. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) +300 J (an increase)
    q = +500 J, W = −200 J (work done by gas) (M1); ΔU = 500 − 200 = +300 J (A1).
  2. (b) It rises: for an ideal gas the internal energy is the total kinetic energy of the molecules, which is proportional to the thermodynamic temperature.
    B1 temperature increases; B1 internal energy of ideal gas is only random KE ∝ T.
Q3Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A gas is compressed at a constant pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa from a volume of 2.5 × 10⁻³ m³ to 1.5 × 10⁻³ m³. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done on the gas. [2]
  2. (b) During the compression 40 J of thermal energy is lost from the gas. Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 100 J
    W = pΔV (M1) = 1.0 × 10⁵ × (2.5 − 1.5) × 10⁻³ = 100 J, done on the gas (A1).
  2. (b) +60 J
    q = −40 J, W = +100 J (M1); ΔU = −40 + 100 = +60 J (A1).
Q4Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the internal energy of an ideal gas consists only of the kinetic energy of its molecules. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a change in internal energy of an ideal gas is proportional to the change in its thermodynamic temperature. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) There are no intermolecular forces in an ideal gas (except during collisions), so the molecules have no potential energy; the internal energy is the random kinetic energy only.
    B1 no intermolecular forces; B1 so potential energy is zero.
  2. (b) Mean kinetic energy of a molecule = 3/2 kT, so the total kinetic energy of N molecules is 3/2 NkT; ΔU = 3/2 NkΔT ∝ ΔT.
    B1 mean KE ∝ T (= 3/2 kT); B1 U = N × mean KE so ΔU ∝ ΔT.
Q5Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A block of ice is melting in a glass of water at 0 °C on a summer afternoon in Lahore.

  1. (a) State what happens to the kinetic energy and to the potential energy of the molecules while the ice melts. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the increase in internal energy when 0.25 kg of ice melts at 0 °C. Assume the work done is negligible. (specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹) [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the internal energy increases even though the temperature does not. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kinetic energy stays the same (constant temperature); potential energy increases (bonds broken / molecules move further apart in the disordered liquid).
    B1 KE constant; B1 PE increases.
  2. (b) 8.35 × 10⁴ J
    q = mL = 0.25 × 3.34 × 10⁵ (M1); W ≈ 0, so ΔU = q = 8.35 × 10⁴ J (A1).
  3. (c) Internal energy includes potential energy, and this increases during melting.
    B1.

  1. (a) For each process, state whether q, W and ΔU for the gas are positive, negative or zero:(i) an ideal gas is heated in a sealed rigid container;(ii) an ideal gas expands slowly at constant temperature;(iii) an ideal gas is compressed so quickly that no thermal energy is transferred. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) q +, W 0, ΔU +; (ii) q +, W −, ΔU 0; (iii) q 0, W +, ΔU +.
    2 marks per process (all three signs correct 2 marks; two correct 1 mark): (i) volume constant so W = 0, ΔU = q > 0; (ii) T constant so ΔU = 0, gas does work so W < 0, hence q = −W > 0; (iii) q = 0, work done on gas W > 0, so ΔU > 0.

Q13Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Student A says: "When a gas is held at constant volume, no work is done, so its internal energy cannot change." Evaluate this statement. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why an ideal gas heated at constant volume gets hotter. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The statement is incorrect. At constant volume W = 0, so ΔU = q: supplying thermal energy (q > 0) still increases the internal energy.

  2. (b) The increase in internal energy is the increase in the kinetic energy of the molecules, which for an ideal gas is proportional to T, so T increases.
    1 mark for internal energy increase = kinetic energy increase; 1 mark for U ∝ T.

Q17Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain, in terms of atoms, why the internal energy of a solid increases when it is heated at constant volume. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The atoms vibrate about their fixed positions. Heating increases the average kinetic energy of the vibrations, and the mean separations and interatomic forces change so the average potential energy of the atoms also increases.
    1 mark for increase in average kinetic energy of vibration; 1 mark for potential energy of atoms increasing; 1 mark for internal energy being the sum of both.

    B1 X: ΔU = 0 so q = −W (heat in balances work out); B1 Y: q = 0, W < 0 so ΔU < 0; B1 Y cooler; B1 same V so lower T means lower p (pV = nRT).
  2. (b) X: its pressure is higher at every volume during the expansion, so the area under its p–V curve is larger.
    B1 X; B1 its curve lies above Y's curve, so greater area = greater work.
