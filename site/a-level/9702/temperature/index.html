Q1Specific heat capacity & specific latent heat · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

An electric kettle of power 2.2 kW contains 1.5 kg of water at 22 °C. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

  1. (a) Define specific heat capacity. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the energy needed to heat the water to 100 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the minimum time taken, assuming no energy is lost. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The energy needed per unit mass to raise the temperature of a substance by one kelvin (1 °C).
    B1
  2. (b) 4.91 × 10⁵ J
    C1 Q = mcΔθ = 1.5 × 4200 × 78; A1 4.91 × 10⁵ J
  3. (c) 223 s
    t = Q/P = 223 s (A1)

Q3Specific heat capacity & specific latent heat · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain, in terms of molecules, why the temperature of a pure substance stays constant while it melts, even though energy is being supplied. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the specific latent heat of vaporisation of a substance is much greater than its specific latent heat of fusion. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The energy supplied increases the potential energy of the molecules (breaking bonds / increasing separation), not their kinetic energy; temperature depends on the mean kinetic energy, so it stays constant.
    B1 energy increases potential energy of molecules; B1 mean kinetic energy (temperature) unchanged
  2. (b) On boiling the molecules are separated completely (to large distances), needing much more energy than the small increase in separation on melting; work is also done pushing back the atmosphere as the volume increases greatly.
    B1 much greater increase in separation / all bonds broken; B1 work done against atmosphere on expansion

  1. (a) Calculate the energy supplied by the heater. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the specific heat capacity of aluminium obtained from these results. [2]
  3. (c) The accepted value is about 900 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹. Explain why the experimental value is larger, and suggest one improvement. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12600 J
    E = VIt = 12.0 × 3.50 × 300 (A1)
  2. (b) 1000 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹

A rooftop solar water heater in Lahore has a collecting area of 2.4 m². At noon it receives 650 W of solar radiation per square metre, and 55 % of this is transferred to water flowing through the panel at a steady rate of 0.012 kg s⁻¹. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the useful power absorbed by the water. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the rise in temperature of the water as it passes through the panel. [2]
  3. (c) State and explain how the temperature rise would change if the flow rate were halved, with the same absorbed power. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 858 W
    650 × 2.4 × 0.55 = 858 W (A1)
  2. (b) 17.0 K

    B1

Q14Specific heat capacity & specific latent heat · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) Explain why two different heater powers are used. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the specific latent heat of vaporisation of the liquid. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the rate of energy loss to the surroundings. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The rate of energy loss is the same in both runs (same temperature), so subtracting the two results eliminates the energy lost.
    B1
  2. (b) 2.25 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹

  3. (c) 3.5 W
    M1 50 × 300 = 6.2 × 10⁻³ × 2.25 × 10⁶ + 300h; A1 h = 3.5 W
