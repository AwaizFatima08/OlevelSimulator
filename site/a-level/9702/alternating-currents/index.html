Q1Rectification & smoothing · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by rectification. [1]
  2. (b) A single diode is connected in series with a resistor across a sinusoidal a.c. supply. Describe the variation with time of the pd across the resistor. [2]
  3. (c) State the name given to this type of rectification and one disadvantage of it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The conversion of an alternating current (or voltage) into a direct current (one-directional).
    B1: a.c. → d.c. (current in one direction only).
  2. (b) Positive half-cycles of the sine wave appear; during the negative half-cycles the pd is zero.
    B1: half-cycles of one polarity only (same shape as input); B1: zero for alternate half-cycles (diode reverse-biased).
  3. (c) Half-wave rectification; output is zero for half the time (large ripple, less power).
    B1: half-wave; B1: no output for half of each cycle / mean power only half / needs large smoothing capacitor.
Q2Rectification & smoothing · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Four ideal diodes D1–D4 form a bridge rectifier. The a.c. supply is connected between points P and Q. A load resistor R is connected between points X and Y. The diodes are connected as follows: D1 from P to X, D2 from Q to X, D3 from Y to Q, D4 from Y to P (each diode conducts in the direction stated, e.g. D1 allows current from P to X only).

  1. (a) During the half-cycle when P is positive with respect to Q, state which two diodes conduct, and the direction of the current in R (from X to Y or from Y to X). [2]
  2. (b) During the next half-cycle, when Q is positive with respect to P, state which two diodes conduct and the direction of the current in R. [2]
  3. (c) State the name of this type of rectification and describe the waveform of the pd across R. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) D1 and D3; from X to Y
    B1: D1 (P→X) and D3 (Y→Q); B1: current X to Y.
  2. (b) D2 and D4; still from X to Y
    B1: D2 (Q→X) and D4 (Y→P); B1: current still X to Y.
  3. (c) Full-wave rectification; a series of positive humps with no gaps — every half-cycle appears with the same polarity.
    B1: full-wave; B1: both halves of each cycle give output of the same polarity (|sin| shape).
Q3Rectification & smoothing · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the condition relating the time constant RC of the smoothing circuit to the period T of the a.c. supply for good smoothing. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why this condition is needed. [2]
  3. (c) For a 50 Hz half-wave supply and a load of 2.0 kΩ, suggest a suitable minimum capacitance, explaining your choice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) RC must be much greater than T.
    B1: RC ≫ T.
  2. (b) So that only a small fraction of the charge leaks away between peaks, keeping the fall in pd (ripple) small.
    B1: capacitor discharges very little in one period; B1: pd remains close to peak value — small ripple.
  3. (c) About 100 µF or more (RC = 0.2 s, ten times the 20 ms period).
    B1: period 20 ms; B1: choose C so RC ≥ about 10T, e.g. C ≥ 100 µF (accept other justified values).
Q4Rectification & smoothing · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) One diode in a bridge rectifier breaks and becomes an open circuit. Describe and explain the effect on the output waveform. [2]
  2. (b) State the effect of this fault on the ripple of the smoothed output, giving a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The output becomes half-wave: one set of half-cycles is lost because the current path for those half-cycles goes through the broken diode.
    B1: half-wave output (every other hump missing); B1: in that half-cycle the conducting pair includes the faulty diode, so no complete circuit.
  2. (b) Ripple increases (about doubles) because the capacitor discharges for twice as long between peaks.
    B1: larger ripple with reason.

Q6Rectification & smoothing · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a mobile phone charger must rectify the mains supply before it can charge the battery. [2]
  2. (b) State one reason why a smoothing capacitor is also included. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The battery needs current in one direction to store energy chemically; alternating current would charge and discharge it in turn, giving no net charging.

  2. (b) To reduce the variation (ripple) of the output so that electronic circuits receive a nearly steady pd.
    B1: steadier d.c. / reduces ripple.

A battery charger for a 12 V car battery uses a full-wave rectifier, with ideal diodes, fed from a supply whose peak voltage is 15 V. The output is not smoothed.

Show answer
  1. (a) Both halves of each cycle are used, so the output is positive for the whole cycle, not just for half of it.
    1 mark: the full-wave output is positive for the whole cycle, so both halves are used; 1 mark: the mean is 2V₀/π, twice the half-wave mean V₀/π.

Q11Rectification & smoothing · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A capacitor is connected in parallel with the load resistor of a rectifier circuit.

  1. (a) Explain how the capacitor smooths the output pd. [3]
  2. (b) State and explain the effect on the ripple of (i) increasing the capacitance, (ii) increasing the load resistance. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) As the rectified pd rises, the capacitor charges to the peak value; when the rectified pd falls below the capacitor pd, the diode stops conducting and the capacitor discharges through the load, keeping the pd high until the next peak recharges it.
    B1: capacitor charges as rectified pd rises (to peak); B1: when input falls, capacitor discharges through the load resistor (diode reverse biased so it cannot discharge back through the supply); B1: discharge is slow (time constant ≫ period), so pd falls only a little before the next peak.
  2. (b) (i) ripple decreases; (ii) ripple decreases — both increase the time constant RC so the capacitor discharges more slowly.
    B1: both reduce ripple; B1: larger RC → slower discharge.

  3. (c) For I₀ = 4.0 A and R = 25 Ω, calculate the mean power. [1]

  2. (b) Given that the mean value of sin ωt over a cycle is zero and the mean value of sin² ωt is ½, calculate the r.m.s. value of the voltage. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the mean power this voltage would produce in a 10 Ω resistor. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The r.m.s. value depends on the mean of V², and (a + b)² ≠ a² + b² in general; the r.m.s. values do not add.

Q56Alternating current & r.m.s. values · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A sinusoidal voltage of peak value V₀ is passed through a half-wave rectifier, so the output is V₀ sin(ωt) for the positive half-cycle and zero for the negative half-cycle.

  1. (a) Show that the r.m.s. value of the output over a complete cycle is V₀/2. [3]
  2. (b) For V₀ = 16 V, calculate the mean power dissipated in a 10 Ω resistor, and compare it with the full-wave rectified output of the same peak. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) V_rms² = (1/T) ∫ v² dt. Over the positive half-cycle v² = V₀² sin²θ with mean value V₀²/2 (over π), and the negative half contributes zero. So the mean of v² over the full cycle = ½ × V₀²/2 = V₀²/4 and V_rms = V₀/2.
    1 mark for setting V_rms² as the mean of v²; 1 mark for the mean of sin² being ½ on the positive half and zero on the negative half; 1 mark for V_rms = V₀/2.
  2. (b) Half-wave: V_rms = 8.0 V, P = 64/10 = 6.4 W. Full-wave: V_rms = 16/√2 = 11.3 V, P = 12.8 W, so the half-wave power is half the full-wave power.
    1 mark for 6.4 W; 1 mark for 12.8 W for full-wave and the comparison (half).
Q57Alternating current & r.m.s. values · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A triangular alternating voltage rises linearly from zero to its peak V₀ in a quarter of a period, falls linearly back through zero to −V₀ in the next half-period, and returns to zero in the final quarter period. Its pattern repeats every period T.

  1. (a) Show that the mean value of v² over one period is V₀²/3, and hence that the r.m.s. value is V₀/√3. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the r.m.s. value for a peak of 6.0 V. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In the first quarter-period v = (4V₀/T)t, so the mean of v² over that quarter is (1/(T/4)) ∫₀^(T/4) (4V₀t/T)² dt = V₀²/3. Every quarter-period has the same mean of v², so the mean over T is V₀²/3 and V_rms = V₀/√3.
    1 mark for v = (4V₀/T)t in one quarter; 1 mark for integrating to get V₀²/3; 1 mark for V_rms = V₀/√3 with all quarters equal.
  2. (b) 3.46 V
    V_rms = 6.0 / √3 = 3.46 V. 1 mark for the method, 1 mark for the value.

A sinusoidal voltage of peak 10 V and frequency 50 Hz is sampled at intervals. The values of the voltage at times t after the start of a positive half-cycle are given below.t / ms: 0, 2.5, 5.0, 7.5, 10.0V / V: 0.00, 7.07, 10.00, 7.07, 0.00

  1. (a) Complete the table of V² for these times. [1]
  2. (b) Use the trapezium rule over the half-cycle (0 to 10.0 ms) to estimate the mean value of V². [2]
  3. (c) Hence estimate the r.m.s. voltage, and explain why this equals the r.m.s. over a full cycle. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0, 50.0, 100, 50.0, 0 V²
    B1: each V² is the square of the value in the table: 0, 50.0, 100, 50.0, 0.
  2. (b) Trapezium rule: ∫V² dt ≈ 2.5 × (0 + 50 + 100 + 50 + 0) = 500 V² ms. Mean = 500 / 10.0 = 50.0 V².
    1 mark for the trapezium sum with spacing 2.5 ms; 1 mark for dividing by 10.0 ms to give 50.0 V².
  3. (c) V_rms = √50.0 = 7.1 V. The waveform is symmetrical about zero, so the positive and negative half-cycles have the same mean of V².
    1 mark for the square root (7.1 V); 1 mark for the symmetry argument.

Q60Alternating current & r.m.s. values · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A sinusoidal voltage of peak value 325 V is passed through a full-wave rectifier with ideal diodes and no smoothing.

  1. (a) Show that the r.m.s. value of the rectified output is the same as the r.m.s. value of the original sine wave. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean value of the full-wave rectified output. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The output is |V₀ sinθ|, so its square is V₀² sin²θ, the same as for the original sine. Its mean of v² is therefore V₀²/2 and V_rms = V₀/√2 for both waveforms.
