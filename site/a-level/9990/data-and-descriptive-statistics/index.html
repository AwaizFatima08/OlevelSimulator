Q1Graphs and tables · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A hypothetical study in a Lahore college tested whether background music affects revision. Students learned a list of 30 words either with pop music playing or in silence and then recalled as many words as possible.Condition | Mean number of words recalledMusic | 14.2Silence | 17.6

  1. (a) Name the type of graph that should be used to display the results in the table. Give one reason for your choice. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest a suitable title, a label for the x-axis and a label for the y-axis for this graph. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the difference between the two means. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A bar chart, because the IV (music or silence) is made of separate categories, and the bars show the mean for each condition.
    1 mark: bar chart; 1 mark: reason – discrete/categorical conditions (bars do not touch) / comparing two means.
  2. (b) Title: 'Mean number of words recalled when revising with music and in silence'. x-axis: 'Condition (music or silence)'. y-axis: 'Mean number of words recalled (out of 30)'.
    1 mark each: title mentioning both variables; x-axis = conditions of the IV; y-axis = mean words recalled (must say 'mean').
  3. (c) 3.4 words
    17.6 − 14.2 = 3.4 words (B1).

Q3Graphs and tables · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

In a hypothetical experiment, each participant chose one of two brands of juice, X or Y.   1. (a) Name the type of graph that should be used to display these data. [1]

Show answer
  1. (a) A scatter graph (scattergram).
    1 mark: scatter graph.
  2. (b) Daily screen time (hours) on one axis and sleep duration (hours) on the other, both labelled with units; each teenager plotted as a single point (no lines joining the points); a title stating both co-variables.

Q12Graphs and tables · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

In a hypothetical field experiment in a Karachi shopping mall, a confederate dropped a pile of papers near 80 passers-by. For 50 passers-by the confederate was smartly dressed and for 30 scruffily dressed. 35 people helped the smartly dressed confederate and 12 helped the scruffily dressed confederate.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of people who helped in each condition. [2]

  1. (a) Smartly dressed: 70 %; scruffily dressed: 40 %

Q13Graphs and tables · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Sana drew a bar chart of the mean test scores (out of 100) of students who revised on paper (mean 52) or on a tablet (mean 56). Her y-axis begins at 40 and has no label. The two bars touch each other and the graph has no title.

  1. (a) Identify three problems with Sana's graph. [3]

  1. (a) 1) The y-axis starts at 40, not 0, so the difference between the two conditions looks far larger than it is. 2) The y-axis has no label, so it is not clear that it shows mean scores out of 100. 3) The bars touch, which wrongly suggests continuous data, and there is no title.

Q14Graphs and tables · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A hypothetical study of 20 university students in Kuala Lumpur plotted hours of sport played per week (x-axis, 0–14) against a self-rated mood score (y-axis, 1–10). Q60Normal distribution · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the mean and standard deviation are the best summary statistics when data are normally distributed, but the median and range may be better when data are skewed. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) In a normal distribution the data are symmetrical, so the mean is the typical value (equal to the median and mode) and the SD tells you where about 68 % and 95 % of scores lie.
