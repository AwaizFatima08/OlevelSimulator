Q1Kidney & osmoregulation · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Each human kidney contains about one million nephrons.

  1. (a) Put these parts of a nephron in the order in which filtrate passes through them: distal convoluted tubule, Bowman's capsule, collecting duct, proximal convoluted tubule, loop of Henle. [2]
  2. (b) State the region of the kidney (cortex or medulla) in which the glomeruli are found and the region in which most of each loop of Henle is found. [2]
  3. (c) Name the blood vessel that brings blood to a glomerulus and the vessel that carries blood away from it. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bowman's capsule → proximal convoluted tubule → loop of Henle → distal convoluted tubule → collecting duct
    B2 all correct; B1 if one pair is reversed.
  2. (b) Glomeruli: cortex; loops of Henle: medulla.
    B1 each.
  3. (c) Afferent arteriole in; efferent arteriole out.
    B1 both needed.
Q2Kidney & osmoregulation · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Ali plays cricket for three hours in Lahore on a hot afternoon. He sweats a lot and eats salty pakoras, but drinks very little.

  1. (a) State the effect on the water potential of Ali's blood. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the changes in ADH secretion and in Ali's urine that follow. [3]
  3. (c) Ali then drinks a large glass of water. State how the volume of his urine will change over the next hour, and why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It decreases (becomes more negative).
    B1
  2. (b) More ADH is released from the posterior pituitary; the collecting ducts become more permeable to water, so more water is reabsorbed into the blood; a small volume of concentrated (dark) urine is produced.
    B1 more ADH. B1 more water reabsorbed in collecting duct / DCT. B1 small volume, more concentrated urine.
  3. (c) The volume will increase (dilute urine); blood water potential rises, so less ADH is released and less water is reabsorbed in the collecting ducts.
    B1 more urine / dilute. B1 less ADH / collecting duct less permeable.
Q3Kidney & osmoregulation · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student writes: 'Selective reabsorption happens in Bowman’s capsule and needs no energy because it is passive.' Identify the two errors in this statement and correct them. [2]
  2. (b) Name two substances that are reabsorbed completely from the proximal convoluted tubule under normal conditions. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Error 1: reabsorption happens mainly in the proximal convoluted tubule (and the rest of the tubule), not in Bowman’s capsule. Error 2: much reabsorption is active (uses ATP), e.g. active transport of glucose and sodium ions.
    1 mark for the correct location; 1 mark for stating that the process is active and needs ATP (for example for sodium pumping or co-transport of glucose).
  2. (b) Glucose and amino acids (any two).
    1 mark each for any two of: glucose, amino acids (accept these only; urea and water are only partly reabsorbed).
Q4Kidney & osmoregulation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how ultrafiltration takes place in the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the filtrate in Bowman's capsule has a lower concentration of protein than blood plasma but the same concentration of glucose. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The afferent arteriole is wider than the efferent arteriole, so blood in the glomerulus is at high hydrostatic pressure; water and small solutes are forced out through gaps (fenestrations) in the capillary endothelium; the basement membrane acts as the filter, holding back large proteins (relative molecular mass above about 69 000) and cells; the fluid passes between the podocytes' foot processes (filtration slits) into the capsule space.
    1 mark each for any four of: afferent wider than efferent; high hydrostatic pressure; pores / fenestrations in endothelium; basement membrane is the filter; large proteins and blood cells retained; podocytes with filtration slits; filtrate contains water, glucose, urea, ions, amino acids.
  2. (b) Proteins are too large to pass through the basement membrane, so they stay in the blood; glucose molecules are small and pass freely, so their concentration is the same as in plasma.
    B1 proteins too large / retained by basement membrane. B1 glucose small, filtered freely.
Q5Kidney & osmoregulation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how glucose is reabsorbed from the filtrate in the proximal convoluted tubule. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how the structure of the proximal convoluted tubule cells is adapted for reabsorption. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Na⁺/K⁺ pumps in the basal membrane of the tubule cells actively pump Na⁺ out into the tissue fluid/blood, using ATP; this lowers the Na⁺ concentration inside the cell; Na⁺ diffuses into the cell from the lumen through cotransporter proteins that carry glucose with it (indirect active transport / cotransport); glucose then moves out of the cell into the blood by facilitated diffusion through the basal membrane.
    1 mark each for any four of: Na⁺/K⁺ pump on basal membrane; uses ATP / active; low Na⁺ concentration in cell; Na⁺ moves in by facilitated diffusion with glucose (cotransporter / symporter); glucose against its concentration gradient; glucose leaves by facilitated diffusion into capillaries.
  2. (b) Microvilli on the luminal surface give a large surface area for transport proteins; many mitochondria supply ATP for active transport.
    B1 microvilli → large surface area. B1 many mitochondria → ATP for active transport. (Also accept: many cotransporter proteins / tight junctions.)
Q6Kidney & osmoregulation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a fall in the water potential of the blood is detected and how ADH is released. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how ADH increases the permeability of the collecting duct to water. [4]
  3. (c) Explain why this is an example of negative feedback. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect the fall in water potential (they lose water and shrink); neurosecretory cells (in the hypothalamus) send impulses so ADH is released from the posterior pituitary gland into the blood.
    B1 osmoreceptors in hypothalamus. B1 ADH released from posterior pituitary.
  2. (b) ADH binds to receptors on the cell surface membrane of collecting duct cells; this activates adenylyl cyclase, producing cAMP as a second messenger; cAMP activates a protein kinase (cascade) that phosphorylates proteins; vesicles containing aquaporins move to and fuse with the luminal membrane; more aquaporins in the membrane allow more water to move out by osmosis.
    1 mark each for any four of: ADH binds to receptor (on membrane of collecting duct cells); adenylyl cyclase activated / cAMP produced (second messenger); protein kinase activated / phosphorylation; vesicles with aquaporins; vesicles fuse with luminal (cell surface) membrane; more water channels → more water reabsorbed by osmosis.
  3. (c) The change (low water potential) triggers responses that reverse it – more water is reabsorbed so blood water potential rises back to the set point; as it returns to normal, less ADH is released.
    B1 response reverses the change / returns to set point. B1 less ADH once water potential is restored.
Q7Kidney & osmoregulation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

In a healthy adult the kidneys produce glomerular filtrate at a rate of 125 cm³ per minute.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of filtrate produced per day in dm³. [1]
  2. (b) The person produces 1.5 dm³ of urine per day. Calculate the percentage of the filtrate that is reabsorbed. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why a person with kidney failure who is treated by dialysis must limit how much water and salt they consume. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 180 dm³
    125 × 60 × 24 = 180 000 cm³ = 180 dm³
  2. (b) 99.2 %
    M1 (180 − 1.5) = 178.5 dm³ reabsorbed. A1 178.5 ÷ 180 × 100 = 99.2 %.
  3. (c) Their kidneys cannot remove excess water and salt (cannot form urine normally), so these build up in the blood between dialysis sessions, raising blood volume / pressure and causing oedema.
    B1 kidneys cannot excrete excess water / salt. B1 build-up → high blood pressure / oedema / fluid overload.
Q8Kidney & osmoregulation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

In diabetes insipidus the posterior pituitary gland releases little or no ADH. A patient with this condition used a nasal spray of desmopressin, a drug that acts on the collecting duct in the same way as ADH. Her rate of urine production was measured.Time after spray / h: 0, 1, 2, 4, 8, 12Urine production / cm³ min⁻¹: 10.0, 6.5, 2.0, 1.2, 1.5, 4.0

  1. (a) Before the spray, the patient produced urine at 10.0 cm³ min⁻¹. Calculate the volume of urine she would produce in one day at this rate, in dm³. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why a person who makes no ADH produces a large volume of dilute urine. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the rate of urine production rises again between 8 h and 12 h after the spray. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 14.4 dm³
    B1 10.0 × 60 × 24 = 14 400 cm³ = 14.4 dm³
  2. (b) Without ADH, few aquaporins are inserted into the luminal membranes of the collecting duct cells (vesicles containing aquaporins stay in the cytoplasm); so the collecting duct walls have low permeability to water; little water moves out by osmosis into the medulla (even though its water potential is low), so a large volume of dilute urine passes out.
    1 mark each for any three of: no ADH binding to receptors on collecting duct cells; few aquaporins in the luminal membrane; collecting duct (and DCT) walls less permeable to water; little water reabsorbed by osmosis into the medulla / blood; large volume of dilute urine.
  3. (c) The desmopressin is gradually broken down / removed from the blood; so fewer aquaporins remain in the collecting duct membranes, less water is reabsorbed and urine output increases – another dose is needed.
    B1 drug broken down / excreted / concentration falls. B1 aquaporins removed from membrane / less water reabsorbed.
Q9Kidney & osmoregulation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A person's urine flow rate is 1.0 cm³ per minute. The inulin concentration is 25 g dm⁻³ in the urine and 0.20 g dm⁻³ in the plasma.

  1. (a) Calculate the rate of glomerular filtrate formation in cm³ min⁻¹. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why inulin is a suitable substance for this calculation. [3]
  3. (c) State why glucose concentrations could not be used in the same calculation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 125 cm³ min⁻¹ (1.0 cm³ min⁻¹ × 25 g dm⁻³ ÷ 0.20 g dm⁻³ = 125 cm³ min⁻¹).
    M1 use of GFR = urine flow × (concentration in urine ÷ concentration in plasma); A1 125 cm³ min⁻¹ with units.
  2. (b) Inulin is freely filtered at the glomerulus; it is neither reabsorbed nor secreted by the tubules, so all of it stays in the filtrate and its concentration in the urine depends only on the volume of water removed.
    1 mark each for any three of: freely filtered; not reabsorbed; not secreted; so the amount in the urine depends only on the volume of filtrate lost.
  3. (c) Glucose is reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule, so its concentration in urine is not the same as in filtrate.
    1 mark for stating that glucose is reabsorbed (so the urine concentration would not reflect the filtrate concentration).
Q10Kidney & osmoregulation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Alcohol inhibits the release of ADH from the posterior pituitary gland. Predict the effect on the volume of urine and on the water potential of the blood. Explain your prediction. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the hypothalamus and posterior pituitary restore the blood water potential when it falls below normal, for example after sweating. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Urine volume increases and the water potential of the blood falls (becomes more negative / lower); less ADH means the collecting ducts are less permeable to water, so less water is reabsorbed and the blood loses water.

  2. (b) Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect the fall in blood water potential; the hypothalamus stimulates the posterior pituitary to release more ADH, so the collecting ducts become more permeable to water, more water is reabsorbed and a small volume of concentrated urine is produced.
    1 mark each for any two of: osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect the change; more ADH released from the posterior pituitary; collecting ducts more permeable (more aquaporins); more water reabsorbed, giving small concentrated urine (negative feedback restores the water potential).
Q11Kidney & osmoregulation · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Concentrations of some substances in a healthy person (g dm⁻³):Substance — blood plasma, filtrate in Bowman's capsule, urineProtein — 80.0, 0.05, 0Glucose — 1.0, 1.0, 0Urea — 0.30, 0.30, 20.0

  2. (b) Explain why glucose is present in the filtrate but absent from the urine. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate how many times more concentrated urea is in the urine than in the plasma. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the urea concentration in urine is much higher than in the filtrate, although urea is not actively secreted into the tubule in large amounts. [2]

Q12Kidney & osmoregulation · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the loop of Henle creates a low water potential in the tissue fluid of the medulla. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how the low water potential of the medulla allows concentrated urine to be produced. [2]
  3. (c) Desert gerbils in the Thar desert produce urine far more concentrated than human urine. Suggest how the structure of their kidneys differs from human kidneys. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cells of the ascending limb actively transport Na⁺ and Cl⁻ out of the filtrate into the tissue fluid of the medulla; the ascending limb is impermeable to water, so water cannot follow; this lowers the water potential of the medulla; the descending limb is permeable to water, so water leaves it by osmosis and the filtrate becomes more concentrated as it goes down; the countercurrent arrangement builds up the highest solute concentration (lowest water potential) at the tip of the loop / deep in the medulla.
    1 mark each for any four of: Na⁺ and Cl⁻ pumped out of ascending limb (active transport); ascending limb impermeable to water; low water potential / high solute concentration in medulla tissue fluid; descending limb permeable to water, water leaves by osmosis; filtrate most concentrated at the bottom of the loop; countercurrent multiplier – opposite flow maintains gradient along the whole loop.
  2. (b) Filtrate in the collecting duct passes through the medulla; water moves out of the collecting duct by osmosis into the medulla tissue fluid (when the walls are permeable, with ADH), so the urine becomes concentrated.
    B1 collecting duct passes through medulla. B1 water leaves by osmosis down water potential gradient (when ADH present).
  3. (c) They have relatively longer loops of Henle (thicker medulla), so a steeper water potential gradient builds up and more water can be reabsorbed.
    B1 longer loops of Henle / thicker medulla.
Q13Kidney & osmoregulation · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

In an untreated diabetic patient, the glomerular filtration rate is 0.125 dm³ min⁻¹. The kidney tubules of this patient can reabsorb glucose at a maximum rate of 1.25 mmol min⁻¹. Assume the glucose concentration in the filtrate equals that in the plasma.

  1. (a) Calculate the plasma glucose concentration above which glucose will start to appear in this patient's urine. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate at which glucose is lost in the urine when the plasma glucose concentration is 15 mmol dm⁻³. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why there is a maximum rate at which glucose can be reabsorbed. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why a person with glucose in the urine often feels thirsty and produces large volumes of urine. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10 mmol dm⁻³
    1.25 ÷ 0.125 = 10 mmol dm⁻³
  2. (b) 0.625 mmol min⁻¹
    M1 filtered = 0.125 × 15 = 1.875 mmol min⁻¹. A1 1.875 − 1.25 = 0.625 mmol min⁻¹.
  3. (c) Glucose is reabsorbed by a limited number of cotransporter proteins in the PCT cell membranes; when all are working at their maximum rate (saturated), extra glucose cannot be reabsorbed.
    B1 limited number of carrier / cotransporter proteins. B1 all occupied / saturated.
  4. (d) Glucose in the filtrate lowers its water potential, so less water is reabsorbed by osmosis from the tubules; more water is lost in urine, lowering blood water potential, which stimulates thirst.
    B1 glucose in filtrate lowers water potential / less water reabsorbed by osmosis. B1 large urine volume → dehydration → thirst.
Q14Kidney & osmoregulation · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of sodium ions filtered in one day, in mol. [2]

  3. (c) Explain why so much of the filtered sodium is reabsorbed when only a small amount needs to be excreted. [2]

Q16Stomata & abscisic acid · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a stoma opens in the light. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why stomata opening in the light is an advantage to the plant. [1]
Show answer

    B1
Q17Stomata & abscisic acid · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Describe two structural features of guard cells that allow them to open and close the stoma. [2]
Show answer

  1. (a) A student finds a strong positive correlation between the number of stomata per mm² and the water loss per hour in leaves from 12 different plant species. Explain why the student cannot conclude that stomatal number causes the greater water loss. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest one investigation that could test whether stomatal number causes water loss. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The species differ in other ways (leaf size, cuticle thickness, habitat), any of which could cause both the high stomatal number and the high water loss; correlation does not prove causation.
    1 mark for correlation not proving causation; 1 mark for a confounding factor such as leaf size or cuticle thickness; 1 mark for other species differences affecting both variables.
  2. (b) Use leaves from one species and vary stomatal number (e.g. by blocking stomata with petroleum jelly on one surface) while keeping leaf size and conditions the same; compare water loss.
    1 mark for a single-species comparison; 1 mark for a controlled manipulation of stomatal number.
Q163Skill: conclusions & interpreting data · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A student plots a graph of heart rate against time after exercise. The graph shows an unexpected spike at 2 minutes. Describe how the student should decide whether the spike is an outlier, and explain whether it should be removed. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Check whether the spike is repeated in other repeats or volunteers; check the measurement for a miscount or error; an outlier may be removed only if there is a clear, recorded reason (measurement error); otherwise it is kept and reported.
    1 mark for comparing with repeats/other volunteers; 1 mark for checking for a recording or counting error; 1 mark for removing only with a valid reason and reporting the decision.
Q164Skill: conclusions & interpreting data · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student concludes from a set of biosensor readings that the glucose meter is accurate. The readings are close to each other but all are 0.6 mmol dm⁻³ above a standard solution of known concentration. Evaluate this conclusion. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The readings are precise (close together) but not accurate; they are consistently 0.6 mmol dm⁻³ too high, which indicates a systematic error such as a calibration fault; the conclusion is therefore not supported; the meter must be recalibrated and retested against several standards.
    1 mark for precision not accuracy; 1 mark for systematic error (consistently too high); 1 mark for the conclusion not being supported; 1 mark for recalibration and testing against several standards.
Q165Skill: conclusions & interpreting data · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student claims that a biosensor is more accurate than the laboratory method because the biosensor gave 5.0 mmol dm⁻³ and the laboratory gave 5.1 mmol dm⁻³ for a sample whose true value is 5.0 mmol dm⁻³. Evaluate this claim. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The claim is not supported: each method was tested on one sample only, so no repeats show precision; the difference of 0.1 mmol dm⁻³ is within the normal uncertainty of both methods; accuracy needs several repeats and standards, and a conclusion about which method is better needs the stated uncertainty of each method.
    1 mark for one reading only (no repeats, no precision evidence); 1 mark for the difference being within likely uncertainty; 1 mark for the need for repeats or several standards; 1 mark for stating that the uncertainty of each method must be compared.
