Q1Skill: conclusions & interpreting data · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

One student in Islamabad skipped for different lengths of time and measured her pulse rate immediately afterwards, resting for 15 minutes between trials.0 min: 72 beats per minute1 min: 98 beats per minute2 min: 118 beats per minute3 min: 131 beats per minute4 min: 134 beats per minute

  1. (a) Describe the relationship between the duration of skipping and the pulse rate. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage increase in pulse rate after 2 minutes compared with resting. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why these results cannot be used to draw a conclusion about all students. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pulse rate increases as duration increases, from 72 beats per minute at rest to 134 after 4 minutes; the increase gets smaller each minute and the rate levels off between 3 and 4 minutes.
    B1 positive relationship / increases; B1 levels off (increase of only 3 between 3 and 4 min) with data quote.
  2. (b) 64 %
    M1 (118 − 72)/72 × 100; A1 63.9 ≈ 64 %.
  3. (c) Only one student was tested (no repeats, no range of ages, fitness or sex).
    B1 single individual / small sample.

Q3Skill: conclusions & interpreting data · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

100 wheat grains were placed on filter paper soaked in sodium chloride solutions of different concentration. The percentage that germinated after 7 days was recorded.0 mmol dm⁻³: 96 %50 mmol dm⁻³: 90 %100 mmol dm⁻³: 71 %150 mmol dm⁻³: 38 %200 mmol dm⁻³: 9 %

  1. (a) State a conclusion that can be drawn from the data. [1]
  2. (b) Use your knowledge of water potential to explain the results. [3]
  3. (c) Farmers in parts of Punjab have soils that are becoming more saline. Suggest one further investigation that would help them choose which wheat variety to plant. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Increasing salt concentration decreases the percentage germination of wheat seeds (most sharply above 50 mmol dm⁻³).
    B1 higher salt concentration → lower germination.
  2. (b) Salt lowers the water potential of the solution; the water potential gradient between the solution and the seed is reduced (or reversed), so less water enters the seed by osmosis; water is needed to activate enzymes / mobilise food stores (e.g. amylase hydrolysing starch) so fewer seeds germinate.
    B1 solution has lower (more negative) water potential; B1 less water absorbed by osmosis / imbibition; B1 water needed for enzyme activity / hydrolysis of stores / cell expansion.
  3. (c) Compare germination (or growth) of several varieties at the same salt concentrations found in their soil.
    B1 compare different varieties at a range of salt concentrations / test using their own soil samples.
Q4Skill: conclusions & interpreting data · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A student measured the light intensity and the height of 12 plants of one species growing along a path from under trees into open ground. Spearman's rank correlation coefficient, rs, was calculated as 0.81.

  1. (a) State what the value of rs shows about the relationship between the two variables. [1]
  2. (b) The critical value of rs at p = 0.05 for 12 pairs of readings is 0.587. State what the student can conclude. [2]
  3. (c) The student then concluded that light intensity causes plants to grow taller. Explain why this conclusion is not fully justified by the data. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A strong positive correlation: plants receiving more light grew taller.
    B1 strong positive correlation.
  2. (b) 0.81 > 0.587 so the null hypothesis (no correlation) is rejected; the positive correlation is significant – the probability it is due to chance is less than 5 %.
    B1 compares rs with critical value correctly; B1 reject null / significant correlation (not due to chance).
  3. (c) Correlation does not show cause; other factors that vary with light in the field (e.g. soil moisture, temperature, nutrient levels, trampling) were not controlled and may affect height.
    B1 correlation ≠ causation; B1 named uncontrolled variable that could vary with position.

Show answer

(a) Use buffer solutions at pH 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9; cut potato into discs of equal size (cork borer, same diameter and thickness) or use same mass of blended potato; place discs with 5 cm³ of buffer in a boiling tube connected to a gas syringe / upturned measuring cylinder in water; add 5 cm³ of 3 % hydrogen peroxide (same volume and concentration); keep at 25 °C in a water bath; measure volume of oxygen collected in 2 minutes; repeat 3 times at each pH and calculate mean; calculate rate.

Q26Skill: graphs & charts · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A student in Faisalabad grew tomato plants with three different fertilisers, A, B and C, and calculated the mean yield per plant and the standard error for each.

  1. (a) State the type of chart that should be drawn and describe how the error bars should be added. [3]
  2. (b) The error bars for fertilisers A and B overlap but those for B and C do not. State what this suggests. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A bar chart (fertiliser type is categoric); bars of equal width, not touching; each bar height = mean yield; error bars drawn as a vertical line from mean − 2SE to mean + 2SE (95 % CI) with short horizontal caps.
    B1 bar chart because categoric independent variable; B1 bars separated, labelled axes with units; B1 error bars extend 2 × SE (or 1 SD as stated) above and below the mean.
  2. (b) The difference between A and B may not be significant (could be due to chance); the difference between B and C is likely to be significant.
    B1 A and B not significantly different; B1 B and C probably significantly different.

Q30Skill: measurement, graticule & magnification · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

A photomicrograph of a leaf epidermis has a scale bar labelled 100 µm that is 25 mm long. One epidermal cell measures 38 mm long on the photomicrograph.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual length of the cell, in µm. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the magnification of the photomicrograph. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why measuring several cells and finding the mean gives a better estimate of cell length. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 152 µm
    M1 38/25 × 100; A1 152 µm.
  2. (b) ×250
    B1 25 mm = 25000 µm; 25000/100 = ×250.
  3. (c) Cells vary in size and may be cut at different angles; a mean reduces the effect of this variation.
    B1 variation between cells / sectioning angle.
