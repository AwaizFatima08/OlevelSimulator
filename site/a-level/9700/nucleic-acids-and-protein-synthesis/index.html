Q1Transcription & translation · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the process of transcription in a eukaryotic cell. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) The DNA double helix unwinds and hydrogen bonds break at the gene; one strand acts as the template; free RNA nucleotides pair with complementary bases (U with A); RNA polymerase joins the nucleotides with phosphodiester bonds; this forms a primary transcript, which is spliced (introns removed) to form mRNA
    1 mark each for any five of: occurs in nucleus; DNA unwinds/H bonds broken; only template strand used; free (activated) RNA nucleotides; complementary base pairing, uracil pairs with adenine; RNA polymerase; phosphodiester bonds; primary transcript; introns removed/exons joined to form mRNA.
Q2Transcription & translation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The base sequence of a triplet on the template strand of DNA is GCA. What is the anticodon on the tRNA molecule that brings the matching amino acid? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C GCA
    Template GCA is transcribed into the mRNA codon CGU. The tRNA anticodon is complementary to the codon: GCA. B is the codon, not the anticodon; A contains thymine, which is not found in RNA.
Q3Transcription & translation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the roles of mRNA, tRNA and ribosomes in translation. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) mRNA carries the code (sequence of codons) from the nucleus to the ribosome; tRNA carries a specific amino acid and its anticodon pairs with the complementary codon; the ribosome holds the mRNA and tRNA in place and moves along the mRNA; peptide bonds form between adjacent amino acids on the ribosome
    1 mark each: mRNA – sequence of codons / template; tRNA – brings specific amino acid; anticodon–codon complementary base pairing; ribosome – binding site for mRNA and two tRNAs / catalyses peptide bond formation / moves one codon at a time.
Q4Transcription & translation · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by saying that the genetic code is degenerate. [1]
  2. (b) The coding sequence of the mature mRNA for a polypeptide has 1203 nucleotides, including one stop codon. Calculate the number of amino acids in the polypeptide. [2]
  3. (c) The primary transcript of this gene is much longer than 1203 nucleotides. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Most amino acids are coded for by more than one triplet/codon
    More than one codon for (most) amino acids.
  2. (b) 400
    M1: 1203 ÷ 3 = 401 codons. A1: 401 − 1 stop codon = 400 amino acids.
  3. (c) The primary transcript contains introns (non-coding sequences); these are removed by splicing, and the exons are joined to form the mature mRNA
    1 mark: introns present in primary transcript. 1 mark: introns removed / exons joined (splicing).
Q5Transcription & translation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State where in a eukaryotic cell (i) transcription and (ii) translation take place. [2]
  2. (b) Name the enzyme that catalyses transcription. [1]
  3. (c) Name the type of bond formed between amino acids during translation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) nucleus; (ii) ribosomes in the cytoplasm / on rough ER
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) RNA polymerase
    RNA polymerase. Not DNA polymerase.
  3. (c) Peptide bond
    Peptide bond.
Q6Transcription & translation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

The primary transcript of a gene in a cow contains 8400 nucleotides. Its introns contain 5250 nucleotides in total.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the primary transcript that is made of introns. [1]
  2. (b) Assume that the whole mature mRNA codes for the polypeptide and ends in one stop codon. Calculate the number of amino acids in the polypeptide. [2]
  3. (c) The same gene can produce two different polypeptides in muscle cells and in liver cells. Explain how this is possible. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 62.5 %
    5250 ÷ 8400 × 100 = 62.5 %.
  2. (b) 1049
    M1: mature mRNA = 8400 − 5250 = 3150 nucleotides; 3150 ÷ 3 = 1050 codons. A1: 1050 − 1 = 1049 amino acids.
  3. (c) Alternative splicing: different combinations of exons are kept in the mRNA in different cell types, so the mRNAs have different base sequences and code for different amino acid sequences
    1 mark: different exons included/removed (alternative splicing). 1 mark: different mRNA sequence → different primary structure.
Q7Transcription & translation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Part of an mRNA molecule has this sequence of codons:AUG GCU UUU GAU CCA UGG AAA UAACodons: AUG = Met (start); GCU = Ala; UUU = Phe; GAU = Asp; CCA = Pro; UGG = Trp; AAA = Lys; UAA = stop.

  1. (a) Give the sequence of amino acids coded for by this mRNA. [2]
  2. (b) Give the base sequence of the DNA template strand for the first two codons. [1]
  3. (c) A mutation changes the codon UGG to UGA, which is a stop codon. Explain the effect on the polypeptide. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Met – Ala – Phe – Asp – Pro – Trp – Lys (then stop)
    2 marks all correct in order; 1 mark if one error. Stop codon UAA adds no amino acid.
  2. (b) TAC CGA
    Complementary to AUG GCU, with T instead of U: TAC CGA.
  3. (c) Translation stops at this codon, so the polypeptide is shorter (only Met–Ala–Phe–Asp–Pro); it will have a different structure and probably not function
    1 mark: translation ends early / shorter polypeptide (five amino acids). 1 mark: missing amino acids alter folding/tertiary structure, so protein likely non-functional.
Q8Transcription & translation · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Some events in making a secreted protein are listed.1 tRNA anticodon pairs with an mRNA codon2 RNA polymerase joins RNA nucleotides3 mRNA leaves the nucleus through a nuclear pore4 a peptide bond forms between two amino acids5 introns are removedWhat is the correct order? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 2, 5, 3, 1, 4
    Transcription (2) and splicing (5) occur in the nucleus before the mRNA leaves (3); on the ribosome, tRNA must pair with the codon (1) before a peptide bond can form (4). B splices outside the nucleus, which is wrong.
Q9Transcription & translation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A ribosome adds 15 amino acids per second to a growing polypeptide. Calculate how long it takes one ribosome to make a polypeptide of 450 amino acids. [1]
  2. (b) In a cell making large amounts of this protein, many ribosomes are attached to each mRNA molecule at the same time. Explain the advantage of this. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how a tRNA molecule carries the correct amino acid to the ribosome. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 30 s
    450 ÷ 15 = 30 s.
  2. (b) Many polypeptides are made from one mRNA at the same time, so a large amount of protein is made quickly without extra transcription
    1 mark: several polypeptides translated simultaneously (polysome). 1 mark: faster/greater production of protein from one mRNA.
  3. (c) Each tRNA has a specific anticodon; a specific amino acid is attached to the tRNA (by an enzyme, using ATP) according to that anticodon, at the amino acid binding site
    1 mark: specific amino acid attached to tRNA at binding site (3′ end), using ATP/enzyme. 1 mark: anticodon is specific to that amino acid and pairs with the codon.
Q10Transcription & translation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Rifampicin, an antibiotic used to treat tuberculosis, inhibits bacterial RNA polymerase. Explain why this stops the bacteria making proteins. [2]
  2. (b) Death cap mushrooms contain α-amanitin, which inhibits the RNA polymerase that makes mRNA in human cells. Symptoms of liver failure often appear only two to three days after eating the mushroom. Suggest why. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why rifampicin does not stop transcription in the nuclei of human cells. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) No transcription, so no new mRNA is made; without mRNA no translation can take place
    1 mark: transcription prevented / no mRNA. 1 mark: no template for translation at ribosomes.
  2. (b) mRNA and proteins already present in the liver cells continue to work; as these are broken down over time they are not replaced; when key enzymes/proteins run out the cells die
    1 mark: existing mRNA still translated for a time. 1 mark: existing proteins/enzymes still function. 1 mark: they are gradually broken down and not replaced, so cell function fails later.
  3. (c) Human nuclear RNA polymerase has a different structure/shape so rifampicin does not bind to it
    Different shape of human RNA polymerase (no binding site for rifampicin).
Q11Transcription & translation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The coding (non-template) strand of a short gene section is:5′ ATG GTC ACC TGA 3′Codons: AUG = Met; GUC = Val; ACC = Thr; UGA = stop.

  1. (a) Give the base sequence of the mRNA transcribed from this gene section, showing the 5′ and 3′ ends. [1]
  2. (b) Give the base sequence of the template strand, showing the 5′ and 3′ ends. [1]
  3. (c) Using the information given, state the number of amino acids coded for by this section, and name them in order. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why only one strand of a gene is transcribed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5′ AUG GUC ACC UGA 3′
    Same as coding strand with U in place of T, same direction.
  2. (b) 3′ TAC CAG TGG ACT 5′
    Complementary to the coding strand and antiparallel (accept 5′ TCA GGT GAC CAT 3′).
  3. (c) 3 amino acids: Met – Val – Thr
    1 mark: 3 (UGA is a stop codon and codes for no amino acid). 1 mark: Met, Val, Thr in order.
  4. (d) Only the template strand has the base sequence complementary to the required mRNA; transcribing the other strand would give a different (complementary) sequence of codons and the wrong amino acid sequence
    1 mark: mRNA must be complementary to the template strand only. 1 mark: the other strand would give a different codon sequence/different polypeptide (or a non-functional one).
Q12Transcription & translation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A gene has six exons. In different tissues, exon 1 and exon 6 are always kept, but each of exons 2, 3, 4 and 5 can either be kept or removed during splicing. Calculate the maximum number of different mature mRNAs that this gene could produce. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the existence of alternative splicing means that the human genome, with only about 20 000 protein-coding genes, can produce many more different proteins. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16
    M1: each of 4 exons has 2 possibilities (kept or removed). A1: 2⁴ = 16.
  2. (b) One gene can give several mRNAs with different exon combinations; each codes for a polypeptide with a different primary structure, so the number of proteins exceeds the number of genes
    1 mark: one gene → several mRNAs. 1 mark: different amino acid sequences / different proteins.
Q13Structure of DNA, RNA & ATP · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Name the three components of a DNA nucleotide. [2]
  2. (b) Name the bond that joins adjacent nucleotides in one strand of DNA. [1]
  3. (c) Name the two purine bases found in DNA. [1]
  4. (d) State two differences between the structure of a DNA molecule and the structure of an mRNA molecule. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Deoxyribose (a pentose sugar); a phosphate group; a nitrogenous (organic) base
    All three for 2 marks; any two for 1 mark.
  2. (b) Phosphodiester bond
    Phosphodiester. Do not accept 'hydrogen bond' or 'peptide bond'.
  3. (c) Adenine and guanine
    Both needed.
  4. (d) DNA has deoxyribose, mRNA has ribose; DNA has thymine, mRNA has uracil; DNA is double-stranded, mRNA is single-stranded; DNA is much longer
    1 mark each for any two comparative differences.

    A = T = 18 %, so A + T = 36 % and G + C = 64 %. Since G = C, cytosine = 64 ÷ 2 = 32 %. D gives G + C together, not cytosine alone.
Q15Structure of DNA, RNA & ATP · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a molecule of ATP. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why ATP is well suited to be the immediate energy source in cells. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Adenine (base) joined to ribose (pentose sugar), with a chain of three phosphate groups attached to the ribose
    1 mark each: adenine; ribose; three phosphate groups. (Accept 'a nucleotide with adenine, ribose and three phosphates' as 3 marks.)
  2. (b) Hydrolysis of ATP to ADP and phosphate releases energy in one step / quickly; releases a small, usable amount of energy; it is small and soluble so moves easily in the cell; it is quickly regenerated from ADP + Pi
    1 mark each for any three of: energy released by a single hydrolysis reaction; small manageable quantities of energy (less wasted as heat); water soluble/easily transported; rapidly resynthesised; can phosphorylate other molecules making them more reactive.
Q16Structure of DNA, RNA & ATP · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Strand 1 of a short DNA molecule from a rice gene has the base composition: adenine 22 %, thymine 30 %, guanine 28 %, cytosine 20 %.

  1. (a) Give the percentages of each base in strand 2 (the complementary strand). [2]
  2. (b) Strand 1 is used as the template for transcription. Give the percentages of each base in the mRNA. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage of guanine in the whole double-stranded molecule. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Strand 2: T 22 %, A 30 %, C 28 %, G 20 %
    2 marks all four correct; 1 mark for two or three correct. Base pairing A–T and G–C.
  2. (b) U 22 %, A 30 %, C 28 %, G 20 %
    1 mark: uracil replaces thymine (U 22 %). 1 mark: other three correct.
  3. (c) 24 %
    (28 + 20) ÷ 2 = 24 %.
Q17Structure of DNA, RNA & ATP · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which describes the structure of ATP? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B adenine, ribose and three phosphate groups
    ATP is adenine + ribose (together adenosine) + three phosphate groups, joined by covalent bonds. A has the DNA sugar; C describes ADP; D is wrong because the phosphates are linked covalently, not by hydrogen bonds.
Q18Structure of DNA, RNA & ATP · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by saying that the two strands of DNA are antiparallel. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why adenine pairs with thymine but not with cytosine. [2]
  3. (c) A DNA fragment is 120 base pairs long, and 35 % of its bases are guanine or cytosine. Calculate the total number of hydrogen bonds holding the two strands together. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The strands run in opposite directions: one runs 5′ to 3′ and the other 3′ to 5′ (the deoxyribose sugars point in opposite directions)
    1 mark: opposite directions. 1 mark: reference to 5′/3′ ends or orientation of the sugar–phosphate backbone.
  2. (b) A purine always pairs with a pyrimidine so the molecule has a constant width; adenine and thymine form two hydrogen bonds with each other, but adenine and cytosine cannot form matching hydrogen bonds
    1 mark: purine–pyrimidine pairing (both A and C fit the width rule, so this alone is not enough). 1 mark: A and T form 2 hydrogen bonds / hydrogen-bonding positions match only for A–T (G–C forms 3).
  3. (c) 282
    M1: G–C pairs = 0.35 × 120 = 42; A–T pairs = 78. A1: (42 × 3) + (78 × 2) = 126 + 156 = 282.
Q19Structure of DNA, RNA & ATP · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a tRNA molecule. [3]
  2. (b) A polynucleotide contains 250 nucleotides. State how many molecules of water are needed to hydrolyse it completely into separate nucleotides. [1]
  3. (c) State two differences between the structure of a tRNA molecule and an mRNA molecule. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Single polynucleotide strand of RNA folded into a clover-leaf shape, held by hydrogen bonds between complementary bases; has an anticodon (three bases) at one end/loop; an amino acid attachment site at the 3′ end
    1 mark each for any three of: single strand of RNA nucleotides; folded (clover-leaf) with some base pairing/hydrogen bonds; anticodon of three bases; amino acid binding site (3′ end, CCA).
  2. (b) 249
    One water molecule per phosphodiester bond; 250 − 1 = 249 bonds.
  3. (c) tRNA is folded (clover-leaf) with hydrogen-bonded regions, mRNA is a straight/unfolded strand; tRNA has an anticodon and amino acid binding site, mRNA has codons; tRNA is much shorter (about 80 nucleotides); all tRNA molecules have the same length/shape
    1 mark each for any two valid comparisons.

  1. (a) Describe how a molecule of DNA is replicated by semi-conservative replication. [5]
Show answer

(a) DNA helicase breaks hydrogen bonds between bases and the strands separate; both strands act as templates; free DNA nucleotides pair with complementary bases (A–T, C–G); DNA polymerase joins nucleotides by phosphodiester bonds (in the 5′ to 3′ direction); each new molecule contains one original strand and one new strand
    1 mark each for any five of: helicase; breaks hydrogen bonds/unwinds; both strands are templates; free (activated) nucleotides; complementary base pairing; DNA polymerase; phosphodiester bonds formed; DNA ligase joins fragments (lagging strand); each daughter molecule has one old and one new strand.

Q22DNA replication · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State the role of each of these enzymes in DNA replication: (i) DNA helicase, (ii) DNA polymerase, (iii) DNA ligase. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why one new strand (the leading strand) is made continuously while the other (the lagging strand) is made in short sections. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) unwinds DNA and breaks hydrogen bonds between bases; (ii) joins new nucleotides to form a new strand (forms phosphodiester bonds); (iii) joins short fragments (Okazaki fragments) of the lagging strand
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) DNA polymerase can only add nucleotides in one direction (to the 3′ end, building 5′ to 3′); the two template strands are antiparallel; so on one template the polymerase follows the replication fork, but on the other it must work away from the fork in short fragments that are later joined
    1 mark: polymerase works only 5′ → 3′ / adds to 3′ end. 1 mark: strands are antiparallel. 1 mark: on lagging strand synthesis is away from the fork, so fragments are made and joined by ligase.

  2. (b) Explain how the results after one generation ruled out the conservative model of replication. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25 %
    M1: after 3 generations there are 2³ = 8 molecules from each original; only 2 contain an original ¹⁵N strand. A1: 2 ÷ 8 × 100 = 25 %.
  2. (b) After one generation all DNA was of intermediate (hybrid) density; conservative replication would have given one band of heavy DNA and one band of light DNA
    1 mark: all intermediate/single hybrid band. 1 mark: conservative model predicts heavy + light bands (no hybrid).

  2. (b) A human chromosome contains about 50 times as much DNA, but replication forks in human cells move more slowly. Suggest how human cells can still replicate each chromosome within a few hours. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 38.3 min

  2. (b) Replication starts at many origins along each chromosome at the same time, so many forks work simultaneously
    1 mark: many origins of replication. 1 mark: replication happens simultaneously in many places/replication bubbles join.

Show answer

    Change in base sequence of DNA (in a gene).
  2. (b) Substitution, insertion (addition), deletion
    All three for 1 mark.
  3. (c) Deletion causes a frameshift: every codon after the deletion is changed, so many amino acids change (and a stop codon may appear early); a substitution changes only one codon, which may code for the same amino acid (degenerate code) or only one different amino acid

Q28Gene mutation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Part of an mRNA for an enzyme reads: AUG CUU GAA GGA …Codons: AUG = Met; CUU = Leu; GAA = Glu; GAG = Glu; AAA = Lys; GGA = Gly; UAA = stop.Three different mutations each change the third codon (GAA):Mutation 1: GAA → GAGMutation 2: GAA → AAAMutation 3: GAA → UAA

  2. (b) Explain which mutation is most likely to produce a non-functional protein. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why mutation 2 may have little effect on the function of the protein in some cases. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mutation 1: no change (GAG still codes for Glu); Mutation 2: Glu replaced by Lys; Mutation 3: translation stops – polypeptide ends after Leu (Met–Leu only)
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Mutation 3: a stop codon is formed, so the polypeptide is very short / most amino acids are missing, so it cannot fold into the correct tertiary structure
    1 mark: mutation 3 (nonsense/stop codon). 1 mark: truncated polypeptide, wrong tertiary structure/shape.
  3. (c) If the amino acid is not in the active site/an important region, or Lys has similar properties, the tertiary structure may hardly change
    Change outside active site / does not change folding much.

The mRNA for a short peptide is:AUG GCU AAA GGC UUU GAU UGG UAACodons: AUG = Met; GCU = Ala; AAA = Lys; GGC = Gly; UUU = Phe; GAU = Asp; UGG = Trp; UAA = stop; UUG = Leu; AUU = Ile; GGU = Gly.A mutation in the gene deletes the first G of the fourth codon (GGC).

  1. (a) Give the amino acid sequence coded for by the mutated mRNA, as far as the codons given allow. [2]
  2. (b) State the name for this kind of effect and explain why the stop codon is lost. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the deletion of three adjacent bases is often less harmful than the deletion of one base. [1]
Show answer

    Mutated sequence: AUG GCU AAA GCU UUG AUU GGU AA… 2 marks all correct; 1 mark first three correct and frame shifted.
  2. (b) Frameshift; the reading frame moves one base, so the triplets are read differently and UAA is no longer read as a codon in frame

Q30Gene mutation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The most common mutation causing cystic fibrosis deletes three nucleotides from the CFTR gene, so phenylalanine at position 508 is missing. Explain why the rest of the amino acid sequence is unchanged. [2]
  2. (b) The normal CFTR protein contains 1480 amino acids. Calculate the number of nucleotides in the coding sequence of the normal mRNA, including the stop codon. [1]
  3. (c) CFTR is a channel protein in the cell surface membrane. Suggest why the loss of one amino acid can stop the protein from working. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Three bases = one whole codon is removed, so the reading frame is not shifted; all codons after the deletion are read as normal
    1 mark: three nucleotides correspond to one codon/triplet. 1 mark: no frameshift so downstream codons unchanged.
  2. (b) 4443
    (1480 + 1) × 3 = 4443.
  3. (c) Changes the primary structure, so the polypeptide folds into a different tertiary structure; misfolded protein does not form a working channel / is broken down and does not reach the cell surface membrane
    1 mark: altered folding/tertiary (3D) shape. 1 mark: channel not formed or protein not inserted in the membrane / destroyed by the cell.
