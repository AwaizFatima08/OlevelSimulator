Q1Chloroplast structure & pigments · Core · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

An electron micrograph of a chloroplast from a mango leaf shows:A: two membranes surrounding the whole organelleB: stacks of flattened membrane discs, joined to each other by single membranes (intergranal lamellae)C: a fluid-filled matrix around the membranesD: large, pale, oval granules in the matrix, made of the plant's main storage polysaccharide

  1. (a) Name structures A, B, C and D. [4]
  2. (b) State where in the chloroplast (i) the light-dependent stage and (ii) the Calvin cycle take place. [2]
  3. (c) The stroma also contains circular DNA and 70S ribosomes. Suggest the function of these. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A chloroplast envelope (double membrane); B granum (stack of thylakoids); C stroma; D starch grain.
    1 mark each. Accept 'thylakoids' for B.
  2. (b) (i) thylakoid membranes (grana); (ii) stroma.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) To make some of the chloroplast's own proteins (e.g. part of rubisco / photosystem proteins).
    1 mark: protein synthesis within the chloroplast / codes for and makes chloroplast proteins.
Q2Chloroplast structure & pigments · Core · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the internal structure of a chloroplast is adapted for the light-dependent stage of photosynthesis. [3]
  2. (b) Describe how the stroma is adapted for the light-independent stage. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why chloroplasts in leaf palisade cells store carbohydrate as starch rather than as glucose. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Many thylakoid membranes stacked into grana give a large surface area; they hold the photosystems, electron carriers and ATP synthase; the small thylakoid space lets a proton gradient build up quickly.
    1 mark each for any three of: large surface area of thylakoid membranes / grana; photosystems (chlorophyll) held in membranes; electron transport chain in membrane; ATP synthase in membrane; small volume of thylakoid space for steep H⁺ gradient.
  2. (b) It contains the enzymes of the Calvin cycle, e.g. rubisco, and surrounds the thylakoids so ATP and reduced NADP diffuse only a short distance to it.
    1 mark: enzymes of Calvin cycle / rubisco in stroma. 1 mark: close to thylakoids, short diffusion distance for ATP and reduced NADP (or contains RuBP / CO₂ dissolved).
  3. (c) Starch is insoluble so it does not lower the water potential of the cell / affect osmosis; it is compact and does not leave the chloroplast.
    1 mark insoluble / osmotically inactive; 1 mark compact / large store in small space / cannot diffuse out.
Q3Chloroplast structure & pigments · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Pigments from fenugreek (methi) leaves were separated by thin-layer chromatography. The solvent front moved 9.5 cm from the origin. A yellow-green spot moved 6.4 cm.

  1. (a) Calculate the Rf value of the yellow-green spot. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Carotene has an Rf value of 0.94 in this solvent. Calculate how far carotene moved from the origin. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [1]
  3. (c) State the colour of the spots formed by carotene and by chlorophyll a. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.67
    Rf = distance moved by pigment ÷ distance moved by solvent front = 6.4 ÷ 9.5 (M1) = 0.67 (A1).
  2. (b) 8.9 cm
    0.94 × 9.5 = 8.9 cm.
  3. (c) Carotene: orange (yellow-orange); chlorophyll a: blue-green.
    1 mark each.

  1. (a) Spot 1: 0.45; spot 2: 0.65; spot 3: 0.71; spot 4: 0.95

Q6Chloroplast structure & pigments · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Distinguish between an absorption spectrum and an action spectrum. [2]
  2. (b) Chlorophyll a absorbs very little light between 450 nm and 550 nm, yet the rate of photosynthesis at 480 nm is still quite high. Explain why. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why most leaves appear green. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) An absorption spectrum shows how much light of each wavelength is absorbed by a pigment (or pigments); an action spectrum shows the rate of photosynthesis at each wavelength of light.
    1 mark: absorption = amount of light absorbed at each wavelength. 1 mark: action = rate of photosynthesis at each wavelength.
  2. (b) Accessory pigments such as carotene and xanthophylls (and chlorophyll b) absorb light at these wavelengths (blue-green); they pass the energy to chlorophyll a in the reaction centre of the photosystem; so light at these wavelengths is still used for photosynthesis.
    1 mark: accessory pigments named (carotenoids/chlorophyll b). 1 mark: absorb blue/blue-green light around 450–500 nm. 1 mark: transfer energy to primary pigment / reaction centre chlorophyll a.
  3. (c) Green light (about 500–560 nm) is absorbed least by the pigments, so it is reflected or transmitted to the eye.
    1 mark: green wavelengths reflected/not absorbed.
Q7Chloroplast structure & pigments · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Leaf pigments were spotted at one corner of a square sheet of chromatography paper. Solvent 1 was run up the paper until its front was 10.0 cm from the origin. One spot, X, had moved 6.0 cm and looked brownish-green. The paper was dried, turned through 90° and run in solvent 2, whose front moved 8.0 cm. Spot X split into two spots that had moved 2.4 cm and 5.6 cm in the second direction.

  1. (a) Calculate the Rf value of spot X in solvent 1 and the Rf values of the two new spots in solvent 2. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why spot X contained two pigments but they only separated in solvent 2. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the pigment spots must be identified by Rf value rather than by the distance moved. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Solvent 1: 0.60. Solvent 2: 0.30 and 0.70.
    M1 for distance ÷ solvent front; A1 for all three values.
  2. (b) The two pigments had the same (very similar) solubility in solvent 1 and the same attraction to the paper, so they moved together; in solvent 2 their solubilities differ, so they move at different speeds and separate.
    1 mark: separation depends on solubility in solvent / adsorption to paper; 1 mark: similar in solvent 1 so same Rf; 1 mark: different in solvent 2.
  3. (c) Distance moved depends on how far the solvent travels, which differs between runs; Rf is a ratio that stays constant for a pigment in a given solvent.
    1 mark.
Q8Chloroplast structure & pigments · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The chlorophyll content of leaves from the sunny top and the shaded base of a large mango tree was measured (mg per g of leaf).Sun leaves: chlorophyll a 2.4, chlorophyll b 0.8Shade leaves: chlorophyll a 2.1, chlorophyll b 1.0

  1. (a) Calculate the ratio of chlorophyll a to chlorophyll b for each type of leaf. Give each answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Chlorophyll b is found mainly in the light-harvesting (antenna) complexes of the photosystems. Suggest how the lower ratio in shade leaves is an advantage to the tree. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sun leaves 3.0; shade leaves 2.1
    2.4 ÷ 0.8 = 3.0; 2.1 ÷ 1.0 = 2.1. 1 mark each.
  2. (b) Shade leaves have relatively more light-harvesting pigment around each reaction centre; so they absorb more of the dim light (including wavelengths that chlorophyll a absorbs poorly); more energy is funnelled to the reaction centres, so the rate of photosynthesis in low light is higher.
    1 mark each for: larger antenna / more light harvesting per reaction centre; absorbs wider range / more of the scarce light; more energy passed to chlorophyll a / more photoactivation, so more ATP and reduced NADP in low light.

Q10Chloroplast structure & pigments · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) State three structural features that chloroplasts and mitochondria have in common. [3]
  2. (b) Describe two structural differences between a chloroplast and a mitochondrion. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the ATP synthase in the chloroplast faces the stroma. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Double membrane (envelope); their own circular DNA; 70S ribosomes; ATP synthase in an internal membrane; an internal fluid with enzymes (stroma / matrix).
    1 mark each for any three.
  2. (b) Chloroplast has separate internal thylakoid membranes stacked into grana, while the mitochondrial inner membrane is folded into cristae; chloroplast contains photosynthetic pigments (chlorophyll) and starch grains, mitochondria do not; chloroplasts are usually larger.

    1 mark: H⁺ diffuse from thylakoid space to stroma through ATP synthase; 1 mark: ATP released into stroma where Calvin cycle needs it.
Q11Chloroplast structure & pigments · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a photosystem and explain how it captures light energy. [4]
  2. (b) Some red seaweeds live in coastal seas at depths where most red and blue light has been absorbed by the water and mainly green light remains. These seaweeds contain a red accessory pigment called phycoerythrin. Suggest how phycoerythrin helps them to grow at these depths. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A photosystem is a cluster of pigment molecules held by proteins in the thylakoid membrane; a light-harvesting system (antenna complex) of accessory pigments and chlorophyll molecules surrounds a reaction centre; pigments absorb photons and pass energy from molecule to molecule towards the reaction centre; the reaction centre contains a pair of chlorophyll a molecules (P680 in PSII, P700 in PSI) which, when excited, emit an electron to an electron acceptor.
    1 mark each for any four of: in thylakoid membrane, pigments + proteins; light-harvesting/antenna complex of many pigments; energy transferred to reaction centre; reaction centre = chlorophyll a (P680/P700); excited electron emitted / passed to electron acceptor (photoactivation).
  2. (b) Phycoerythrin absorbs green light (it looks red because it reflects red); the absorbed energy is passed to chlorophyll a in the reaction centres, so the seaweed can still photosynthesise using the light available at depth.
    1 mark: absorbs green light / wavelengths that reach that depth. 1 mark: energy passed to reaction centre chlorophyll so photosynthesis continues.
Q12Chloroplast structure & pigments · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Chloroplasts were broken open and separated into a thylakoid fraction and a stroma fraction. Each was incubated under different conditions.Tube 1: thylakoids, light, water — oxygen releasedTube 2: stroma, light, water — no oxygen releasedTube 3: stroma, dark, CO₂ + ATP + reduced NADP — CO₂ fixed into sugarsTube 4: stroma, dark, CO₂ only — almost no CO₂ fixed

  1. (a) Explain the results for tubes 1 and 2. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the results for tubes 3 and 4. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Thylakoids contain the photosystems and electron transport chains; in light, photolysis of water releases oxygen (tube 1); the stroma has no photosystems, so no oxygen is produced (tube 2); without light, no photoactivation and no oxygen.
    1 mark: thylakoids are site of light-dependent stage / photosystems. 1 mark: photolysis of water in light releases O₂. 1 mark: stroma lacks photosystems/chlorophyll so no O₂.

(b) The stroma contains the Calvin cycle enzymes (rubisco); ATP and reduced NADP, normally supplied by the light-dependent stage, were added, so CO₂ could be fixed even in the dark (tube 3); without ATP and reduced NADP, GP cannot be reduced to triose phosphate and RuBP cannot be regenerated, so fixation stops quickly (tube 4).
    1 mark: stroma contains Calvin cycle enzymes/rubisco. 1 mark: ATP and reduced NADP are the products of the light-dependent stage needed, so light not directly required. 1 mark: without them GP not reduced / RuBP not regenerated so fixation stops.
Q13Chloroplast structure & pigments · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A mutant of a barley plant cannot make chlorophyll b. In bright sunlight it grows almost as fast as normal plants, but in dim light it grows much more slowly.

  1. (a) Explain why the mutant grows more slowly in dim light. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why the mutant grows almost normally in bright sunlight. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Chlorophyll b is an accessory pigment in the light-harvesting system; it absorbs wavelengths (e.g. about 450–480 nm and 640 nm) that chlorophyll a absorbs poorly; without it less light energy is absorbed and passed to the reaction centres; in dim light, light intensity is the limiting factor, so less photoactivation, less ATP and reduced NADP, a slower Calvin cycle and less growth.
    1 mark each for: chlorophyll b = accessory pigment / in antenna complex; absorbs other wavelengths; passes energy to chlorophyll a in reaction centre; light is limiting in dim light so rate falls (fewer electrons, less ATP / reduced NADP).
  2. (b) In bright light there is plenty of light energy even with fewer pigments, so light is no longer the limiting factor; another factor such as CO₂ concentration or temperature limits the rate in both plants.
    1 mark: light not limiting / enough absorbed by chlorophyll a and carotenoids; 1 mark: rate limited by CO₂ / temperature in both.
Q14Chloroplast structure & pigments · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A filament of a green alga was placed on a microscope slide with aerobic bacteria that move towards oxygen. Light was passed through a prism so that each part of the filament received a different band of wavelengths. Q15Chloroplast structure & pigments · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Seedlings were grown on a herbicide that stops the synthesis of carotenoids but not of chlorophyll. In bright light the new leaves were white. In very dim light the new leaves were pale green.

  1. (a) Without carotenoids, excess absorbed light energy is not dissipated safely; chlorophyll is damaged/destroyed (photo-oxidised) in bright light so the leaves are white; in dim light little excess energy is absorbed, so chlorophyll survives and the leaves are green.

The rate of photosynthesis of a cucumber plant was measured at two CO₂ concentrations. Temperature was kept at 25 °C.Light intensity / arbitrary units: 0, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10Rate at 0.04 % CO₂ / arbitrary units: 0, 3.2, 5.4, 5.9, 6.0, 6.0Rate at 0.10 % CO₂ / arbitrary units: 0, 3.2, 6.1, 8.8, 10.4, 10.8

  1. (a) The factor that is in shortest supply (furthest from its optimum) and so restricts the rate of a process; increasing it increases the rate.

Q17Limiting factors · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
