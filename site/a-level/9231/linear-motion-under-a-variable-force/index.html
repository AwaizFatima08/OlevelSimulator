Q1Force depending on velocity · Core · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

A particle P of mass 2 kg moves in a straight line on a smooth horizontal surface. The only horizontal force on P is a resistance of magnitude 4v N, where v m s⁻¹ is its speed. Initially P has speed 10 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that v = 10e⁻²ᵗ, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed of P at time t s. [3]
  2. (b) Find the time taken for the speed of P to fall to 2 m s⁻¹. [2]
  3. (c) Find the distance travelled by P in this time. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 Newton's second law: 2 dv/dt = −4v; M1 separate and integrate: ln v = −2t + c; A1 v = 10 at t = 0 gives v = 10e⁻²ᵗ.
  2. (b) 0.805 s
    M1 10e⁻²ᵗ = 2; A1 t = (1/2) ln 5 = 0.805 s.
  3. (c) 4 m
    M1 x = ∫ 10e⁻²ᵗ dt = 5(1 − e⁻²ᵗ); M1 e⁻²ᵗ = 1/5; A1 x = 5(4/5) = 4 m.
Q2Force depending on velocity · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

An ice-hockey puck of mass 0.5 kg slides in a straight line across horizontal ice. Air resistance and friction together are modelled as a single resisting force of magnitude 0.1v² N, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed. The puck starts with speed 20 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that v = 20e^(−0.2x), where v m s⁻¹ is the speed of the puck when it has travelled x m. [4]
  2. (b) Find the distance travelled when the speed has fallen to 5 m s⁻¹. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 use acceleration v dv/dx: 0.5v dv/dx = −0.1v²; A1 dv/dx = −0.2v; M1 integrate: ln v = −0.2x + c; A1 v = 20 at x = 0 gives result.
  2. (b) 6.93 m
    M1 e^(−0.2x) = 1/4; A1 x = 5 ln 4 = 6.93 m.
Q3Force depending on velocity · Core · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A car of mass 800 kg starts from rest on a straight horizontal road. The engine produces a constant driving force of 2400 N and the resistance to motion is 40v N, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed of the car after t seconds.

  1. (a) Find the greatest speed the car could approach under this model. [1]
  2. (b) Show that v = 60(1 − e^(−t/20)). [4]
  3. (c) Find the time taken for the car to reach 30 m s⁻¹. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 60 m s⁻¹
    B1 2400 − 40v = 0 when v = 60.
  2. (b) Shown
    M1 800 dv/dt = 2400 − 40v; M1 separate: ∫ dv/(60 − v) = ∫ dt/20; A1 −ln(60 − v) = t/20 + c; A1 v = 0 at t = 0 gives result.
  3. (c) 13.9 s
    M1 e^(−t/20) = 1/2; A1 t = 20 ln 2 = 13.9 s.
Q4Force depending on velocity · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A small stone of mass 0.2 kg is released from rest and falls vertically through the air. The air resistance is 0.05v N, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed after t s. Take g = 10 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Show that dv/dt = 10 − 0.25v and hence find v in terms of t. [4]
  2. (b) Find the speed of the stone after 3 seconds. [1]
  3. (c) State the terminal speed of the stone. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) v = 40(1 − e^(−t/4))
    M1 0.2 dv/dt = 0.2(10) − 0.05v; A1 dv/dt = 10 − 0.25v; M1 separate and integrate: −4 ln(40 − v) = t + c; A1 v = 40(1 − e^(−t/4)).
  2. (b) 21.1 m s⁻¹
    B1 40(1 − e^(−0.75)) = 21.1 m s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 40 m s⁻¹
    B1 v → 40 as t → ∞.
Q5Force depending on velocity · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A cyclist and her bicycle have total mass 80 kg. She rides along a straight horizontal road in Islamabad, working at a constant power of 240 W. The resistance to motion is 2v N, where v m s⁻¹ is her speed.

  1. (a) Show that 80v dv/dt = 240 − 2v². [2]
  2. (b) Find the greatest speed that the cyclist can approach. [2]
  3. (c) Find the acceleration of the cyclist when her speed is 5 m s⁻¹. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 driving force = power/speed = 240/v; A1 80 dv/dt = 240/v − 2v, multiply by v.
  2. (b) √120 ≈ 11.0 m s⁻¹
    M1 240 − 2v² = 0; A1 v = √120 = 11.0 m s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 0.475 m s⁻²
    M1 80a = 240/5 − 2(5); A1 a = 38/80 = 0.475 m s⁻².
Q6Force depending on velocity · Core · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

A particle P of mass 3 kg moves along the positive x-axis on a smooth horizontal surface. It passes through the origin O with speed 8 m s⁻¹ and is subject to a resisting force of magnitude 6v N, where v m s⁻¹ is its speed when its displacement from O is x m.

  1. (a) Using acceleration in the form v dv/dx, show that v = 8 − 2x. [3]
  2. (b) Find the value of x when v = 2, and explain why P never reaches the point where x = 4. [3]
  3. (c) Find the time taken for P to travel from O to the point where x = 3. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 3v dv/dx = −6v; A1 dv/dx = −2; A1 v = 8 − 2x using v = 8 at x = 0.
  2. (b) x = 3; v → 0 only as t → ∞
    B1 x = 3; M1 from 3 dv/dt = −6v, v = 8e⁻²ᵗ, which is never zero; A1 so x = 4 − 4e⁻²ᵗ < 4 for all t.
  3. (c) 0.693 s
    M1 8e⁻²ᵗ = 2; A1 t = (1/2) ln 4 = 0.693 s.
Q7Force depending on velocity · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

A ball of mass 0.1 kg is projected vertically upwards with speed 15 m s⁻¹. Air resistance has magnitude 0.02v² N, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed of the ball. Take g = 10 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Find the greatest height above the point of projection reached by the ball. [4]
  2. (b) Find the time taken to reach this greatest height. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.26 m
    M1 0.1v dv/dx = −1 − 0.02v²; A1 v dv/dx = −(10 + 0.2v²); M1 x = ∫₀¹⁵ v/(10 + 0.2v²) dv = 2.5 ln((10 + 45)/10); A1 2.5 ln 5.5 = 4.26 m.
  2. (b) 0.799 s
    M1 dv/dt = −0.2(50 + v²); M1 t = 5∫₀¹⁵ dv/(50 + v²); A1 = (5/√50) tan⁻¹(15/√50); A1 0.799 s.
Q8Force depending on velocity · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

  1. (a) Find the greatest possible speed of the car under this model. [2]
  2. (b) Show that 1200v dv/dt = 30 000 − 25v². [1]
  3. (c) Find the time taken for the speed to increase from 10 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) √1200 ≈ 34.6 m s⁻¹
    M1 30000/v = 25v; A1 v = √1200 = 34.6 m s⁻¹.
  2. (b) Shown
    B1 1200 dv/dt = 30 000/v − 25v, multiplied by v.
  3. (c) 7.64 s
    M1 separate: t = ∫ 1200v/(30 000 − 25v²) dv; M1 = −24 ln(30 000 − 25v²); A1 24 ln(27 500/20 000); A1 7.64 s.
Q9Force depending on velocity · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A block of mass 2 kg slides on a horizontal surface. The total resistance to its motion is (4 + 2v) N, where v m s⁻¹ is its speed. The block is set moving with speed 6 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Find the time taken for the block to come to rest. [3]
  2. (b) Find the distance travelled by the block before it comes to rest. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) ln 4 ≈ 1.39 s
    M1 2 dv/dt = −(4 + 2v); M1 ∫₆⁰ dv/(2 + v) = −t; A1 t = ln 4 = 1.39 s.

    M1 v dv/dx = −(2 + v); M1 x = ∫₀⁶ v/(2 + v) dv = ∫₀⁶ (1 − 2/(2 + v)) dv; A1 6 − 2 ln 4; A1 3.23 m.

Q12Force depending on velocity · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A motor boat of mass 400 kg is moving in a straight line across a lake at 9 m s⁻¹ when its engine is switched off. The water resistance is modelled as 50v^(3/2) N, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed.

  1. (a) Find the time taken for the speed to fall from 9 m s⁻¹ to 4 m s⁻¹. [4]
  2. (b) Find the distance travelled in this time. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8/3 s ≈ 2.67 s

Q13Force depending on velocity · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

A train of mass 50 000 kg starts from rest on a straight level track. The engine exerts a constant driving force of 40 000 N and the resistance to motion is kv N, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed and k is a constant.

  1. (a) Given that the greatest speed the train can approach is 40 m s⁻¹, show that k = 1000. [1]
  2. (b) Find the time taken for the train to reach 20 m s⁻¹ from rest. [3]
  3. (c) Find the distance travelled in this time. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    B1 at top speed 40 000 = 40k so k = 1000.
  2. (b) 50 ln 2 ≈ 34.7 s
    M1 50 000 dv/dt = 40 000 − 1000v; M1 t = 50∫₀²⁰ dv/(40 − v); A1 50 ln 2 = 34.7 s.
  3. (c) 386 m
    M1 50v dv/dx = 40 − v; M1 x = 50∫₀²⁰ (40/(40 − v) − 1) dv; A1 50(40 ln 2 − 20); A1 386 m.
Q14Force depending on velocity · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 9 marks

  2. (b) Find the speed of the particle when it returns to the point of projection. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 50 ln 1.9 ≈ 32.1 m
    M1 upwards: v dv/dx = −(10 + 0.01v²); M1 H = ∫₀³⁰ v/(10 + 0.01v²) dv = 50 ln((10 + 9)/10); A1 50 ln 1.9; A1 32.1 m.
  2. (b) 21.8 m s⁻¹
    M1 downwards: v dv/dy = 10 − 0.01v², y measured down from the top; M1 H = −50 ln(1 − 0.001w²); M1 equate: 1/(1 − 0.001w²) = 1.9; A1 w² = 1000(1 − 1/1.9) = 473.7; A1 w = 21.8 m s⁻¹.

Q17Force depending on velocity · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 9 marks

A package of mass 1 kg is dropped from rest from a hovering drone. Air resistance has magnitude 0.5v N, where v m s⁻¹ is the speed of the package t s after release. Take g = 10 m s⁻².

Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 dv/dt = 10 − 0.5v; A1 v = 20(1 − e^(−t/2)); M1 x = ∫ v dt; A1 20t + 40e^(−t/2) + c; A1 c = −40 from x = 0 at t = 0.
  2. (b) T ≈ 4.26

Q22Force depending on time · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Q24Force depending on time · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 9 marks

A particle of mass 2 kg moves in a straight line on a horizontal surface. At time t s it is acted on by a force of 4t N in the direction of motion and a resistance of 4v N, where v m s⁻¹ is its speed. When t = 0, v = 3.

  1. (a) Show that v = t − 1/2 + (7/2)e⁻²ᵗ. [5]
  2. (b) Find the least speed of the particle, giving your answer in exact form. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 2 dv/dt = 4t − 4v so dv/dt + 2v = 2t; M1 integrating factor e²ᵗ; M1 ve²ᵗ = ∫ 2te²ᵗ dt = (t − 1/2)e²ᵗ + c; A1 c = 7/2 from v(0) = 3; A1 result.
  2. (b) (1/2) ln 7 ≈ 0.973 m s⁻¹
    M1 dv/dt = 1 − 7e⁻²ᵗ = 0; A1 t = (1/2) ln 7; M1 v = (1/2) ln 7 − 1/2 + (7/2)(1/7); A1 (1/2) ln 7 = 0.973 m s⁻¹.
Q25Force depending on time · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A small robot of mass 5 kg starts from rest and moves along a straight horizontal track in a warehouse in Karachi. At time t s the resultant force on it is 10/(1 + t) N in the direction of motion. Its speed is v m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Find v in terms of t. [2]
  2. (b) Find the time at which v = 4 and the distance travelled up to that time, giving the distance in exact form. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) v = 2 ln(1 + t)
    M1 5 dv/dt = 10/(1 + t); A1 v = 2 ln(1 + t) using v(0) = 0.
  2. (b) t = e² − 1 ≈ 6.39 s; x = 2(e² + 1) ≈ 16.8 m
    B1 t = e² − 1; M1 x = ∫ 2 ln(1 + t) dt by parts; A1 x = 2[(1 + t) ln(1 + t) − t]; M1 substitute t = e² − 1: 2[2e² − e² + 1]; A1 2(e² + 1) = 16.8 m.
Q26Force depending on displacement · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A particle P of mass 0.5 kg moves along the x-axis. It starts from rest at the origin O and is acted on by a force of (4 − 2x) N in the positive x-direction, where x m is its displacement from O. Its speed is v m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that v² = 16x − 4x². [3]
  2. (b) Find the greatest speed of P and the value of x at which P next comes to rest. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 0.5v dv/dx = 4 − 2x; M1 integrate: v²/4 = 4x − x² + c; A1 c = 0, v² = 16x − 4x².
  2. (b) 4 m s⁻¹; x = 4
    M1 force zero (or v² greatest) at x = 2; A1 v² = 32 − 16 = 16, v = 4; B1 v = 0 when x = 4.
Q27Force depending on displacement · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A particle of mass 1.5 kg starts from rest at a point O and moves along a straight line. When its displacement from O is x m, the only force acting on it is 3/(x + 1)² N directed away from O. Its speed is v m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that v² = 4x/(x + 1). [4]
  2. (b) Find the speed of the particle when x = 3, and state the value that the speed approaches as x increases. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 1.5v dv/dx = 3/(x + 1)²; M1 v²/2 = −2/(x + 1) + c; A1 c = 2; A1 v² = 4 − 4/(x + 1) = 4x/(x + 1).
  2. (b) √3 ≈ 1.73 m s⁻¹; approaches 2 m s⁻¹
    B1 v² = 12/4 = 3; B1 v → 2.
Q28Force depending on displacement · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A particle of mass 1 kg moves along the positive x-axis. It is projected from the origin O with speed 5 m s⁻¹ in the positive x-direction. When its displacement from O is x m, it is acted on by a single force of magnitude (2x + 3x²) N directed towards O. Its speed is v m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that v² = 25 − 2x² − 2x³. [3]
  2. (b) Find the distance from O at which the particle first comes to instantaneous rest, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 v dv/dx = −(2x + 3x²); M1 v²/2 = −x² − x³ + c; A1 c = 12.5.
  2. (b) 2.03 m
    M1 x³ + x² = 12.5; M1 numerical method, e.g. f(x) = x³ + x² − 12.5 changes sign between 2.025 and 2.035 (or iteration); A1 2.03 m.
Q29Force depending on displacement · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A probe of mass m kg is launched vertically upwards from the surface of the Earth with speed 4000 m s⁻¹. When the probe is at distance x m from the centre of the Earth, the gravitational force on it is mgR²/x² N, where R = 6.4 × 10⁶ m is the radius of the Earth and g = 10 m s⁻². Air resistance is ignored. The speed of the probe is v m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that, while the probe is moving upwards, v dv/dx = −gR²/x². [1]
  2. (b) Find the greatest height above the Earth's surface reached by the probe. Give your answer in kilometres, correct to 3 significant figures. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    B1 mv dv/dx = −mgR²/x², divide by m.
  2. (b) 914 km
    M1 integrate: v²/2 = gR²/x + c; M1 at x = R, v = 4000: c = 8 × 10⁶ − gR; M1 v = 0: gR²/X = gR − 8 × 10⁶; A1 X = 7.314 × 10⁶ m; A1 height X − R = 914 km.
Q30Force depending on displacement · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 9 marks

A particle of mass 2 kg moves along the positive x-axis. It is released from rest at the point where x = 2. When its displacement from the origin O is x m, the only force acting on it is an attraction towards O of magnitude 32/x³ N. Its speed is v m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that v² = 4(4 − x²)/x². [3]
  2. (b) Find the speed of the particle when x = 1. [1]
  3. (c) Find the time taken for the particle to move from x = 2 to x = 1. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 2v dv/dx = −32/x³; M1 v²/2 = 8/x² + c; A1 c = −2 from v = 0 at x = 2, so v² = 16/x² − 4 = 4(4 − x²)/x².
  2. (b) √12 ≈ 3.46 m s⁻¹
    B1 v² = 12, v = 2√3 = 3.46 m s⁻¹.
  3. (c) √3/2 ≈ 0.866 s
    M1 dx/dt = −2√(4 − x²)/x (moving towards O); M1 t = ∫₁² x/(2√(4 − x²)) dx; M1 = [−(1/2)√(4 − x²)]₁²; A1 (1/2)√3; A1 0.866 s.
