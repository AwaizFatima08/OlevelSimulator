Q1Oblique collisions of spheres · Core · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

The spheres are smooth and have equal radii. The line of centres is the line joining their centres at the instant of impact.

  1. (a) Sphere A, of mass 2 kg, moving with speed 5 m s⁻¹, strikes sphere B, of mass 3 kg, which is at rest. Before impact, A's velocity makes an angle of 60° with the line of centres. The coefficient of restitution is 0.5. Find the speed of B after the collision. [4]
  2. (b) Find the speed of A after the collision and the angle its new direction makes with the line of centres. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.50 m s⁻¹
    B1 component of A along line 5cos 60° = 2.5; M1 momentum 2(2.5) = 2v_A + 3v_B; M1 restitution v_B − v_A = 0.5(2.5); A1 v_B = 1.50.
  2. (b) 4.34 m s⁻¹ at 86.7° to the line of centres
    B1 v_A = 0.250, perpendicular component unchanged 5sin 60° = 4.33; M1 speed √(v_A² + 4.33²); A1 4.34 m s⁻¹, 86.7°.
Q2Oblique collisions of spheres · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The spheres are smooth and have equal radii. The line of centres is the line joining their centres at the instant of impact.

  1. (a) Two spheres A and B each have mass m. B is at rest and A moves with speed 6 m s⁻¹ at 30° to the line of centres when they collide. The collision is perfectly elastic (e = 1). Find the velocity components of A and of B after the collision, along and perpendicular to the line of centres. [4]
  2. (b) Show that A and B move off at right angles to each other, and that kinetic energy is conserved. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: 0 along, 3 m s⁻¹ perpendicular; B: 3√3 m s⁻¹ along the line, 0 perpendicular
    B1 components of A: 6cos 30° = 3√3 along, 3 perpendicular; M1 momentum m(3√3) = mv_A + mv_B; M1 v_B − v_A = 3√3; A1 v_A = 0, v_B = 3√3.
  2. (b) Shown
    B1 A moves perpendicular to the line, B along it; B1 ½m(27 + 9) = ½m(9) + ½m(27).
Q3Oblique collisions of spheres · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) Spheres A and B have equal mass. B is at rest. A moves with speed u in a direction making an angle α with the line of centres, where tan α = ¾. The coefficient of restitution is ⅔. Find, in terms of u, the speeds of A and B after the collision. [6]

Q6Oblique collisions of spheres · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

The spheres are smooth and have equal radii. The line of centres is the line joining their centres at the instant of impact.

  1. (a) Sphere A, of mass m, moving with speed u, collides with sphere B, of mass 2m, which is at rest. Before impact A's direction makes an angle of 45° with the line of centres. After the impact A moves perpendicular to the line of centres. Find the coefficient of restitution e. [4]
  2. (b) Find the fraction of the original kinetic energy lost in the collision. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) e = ½
    M1 momentum along line mu/√2 = mv_A + 2mv_B; M1 restitution v_B − v_A = e u/√2; M1 v_A = 0 gives v_B = u/(2√2) and v_B = eu/√2; A1 e = ½.
  2. (b) ¼
    M1 KE after = ½m(u/√2)² + ½(2m)(u/(2√2))² = 3mu²/8; M1 loss = ½mu² − 3mu²/8 = mu²/8; A1 fraction ¼.
Q7Oblique collisions of spheres · Standard · Paper 3 style · 9 marks

The spheres are smooth and have equal radii. The line of centres is the line joining their centres at the instant of impact.

  1. (a) Sphere A, of mass 3m, and sphere B, of mass m, are moving towards each other. Immediately before impact, A has speed 4u at 60° to the line of centres and B has speed 2u at 60° to the line of centres, each velocity having a component directed towards the other sphere. The coefficient of restitution is ⅓. Find the components of the velocities of A and B along the line of centres after the impact. [5]
  2. (b) Find the speed of each sphere after the impact and the angle between the direction of motion of B and the line of centres. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: u, B: 2u (both in the original direction of A's component)
    B1 along-line components 2u and −u (taking A→B as positive); M1 3m(2u) + m(−u) = 3mv_A + mv_B; M1 v_B − v_A = ⅓(2u + u); A1 v_A = u; A1 v_B = 2u.
  2. (b) A: √13 u, B: √7 u; B at 40.9°
    B1 perpendicular components 2√3u (A) and √3u (B) unchanged; M1 speeds by Pythagoras; A1 √13u and √7u; A1 tan⁻¹(√3/2) = 40.9°.

Q18Oblique collisions of spheres · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

The spheres are smooth and have equal radii. The line of centres is the line joining their centres at the instant of impact.

  1. (a) Sphere A moves with speed u at an angle α to the line of centres and strikes an identical sphere B at rest. The coefficient of restitution is e (e < 1). After the impact A moves at an angle β to the line of centres. Show that tan β = 2tan α/(1 − e). [4]
  2. (b) Given that α = 20° and β = 45°, find e correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why β > α for every value of e with 0 ≤ e < 1. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 momentum u cos α = v_A + v_B; M1 v_B − v_A = eu cos α; A1 v_A = ½(1 − e)u cos α; A1 tan β = u sin α / v_A (AG).
  2. (b) 0.272
    M1 1 − e = 2tan 20°; A1 e = 0.272.
  3. (c) 2/(1 − e) > 1 so tan β > tan α
    B1 2/(1 − e) ≥ 2 > 1 and tan is increasing on (0°, 90°).
Q19Oblique collisions of spheres · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

  1. (a) Sphere A, of mass m, moving with speed u at 30° to the line of centres, strikes sphere B, of mass M, which is at rest. The coefficient of restitution is e. After impact, A moves at right angles to its original direction. Show that M/m = 4/(3e − 1). [6]

    B1 3e − 1 > 0 so e > ⅓; B1 4/(3e − 1) is least when e = 1, giving 2.

Q25Collision with a barrier · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The sphere is small and smooth, and the barrier (wall, cushion or plane) is smooth and fixed.

  1. (a) A small smooth sphere falls vertically and hits a smooth plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal, with speed 6 m s⁻¹ just before impact. The coefficient of restitution is 0.5. Find the components of the sphere's velocity parallel and perpendicular to the plane immediately after impact. [3]
  2. (b) Find the speed of the sphere immediately after impact and the angle its velocity makes with the plane. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Parallel: 3 m s⁻¹ down the line of greatest slope; perpendicular: 2.60 m s⁻¹ away from the plane
    B1 parallel component 6sin 30° = 3 unchanged; B1 normal component 6cos 30° = 3√3; B1 after: 0.5 × 3√3 = 1.5√3 = 2.60.
  2. (b) 3.97 m s⁻¹ at 40.9° to the plane
    M1 √(3² + (1.5√3)²) = √15.75; A1 3.97; A1 tan⁻¹(1.5√3/3) = 40.9°.
Q26Collision with a barrier · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

The sphere is small and smooth, and the barrier (wall, cushion or plane) is smooth and fixed.

  1. (a) A sphere moving on a smooth horizontal table at 4 m s⁻¹ hits a straight smooth cushion and rebounds with speed 3 m s⁻¹. The coefficient of restitution is 0.5. Find the angle between the sphere's original path and the cushion. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 49.8°
    B1 components 4cos α and 4sin α; B1 after: 4cos α and 2sin α; M1 16cos²α + 4sin²α = 9; M1 16 − 12sin²α = 9, sin²α = 7/12; A1 α = 49.8°.
Q27Collision with a barrier · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The sphere is small and smooth, and the barrier (wall, cushion or plane) is smooth and fixed.

  1. (a) A sphere hits a smooth wall with its path making an angle α with the wall. The coefficient of restitution is e. Show that the fraction of kinetic energy lost is (1 − e²)sin²α. [3]
  2. (b) Given that e = 0.6 and that 36% of the kinetic energy is lost, find α. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 components v cos α (unchanged) and v sin α → ev sin α; M1 loss ∝ v²sin²α − e²v²sin²α; A1 divide by v² (AG).
  2. (b) 48.6°
    M1 0.64 sin²α = 0.36; A1 sin α = 0.75; A1 α = 48.6°.
Q28Collision with a barrier · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

The sphere is small and smooth, and the barrier (wall, cushion or plane) is smooth and fixed.

  1. (a) A ball of mass 0.15 kg, modelled as a small smooth sphere, slides at 10 m s⁻¹ across smooth horizontal ice and strikes a straight vertical barrier, its path making an angle α with the barrier. The coefficient of restitution is 0.64. After the impact the ball moves at right angles to its original path. Show that tan²α = 1/e and find α. [5]
  2. (b) Find the magnitude of the impulse exerted by the barrier on the ball. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) α = 51.3°
    B1 before (10cos α, −10sin α); B1 after (10cos α, 10e sin α); M1 scalar product zero: 100cos²α − 100e sin²α = 0; A1 tan²α = 1/e (AG); A1 tan α = 1.25, α = 51.3°.
  2. (b) 1.92 N s
    M1 0.15 × 10sin α × (1 + 0.64); A1 1.92 N s.
Q29Direct collisions & energy · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Two smooth spheres A (mass 3 kg) and B (mass 2 kg), of equal radius, move towards each other along the same straight line with speeds 4 m s⁻¹ and 1 m s⁻¹ respectively, and collide directly. The coefficient of restitution is 0.6. Find the velocities of A and B after the collision. [4]
  2. (b) Find the kinetic energy lost in the collision. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: 0.800 m s⁻¹, B: 3.80 m s⁻¹, both in A's original direction
    M1 3(4) + 2(−1) = 3v_A + 2v_B; M1 v_B − v_A = 0.6(5) = 3; A1 v_A = 0.8; A1 v_B = 3.8.
  2. (b) 9.60 J
    M1 (24 + 1) − (0.96 + 14.44); A1 9.60 J.
Q30Direct collisions & energy · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Three smooth spheres A, B and C, of equal radius and masses m, 2m and 4m, lie at rest in a straight line on a smooth horizontal surface, in that order. A is projected towards B with speed u. The coefficient of restitution between each pair is e. Find, in terms of e and u, the velocities of A and B after their collision. [4]
  2. (b) B then collides with C. Find the value of e for which B is brought to rest by this collision, and show that A is then also at rest. [4]
  3. (c) For this value of e, find the total kinetic energy lost in terms of m and u. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) v_A = (1 − 2e)u/3, v_B = (1 + e)u/3
    M1 mu = mv_A + 2mv_B; M1 v_B − v_A = eu; A1 v_A; A1 v_B.
  2. (b) e = ½; v_A = 0
    M1 B–C collision: 2m v_B = 2m w_B + 4m w_C, w_C − w_B = e v_B; M1 w_B = (2 − 4e)v_B/6; A1 w_B = 0 ⇔ e = ½; A1 v_A = (1 − 1)u/3 = 0.
  3. (c) 3mu²/8
    M1 v_B = u/2, w_C = (1 + e)v_B/3 = u/4; only C moves: ½(4m)(u/4)² = mu²/8; A1 loss ½mu² − mu²/8 = 3mu²/8.
