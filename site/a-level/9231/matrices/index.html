Q1Invariant points & lines · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The matrix M = (3 1; 2 2) represents a transformation T of the x-y plane. Find the equations of the invariant lines of T that pass through the origin. [4]
  2. (b) Find the image of the point (2, 2) under T. Hence state the factor by which T multiplies the distance from the origin of each point on y = x. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = x and y = −2x
    M1 image of (x, mx) is (3x + mx, 2x + 2mx); M1 2 + 2m = m(3 + m); A1 m² + m − 2 = 0; A1 m = 1 or m = −2, so y = x and y = −2x.
  2. (b) (8, 8); factor 4
    B1 (3 × 2 + 2, 2 × 2 + 2 × 2) = (8, 8); B1 factor 4.
Q2Invariant points & lines · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The transformation S of the plane is represented by the matrix (1 4; 1 1). Find the invariant lines of S through the origin, giving your answers in the form y = mx. [4]
  2. (b) Show that the line x = 0 is not invariant under S. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = ½x and y = −½x
    M1 image of (x, mx) is (x + 4mx, x + mx); M1 1 + m = m(1 + 4m); A1 4m² = 1; A1 m = ±½.
  2. (b) Shown
    B1 (0, 1) maps to (4, 1), which is not on x = 0.
Q3Invariant points & lines · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The matrix M = (3 −2; 1 0) represents the transformation T. Show that every point on the line y = x is an invariant point of T. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the other invariant line of T through the origin. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the points on the line in part (b) are not invariant points. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 M(t, t)ᵀ = (3t − 2t, t)ᵀ; A1 = (t, t)ᵀ for all t.
  2. (b) y = ½x
    M1 image of (x, mx) is ((3 − 2m)x, x); M1 1 = m(3 − 2m) gives 2m² − 3m + 1 = 0; A1 m = 1 (already found) or m = ½: y = ½x.
  3. (c) (2, 1) maps to (4, 2): the line maps to itself but each point moves (distances from O are doubled).
    B1 e.g. M(2, 1)ᵀ = (4, 2)ᵀ ≠ (2, 1)ᵀ.
Q4Invariant points & lines · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The transformation T is a reflection in the x-axis followed by a stretch, scale factor 3, parallel to the y-axis. Find the matrix M that represents T. [2]
  2. (b) State the invariant points of T and the invariant lines of T through the origin. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) M = (1 0; 0 −3)
    M1 stretch × reflection: (1 0; 0 3)(1 0; 0 −1); A1.
  2. (b) Invariant points: the x-axis; invariant lines through O: y = 0 and x = 0
    B1 points with −3y = y, i.e. y = 0; B1 y = 0; B1 x = 0 (as (0, y) maps to (0, −3y)).
Q5Invariant points & lines · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The matrix M = (4 1; 2 3) represents a transformation T of the x-y plane. Find the equations of the two invariant lines of T through the origin. [4]
  2. (b) Find the image of the point (2, −4) under T, and state the factor by which T stretches points on this line. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = x and y = −2x
    Characteristic equation (4 − λ)(3 − λ) − 2 = 0, so λ² − 7λ + 10 = 0 and λ = 2 or 5. For λ = 5: −x + y = 0 gives y = x; check M(1, 1) = (5, 5). For λ = 2: 2x + y = 0 gives y = −2x; check M(1, −2) = (2, −4). M1 eigenvalues, M1 for each eigenvector, A1 A1 equations.
  2. (b) (4, −8); factor 2
    M(2, −4) = (8 − 4, 4 − 12) = (4, −8) = 2 × (2, −4), so points on y = −2x are stretched by factor 2.
Q6Invariant points & lines · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The transformation H is represented by the matrix (1 3; 0 1). Describe H fully. [2]
  2. (b) Find the invariant points of H. [1]
  3. (c) Show that every line y = c, where c is a constant, is invariant under H, and show that the x-axis is the only invariant line of H through the origin. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shear with the x-axis invariant, (x, y) → (x + 3y, y)
    B1 shear; B1 x-axis invariant and e.g. (0, 1) maps to (3, 1).
  2. (b) All points on the x-axis (y = 0)
    B1 (x + 3y, y) = (x, y) requires y = 0.
  3. (c) Shown
    B1 (x, c) maps to (x + 3c, c), which lies on y = c; M1 for y = mx: m = m(1 + 3m) gives 3m² = 0 so m = 0; A1 x = 0 is not invariant since (0, 1) maps to (3, 1).

  2. (b) Find the equation of the other invariant line through the origin. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = −3/2
    M1 image of (1, 2) is (a + 4, 5); M1 5 = 2(a + 4); A1 a = −3/2.

Q9Invariant points & lines · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The transformation T is represented by M = (2 −1; 1 0). Show that every point on y = x is invariant under T. [1]
  2. (b) Show that y = x is the only invariant line of T that passes through the origin. [3]
  3. (c) Show that every line y = x + c is invariant under T. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    B1 M(t, t)ᵀ = (2t − t, t)ᵀ = (t, t)ᵀ.
  2. (b) Shown
    M1 image of (x, mx) is ((2 − m)x, x); M1 1 = m(2 − m) gives (m − 1)² = 0, so m = 1 only; A1 and x = 0 is not invariant since (0, 1) maps to (−1, 0).
  3. (c) Shown
    M1 (x, x + c) maps to (x − c, x); A1 x = (x − c) + c, so the image lies on y = x + c.
Q10Invariant points & lines · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The transformation T is represented by N = (0 4; 1 3). Find the equations of the two invariant lines of T through the origin. [4]
  2. (b) Hence find the image of the point (8, 8) under T. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = x and x + 4y = 0
    Characteristic equation λ² − 3λ − 4 = 0, so λ = 4 or −1. For λ = 4: N(x, y) = (4y, x + 3y) = 4(x, y) gives x = y. For λ = −1: 4y = −x, so x + 4y = 0. M1 eigenvalues, M1 and M1 for the eigenvectors, A1 A1 equations.
  2. (b) (32, 32)
    (8, 8) lies on y = x, which has eigenvalue 4, so the image is 4(8, 8) = (32, 32). Check: N(8, 8) = (32, 32).
Q11Invariant points & lines · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The matrix S = (2 1; 0 2) represents a transformation. Show that y = 0 is the only invariant line of S through the origin. [4]
  2. (b) Find all the invariant points of S. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Proof (see working).
    Lines through the origin: y = mx. A direction (1, m) is invariant if S(1, m) = (2 + m, 2m) = λ(1, m), so λ = 2 + m and 2m = λm = (2 + m)m, giving m² = 0 and m = 0. The vertical line x = 0 is not invariant because S(0, 1) = (1, 2) is not parallel to (0, 1). M1 setting up y = mx, M1 eigenvalue condition, A1 m = 0, A1 vertical line excluded.
  2. (b) Only the origin (0, 0)
    S(x, y) = (x, y) requires 2y = y, so y = 0, and 2x + y = x, so x = 0.
Q12Invariant points & lines · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The transformation T is a reflection in the line y = √3 x. Write down the matrix that represents T, giving exact values. [2]
  2. (b) State the invariant lines of T through the origin. [2]
  3. (c) Show that every line of the form y = −x/√3 + c is invariant under T, but that its points other than the one on the mirror line are not invariant. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (−½ √3/2; √3/2 ½)
    M1 mirror at 60° to the x-axis, matrix (cos 120° sin 120°; sin 120° −cos 120°); A1.
  2. (b) y = √3 x (a line of invariant points) and y = −x/√3
    B1 the mirror line; B1 the line through O perpendicular to it.
  3. (c) Shown
    M1 apply the matrix to a general point (x, c − x/√3): image X = −x + (√3/2)c, Y = x/√3 + c/2; M1 −X/√3 + c = x/√3 − c/2 + c = Y, so the image lies on the same line; A1 X = x only when x = √3c/4, which is the point where the line meets the mirror y = √3x, so all other points on the line move.
Q13Invariant points & lines · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The transformation T is represented by M = (2 1; 2 3). Find the line of invariant points of T. [2]
  2. (b) Find all the invariant lines of T, including those that do not pass through the origin. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −x

Q14Invariant points & lines · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

Matrices are written row by row, with rows separated by semicolons; for example (1 2; 3 4) has first row 1, 2 and second row 3, 4.

  1. (a) The matrix M = (3 p; 1 1), where p is a constant, represents a transformation T. Show that the line y = mx is invariant under T if and only if pm² + 2m − 1 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Find the value of p for which T has exactly one invariant line through the origin, and state the equation of that line. [3]
  3. (c) Find the set of values of p for which T has no invariant lines through the origin. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 image of (x, mx) is ((3 + pm)x, (1 + m)x); A1 1 + m = m(3 + pm) rearranges to pm² + 2m − 1 = 0 (AG).
  2. (b) p = −1; y = x
    M1 repeated root: discriminant 4 + 4p = 0; A1 p = −1 (note p = 0 gives two lines, y = ½x and x = 0); A1 −m² + 2m − 1 = 0 gives m = 1, line y = x.
  3. (c) p < −1
    M1 4 + 4p < 0; A1 p < −1 (x = 0 is invariant only when p = 0).
Q15Invariant points & lines · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A transformation T has eigenvectors (1, 2) with eigenvalue 4, and (1, −1) with eigenvalue 1. The matrix of T is M = (a b; c d). Find M. [5]
  2. (b) Hence find the image of the point (1, 2) under T. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) M = (2 1; 2 3)
    M(1, 2) = 4(1, 2) gives a + 2b = 4 and c + 2d = 8. M(1, −1) = (1, −1) gives a − b = 1 and c − d = −1. M1 equations. Solving a + 2b = 4 and a − b = 1 gives b = 1, a = 2. Solving c + 2d = 8 and c − d = −1 gives d = 3, c = 2. M1, A1 a and b, A1 c and d, A1 check.
  2. (b) (4, 8)
    M(1, 2) = (2 + 2, 2 + 6) = (4, 8), consistent with the eigenvalue 4 for the eigenvector (1, 2).

Q24Inverse, determinant & matrix algebra · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The matrix C = (1 k 2; 0 1 k; k 0 1), where k is a constant. Show that det C = k³ − 2k + 1. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact values of k for which C is singular. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) det C = k³ − 2k + 1 (shown).
    Expanding along the first row: 1(1 − 0) − k(0 − k²) + 2(0 − k) = 1 + k³ − 2k. M1 expansion, M1 signs, A1 result.
  2. (b) k = 1, k = (−1 + √5)/2, k = (−1 − √5)/2
    k³ − 2k + 1 = (k − 1)(k² + k − 1) = 0. So k = 1 or k² + k − 1 = 0, giving k = (−1 ± √5)/2. M1 factor, M1 quadratic formula, A1 all three values.

Q26Inverse, determinant & matrix algebra · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The 2 × 2 matrices A and B have det A = 4 and det B = −3. Find det(2A). [1]
  2. (b) Find det(A⁻¹B²). [2]
  3. (c) Find det(2AB⁻¹). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16
    det(kA) = k² det A for a 2 × 2 matrix, so det(2A) = 4 × 4 = 16.
  2. (b) 9/4
    det(A⁻¹B²) = (1/4)(−3)² = 9/4.

Show answer
  1. (a) 2k − 1
    det M = 2k − 1.
  2. (b) k = 5/2 or k = −3/2
    The area of the ellipse is |det M| × π, so |2k − 1| = 4. Then 2k − 1 = 4 gives k = 5/2, and 2k − 1 = −4 gives k = −3/2. M1 use of the area scale factor, M1 absolute value, A1 both values.
Q60Area scale factor · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The matrix M = (2 −1; 4 k). Find det M in terms of k. [1]
  2. (b) A region of area 2 square units is mapped by M onto a region of area 16 square units. Find the possible values of k. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2k + 4
    det M = 2k − (−1)(4) = 2k + 4.
  2. (b) k = 2 or k = −6
    Area scale factor = 16/2 = 8, so |2k + 4| = 8. 2k + 4 = 8 gives k = 2. 2k + 4 = −8 gives k = −6.
