Q1Area enclosed · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 3(1 + cos θ), for 0 ≤ θ < 2π. Show that the area of the region enclosed by C is given by (9/2)∫ (1 + 2cos θ + cos²θ) dθ, with limits 0 and 2π. [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact area enclosed by C. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 use of ½∫r² dθ; A1 r² = 9(1 + 2cos θ + cos²θ), limits 0 to 2π (AG).
  2. (b) 27π/2
    M1 cos²θ = ½(1 + cos 2θ); M1 integrate: [θ + 2sin θ + ½θ + ¼sin 2θ] from 0 to 2π = 3π; A1 (9/2)(3π) = 27π/2.
Q2Area enclosed · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 2θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. State the polar coordinates of the end point of C other than the pole. [1]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of the region bounded by C and the half-line θ = π, and give its value correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2π, π)
    B1 r = 2π when θ = π.
  2. (b) 2π³/3 ≈ 20.7
    M1 ½∫4θ² dθ from 0 to π; A1 [2θ³/3] = 2π³/3; A1 20.7.
Q3Area enclosed · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 4 sin 2θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ ½π. Find the values of θ at which C passes through the pole. [1]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of the loop formed by C. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) θ = 0 and θ = ½π
    B1 sin 2θ = 0.
  2. (b) 2π
    M1 ½∫16 sin²2θ dθ = 4∫(1 − cos 4θ) dθ; M1 [4θ − sin 4θ] from 0 to ½π; A1 2π.
Q4Area enclosed · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A spiral has polar equation r = e^(θ/2), for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. Find the area of the region bounded by the spiral, the initial line and the half-line θ = π. Give your answer in exact form and correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) ½(e^π − 1) ≈ 11.1
    M1 ½∫e^θ dθ; M1 [½e^θ] from 0 to π; A1 ½(e^π − 1) = 11.1.
Q5Area enclosed · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A garden designer in Islamabad models the edge of a flower bed by the curve r = 2 + sin θ, for 0 ≤ θ < 2π, where r is in metres. Show that r > 0 for all θ and state the greatest and least values of r. [2]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of the flower bed. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Greatest 3 (at θ = ½π), least 1 (at θ = 3π/2)
    B1 since −1 ≤ sin θ ≤ 1, 1 ≤ r ≤ 3 so r > 0; B1 both values with θ.
  2. (b) 9π/2 m²
    M1 ½∫(4 + 4sin θ + sin²θ) dθ; M1 sin²θ = ½(1 − cos 2θ); M1 [4θ − 4cos θ + ½θ − ¼sin 2θ] from 0 to 2π = 9π; A1 9π/2.
Q6Area enclosed · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 1 + tan θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ ¼π. Show that r² = sec²θ + 2tan θ. [1]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of the region bounded by C, the initial line and the half-line θ = ¼π. Give your answer in the form a + b ln 2. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    B1 (1 + tan θ)² = 1 + tan²θ + 2tan θ = sec²θ + 2tan θ (AG).
  2. (b) ½ + ½ ln 2
    M1 ½∫(sec²θ + 2tan θ) dθ; A1 [tan θ + 2ln sec θ]; M1 limits: 1 + 2ln √2 = 1 + ln 2; A1 ½(1 + ln 2).
Q7Area enclosed · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 2sin θ √(cos θ), for 0 ≤ θ ≤ ½π. Use the substitution u = sin θ to find the exact area of the region enclosed by C. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2/3
    M1 area = ½∫4sin²θ cos θ dθ; M1 du = cos θ dθ, limits 0 to 1; M1 2∫u² du = [2u³/3]; A1 2/3.
Q8Area enclosed · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The curves C₁ and C₂ have polar equations r = 3cos θ and r = 1 + cos θ respectively, for −½π ≤ θ ≤ ½π. Find the polar coordinates of the points of intersection of C₁ and C₂, other than the pole. [2]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of the region that is inside C₁ and outside C₂. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (3/2, π/3) and (3/2, −π/3)
    M1 3cos θ = 1 + cos θ gives cos θ = ½; A1 both points.
  2. (b) π
    M1 area = 2 × ½∫[9cos²θ − (1 + cos θ)²] dθ from 0 to π/3; M1 integrand 3 + 4cos 2θ − 2cos θ; A1 [3θ + 2sin 2θ − 2sin θ]; M1 limits: π + √3 − √3; A1 π.
Q9Area enclosed · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The circles C₁ and C₂ have polar equations r = 4sin θ and r = 4cos θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ ½π. Find the polar coordinates of the point, other than the pole, where they meet. [2]
  2. (b) Find the exact area of the region common to the interiors of both circles. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2√2, ¼π)
    M1 tan θ = 1; A1 r = 4/√2 = 2√2.
  2. (b) 2π − 4
    M1 by symmetry area = 2 × ½∫16 sin²θ dθ from 0 to ¼π; M1 = 8∫(1 − cos 2θ) dθ; A1 [8θ − 4sin 2θ]; M1 limits 2π − 4; A1 2π − 4.
Q10Area enclosed · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = k(2 + cos θ), for 0 ≤ θ < 2π, where k is a positive constant. Show that the area enclosed by C is 9πk²/2. [4]
  2. (b) The area enclosed by C is 36π. Find the exact value of k and the greatest distance of a point on C from the pole. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 ½k²∫(4 + 4cos θ + cos²θ) dθ; M1 cos²θ = ½(1 + cos 2θ); A1 integral over [0, 2π] = 8π + π = 9π; A1 9πk²/2 (AG).
  2. (b) k = 2√2; greatest distance 6√2
    M1 9πk²/2 = 36π; A1 k² = 8, k = 2√2; B1 r max = 3k = 6√2 at θ = 0.
Q11Area enclosed · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = (1 + cos θ)√(sin θ), for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. Show that the area of the region enclosed by C is 4/3. [5]
  2. (b) Find the value of θ, for 0 < θ < π, at which r = 0 other than at the end points, or show that there is none. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4/3 (shown)
    M1 ½∫(1 + cos θ)² sin θ dθ; M1 substitution u = 1 + cos θ, du = −sin θ dθ, limits 2 → 0; A1 ½∫u² du from 0 to 2; M1 ½[u³/3]; A1 4/3 (AG).
  2. (b) None: r = 0 only when θ = 0 or θ = π
    B1 sin θ > 0 and 1 + cos θ > 0 for 0 < θ < π.
Q12Area enclosed · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r² = 9cos 2θ. It consists of two loops, one for −¼π ≤ θ ≤ ¼π and one for ¾π ≤ θ ≤ 5π/4. Show that the total area enclosed by C is 9. [3]
  2. (b) The circle D has polar equation r = 3/√2. Find the values of θ in −¼π ≤ θ ≤ ¼π at which D meets C. [2]
  3. (c) Find the exact total area of the parts of the region enclosed by C that lie outside D. Give the answer correct to 3 significant figures as well. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9 (shown)
    M1 one loop = ½∫9cos 2θ dθ from −¼π to ¼π; A1 = 9/2; A1 total 9 (AG).
  2. (b) θ = ±π/6
    M1 9cos 2θ = 9/2; A1 cos 2θ = ½, θ = ±π/6.
  3. (c) 9√3/2 − 3π/2 ≈ 3.08
    M1 one loop: ½∫(9cos 2θ − 9/2) dθ from −π/6 to π/6; A1 [9/4 sin 2θ − 9θ/4] gives 9√3/4 − 3π/4; M1 double for two loops; A1 9√3/2 − 3π/2 = 3.08.
Q13Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 3 sin 2θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ ½π. Find the polar coordinates of the point of C that is furthest from the pole. [2]
  2. (b) State the equations of the tangents to C at the pole. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (3, ¼π)
    M1 sin 2θ = 1 or dr/dθ = 6cos 2θ = 0; A1 θ = ¼π, r = 3.
  2. (b) θ = 0 and θ = ½π
    M1 r = 0 when sin 2θ = 0; A1 both.
Q14Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 4(1 + cos θ), for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. Show that, at a point of C where the tangent is parallel to the initial line, 2cos²θ + cos θ − 1 = 0. [3]
  2. (b) Hence find the polar coordinates of this point, for 0 < θ < π, and its exact distance from the initial line. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 y = r sin θ = 4sin θ + 2sin 2θ; M1 dy/dθ = 4cos θ + 4cos 2θ = 0; A1 2cos²θ + cos θ − 1 = 0 (AG).
  2. (b) (6, π/3); distance 3√3
    M1 (2cos θ − 1)(cos θ + 1) = 0, cos θ = ½; A1 θ = π/3, r = 6; A1 6 sin(π/3) = 3√3.
Q15Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 2(1 − cos θ), for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. Find the polar coordinates of the point of C, with 0 < θ < π, at which the tangent is perpendicular to the initial line. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (1, π/3)
    M1 x = r cos θ = 2cos θ − 2cos²θ; M1 dx/dθ = −2sin θ + 4sin θ cos θ = 2sin θ(2cos θ − 1); M1 = 0 with sin θ ≠ 0 so cos θ = ½; A1 θ = π/3, r = 1.
Q16Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = θ sin θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. Show that, at the point of C furthest from the pole, tan θ + θ = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Verify that this equation has a root between 2 and 2.1. [2]
  3. (c) Given that the root is θ = 2.029 correct to 3 decimal places, find the greatest distance of a point on C from the pole, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 dr/dθ = sin θ + θ cos θ = 0; A1 divide by cos θ: tan θ + θ = 0 (AG).
  2. (b) tan 2 + 2 = −0.185 < 0, tan 2.1 + 2.1 = 0.390 > 0
    M1 evaluate at both ends; A1 change of sign (continuous on [2, 2.1]) so a root.
  3. (c) 1.82
    M1 r = θ sin θ at θ = 2.029; A1 1.82.
Q17Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 3 + 2cos θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. Show that at the point P of C furthest from the initial line, 4cos²θ + 3cos θ − 2 = 0. [3]
  2. (b) Find the polar coordinates of P and its distance from the initial line, giving values correct to 3 significant figures. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 y = 3sin θ + 2sin θ cos θ = 3sin θ + sin 2θ; M1 dy/dθ = 3cos θ + 2cos 2θ = 0; A1 3cos θ + 4cos²θ − 2 = 0 (AG).
  2. (b) P = (3.85, 1.13); distance 3.48
    M1 cos θ = (−3 + √41)/8 = 0.4254 (reject negative root < −1); A1 θ = 1.13; A1 r = 3.85; A1 r sin θ = 3.48.
Q18Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 1 + 2cos θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ ⅔π. State the equation of the tangent to C at the pole. [1]
  2. (b) Show that at the point of C furthest from the initial line, 4cos²θ + cos θ − 2 = 0. [3]
  3. (c) Hence find this greatest distance from the initial line, correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) θ = ⅔π
    B1 r = 0 when cos θ = −½.
  2. (b) Shown
    M1 y = sin θ + sin 2θ; M1 dy/dθ = cos θ + 2cos 2θ = 0; A1 4cos²θ + cos θ − 2 = 0 (AG).
  3. (c) 1.76
    M1 cos θ = (−1 + √33)/8 = 0.5931; M1 θ = 0.936, r = 2.19; A1 r sin θ = 1.76.
Q19Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 1 + θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π. Show that the point of C furthest from the initial line satisfies tan θ = −(1 + θ). [3]
  2. (b) Verify that this equation has a root α between 1.9 and 1.95. [2]
  3. (c) Use α = 1.903 to find the greatest distance of a point of C from the initial line, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 y = (1 + θ)sin θ; M1 dy/dθ = sin θ + (1 + θ)cos θ = 0; A1 divide by cos θ (AG).
  2. (b) f(θ) = tan θ + 1 + θ: f(1.9) = −0.027, f(1.95) = 0.441
    M1 values at both ends; A1 sign change, f continuous on the interval.
  3. (c) 2.74
    M1 (1 + α)sin α; A1 2.74.
Q20Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = a + cos θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ π, where a is a positive constant. Show that dx/dθ = −sin θ(a + 2cos θ), where x = r cos θ. [2]
  2. (b) Find the set of values of a for which C has a point with 0 < θ < π at which the tangent is perpendicular to the initial line. [2]
  3. (c) For a = 1, find the polar coordinates of this point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 x = a cos θ + cos²θ; A1 dx/dθ = −a sin θ − 2sin θ cos θ (AG).
  2. (b) 0 < a < 2
    M1 need cos θ = −a/2 with −1 < cos θ < 1; A1 0 < a < 2.
  3. (c) (½, ⅔π)
    M1 cos θ = −½, θ = ⅔π; A1 r = 1 − ½ = ½.
Q21Greatest distance & tangent conditions · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 4 sin θ cos²θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ ½π. Show that the greatest value of r occurs where tan²θ = ½. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact greatest value of r, and the value of θ at which it occurs correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 dr/dθ = 4(cos³θ − 2sin²θ cos θ); M1 = 4cos θ(cos²θ − 2sin²θ) = 0; A1 cos θ ≠ 0 at a maximum inside the range, so tan²θ = ½ (AG).
  2. (b) r = 8√3/9; θ = 0.615
    M1 sin θ = 1/√3, cos²θ = ⅔; A1 r = 4(1/√3)(⅔) = 8√3/9; A1 θ = tan⁻¹(1/√2) = 0.615.
Q22Sketching polar curves · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 2(1 − cos θ), for 0 ≤ θ < 2π. Copy and complete the table of values: θ = 0, ½π, π, 3π/2. [2]
  2. (b) Sketch C, labelling the points where it crosses the initial line and the line θ = ½π. Describe its symmetry and its shape at the pole. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) r = 0, 2, 4, 2
    B1 two correct; B1 all four.
  2. (b) Cardioid, symmetric about the initial line, with a cusp at the pole pointing along θ = 0; passes through (2, ½π), (4, π), (2, 3π/2)
    B1 closed heart shape with r largest (4) at θ = π; B1 symmetry about the initial line; B1 cusp at the pole, tangent θ = 0.
Q23Sketching polar curves · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the spiral r = 3θ for 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π, marking the polar coordinates of the points where it meets the lines θ = ½π, θ = π and θ = 2π. [3]
  2. (b) State the distance between the two points where the spiral crosses the initial line (including the pole). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (3π/2, ½π), (3π, π), (6π, 2π); r increases steadily from 0
    B1 spiral starting at the pole, tangent θ = 0 there; B1 r increasing with θ, going anticlockwise; B1 three points correct.
  2. (b) 6π
    B1 r = 0 at θ = 0 and r = 6π at θ = 2π.
Q24Sketching polar curves · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 2cos 3θ, where r ≥ 0. Find the intervals of θ in 0 ≤ θ < 2π for which r ≥ 0. [3]
  2. (b) Sketch C, stating the greatest value of r and the directions of the lines of symmetry of its loops. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0 ≤ θ ≤ π/6, π/2 ≤ θ ≤ 5π/6, 7π/6 ≤ θ ≤ 3π/2, 11π/6 ≤ θ < 2π
    M1 cos 3θ ≥ 0 when 3θ in [0, π/2], [3π/2, 5π/2], …; A1 at least two intervals; A1 all.
  2. (b) Three equal loops (petals), r max = 2 along θ = 0, ⅔π, 4π/3; tangents at the pole θ = π/6, π/2, 5π/6, 7π/6, 3π/2, 11π/6
    B1 three loops; B1 tips at distance 2 along θ = 0, ⅔π, 4π/3; B1 loops meet only at the pole with correct tangents.
Q25Sketching polar curves · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 4cos 2θ, for −¼π ≤ θ ≤ ¼π and ¾π ≤ θ ≤ 5π/4. Show that r ≥ 0 throughout these intervals and find the tangents to C at the pole. [3]
  2. (b) Sketch C and state the polar coordinates of the points of C furthest from the pole. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tangents θ = ±¼π (equivalently θ = ¾π, 5π/4)
    B1 2θ lies in [−½π, ½π] or [3π/2, 5π/2] so cos 2θ ≥ 0; M1 r = 0 when cos 2θ = 0; A1 θ = ±¼π, ¾π, 5π/4.
  2. (b) Two loops along the initial line, tips at (4, 0) and (4, π)
    B1 two loops through the pole, symmetric about the initial line; B1 correct tangents at the pole; B1 (4, 0) and (4, π).
Q26Sketching polar curves · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 2 + cos 2θ, for 0 ≤ θ < 2π. Find the greatest and least values of r and the values of θ where they occur. [3]
  2. (b) Show that C is symmetric about both the initial line and the line θ = ½π. [2]
  3. (c) Sketch C. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Greatest 3 at θ = 0, π; least 1 at θ = ½π, 3π/2
    M1 dr/dθ = −2sin 2θ = 0; A1 r = 3 at θ = 0, π; A1 r = 1 at θ = ½π, 3π/2.
  2. (b) Shown
    B1 replacing θ by −θ leaves r unchanged; B1 replacing θ by π − θ leaves r unchanged.
  3. (c) Closed curve not passing through the pole: r = 3 on the initial line (θ = 0, π) and r = 1 on θ = ½π, 3π/2; it is pinched in (concave, a 'peanut' shape) where it crosses θ = ½π and θ = 3π/2, and symmetric about both lines
    B1 closed curve, r > 0 throughout, with correct extremes 3 and 1 on the correct lines; B1 both symmetries shown and the curve dented inwards (not oval) near θ = ½π and 3π/2.
Q27Sketching polar curves · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) Find the greatest value of r and sketch C, stating the equations of the tangents at the pole. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) π/3 ≤ θ ≤ 5π/3
    M1 cos θ ≤ ½; A1 π/3 ≤ θ ≤ 5π/3.
  2. (b) r max = 3 at θ = π; tangents θ = π/3 and θ = 5π/3
    B1 r max = 3 at θ = π; B1 tangents θ = π/3, 5π/3; B1 closed loop through the pole, lying mainly to the left of the pole; B1 symmetric about the initial line.
Q28Polar-Cartesian conversion · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r = 4cos θ + 2sin θ. Find the Cartesian equation of C. [2]
  2. (b) Hence show that C is a circle and state its centre and radius. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x² + y² = 4x + 2y
    M1 multiply by r: r² = 4r cos θ + 2r sin θ; A1 x² + y² = 4x + 2y.
  2. (b) Centre (2, 1), radius √5
    M1 (x − 2)² + (y − 1)² = 5; A1 centre and radius.
Q29Polar-Cartesian conversion · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The curve C has polar equation r(1 − cos θ) = 2. Show that the Cartesian equation of C is y² = 4x + 4. [3]
  2. (b) Find the polar coordinates of the point of C that is closest to the pole. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 r = 2 + r cos θ = 2 + x; M1 x² + y² = (2 + x)²; A1 y² = 4x + 4 (AG).
  2. (b) (1, π)
    M1 r = 2/(1 − cos θ) least when cos θ = −1; A1 r = 1, θ = π (the point (−1, 0)).
Q30Polar-Cartesian conversion · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A curve has Cartesian equation (x² + y²)² = 4xy. Show that its polar equation is r² = 2sin 2θ. [2]
  2. (b) State the values of θ, for 0 ≤ θ < 2π, for which the curve exists, and find the total area it encloses. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 r⁴ = 4r² cos θ sin θ; A1 r² = 2sin 2θ (AG).
  2. (b) 0 ≤ θ ≤ ½π and π ≤ θ ≤ 3π/2; total area 2
    B1 sin 2θ ≥ 0 intervals; M1 one loop ½∫2sin 2θ dθ from 0 to ½π; A1 = 1; A1 total 2.
