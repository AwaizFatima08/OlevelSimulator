Q11Wilcoxon signed-rank test · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) In a Wilcoxon signed-rank test with 15 non-zero differences, P denotes the sum of the ranks of the positive differences. Find the set of values of P for which H₀: median difference = 0 is rejected in favour of H₁: median difference ≠ 0 at the 5% significance level. [4]
  2. (b) The experimenter actually obtains P = 96. One difference, with rank 7, was later found to have been recorded with the wrong sign: it should have been negative, not positive. Determine whether the conclusion of the test changes. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) P ≤ 25 or P ≥ 95
    B1 P + Q = 120; B1 critical value 25; M1 reject when min(P, 120 − P) ≤ 25; A1 P ≤ 25 or P ≥ 95.
  2. (b) Yes: corrected P = 89, so H₀ is no longer rejected
    M1 corrected P = 96 − 7 = 89, Q = 31; A1 T = 31 > 25; A1 originally T = 24 ≤ 25 (reject), now do not reject: conclusion changes.

  1. (a) A chemist in Riyadh compares the yield (g) of a reaction with a new catalyst (B, 7 trials) and the old catalyst (A, 4 trials).A: 8.2, 9.7, 7.5, 8.9B: 10.4, 9.1, 11.8, 10.9, 9.9, 12.3, 10.1Test at the 1% significance level whether the new catalyst gives a higher median yield. [6]
  2. (b) Find the smallest possible value of R_A. Given that the critical value for a 0.5% one-tailed test with these sample sizes is 10, state what the data would have to look like for H₀ to be rejected at that level. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) R_A = 11; W = 11; critical value 11; reject H₀: evidence that the new catalyst gives higher yields
    B1 H₀: equal medians, H₁: median for B is greater; M1 ranks: 7.5(A)→1, 8.2(A)→2, 8.9(A)→3, 9.1(B)→4, 9.7(A)→5, 9.9(B)→6, 10.1(B)→7, 10.4(B)→8, 10.9(B)→9, 11.8(B)→10, 12.3(B)→11; A1 R_A = 11; A1 W = 11; B1 critical value (m = 4, n = 7, 1% one-tailed) 11; A1 reject H₀; strong evidence that the new catalyst increases the median yield.
  2. (b) Smallest R_A = 1 + 2 + 3 + 4 = 10; need W = 10, i.e. all four old-catalyst yields lower than every new-catalyst yield
    B1 10; B1 W ≤ 10 only when R_A = 10, so the four A values must take ranks 1 to 4.
Q27Wilcoxon rank-sum test · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A Wilcoxon rank-sum test is to compare a sample of 20 tomato plants grown with compost and 25 grown without, using the heights after 8 weeks. The test is one-tailed at the 5% level, H₁ being that compost-grown plants tend to be taller. The plants are ranked from tallest (rank 1). Give the mean and variance of the rank sum R of the compost sample under H₀. [2]
  2. (b) Using a normal approximation with continuity correction, find the largest value of R that leads to H₀ being rejected. [4]
  3. (c) The compost plants have R = 382. State the conclusion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mean 460, variance 1916.67
    B1 20 × 46/2 = 460; B1 20 × 25 × 46/12 = 1916.67.
  2. (b) R = 387
    M1 (R + 0.5 − 460)/43.780 < −1.645; M1 R < 387.48; A1 R ≤ 387; A1 so the largest R is 387.
  3. (c) Reject H₀: evidence that compost-grown plants tend to be taller
    B1 382 ≤ 387.
Q28Choice of test & assumptions · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A researcher measures the cholesterol level of 9 patients before and after a change of diet. She considers a sign test, a Wilcoxon signed-rank test and a paired-sample t-test. State the assumption about the population of differences needed for (i) the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and (ii) the paired-sample t-test. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the Wilcoxon signed-rank test is generally preferred to the sign test when its assumption holds. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) the differences have a symmetric distribution; (ii) the differences are normally distributed
    B1 symmetric; B1 normal.
  2. (b) It uses the magnitudes of the differences as well as their signs, so it uses more information and is more powerful
    B1 uses sizes (ranks) of differences, not just signs; B1 more powerful / more likely to detect a real difference.
Q29Choice of test & assumptions · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) For each situation, name the most suitable non-parametric test and give a reason.(i) The weights of 8 randomly chosen goats from one farm and 10 from another farm are compared; nothing is known about the distribution of weights.(ii) 25 people each rate two films as 'better' or 'worse' than the other, and only the preferences are recorded.(iii) The times of 12 runners on two different tracks are compared; the differences can be assumed to have a symmetric distribution. [3]
  2. (b) In (iii), a student instead uses a sign test. Explain why this is valid but less appropriate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Wilcoxon rank-sum test: two independent samples; (ii) sign test: only the direction of each preference is known; (iii) Wilcoxon signed-rank test: paired data with symmetric differences
    B1 each test with a valid reason.
  2. (b) The sign test needs no symmetry assumption so is valid, but it ignores the sizes of the differences and is less powerful
    B1 valid but wastes information.
Q30Choice of test & assumptions · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) The differences (new method − old method) in the yield of a reaction for 10 paired trials are:3.1, −0.4, 2.6, 1.8, −0.7, 4.2, 2.2, 0.9, 3.5, −0.2Carry out a one-tailed sign test and a one-tailed Wilcoxon signed-rank test, each at the 5% significance level, to test whether the new method increases the median yield. [7]
  2. (b) Explain why the two tests can reach different conclusions, and state which you would rely on, with a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sign test: P(X ≥ 7) = 0.172, do not reject H₀; Wilcoxon: T = 6, critical value 10, reject H₀
    B1 H₀: median difference 0, H₁: > 0; M1 X ~ B(10, 0.5), X = 7; A1 P(X ≥ 7) = 0.172, do not reject; M1 ranks: −0.2→1, −0.4→2, −0.7→3, 0.9→4, 1.8→5, 2.2→6, 2.6→7, 3.1→8, 3.5→9, 4.2→10; A1 P = 49, Q = 6, T = 6; B1 critical value 10; A1 reject H₀.
  2. (b) The negative differences are all small, which the Wilcoxon test takes into account but the sign test ignores; rely on the Wilcoxon test if the differences are symmetric, otherwise the sign test
    B1 Wilcoxon uses magnitudes (small negative ranks); B1 choice with the symmetry condition.
