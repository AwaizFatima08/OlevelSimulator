Q3De Moivre: multiple angles & powers · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Use de Moivre's theorem to show that sin 3θ = 3sin θ − 4sin³θ. [4]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫ sin³θ dθ with limits 0 and ½π. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 (cos θ + i sin θ)³ = cos 3θ + i sin 3θ; M1 expand, imaginary part 3cos²θ sin θ − sin³θ; M1 cos²θ = 1 − sin²θ; A1 3sin θ − 4sin³θ (AG).
  2. (b) 2/3
    M1 sin³θ = ¼(3sin θ − sin 3θ); M1 [¼(−3cos θ + ⅓cos 3θ)] from 0 to ½π; A1 ¼(3 − ⅓) = 2/3.

Q6De Moivre: multiple angles & powers · Standard · Paper 2 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) Use de Moivre's theorem to show that tan 3θ = (3t − t³)/(1 − 3t²), where t = tan θ. [4]
  2. (b) By considering tan 3θ = 1, show that t = tan(π/12) is a root of t³ − 3t² − 3t + 1 = 0. [2]
  3. (c) Hence find the exact value of tan(π/12). [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 cos 3θ = c³ − 3cs², sin 3θ = 3c²s − s³; M1 divide; M1 by c³; A1 (AG).
  2. (b) Shown

Q26nth roots & roots of unity · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Show that the equation (z + 1)⁵ = z⁵ can be written as ((z + 1)/z)⁵ = 1 and explain why it has exactly four roots. [2]
  2. (b) Show that the roots are z = 1/(ω − 1), where ω = e^(2kπi/5), k = 1, 2, 3, 4, and deduce that each root has real part −½. [4]

Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    B1 z = 0 is not a root, so divide by z⁵; B1 (z + 1)/z = ω, a fifth root of unity with ω ≠ 1 (ω = 1 gives 1 = 0); the equation is quartic, four roots.
  2. (b) Re z = −½
    M1 z + 1 = ωz so z = 1/(ω − 1); M1 ω − 1 = e^(iφ) − 1 = 2i sin(φ/2) e^(iφ/2) with φ = 2kπ/5; M1 1/(ω − 1) = −(i/2)e^(−iφ/2)/sin(φ/2) = −½ − (i/2)cot(φ/2); A1 real part −½ (AG).
  3. (c) −½ ± 0.688i, −½ ± 0.162i
    M1 y = −½cot(kπ/5); A1 ±0.688 and ±0.162.

Q28Summing series with complex numbers · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Let z = cos θ + i sin θ. By considering the infinite geometric series 1 + ½z + ¼z² + ⅛z³ + …, show that 1 + ½cos θ + ¼cos 2θ + ⅛cos 3θ + … = (4 − 2cos θ)/(5 − 4cos θ). [6]

Show answer
  1. (a) Shown

  2. (b) 4/5
    B1 (4 − 0)/(5 − 0) = 4/5.
Q29Summing series with complex numbers · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) By considering the sum of a geometric series in z = e^(iθ), show that, for sin θ ≠ 0, Σ (r = 1 to n) cos(2r − 1)θ = sin 2nθ / (2sin θ). [6]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of cos(π/7) + cos(3π/7) + cos(5π/7). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 Σ e^((2r − 1)iθ) = e^(iθ)(1 − e^(2niθ))/(1 − e^(2iθ)); M1 = (e^(2niθ) − 1)/(e^(iθ) − e^(−iθ)); M1 denominator 2i sin θ; M1 real part of (cos 2nθ − 1 + i sin 2nθ)/(2i sin θ); A1 sin 2nθ/(2sin θ); A1 (AG) with sum of real parts = sum of cosines.
  2. (b) ½
    M1 n = 3, θ = π/7: sin(6π/7)/(2sin(π/7)); A1 sin(6π/7) = sin(π/7), so ½.
Q30Summing series with complex numbers · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Show that 1 + e^(iθ) = 2cos(½θ) e^(iθ/2). [2]
  2. (b) By considering (1 + e^(iθ))ⁿ and the binomial theorem, show that Σ (r = 0 to n) C(n, r) sin rθ = 2ⁿ cosⁿ(½θ) sin(½nθ), where C(n, r) is the binomial coefficient. [4]
  3. (c) Hence find the exact value of Σ (r = 0 to 6) C(6, r) sin(rπ/3). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 e^(iθ/2)(e^(−iθ/2) + e^(iθ/2)); A1 = 2cos(½θ)e^(iθ/2) (AG).
  2. (b) Shown
    M1 (1 + e^(iθ))ⁿ = Σ C(n, r)e^(irθ); M1 = 2ⁿcosⁿ(½θ)e^(inθ/2) by de Moivre; M1 take imaginary parts; A1 (AG).
  3. (c) 0
    M1 2⁶cos⁶(π/6) sin(π); A1 0.
