Q1Digital certificates & signatures · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by a digital certificate. [2]
  2. (b) Give four items of data that are found in a digital certificate. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) An electronic document issued by a trusted certificate authority that links (binds) a public key to the identity of its owner, so that others can trust the key belongs to that person or organisation.
    B1 issued/signed by a certificate authority. B1 confirms that a public key belongs to a named owner.
  2. (b) Any four of: owner's (subject's) name/organisation or domain; owner's public key; serial number; validity period (start and expiry dates); name of the issuing CA; the CA's digital signature; the algorithm used for the signature; certificate version.
    1 mark each for any four.
Q2Digital certificates & signatures · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the purpose of a digital signature. [2]
  2. (b) State which key is used to create a digital signature, and which key is used to check it. [2]
  3. (c) State whether a digital signature keeps the content of the message secret. Give a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) To prove that a message really came from the stated sender (authentication / non-repudiation) and that it has not been altered since it was signed (integrity).
    B1 authenticity of sender / non-repudiation. B1 integrity – message not altered.
  2. (b) Created with the sender's private key; checked with the sender's public key.
    B1 each.
  3. (c) No – the message itself is not encrypted by signing (and the signature can be checked by anyone with the public key).
    B1.
Q3Digital certificates & signatures · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe the role of a certificate authority (CA). [3]
  2. (b) Explain why a web browser can check a certificate signed by a CA without contacting that CA first. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A CA is a trusted organisation that checks the identity of people or organisations applying for certificates, issues digital certificates that link their identity to their public key, signs the certificates with its own private key, and revokes certificates that are no longer valid.
    1 mark each, max 3: trusted third party; verifies applicant's identity; issues certificates; signs them with the CA's private key; maintains revocation list / revokes certificates.
  2. (b) The browser/operating system already stores the trusted root CAs' certificates, including their public keys, so it can check the CA's signature directly.
    B1.
Q4Digital certificates & signatures · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between a digital signature and a digital certificate. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A digital signature is created by the sender for a particular message (an encrypted hash of that message) to prove who sent it and that it is unchanged. A digital certificate is issued by a CA to an owner and is used to prove that a public key belongs to that owner. The certificate itself contains the CA's digital signature, and it supplies the public key used to check the sender's signature.
    B1 signature – made by sender, unique to each message, proves origin/integrity. B1 certificate – issued by CA, proves ownership of public key. B1 link: certificate provides the trusted public key used to verify signatures / certificate is signed by the CA.
Q5Digital certificates & signatures · Core · Paper 3 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) A digital certificate issued for a school website contains an expiry date and the name of the issuing certificate authority. Explain why each of these items is included. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The expiry date limits how long the certificate can be trusted, so a certificate that was compromised or is out of date is not accepted after that date. The issuer name identifies the certificate authority, so the browser can check that the authority is trusted.
    1 mark: expiry date limits the period of trust. 1 mark: issuer identifies the trusted authority whose signature is checked.
Q6Digital certificates & signatures · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Mr Khan, a contractor in Multan, sends a signed quotation by email to a client.

  1. (a) Describe how Mr Khan's software creates the digital signature for the quotation. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the signature is different for every quotation he sends. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The quotation is put through a hashing algorithm to produce a message digest (hash). The digest is encrypted using Mr Khan's private key; this encrypted digest is the digital signature. The signature is attached to the quotation and both are sent.
    B1 hash/digest of the message produced. B1 digest encrypted with Mr Khan's private key. B1 signature sent with the message.
  2. (b) The digest depends on the content of the message, so different quotations give different digests and therefore different signatures.
    B1.
Q7Digital certificates & signatures · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A client receives a message and a digital signature. Describe how the client's software checks the signature. [4]
  2. (b) State two possible reasons why the two digests might not match. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It obtains the sender's public key (from the sender's digital certificate). It decrypts the signature with that public key to get the original message digest. It applies the same hashing algorithm to the received message to make a new digest. It compares the two digests: if they match, the message is authentic and unchanged; if not, it has been altered or did not come from the sender.
    1 mark each: public key of sender obtained (certificate); signature decrypted with sender's public key; received message hashed with the same algorithm; digests compared – match means authentic and unaltered.
  2. (b) The message was altered (tampered with or corrupted) in transmission; it was signed with a different private key (not from the claimed sender / wrong public key used).
    1 mark each.

Q10Digital certificates & signatures · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A simple hash function adds the ASCII codes of every character in a message and then takes the remainder when divided by 97. The message NOTICE is hashed and its digest is signed by the sender. The recipient receives the message N0TICE (with a zero instead of the letter O) and the original signature. Calculate the digest the recipient computes for N0TICE, and explain what the recipient does next. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The digest of N0TICE is 31 (ASCII sum 419; 419 mod 97 = 31). The original digest of NOTICE is 62. Because the digests differ, the recipient decides the message has been changed and rejects it.
    1 mark: ASCII sum 419 for N0TICE (zero is 48, letter O is 79). 1 mark: 419 mod 97 = 31. 1 mark: compares with the digest decrypted from the signature (62). 1 mark: concludes the message was altered and rejects it.
Q11Digital certificates & signatures · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A simplified example of signing uses tiny keys. The sender's private key is (d = 27, n = 55) and public key is (e = 3, n = 55).The 'digest' of a message is the sum of the ASCII codes of its characters MOD 55. The signature is digest^d MOD n; it is checked by calculating signature^e MOD n.ASCII codes: P 80, A 65, Y 89, space 32, 0 48, 5 53, 9 57.

  1. (a) Calculate the digest of the message PAY 500. [2]
  2. (b) You are told that 30²⁷ MOD 55 = 35, so the signature is 35. Show how the receiver checks this signature. [2]
  3. (c) An attacker changes the message to PAY 900 but keeps the signature 35. Show how the receiver detects this. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the attacker cannot create a new valid signature for PAY 900. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 30
    M1 80 + 65 + 89 + 32 + 53 + 48 + 48 = 415. A1 415 MOD 55 = 30.
  2. (b) 35³ = 42 875; 42 875 MOD 55 = 30, which equals the digest of the received message, so the signature is valid.
    M1 35³ MOD 55 calculated = 30. A1 compared with digest of received message – match.
  3. (c) Digest of PAY 900 = 419 MOD 55 = 34. Checking the signature still gives 30. 34 ≠ 30, so the message has been altered.
    M1 new digest 34. A1 does not match 30 → tampering detected.
  4. (d) A valid signature needs the sender's private key, which the attacker does not have.
    B1.
Q12Digital certificates & signatures · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A software company in Lahore distributes updates for its accounting app. Each update is code-signed: it carries a digital signature and the company's digital certificate.

  1. (a) Explain how code signing protects users who install an update. [3]
  2. (b) The company discovers that its private key has been stolen. Describe what should happen and why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The user's computer checks the certificate (signed by a trusted CA) to get the company's genuine public key, then verifies the update's signature with it. If the update has been changed (e.g. malware inserted) or was not produced by the company, the digests will not match and the installation is blocked/warned.
    B1 certificate verified to obtain trusted public key. B1 signature verified / digest comparison. B1 tampered or fake updates detected and not installed.
  2. (b) The company tells the CA, which revokes the certificate (adds it to its revocation list), so software will no longer trust signatures made with that key. The company generates a new key pair, obtains a new certificate and re-signs its updates. Otherwise the thief could sign malware that users' computers would accept as genuine.
    B1 certificate revoked by CA. B1 new key pair/certificate obtained. B1 reason – thief could sign malicious software as the company.
Q13Digital certificates & signatures · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a digital signature encrypts a hash of the message rather than the whole message. [2]
  2. (b) State three properties a hashing algorithm needs for use in digital signatures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A hash is short and of fixed length, so encrypting it with the slow asymmetric algorithm is much quicker than encrypting a long message; the hash still represents the whole message because any change to the message changes the hash.
    B1 hash is small/fixed length – faster to encrypt/decrypt. B1 still detects any change to the message.
  2. (b) It always gives the same digest for the same message; a tiny change in the message gives a completely different digest; it is not possible to work back from the digest to the message (one-way); it is very unlikely that two different messages give the same digest (collision resistant); it produces a fixed-length output.
    1 mark each for any three.

  2. (b) Explain why encryption does not stop data from being intercepted. [1]

  1. (a) (i) The original data/message before it is encrypted (readable). (ii) The data after it has been encrypted, which cannot be understood without decrypting it. (iii) A value used by the encryption algorithm to convert plaintext into ciphertext (and/or to decrypt it).

Q17Encryption & keys · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe symmetric encryption. [2]
  2. (b) Describe asymmetric encryption. [2]
  3. (c) Give one advantage of symmetric encryption over asymmetric encryption. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The same secret key is used to encrypt and to decrypt the data; the sender and receiver must both have this key.
    B1 same key for both. B1 key must be shared/kept secret by both parties.
  2. (b) A matching pair of keys is used: a public key, which can be given to anyone, and a private key, which is kept secret by its owner. Data encrypted with one key of the pair can only be decrypted with the other.
    B1 public and private key pair. B1 what one key encrypts only the other can decrypt / private key never shared.
  3. (c) It is faster / needs less processing, so it is better for large amounts of data.
    B1.

Q21Encryption & keys · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain the key distribution problem that occurs with symmetric encryption. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the key distribution problem does not arise in the same way with asymmetric encryption. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The secret key must be given to the receiver before any encrypted data can be sent. If the key is sent over the network it could be intercepted, and then anyone with it can decrypt all the messages.
    B1 key must be shared with the receiver. B1 it may be intercepted in transit, compromising all data.
  2. (b) Only the public key is ever sent to other people, and it can be shared openly because it cannot be used to decrypt messages encrypted with it. The private key, which does the decrypting, stays with its owner and never has to be transmitted, so there is no secret key to intercept.
    1 mark each, max 3: public key can be published/sent openly; public key cannot decrypt what it encrypted; private key is never transmitted/kept by owner; so an interceptor gains nothing useful. (Allow: the public key must still be shown to be genuine, e.g. by a digital certificate.)

Q29Encryption & keys · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A student writes: "If I encrypt a message with my private key, nobody else can read it." Explain why this statement is wrong and state what encrypting with your private key does provide. [3]

    B1 sign with sender's private key. B1 encrypt with receiver's public key (and correct decryption order described).

  1. (a) State the purpose of the Transport Layer Security (TLS) protocol. [2]
  2. (b) Give two situations where TLS is used. [2]
  3. (c) State the relationship between SSL and TLS. [1]

  1. (a) To provide secure communication between a client and a server over a network: data is encrypted so it stays private, the server (and optionally the client) is authenticated, and data integrity is checked.

  2. (b) Online banking / shopping websites (HTTPS); email between client and mail server; instant messaging; VoIP calls; logging in to accounts.
    1 mark each for two.
  3. (c) TLS is the newer, more secure protocol that replaced SSL (it was developed from SSL).
    B1.

Q35SSL/TLS protocols · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the steps of a TLS handshake when a browser connects to a secure website. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why the session key is discarded when the session ends. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The browser (client) sends a 'hello' message with the TLS versions and cipher suites (encryption methods) it supports and a random number. The server replies choosing the version and cipher suite and sends its digital certificate (containing its public key). The browser checks the certificate with the CA's public key. The client and server then agree a shared session key (e.g. the client sends secret data encrypted with the server's public key, or a key-exchange method is used). Both confirm the handshake is finished, and the rest of the session is encrypted with the symmetric session key.
    1 mark each, max 5: client hello with supported versions/ciphers; server hello chooses cipher; server sends certificate/public key; client verifies certificate; session key agreed/exchanged using asymmetric encryption; encrypted communication begins with symmetric key.
  2. (b) A new key for each session means that if one session key is discovered, only that session's data is exposed, not past or future sessions.
    B1.
Q36SSL/TLS protocols · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) TLS is made up of two layers: the handshake protocol and the record protocol. Describe the role of each. [4]
  2. (b) State which layer of the TCP/IP model TLS works between, and give the protocol formed when HTTP is used over TLS. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Handshake protocol: lets the client and server authenticate each other, agree on the encryption algorithms to use and set up the shared session keys. Record protocol: carries the application data once the handshake is done; it divides the data into blocks (records), adds a message authentication code to check integrity, and encrypts them with the session key.
    B1 handshake – authentication. B1 handshake – agree cipher/keys. B1 record – encrypts and transmits data. B1 record – integrity checking / splitting into records.
  2. (b) Between the application layer and the transport layer; HTTPS.
    B1 between application and transport (TCP). B1 HTTPS.

Q38SSL/TLS protocols · Standard · Paper 3 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) In the TLS handshake the client's hello message lists the encryption methods that the client supports. Explain why the server must choose a method from this list. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both sides must use the same encryption method to communicate. The server can only choose a method the client has said it supports; otherwise the client could not decrypt the data.

Q39SSL/TLS protocols · Standard · Paper 3 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Describe the purpose of the pre-master secret in the TLS handshake and explain why the client generates it at random. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The pre-master secret is used by both sides to produce the same session key. The client creates it at random so that an attacker who does not know it cannot work out the session key, and each session gets a different key.
    1 mark: used to create the shared session key. 1 mark: random value gives unpredictable, unique keys for each session.

Q41SSL/TLS protocols · Standard · Paper 3 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the server proves to the client that it owns the private key that matches its certificate during the TLS handshake. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The server uses its private key to decrypt the pre-master secret sent by the client. If it can do this and use the result to produce the correct messages, only the holder of the private key could have done so.

Q42SSL/TLS protocols · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

A user in Karachi connects to an online shop. The browser shows https:// and a padlock.

  1. (a) Explain why TLS uses asymmetric encryption during the handshake but symmetric encryption for the rest of the session. [3]
  2. (b) The user assumes that the padlock means the shop is honest. Explain why this is not necessarily true. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Asymmetric encryption lets the two sides agree a secret key safely over an insecure network without having shared a key before, and the certificate authenticates the server. But it is slow, so once the shared session key is set up, the much faster symmetric encryption is used for the bulk of the data.
    B1 asymmetric solves key exchange/authentication. B1 asymmetric is slow / processor-intensive. B1 symmetric faster for large amounts of data.
  2. (b) The padlock only shows that the connection is encrypted and that the certificate matches the domain; a criminal can obtain a valid certificate for their own fake domain, so the site itself may still be fraudulent.
    B1 padlock = encrypted connection / certificate valid for the domain. B1 does not prove the owner is trustworthy – fake sites can have certificates.

Q44SSL/TLS protocols · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Give two reasons why TLS creates a new session key for every connection instead of reusing one key for a long period. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two of: if one session key is stolen, only that session's data is exposed; each connection uses different random values, so keys cannot be reused across sessions; less data is protected by any single key, which reduces the risk from long-term use of one key.
    1 mark each for any two valid reasons.
Q45SSL/TLS protocols · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain how TLS uses a message authentication code (MAC) to protect the data that is sent over the connection. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each record of data has a MAC calculated from the data and the session key. The receiver recalculates the MAC on arrival and compares it. If the data was altered in transit the values differ, so the change is detected.
    1 mark: MAC calculated from the data and a secret key. 1 mark: receiver recalculates and compares. 1 mark: a mismatch shows the data has been altered.

Q50Quantum cryptography · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) Give two benefits and two drawbacks of quantum cryptography. [4]

Q51Quantum cryptography · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Alice sends 8 photons to Bob. Alice's bit values are 1, 0, 1, 1, 0, 0, 1, 0. Alice's bases are R, D, R, R, D, D, R, D. Bob's bases are R, R, R, D, D, D, R, R (R = rectilinear, D = diagonal). (i) State the positions where the bases match. (ii) State the sifted key formed from the matching positions. (iii) Explain why Alice and Bob discard the other positions. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) positions 1, 3, 5, 6 and 7. (ii) 1 1 0 0 1 (11001). (iii) When the bases differ, Bob's measurement has a random result, so the bit cannot be used as part of the key.

Q52Quantum cryptography · Standard · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Alice and Bob compare a sample of 100 sifted bits over a public channel. 15 of the bits differ. Calculate the error rate and explain what this suggests. [3]

  1. (a) 15%. 15 ÷ 100 × 100 = 15%. A rate this high suggests an eavesdropper has measured some photons and disturbed them, so the key cannot be trusted and must be discarded.

Q53Quantum cryptography · Standard · Paper 3 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain why an eavesdropper cannot simply copy a photon in order to measure it later without being detected in quantum key distribution. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Quantum states cannot be copied exactly (the no-cloning principle). The eavesdropper must measure the photon, which changes its state, so the change is visible when Alice and Bob compare some of their bits.
    1 mark: a quantum state cannot be copied exactly. 1 mark: measuring the photon disturbs it, which causes detectable errors.

Q57Quantum cryptography · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) An eavesdropper measures every photon in a random basis and sends a new photon on to Bob. Assume there are 400 sifted bits. Calculate the expected number of errors Alice and Bob would find when they compare bits, and explain your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 100 errors expected. When the eavesdropper's basis is wrong (50% of the time), the photon result is random, so only half of those bits are wrong. The eavesdropper uses the wrong basis for half the photons, so 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 of sifted bits are wrong. 400 × 0.25 = 100.

Q59Quantum cryptography · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 3 marks

Q60Quantum cryptography · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) During BB84, Alice and Bob announce their bases over a public channel, but the key is still secret. Explain why announcing the bases does not reveal the key. [3]
Show answer
