Q1Acyl chlorides · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name three reagents that convert ethanoic acid into ethanoyl chloride. [3]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the reaction of ethanoic acid with SOCl₂. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why SOCl₂ is often preferred for this preparation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) phosphorus(V) chloride, PCl₅; phosphorus(III) chloride, PCl₃ (with heat); sulfur dichloride oxide (thionyl chloride), SOCl₂
    B1 each
  2. (b) CH₃COOH + SOCl₂ → CH₃COCl + SO₂ + HCl
    B1
  3. (c) The by-products SO₂ and HCl are gases and escape, so the acyl chloride is easy to separate.
    B1
Q2Acyl chlorides · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give the organic product of the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with each of: (i) water (ii) ethanol (iii) ammonia (iv) methylamine. [4]
  2. (b) Write an equation for reaction (ii). [1]
  3. (c) State what is seen when ethanoyl chloride is added to water. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH (ii) ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOC₂H₅ (iii) ethanamide, CH₃CONH₂ (iv) N-methylethanamide, CH₃CONHCH₃
    B1 each
  2. (b) CH₃COCl + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + HCl
    B1
  3. (c) Steamy (white) fumes of HCl; vigorous reaction, heat given out.
    B1
Q3Acyl chlorides · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with propan-1-ol, and name the organic product. [2]
  2. (b) State one reason this reaction is preferred to heating ethanoic acid with propan-1-ol. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + HCl; the product is propyl ethanoate
    1 mark for correct equation with HCl; 1 mark for naming the ester (propyl ethanoate).
  2. (b) It is faster and goes to completion, because the reaction is not reversible.
    Acyl chloride esterification is not an equilibrium, unlike the acid-catalysed esterification.
Q4Acyl chlorides · Core · Paper 2 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Ethanoyl chloride reacts with ethanol at room temperature without a catalyst, whereas ethanoic acid and ethanol need a concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst and heating. Explain the difference. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The C–Cl bond in the acyl chloride is much more polar than the C–OH in the acid, so the carbonyl carbon is far more electrophilic and attacked readily. The reaction is also not reversible, so no catalyst or heating is needed to drive it.
    1 mark for more electrophilic carbonyl carbon; 1 mark for the reaction being non-reversible/faster.

  2. (b) Describe the mechanism in words, stating where each pair of electrons moves. (You may describe curly arrows in words.) [4]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbonyl carbon of ethanoyl chloride is attacked much more readily than the carbon of chloroethane. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nucleophilic addition–elimination
    B1
  2. (b) 1 The lone pair on the N of NH₃ attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon.2 At the same time the π electrons of C=O move onto the oxygen, giving a tetrahedral intermediate with O⁻ and N⁺.3 The lone pair on O⁻ reforms the C=O and the C–Cl bond pair moves onto Cl, which leaves as Cl⁻.4 Cl⁻ (or another NH₃) removes H⁺ from the N, giving CH₃CONH₂ and HCl (NH₄Cl).

    B1
Q6Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of phenol with benzoyl chloride, C₆H₅COCl. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why phenyl benzoate is made from benzoyl chloride and not by heating benzoic acid with phenol and an acid catalyst. [1]
  3. (c) 2.35 g of phenol is reacted with excess benzoyl chloride. The yield of phenyl benzoate is 78.0%. Calculate the mass of phenyl benzoate obtained. [Mr: phenol 94.0; phenyl benzoate 198.0] [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₆H₅OH + C₆H₅COCl → C₆H₅COOC₆H₅ + HCl
    B1
  2. (b) Phenol reacts only very slowly with carboxylic acids (its lone pair is delocalised into the ring, so it is a poor nucleophile); the acyl chloride is much more reactive.
    B1
  3. (c) 3.86 g
    M1 n(phenol) = 2.35/94.0 = 0.0250 molM1 theoretical mass = 0.0250 × 198.0 = 4.95 gA1 × 0.780 = 3.86 g
Q7Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A few drops of each of ethanoyl chloride, chloroethane and chlorobenzene are added to separate test-tubes containing aqueous silver nitrate in ethanol at room temperature.

  1. (a) Describe what you would see in each tube. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the difference in reactivity between chloroethane and chlorobenzene. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ethanoyl chloride: white precipitate immediately (with steamy fumes). Chloroethane: white precipitate forms slowly (on warming). Chlorobenzene: no precipitate.
    B1 each
  2. (b) In chlorobenzene a lone pair on Cl overlaps with the delocalised π system of the ring, giving the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character so it is stronger and harder to break; the ring electrons also repel nucleophiles.
    M1 Cl lone pair delocalised into ringM2 C–Cl bond stronger / partial double bond

Q9Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Propanoyl chloride reacts with excess methylamine, CH₃NH₂. Write the equation for the reaction and name the organic product. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why two moles of methylamine are needed for each mole of acyl chloride. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃CH₂COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻; the product is N-methylpropanamide
    1 mark for organic product and amide; 1 mark for the second equivalent of amine forming the salt.
  2. (b) One mole acts as the nucleophile; the second neutralises the HCl formed, which would otherwise protonate the amine and stop the reaction.
    Accept any statement that one amine is used to neutralise HCl.

  1. (a) A liquid acyl chloride, X, contains by mass: C 38.9%, H 5.4%, Cl 38.3%, O 17.3%. Calculate its empirical formula. (Ar: C 12.0, H 1.0, Cl 35.5, O 16.0) [3]

  1. (a) C₃H₅ClO

Q11Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) 0.845 g of propanoyl chloride, C₂H₅COCl (Mr = 92.5), is added to excess water. The solution is titrated with 0.250 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. Explain why 2 mol of NaOH are needed for each mole of propanoyl chloride. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of NaOH, in cm³ to 3 significant figures, needed to neutralise all the acid formed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hydrolysis gives propanoic acid and HCl, which together provide two acidic protons per mole of acyl chloride.
    1 mark for propanoic acid and HCl formed; 1 mark for two H⁺ per mole.
  2. (b) 73.1 cm³
    n(acyl chloride) = 0.845/92.5 = 9.14 × 10⁻³ mol; n(NaOH) = 2 × that = 1.83 × 10⁻² mol; volume = 1.83 × 10⁻²/0.250 = 73.1 cm³.
Q12Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Phenol is dissolved in aqueous sodium hydroxide and ethanoyl chloride is added. Write an equation for the reaction and name the ester formed. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the reaction with phenol is carried out in aqueous NaOH rather than with phenol alone. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₆H₅O⁻Na⁺ + CH₃COCl → CH₃COOC₆H₅ + NaCl; the ester is phenyl ethanoate
    1 mark for the ester and salt products; 1 mark for the name.
  2. (b) NaOH converts phenol into the more nucleophilic phenoxide ion, which attacks the acyl chloride faster; the NaOH also neutralises the HCl formed.
    1 mark for phenoxide being more nucleophilic; 1 mark for neutralising HCl or driving the reaction.

Q15Acyl chlorides · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Ethanoyl chloride (3.93 g) is mixed with ethanol (2.50 g). Calculate the amounts in mol of each reactant, and identify the limiting reagent. (Mr: CH₃COCl 78.5, C₂H₅OH 46.0) [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum mass, in g, of ethyl ethanoate that can form. (Mr ethyl ethanoate = 88.0) [2]
  3. (c) The actual yield of ethyl ethanoate is 3.30 g. Calculate the percentage yield. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ethanoyl chloride: 0.0501 mol; ethanol: 0.0543 mol; limiting reagent is ethanoyl chloride
    1 mark for both amounts; 1 mark for identifying the limiting reagent with the 1:1 ratio.
  2. (b) 4.41 g
    Maximum from the limiting reagent: 0.0501 × 88.0 = 4.41 g. Using 0.0500 mol gives 4.40 g; either is accepted.
