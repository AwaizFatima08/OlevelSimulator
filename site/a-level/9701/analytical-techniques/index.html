Q6Proton (1H) NMR · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The ¹H NMR spectrum of ethanol shows peaks at δ 1.2 (3H), δ 2.6 (1H) and δ 3.7 (2H). Assign each peak and give its splitting pattern. [3]
  2. (b) A few drops of D₂O are added and the spectrum is run again. State and explain the change. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the O–H peak is not split, and does not split the CH₂ peak. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) δ 1.2 CH₃ triplet; δ 2.6 O–H singlet; δ 3.7 CH₂ (H–C–O) quartet
    B1 each.
  2. (b) The δ 2.6 peak disappears: the O–H proton exchanges with deuterium (CH₃CH₂OH + D₂O ⇌ CH₃CH₂OD + HOD) and D does not absorb in this region.
    B1 OH peak disappears; B1 exchange with D, which gives no ¹H signal.
  3. (c) The O–H proton exchanges rapidly between molecules, so no coupling is seen.
    B1.

Q10Proton (1H) NMR · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Compound X, C₄H₁₀O, gives a ¹H NMR spectrum with two peaks: δ 1.2 (6H, triplet) and δ 3.4 (4H, quartet). Identify X and explain why only two peaks are seen although the molecule has ten hydrogen atoms. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) X is diethyl ether, CH₃CH₂OCH₂CH₃. Both ethyl groups are identical, so the six CH₃ hydrogens are equivalent, as are the four CH₂ hydrogens. Each CH₃ is split by the two neighbouring CH₂ hydrogens (triplet); each CH₂ by the three CH₃ hydrogens (quartet).
    B1 identifies diethyl ether; B1 two equivalent ethyl groups give two environments; B1 triplet/quartet from n+1 rule (2 neighbours and 3 neighbours); B1 number of H in each peak consistent (6:4 = 3:2).
Q11Proton (1H) NMR · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) 1,1-dichloroethane, CH₃CHCl₂. The CH₃ (3H) is a doublet because it is next to one CH hydrogen; the CHCl₂ (1H) is a quartet because it is next to three CH₃ hydrogens. Its δ is high because the two Cl atoms are electron-withdrawing.
    B1 1,1-dichloroethane identified; B1 CH₃ doublet (1 neighbour); B1 CH quartet (3 neighbours); B1 high δ for CHCl₂ due to two electronegative Cl atoms.

Q57Chromatography (TLC, GC) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A student runs a TLC plate of a mixture of glycine and alanine. In solvent 1 one spot appears with Rf 0.30. In solvent 2 the solvent front travels 10.0 cm; spots are at 2.6 cm (glycine) and 4.5 cm (alanine).

  1. (a) Explain why the two amino acids in a mixture give overlapping spots in solvent 1 but are separated in solvent 2. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the Rf of each amino acid in solvent 2. [2]
  3. (c) In gas chromatography a sample is too involatile to be analysed. Suggest one reason, and suggest an alternative. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In solvent 1 they have the same (very similar) Rf because their relative affinities for stationary and mobile phase are similar; in solvent 2 their solubilities differ, so they travel different distances.
    B1 same Rf in solvent 1; B1 different relative solubility/affinity in solvent 2.
  2. (b) glycine 0.26; alanine 0.45
    B1 each.
  3. (c) It would decompose / not vaporise at the column temperature (e.g. has strong hydrogen bonding, high bp); use TLC (or HPLC).
    B1 reason; B1 alternative.
Q58Chromatography (TLC, GC) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student reports Rf = 0.45 for a dye from a TLC run in a solvent of unknown composition. Explain why the value can only be compared with another Rf if the solvent, the plate and the temperature are the same, and explain why Rf can never be greater than 1.0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rf depends on the balance between solubility in the mobile phase and adsorption on the stationary phase; changing the solvent, plate or temperature changes this balance, so only results obtained under identical conditions can be compared. Rf is the distance moved by the spot divided by the distance moved by the solvent front, and a spot cannot travel further than the front, so Rf ≤ 1.0.
    B1 Rf depends on the balance between solubility in the mobile phase and adsorption to the stationary phase; B1 changing solvent, plate or temperature changes this balance; B1 results are comparable only under identical conditions; B1 a spot cannot move further than the solvent front, so Rf ≤ 1.0.
Q59Chromatography (TLC, GC) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) The GC separates components by retention time; each component passes into the mass spectrometer as it elutes. The MS gives a mass spectrum for each peak, showing the molecular ion and fragment ions, which identifies the compound (and confirms its purity).

Q75Carbon-13 NMR · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Show answer
