Q1Ideal gas equation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Write the ideal gas equation and give the SI unit of each quantity in it. [2]
  2. (b) A balloon at a school science fair in Lahore contains 0.150 mol of nitrogen gas at 25 °C and 101 kPa. Calculate the volume of the gas in cm³. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) pV = nRT; p in Pa, V in m³, n in mol, R in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, T in K
    B1 equation. B1 all four units correct (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹).
  2. (b) 3680 cm³
    M1 V = nRT/p = 0.150 × 8.31 × 298 ÷ 101000 = 0.00368 m³. A1 × 10⁶ = 3680 cm³.
Q2Ideal gas equation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A sealed 2.00 dm³ cylinder contains a gas at 300 K and 200 kPa. What is the amount of gas in the cylinder? (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)A 0.0160 molB 0.160 molC 1.60 molD 160 mol [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 0.160 mol
    n = pV/RT = (200 × 10³ × 2.00 × 10⁻³) ÷ (8.31 × 300) = 0.160 mol. D comes from using V in dm³ (2.00) with p in Pa; A and C come from slips of a power of ten in p or V.

    B1 SO₂; CO₂ = 44.0, NO₂ = 46.0, Cl₂ = 71.0 do not fit.

Q5Ideal gas equation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Four flasks each contain an ideal gas. Which flask contains the greatest number of gas molecules?A 100 kPa, 2.0 dm³, 300 KB 200 kPa, 1.0 dm³, 600 KC 150 kPa, 2.0 dm³, 400 KD 50 kPa, 5.0 dm³, 300 K [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D 50 kPa, 5.0 dm³, 300 K

Q6Ideal gas equation · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Methane, CH₄, is supplied to homes in Karachi through gas pipes. Show that the density of a gas is given by ρ = pMr/RT, where Mr is used in g mol⁻¹. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the density of methane at 25 °C and 101 kPa in g dm⁻³. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) n = m/Mr, so pV = (m/Mr)RT, and ρ = m/V = pMr/RT
    B1 substitution of n = m/Mr and rearrangement.
  2. (b) 0.653 g dm⁻³
    M1 ρ = 101000 × 16.0 ÷ (8.31 × 298) = 653 g m⁻³. A1 ÷ 1000 = 0.653 g dm⁻³.

  1. (a) A gas lighter is weighed, then butane is released from it into an inverted measuring cylinder full of water. Results: loss in mass of lighter = 0.226 g; volume of gas collected = 95.0 cm³; temperature = 21 °C; pressure = 100.8 kPa. Calculate the Mr of the gas. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) The true Mr of butane, C₄H₁₀, is 58.0. Calculate the percentage difference between your value and 58.0. [1]
  3. (c) The lighter was not dried after the gas was collected under water. Explain the effect on the value of Mr. [2]

  1. (a) 57.7
    M1 n = pV/RT = 0.00392 mol. M1 Mr = 0.226 ÷ n. A1 57.7.
  2. (b) 0.59 %

Q18Ideal gas equation · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Flask P (2.00 dm³) contains nitrogen at 150 kPa. Flask Q (3.00 dm³) contains oxygen at 50.0 kPa. The flasks are joined by a tube of negligible volume and the tap between them is opened. The temperature stays constant. What is the final pressure?A 60.0 kPaB 90.0 kPaC 100 kPaD 200 kPa [1]

  1. (a) B 90.0 kPa

Q22Kinetic theory & diffusion · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement is NOT an assumption of the kinetic theory of an ideal gas?A Molecules move randomly in straight lines between collisions.B Collisions between molecules are elastic.C There are no intermolecular forces between molecules.D The volume of the molecules is a large fraction of the volume of the container. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D The volume of the molecules is a large fraction of the volume of the container.
    An ideal gas assumes the molecules themselves have negligible volume. A, B and C are all genuine assumptions; C (no forces) is a common distractor because real gases do have forces.
Q23Kinetic theory & diffusion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Use the kinetic theory to explain why the pressure of a gas in a sealed container increases when it is heated. [2]
  2. (b) Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia is placed at one end of a 100 cm glass tube and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other end. A white ring forms after a few minutes. State where the ring forms and explain why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Molecules move faster / have more kinetic energy, so they collide with the walls more often and with more force
    B1 increased average speed / kinetic energy. B1 more frequent and harder collisions with the walls.
  2. (b) Nearer the HCl end; NH₃ (Mr 17.0) diffuses faster than HCl (Mr 36.5); white solid is NH₄Cl
    B1 closer to the hydrochloric acid end. B1 NH₃ has smaller Mr so its molecules move faster at the same temperature. B1 NH₃ + HCl → NH₄Cl (white solid).
Q24Kinetic theory & diffusion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Hydrogen and oxygen are at the same temperature. State how the average kinetic energies of their molecules compare and explain why hydrogen molecules move faster on average. [2]
  2. (b) Gas molecules at room temperature move at several hundred metres per second, yet the smell of an opened bottle of attar takes many seconds to cross a still room. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Same average kinetic energy; KE = ½mv², so lighter H₂ molecules have a higher average speed
    B1 same average Ek at same T. B1 smaller mass → greater speed.
  2. (b) Molecules collide very often with air molecules and change direction, so their net movement is slow (random walk)
    B1 frequent collisions with air molecules. B1 random changes of direction so net displacement is small.

Q26Ideal & real gases · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Place helium, nitrogen and ammonia in order of increasing deviation from ideal behaviour at 300 K and 500 kPa. Explain your order. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why ammonia gas is liquefied much more easily than nitrogen gas when compressed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) He < N₂ < NH₃
    B1 order. B1 He atoms are small with very weak London dispersion forces. B1 NH₃ molecules form hydrogen bonds (strongest intermolecular forces), so NH₃ deviates most.
  2. (b) Hydrogen bonding between NH₃ molecules is much stronger than the forces between N₂ molecules
    B1 stronger intermolecular forces (hydrogen bonds).
Q27Ideal & real gases · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) For 1.00 mol of a gas X at 300 K, the ratio pV/nRT is measured at several pressures:100 kPa: 0.9955000 kPa: 0.80040 000 kPa: 1.35For an ideal gas the ratio is always 1.00. Calculate the volume that 1.00 mol of an ideal gas would occupy at 5000 kPa and 300 K, and the actual volume of 1.00 mol of X at these conditions. Give both in dm³ to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the ratio is less than 1 at 5000 kPa but greater than 1 at 40 000 kPa. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ideal 0.499 dm³; actual 0.399 dm³
    M1 V = nRT/p = 8.31 × 300 ÷ 5.00 × 10⁶ m³. A1 0.499 dm³. A1 × 0.800 = 0.399 dm³.
  2. (b) At 5000 kPa attractive forces pull molecules together, reducing volume/pressure; at 40 000 kPa the volume of the molecules themselves dominates, so the gas cannot be compressed as much
    B1 < 1: intermolecular attractions make the volume smaller than ideal. B1 > 1: molecular volume no longer negligible / repulsion, so volume larger than ideal.
Q28Giant structures (lattices) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which substance has a giant covalent (giant molecular) lattice in the solid state?A iodine, I₂B silicon(IV) oxide, SiO₂C carbon dioxide, CO₂D magnesium oxide, MgO [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B silicon(IV) oxide, SiO₂

Q29Giant structures (lattices) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Diamond and graphite are both forms of carbon with giant lattices. Explain why graphite conducts electricity but diamond does not. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why both have very high melting points. [1]
  3. (c) Describe the lattice structure of copper and explain why copper can be drawn into wires. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In graphite each C uses 3 electrons in bonding; the 4th is delocalised between layers and can move. In diamond all 4 outer electrons are in localised covalent bonds
    B1 graphite: one delocalised electron per atom that is mobile. B1 diamond: all electrons held in σ bonds / no mobile charge carriers.
  2. (b) Many strong covalent bonds must be broken
    B1 strong covalent bonds throughout the giant structure.
  3. (c) Lattice of Cu²⁺/positive ions in a sea of delocalised electrons; layers of ions can slide over each other without breaking the metallic bonding
    B1 regular lattice of positive ions. B1 delocalised electrons. B1 layers slide; bonding is non-directional so it is kept.
