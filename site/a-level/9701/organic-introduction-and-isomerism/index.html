Q1Stereoisomerism · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃, shows cis/trans (geometrical) isomerism. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the difference between cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why but-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃, does not show cis/trans isomerism. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) There is no rotation about the C=C double bond (π bond), and each carbon of the C=C carries two different groups (H and CH₃).
    M1 restricted rotation about C=C / π bondM2 each C of the C=C has two different groups
  2. (b) In the cis isomer both CH₃ groups are on the same side of the double bond; in the trans isomer they are on opposite sides.
    B1
  3. (c) One carbon of the C=C carries two identical groups (two H atoms).
    B1
Q2Stereoisomerism · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which compound shows cis/trans isomerism? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C CH₃CH=CHCl
    In CH₃CH=CHCl each carbon of the C=C has two different groups (CH₃/H and Cl/H). In each of the others, one carbon of the C=C carries two identical groups.
Q3Stereoisomerism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

1,2-dichloroethene, ClCH=CHCl, exists as cis and trans isomers.

  1. (a) Describe, in words, the arrangement of the atoms in each isomer. [2]
  2. (b) The boiling points are 60 °C and 48 °C. Deduce which isomer has the higher boiling point and explain. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) cis: both Cl atoms on the same side of the C=C (and both H atoms on the other side); trans: the Cl atoms on opposite sides. All six atoms lie in one plane.
    B1 cis descriptionB1 trans description
  2. (b) cis (60 °C). The cis molecule is polar because its C–Cl dipoles do not cancel, so it has permanent dipole–permanent dipole forces as well as London dispersion forces. In the trans isomer the dipoles cancel, so it is non-polar.
    B1 cis higherM1 cis has a net dipole / permanent dipole–dipole forcesM1 trans dipoles cancel (symmetrical), only London forces
Q4Stereoisomerism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Pent-3-en-2-ol has the structural formula CH₃CH(OH)CH=CHCH₃.

  1. (a) Identify the chiral centre in pent-3-en-2-ol. [1]
  2. (b) State the two types of stereoisomerism shown by this compound. [1]
  3. (c) Deduce the total number of stereoisomers of pent-3-en-2-ol. Explain your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Carbon-2 (the carbon bonded to OH, H, CH₃ and CH=CHCH₃).
    B1
  2. (b) Optical isomerism and cis/trans (geometrical) isomerism.
    B1 both
  3. (c) 4
    M1 two forms about the C=C × two enantiomers at C-2A1 2 × 2 = 4
Q5Stereoisomerism · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which compound shows optical isomerism but not cis/trans isomerism? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A CH₃CHBrCH₂CH₃
    2-bromobutane has a carbon bonded to four different groups (H, Br, CH₃, C₂H₅) and no C=C. D shows both types; B shows only cis/trans; C shows neither.
Q6Stereoisomerism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Pentan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃, is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid. Three different alkenes, each C₅H₁₀, are formed.

  1. (a) Name the three alkenes formed and give their structural formulae. [3]
  2. (b) State the relationship between two of the products and explain why they are different compounds. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why pentan-3-ol, CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃, gives only two alkene products when dehydrated in the same way. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) pent-1-ene CH₂=CHCH₂CH₂CH₃; cis-pent-2-ene and trans-pent-2-ene, both CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₃
    B1 pent-1-eneB1 pent-2-eneB1 cis and trans identified as the two forms of pent-2-ene
  2. (b) cis-pent-2-ene and trans-pent-2-ene are stereoisomers (geometrical isomers): same structural formula but different arrangement in space; they cannot interconvert because there is no rotation about the C=C.
    M1 cis/trans (stereo) isomersM2 restricted rotation about C=C
  3. (c) Loss of H from either neighbouring carbon gives the same structure, pent-2-ene, which exists as cis and trans forms.
    B1
Q7Stereoisomerism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Butenedioic acid, HOOC–CH=CH–COOH, has a cis isomer (maleic acid) and a trans isomer (fumaric acid). Fumaric acid is used as a food acidity regulator.

  1. (a) Describe the difference in structure between the two isomers. [1]
  2. (b) Fumaric acid melts at 287 °C but maleic acid melts at 135 °C. Suggest an explanation. [3]
  3. (c) On heating, maleic acid loses water to form a cyclic anhydride, but fumaric acid does not. Suggest why. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) In maleic acid (cis) both COOH groups are on the same side of the C=C; in fumaric acid (trans) they are on opposite sides.
    B1
  2. (b) Fumaric acid molecules are more symmetrical and pack more closely in the lattice and form hydrogen bonds between molecules in all directions. In maleic acid the two COOH groups are close enough to form an intramolecular hydrogen bond, leaving fewer intermolecular hydrogen bonds, and the molecules pack less well.
    M1 trans packs better / more symmetricalM1 intermolecular hydrogen bonding in fumaric acidM1 cis forms intramolecular hydrogen bond, so fewer intermolecular
  3. (c) Only in the cis isomer are the two COOH groups close enough to react with each other.
    B1

Q18Optical isomerism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
