Q1Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which Period 3 oxide reacts with both dilute hydrochloric acid and aqueous sodium hydroxide?A Al₂O₃B MgOC SO₂D Na₂O [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Al₂O₃
    Al₂O₃ is amphoteric: it forms AlCl₃ with HCl and Na[Al(OH)₄] with NaOH. MgO is basic and reacts only with acids; SO₂ is acidic; Na₂O is basic.
Q2Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which oxide does not react with water but reacts with hot, concentrated aqueous sodium hydroxide?A SiO₂B P₄O₁₀C MgOD Na₂O [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A SiO₂
    SiO₂ is insoluble in water (giant covalent) but is acidic: SiO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SiO₃ + H₂O. P₄O₁₀ is also acidic but reacts vigorously with water to form H₃PO₄.
Q3Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) In which compound does the Period 3 element have an oxidation number of +6?A SO₃B SO₂C PCl₅D P₄O₁₀ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A SO₃
    In SO₃, S = +6 (3 × −2 = −6). SO₂ has S +4; PCl₅ and P₄O₁₀ both have P +5.
Q4Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of each oxide with water: (i) Na₂O (ii) P₄O₁₀ (iii) SO₂ (iv) SO₃. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest the approximate pH of the solution formed from Na₂O and from SO₃. [2]
  3. (c) Magnesium oxide is added to water and the pH is tested. Suggest the pH and explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH(ii) P₄O₁₀ + 6H₂O → 4H₃PO₄(iii) SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃(iv) SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄
    B1 each balanced equation (state symbols not required).
  2. (b) Na₂O: about 13–14; SO₃: about 0–1
    B1 each (accept 13–14 and 0–1).
  3. (c) About 9: MgO is only slightly soluble, forming a little Mg(OH)₂ (weakly alkaline).
    B1 pH 8–10 with reason.

Q7Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A colourless liquid chloride of a Period 3 element is added to water. Steamy fumes are given off, a white solid forms and the pH of the mixture is about 2. What is the chloride?A SiCl₄B AlCl₃C MgCl₂D NaCl [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A SiCl₄
    SiCl₄ is a liquid at room temperature and is fully hydrolysed: SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl, giving white SiO₂ and a strongly acidic solution. AlCl₃ is a solid at room temperature, so it does not fit the description of a liquid.

  3. (c) Which other Period 3 oxide reacts with sodium hydroxide but not with dilute hydrochloric acid, while also being insoluble in water? Write the equation. [2]

    B1 strong bonding in the lattice / high lattice energy.

    B1 SiO₂; B1 equation.

Q10Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

3.40 g of silicon tetrachloride is added to an excess of water: SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl. Ar: Si 28.1, Cl 35.5; Mr SiO₂ = 60.1.

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of silicon(IV) oxide formed. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the amount of hydrogen chloride formed. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why a technician opening an old bottle of SiCl₄ in a humid Karachi laboratory sees white fumes. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.20 g
    M1 n(SiCl₄) = 3.40/170.1 = 0.0200 mol = n(SiO₂); A1 × 60.1 = 1.20 g.
  2. (b) 0.0800 mol
    B1 4 × 0.0200 = 0.0800 mol.
  3. (c) SiCl₄ reacts with water vapour in the air, producing HCl gas (which forms droplets with moisture) and fine SiO₂.
    B1 hydrolysis by moisture in air releasing HCl.
Q11Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Magnesium chloride solution has a pH of about 6.5, whereas sodium chloride solution has a pH of 7. Suggest why. [2]
  2. (b) State the type of bonding in MgCl₂ and in SiCl₄ and use it to explain why one dissolves while the other is hydrolysed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mg²⁺ is smaller and more highly charged than Na⁺ (higher charge density), so it polarises the water molecules around it; a little H⁺ is released, so slightly acidic. Na⁺ does not do this.
    B1 Mg²⁺ higher charge density; B1 polarises/hydrolyses water, releasing H⁺.
  2. (b) MgCl₂ ionic: ions are hydrated and simply dissolve. SiCl₄ covalent (simple molecular): water molecules attack the Si atom, breaking Si–Cl bonds (hydrolysis).
    B1 MgCl₂ ionic dissolves; B1 SiCl₄ covalent, Si–Cl bonds broken by water.
Q12Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the structure and bonding of: Na₂O, Al₂O₃, SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₃. [3]
  2. (b) Describe how the acid–base nature of the oxides changes across Period 3 and link it to the structure. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Na₂O giant ionic; Al₂O₃ giant ionic (with some covalent character); SiO₂ giant covalent; P₄O₁₀ and SO₃ simple molecular (covalent).
    B1 ionic pair; B1 SiO₂ giant covalent; B1 P₄O₁₀ and SO₃ simple molecular.
  2. (b) From basic (Na₂O, MgO, ionic, contain O²⁻ that accepts H⁺) through amphoteric (Al₂O₃) to acidic (SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃, covalent oxides of non-metals that react with water/bases).
    B1 basic → amphoteric → acidic; B1 ionic oxides basic (O²⁻ ions), covalent oxides acidic.

  1. (a) Write an equation for the complete neutralisation of P₄O₁₀ by aqueous sodium hydroxide, forming sodium phosphate. [1]

    B1 a basic gas, e.g. NH₃, with reason.
Q14Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

0.310 g of sodium oxide is dissolved in water and the solution is made up to 500 cm³. Ar: Na 23.0, O 16.0.

  1. (a) Calculate the concentration of hydroxide ions in the solution. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of 0.100 mol/dm³ HCl needed to neutralise 25.0 cm³ of this solution. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0200 mol/dm³
    M1 n(Na₂O) = 0.310/62 = 0.00500 mol → n(OH⁻) = 0.0100 mol; A1 ÷ 0.500 = 0.0200 mol/dm³.
  2. (b) 5.00 cm³
    M1 n(OH⁻) in 25.0 cm³ = 0.00050 mol = n(HCl); A1 V = 5.00 cm³.
Q15Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A student adds 2.085 g of phosphorus(V) chloride to water and makes the solution up to 250.0 cm³. A 25.0 cm³ portion is titrated with 0.500 mol/dm³ NaOH(aq). Assume all three hydrogen atoms of H₃PO₄ react with the alkali. Ar: P 31.0, Cl 35.5.

  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of PCl₅ with water. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the amount, in mol, of PCl₅ in the 25.0 cm³ portion. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the volume of 0.500 mol/dm³ NaOH needed for complete neutralisation of the 25.0 cm³ portion. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) PCl₅ + 4H₂O → H₃PO₄ + 5HCl
    B1.
  2. (b) 0.00100 mol (1.00 × 10⁻³ mol)
    M1 Mr PCl₅ = 31.0 + 5(35.5) = 208.5; 2.085/208.5 = 0.0100 mol; A1 ÷ 10 = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol.
  3. (c) 16.0 cm³
    M1 1 PCl₅ → 5 HCl + 1 H₃PO₄ → 5 + 3 = 8 mol NaOH; n(NaOH) = 0.0080 mol; A1 V = 0.0080/0.500 = 16.0 cm³.
Q16Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

X is a Period 3 element. Its oxide is a white solid with a high melting point that is insoluble in water but dissolves in both dilute HCl and NaOH(aq). Its chloride is a white solid that sublimes at about 180 °C, fumes in moist air, and exists as a dimer in the vapour.

  1. (a) Identify element X. [1]
  2. (b) Write equations for: (i) X burning in oxygen; (ii) X heated in chlorine to form its chloride; (iii) the formation of the dimer of the chloride. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the chloride of X forms steamy fumes in moist air. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest the pH of a solution of the chloride of X in a large volume of water. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Aluminium
    B1.
  2. (b) (i) 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃(ii) 2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃(iii) 2AlCl₃ → Al₂Cl₆
    B1 each (for (ii) accept 2Al + 3Cl₂ → Al₂Cl₆).
  3. (c) It is hydrolysed by water vapour, releasing HCl gas.
    B1.
  4. (d) About 3
    B1 accept 2–4.
Q17Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of aluminium chloride with a small amount of water. [1]
  2. (b) In a large volume of water, aluminium chloride forms [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ ions. Explain why this solution is acidic. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the pH of aqueous NaCl is 7 while that of aqueous AlCl₃ is about 3. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) AlCl₃ + 3H₂O → Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl
    B1.
  2. (b) Al³⁺ is small and highly charged (high charge density). It polarises the O–H bonds of the co-ordinated water molecules, weakening them, so an H⁺ is released: [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ ⇌ [Al(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺ + H⁺.
    B1 high charge density of Al³⁺; B1 polarises/weakens O–H bond in water ligand; B1 equation or statement that H⁺ is released.
  3. (c) Na⁺ has a low charge density and does not polarise water, so no H⁺ is released.
    B1.
Q18Period 3 oxides & chlorides · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Five chlorides of Period 3 elements, A to E, are tested.A: white solid, m.p. 801 °C, dissolves in water to give pH 7.B: white solid, m.p. 714 °C, dissolves to give pH 6.5.C: white solid, sublimes at 178 °C, pH 3 in water.D: colourless liquid, b.p. 58 °C, reacts violently with water giving a white solid and pH 1–2.E: pale yellow solid, sublimes at about 160 °C, reacts violently with water; the solution contains two acids.

  1. (a) Identify each of chlorides A to E. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why chloride D has a low boiling point but chloride A has a high melting point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A NaCl; B MgCl₂; C AlCl₃; D SiCl₄; E PCl₅
    B1 each.
  2. (b) D (SiCl₄) is simple molecular with weak intermolecular (id–id) forces; A (NaCl) is giant ionic with strong electrostatic attraction between many ions.
    B1 D simple molecular, weak intermolecular forces; B1 A giant ionic, strong attractions throughout.

  1. (a) Which statement explains why atomic radius decreases from sodium to chlorine?A Nuclear charge increases while shielding stays about the same, so outer electrons are pulled closer.B Number of electron shells decreases across the period.C Shielding increases across the period because more electrons are added.D The outer electrons are in sub-shells of higher energy across the period. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Nuclear charge increases while shielding stays about the same, so outer electrons are pulled closer.
    Across Period 3 electrons enter the same (third) shell; proton number rises, shielding by inner shells is almost constant, so effective attraction increases. B is wrong: all have three shells.
Q20Periodic trends (radius, IE, electronegativity) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State and explain the general trend in first ionisation energy across Period 3. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the first ionisation energy of aluminium is lower than that of magnesium. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the first ionisation energy of sulfur is lower than that of phosphorus. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Increases: nuclear charge increases, same shell / similar shielding, so outer electron more strongly attracted and smaller radius.
    B1 increases; B1 explanation.
  2. (b) Al loses an electron from the 3p sub-shell, which is higher in energy than 3s and is shielded by the 3s electrons.
    B1 3p electron removed; B1 higher energy / further from nucleus / extra shielding by 3s.
  3. (c) In S the electron removed is from a 3p orbital containing a pair of electrons; repulsion between the paired electrons makes it easier to remove. P has three unpaired 3p electrons.
    B1 paired electrons in one 3p orbital in S; B1 spin-pair repulsion.
Q21Periodic trends (radius, IE, electronegativity) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Pauling electronegativity values: Na 0.9, Mg 1.3, Al 1.6, Si 1.9, P 2.2, S 2.6, O 3.4.

  1. (a) Calculate the electronegativity difference between oxygen and each element. [2]
  2. (b) Use your answers to describe how the bonding in the oxides changes across the period. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why electronegativity increases across Period 3. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Na 2.5, Mg 2.1, Al 1.8, Si 1.5, P 1.2, S 0.8
    B2 all six correct (B1 for four correct).
  2. (b) Large difference → ionic (Na₂O, MgO); decreasing difference → increasing covalent character, Al₂O₃ intermediate, SiO₂ to SO₃ covalent.
    B1 large difference ionic; B1 smaller differences covalent, gradual change.
  3. (c) Increasing nuclear charge with similar shielding and smaller atomic radius attracts bonding electrons more strongly.
    B1.
Q22Periodic trends (radius, IE, electronegativity) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The first five successive ionisation energies (kJ/mol) of a Period 3 element are 578, 1817, 2745, 11577, 14842.

  1. (a) Calculate the ratio IE₄ ÷ IE₃ and the ratio IE₃ ÷ IE₂ to 2 decimal places. [1]
  2. (b) Use your answers to identify the element. Explain your choice. [2]
  3. (c) Write an equation, with state symbols, for the third ionisation energy of this element. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) IE₄/IE₃ = 4.22; IE₃/IE₂ = 1.51
    B1 both.
  2. (b) Aluminium: the big jump is between the 3rd and 4th electrons, so 3 electrons in the outer shell (Group 13); the 4th electron is removed from the inner (second) shell, closer to the nucleus with less shielding.
    B1 aluminium/Group 13; B1 large jump after 3rd electron because 4th comes from inner shell.
  3. (c) Al²⁺(g) → Al³⁺(g) + e⁻
    B1 including (g).
Q23Periodic trends (radius, IE, electronegativity) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Ionic radii (nm): Na⁺ 0.095, Mg²⁺ 0.065, Al³⁺ 0.050, P³⁻ 0.212, S²⁻ 0.184, Cl⁻ 0.181.

  1. (a) Explain why the radius decreases from Na⁺ to Al³⁺. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why P³⁻ is much larger than Al³⁺. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the radius decreases from P³⁻ to Cl⁻. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The ions are isoelectronic (10 electrons, 1s²2s²2p⁶); nuclear charge increases from 11 to 13, so the electrons are pulled closer.
    B1 same number of electrons/isoelectronic; B1 increasing nuclear charge.
  2. (b) P³⁻ has 18 electrons in three shells, while Al³⁺ has 10 electrons in two shells; the extra shell is further from the nucleus.
    B1 P³⁻ has an extra (third) shell; B1 more shielding / electrons further out.
  3. (c) Isoelectronic (18 electrons) with nuclear charge increasing from 15 to 17.
    B1.
Q24Structure & melting points across Period 3 · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which Period 3 element has the highest melting point?A siliconB aluminiumC phosphorusD sulfur [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A silicon
    Silicon has a giant covalent structure; melting breaks many strong covalent bonds (m.p. 1410 °C). Aluminium is metallic (660 °C); phosphorus and sulfur are simple molecular.
Q25Structure & melting points across Period 3 · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State and explain the trend in electrical conductivity from sodium to aluminium. [2]
  2. (b) Silicon is a semiconductor. Describe the electrical conductivity of phosphorus, sulfur and chlorine and explain it. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one use of silicon that depends on its electrical properties, for example in solar panels on rooftops in Pakistan. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Increases: number of delocalised electrons per atom increases (1, 2, 3).
    B1 increases; B1 more delocalised electrons.
  2. (b) Non-conductors (insulators): simple molecular, electrons held in covalent bonds, no delocalised electrons or ions.
    B1 do not conduct; B1 no mobile charged particles.
  3. (c) Photovoltaic (solar) cells / computer chips / transistors.
    B1.
Q26Structure & melting points across Period 3 · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Melting points (°C): Na 98, Mg 650, Al 660, Si 1410, P 44, S 115, Cl −101, Ar −189.

  1. (a) Explain the melting points of sodium, magnesium and aluminium in terms of structure and bonding. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why silicon has the highest melting point. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why phosphorus, sulfur, chlorine and argon have low melting points. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Giant metallic; melting point increases as ion charge increases and more delocalised electrons per atom → stronger metallic bonding.
    B1 giant metallic; B1 stronger bonding Na → Al.
  2. (b) Giant covalent lattice; many strong covalent bonds must be broken.
    B1.
  3. (c) Simple molecular (P₄, S₈, Cl₂) or single atoms (Ar); only weak instantaneous dipole–induced dipole forces between particles need to be overcome.
    B1 simple molecules/atoms; B1 weak id–id forces overcome, not covalent bonds.
Q27Structure & melting points across Period 3 · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Melting points (°C): S 115, P 44, Cl −101, Ar −189.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of electrons in one particle of each: S₈, P₄, Cl₂ and Ar. [2]
  2. (b) Use your answers to explain the order of melting points S > P > Cl > Ar. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the melting point of sulfur is far below that of silicon, although a sulfur molecule has many more electrons than a silicon atom. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) S₈ 128, P₄ 60, Cl₂ 34, Ar 18
    B2 all correct (B1 for three).
  2. (b) All are simple molecular/atomic with id–id forces only. More electrons → larger, more polarisable electron cloud → stronger id–id forces. S₈ (128) > P₄ (60) > Cl₂ (34) > Ar (18), so melting point follows the same order.
    B1 id–id forces only; B1 strength increases with number of electrons; B1 order linked to the electron counts.
  3. (c) Melting sulfur only overcomes weak intermolecular forces; melting silicon breaks strong covalent bonds in a giant lattice.
    B1.

  1. (a) Which observation is correct when the element is heated in oxygen?A Magnesium burns with a brilliant white flame forming a white solid.B Sodium burns with a blue flame forming a yellow solid.C Sulfur burns with a yellow flame forming a white solid.D Phosphorus does not react unless it is in the form of a fine powder. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Magnesium burns with a brilliant white flame forming a white solid.
    Mg burns with an intense white flame giving white MgO. Sodium burns with a yellow/orange flame giving a white solid (B wrong); sulfur burns with a blue flame forming colourless SO₂ gas (C wrong); white phosphorus ignites readily (D wrong).
Q29Period 3 elements: reactions with oxygen & chlorine · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Write equations for the reactions of sodium, magnesium, aluminium, phosphorus and sulfur with oxygen. (Assume phosphorus is in excess oxygen.) [5]
  2. (b) State the oxidation number of the Period 3 element in each product. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀S + O₂ → SO₂
    B1 each balanced equation (accept 2P₂O₅ or 4P + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀).
  2. (b) +1, +2, +3, +5, +4
    B1 all.
Q30Period 3 elements: reactions with oxygen & chlorine · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Ar: Al 27.0, Cl 35.5. Molar gas volume at room conditions = 24.0 dm³.

  1. (a) Write equations for the reactions of chlorine with sodium, magnesium, aluminium, silicon and phosphorus (chlorine in excess). [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of chlorine, in cm³ at room conditions, needed to react completely with 0.540 g of aluminium. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the maximum mass of aluminium chloride that can form. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl; Mg + Cl₂ → MgCl₂; 2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃ (or Al₂Cl₆); Si + 2Cl₂ → SiCl₄; 2P + 5Cl₂ → 2PCl₅
    B3 all five (B2 four, B1 three).
  2. (b) 720 cm³
    M1 n(Al) = 0.540/27.0 = 0.0200 mol; n(Cl₂) = 1.5 × 0.0200 = 0.0300 mol; A1 × 24.0 dm³ = 720 cm³.
  3. (c) 2.67 g
    B1 0.0200 × 133.5 = 2.67 g.
