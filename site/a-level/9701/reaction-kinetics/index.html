Q1Rate equations & orders · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A and B react in solution. Initial rates were measured at constant temperature.Expt 1: [A] = 0.10, [B] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, rate = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Expt 2: [A] = 0.20, [B] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, rate = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Expt 3: [A] = 0.10, [B] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³, rate = 1.8 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

  1. (a) Deduce the order of reaction with respect to A and with respect to B. Explain your reasoning. [2]
  2. (b) Write the rate equation and calculate the rate constant, k, including its units. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the initial rate when [A] = 0.25 mol dm⁻³ and [B] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) First order in A; second order in B
    B1 expts 1→2: [A] doubles, rate doubles → first order in A; B1 expts 1→3: [B] ×3, rate ×9 → second order in B.
  2. (b) rate = k[A][B]²; k = 0.20 dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹
    B1 rate equation; M1 k = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (0.10 × 0.10²) = 0.20; A1 units dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹
    B1 0.20 × 0.25 × 0.040².
Q2Rate equations & orders · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

For the reaction X + 2Y → Z, the rate equation is rate = k[X][Y]².

  1. (a) State the order of reaction with respect to X, with respect to Y, and overall. [2]
  2. (b) State the factor by which the rate changes when the concentrations of both X and Y are doubled. [1]
  3. (c) State the factor by which the rate changes if [X] is tripled and [Y] is halved. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) X: 1; Y: 2; overall 3
    B1 X and Y; B1 overall.
  2. (b) 8 times
    B1 2 × 2² = 8.
  3. (c) 0.75 (rate falls to three-quarters)
    B1 3 × (½)² = 0.75.
Q3Rate equations & orders · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

The hydrolysis of 2-chloro-2-methylpropane, (CH₃)₃CCl, in water was studied at 25 °C. Initial rates were measured for three mixtures:Experiment 1: [(CH₃)₃CCl] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, [OH⁻] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, initial rate = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Experiment 2: [(CH₃)₃CCl] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³, [OH⁻] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, initial rate = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Experiment 3: [(CH₃)₃CCl] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, [OH⁻] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³, initial rate = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

  1. (a) Deduce the order of reaction with respect to (CH₃)₃CCl and with respect to OH⁻. Explain how the data show each order. [3]
  2. (b) Write the rate equation for the reaction. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the rate constant, k, and give its units. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) First order with respect to (CH₃)₃CCl; zero order with respect to OH⁻.
    1 mark: doubling [(CH₃)₃CCl] doubles the rate (experiments 1 and 2), so first order. 1 mark: tripling [OH⁻] has no effect on the rate (experiments 1 and 3), so zero order. 1 mark: orders are found from the ratio of rates when only one concentration changes.
  2. (b) rate = k[(CH₃)₃CCl]
    Only a reactant with a non-zero order appears in the rate equation.
  3. (c) k = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹
    k = rate ÷ [(CH₃)₃CCl] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 0.10 = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹. 1 mark rearrangement, 1 mark value, 1 mark units (s⁻¹, as the reaction is first order overall).
Q4Rate equations & orders · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A reaction has the rate equation rate = k[A]²[B]. The rate constant at the temperature used is k = 0.035 dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹. Calculate the initial rate when [A] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³ and [B] = 0.50 mol dm⁻³. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) State the overall order of this reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.58 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹
    rate = 0.035 × (0.30)² × 0.50 = 0.035 × 0.090 × 0.50 = 1.575 × 10⁻³ = 1.58 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. 1 mark squaring [A]; 1 mark the product; 1 mark 3 s.f. with units.
  2. (b) 3 (second order in A and first order in B)
    Overall order is the sum of the individual orders: 2 + 1 = 3.
Q5Rate equations & orders · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A reaction is zero order with respect to Y and first order with respect to X. Give the units of the rate constant, k. [1]
  2. (b) The rate constant of a reaction has units dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹. Deduce the overall order of the reaction. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the units of k depend on the overall order of the reaction, although the units of the rate do not. [2]
  4. (d) A reaction is first order with respect to A and second order with respect to B. Deduce the units of k. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) s⁻¹
    rate = k[X], so k = (mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹) ÷ (mol dm⁻³) = s⁻¹.
  2. (b) 2 (second order overall)
    For second order, rate = k[A]² so k = (mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹) ÷ (mol dm⁻³)² = dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.
  3. (c) The rate is always in mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹, but rate = k × (concentration terms), so k = rate ÷ (concentration)ⁿ and its units change with the overall order n.
    1 mark: rate always has units mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. 1 mark: k = rate ÷ concentration^(overall order), so the units of k depend on n.
  4. (d) dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹
    rate = k[A][B]², overall order 3. k = (mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹) ÷ (mol dm⁻³)³ = mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹. 1 mark for the expression for k, 1 mark for the units.
Q6Rate equations & orders · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Propanone reacts with iodine in acid solution:CH₃COCH₃ + I₂ → CH₃COCH₂I + HIThe rate equation is rate = k[CH₃COCH₃][H⁺]. With [CH₃COCH₃] = 0.40 mol dm⁻³, [H⁺] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³ and [I₂] = 0.0020 mol dm⁻³, the initial rate is 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.

  1. (a) State what the rate equation tells you about the role of iodine in the rate-determining step. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate constant and give its units. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the initial rate when [CH₃COCH₃] = 0.60 mol dm⁻³, [H⁺] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³ and [I₂] is doubled. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest how the rate of this reaction could be followed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Iodine is not involved in the rate-determining step (it reacts in a later, fast step).
    B1 zero order → I₂ not in / after the RDS.
  2. (b) k = 4.25 × 10⁻⁴ dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
    M1 k = 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (0.40 × 0.20) = 4.25 × 10⁻⁴; A1 units dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 2.55 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹
    M1 [I₂] has no effect; A1 4.25 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.60 × 0.10 = 2.55 × 10⁻⁵.
  4. (d) Use a colorimeter to measure the decrease in the brown/yellow iodine colour over time.
    B1 colorimetry (or titrating quenched samples with thiosulfate).

Q8Rate equations & orders · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Nitrogen monoxide reacts with hydrogen: 2NO(g) + 2H₂(g) → N₂(g) + 2H₂O(g)Initial rates were measured at 1000 K:Experiment 1: [NO] = 0.0050 mol dm⁻³, [H₂] = 0.0020 mol dm⁻³, rate = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Experiment 2: [NO] = 0.0100 mol dm⁻³, [H₂] = 0.0020 mol dm⁻³, rate = 4.8 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Experiment 3: [NO] = 0.0050 mol dm⁻³, [H₂] = 0.0040 mol dm⁻³, rate = 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

  1. (a) Deduce the order of reaction with respect to NO, with respect to H₂ and the overall order. [3]
  2. (b) Write the rate equation and calculate the rate constant, k, with its units. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the order with respect to H₂ is not the same as its coefficient in the equation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Order 2 with respect to NO; order 1 with respect to H₂; overall order 3.
    1 mark: [NO] doubled, rate × 4 so order 2. 1 mark: [H₂] doubled, rate × 2 so order 1. 1 mark: overall = 2 + 1 = 3.
  2. (b) rate = k[NO]²[H₂]; k = 2.4 × 10³ dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹
    k = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (0.0050² × 0.0020) = 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (2.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³) = 2400 dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹. 1 mark rate equation; 1 mark rearrangement; 1 mark value and units.
  3. (c) The order is determined experimentally from the data and reflects the rate-determining step, which involves only one H₂ molecule; the equation’s coefficient does not necessarily give the order.
    1 mark: orders come from experiments, not from the balanced equation. 1 mark: the rate-determining step (and so the rate equation) may involve fewer molecules than the equation shows.
Q9Rate equations & orders · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Propanone reacts with bromine in acidic solution:CH₃COCH₃(aq) + Br₂(aq) → CH₃COCH₂Br(aq) + HBr(aq)Experiment 1: [CH₃COCH₃] = 0.80 mol dm⁻³, [H⁺] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, [Br₂] = 0.0010 mol dm⁻³, initial rate = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Experiment 2: [CH₃COCH₃] = 0.80 mol dm⁻³, [H⁺] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, [Br₂] = 0.0020 mol dm⁻³, initial rate = 2.4 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹The rate equation is rate = k[CH₃COCH₃][H⁺].

  1. (a) Explain why the rate does not change when [Br₂] is doubled. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate constant, k, with its units. [2]
  3. (c) Predict the initial rate when [CH₃COCH₃] = 0.40 mol dm⁻³, [H⁺] = 0.25 mol dm⁻³ and [Br₂] = 0.0050 mol dm⁻³ at the same temperature. [2]

    1 mark: forward and reverse rates equal. 1 mark: no net change in amounts.
Q154Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) For an exothermic reaction at equilibrium, the temperature is raised. Both the forward and the reverse rates increase. Explain why the reverse rate increases by more than the forward rate. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Raising the temperature increases the proportion of molecules with energy ≥ Ea for both reactions. For an exothermic reaction the activation energy of the reverse reaction is larger than that of the forward reaction (Ea(reverse) = Ea(forward) + |ΔH|), so the reverse rate increases by a larger factor.
    1 mark: both rates increase because more molecules exceed Ea. 1 mark: Ea of reverse reaction is larger for exothermic reaction. 1 mark: larger Ea gives a larger increase in rate for the same temperature rise, so reverse increases more.
Q155Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Core · Paper 2 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) A reaction vessel contains only the products of a reversible reaction at time zero. State which of the forward or reverse rates is zero at the start, and explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The forward rate is zero at the start, because there are no reactants present; the reverse rate starts at a positive value because the products are present and react.
    1 mark: forward rate zero. 1 mark: no reactant to react.
Q156Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hydrogen and iodine are sealed in a flask at 450 °C: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).

  1. (a) Describe how the rates of the forward and backward reactions change from the moment H₂ and I₂ are mixed until equilibrium is reached. [3]
  2. (b) State the effect of adding a catalyst on (i) the rates of the forward and backward reactions (ii) the position of equilibrium (iii) the time taken to reach equilibrium. [3]
  3. (c) The forward reaction is exothermic. Explain why raising the temperature decreases the equilibrium yield of HI even though both rates increase. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Forward rate is greatest at the start and decreases as [H₂] and [I₂] fall; backward rate starts at zero and increases as [HI] rises; at equilibrium the two rates are equal (and non-zero).
    B1 forward decreases; B1 backward increases from zero; B1 equal at equilibrium.
  2. (b) (i) both increase by the same factor (ii) no change (iii) decreases
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) The backward (endothermic) reaction has the larger Ea, so its rate increases by a greater proportion; equilibrium shifts left.
    B1 endothermic direction favoured/its rate increases more; B1 equilibrium shifts to the left.
Q157Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The equilibrium N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) is established in a sealed syringe at constant temperature. The pressure is then increased by pushing the plunger in.

  1. (a) State which rate increases by the larger factor when the pressure is increased, the forward rate or the reverse rate, and explain your answer in terms of collisions. [4]
  2. (b) State the effect of this change on the equilibrium position. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The reverse rate increases by the larger factor. The reverse reaction depends on collisions between two NO₂ molecules (rate ∝ [NO₂]²), while the forward reaction depends on a single N₂O₄ molecule breaking up (rate ∝ [N₂O₄]). When pressure rises, concentrations rise; the reverse rate increases with the square of the concentration factor, so it increases more.
    1 mark: reverse rate increases more. 1 mark: forward rate is proportional to [N₂O₄] (one molecule). 1 mark: reverse rate ∝ [NO₂]² (two molecules must collide). 1 mark: concentrations increase, so the faster-increasing rate is the reverse.
  2. (b) The equilibrium moves to the side with fewer gas molecules (N₂O₄), since the reverse rate becomes greater than the forward rate until equilibrium is re-established.
    Fewer gas molecules favoured by increased pressure.
Q158Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

For the equilibrium PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g) at a fixed temperature, Kc = [PCl₃][Cl₂] ÷ [PCl₅] = 0.0250 mol dm⁻³. At equilibrium [PCl₅] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³ and [PCl₃] = [Cl₂].

  1. (a) Calculate [PCl₃] at equilibrium, to 2 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) PCl₅ dissociation is endothermic. State and explain the effect of raising the temperature on the value of Kc. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.071 mol dm⁻³
    [PCl₃] = [Cl₂] = x, so x² = Kc × [PCl₅] = 0.0250 × 0.20 = 5.0 × 10⁻³. x = √(5.0 × 10⁻³) = 0.0707, which is 0.071 mol dm⁻³ to 2 s.f. 1 mark for the rearrangement, 1 mark for the value.
  2. (b) Kc increases. The forward reaction is endothermic, so raising the temperature increases the forward rate more than the reverse rate, so the equilibrium moves to the right and the value of [PCl₃][Cl₂] ÷ [PCl₅] increases.
    1 mark: Kc increases. 1 mark: an endothermic forward reaction is favoured by a higher temperature, so the equilibrium moves to the right.
Q159Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

For the reaction A + B ⇌ C, the rate constants are kf = 4.0 × 10⁻³ dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹ and kr = 0.20 s⁻¹. A mixture has [A] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, [B] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³ and [C] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³.

  1. (a) Calculate the forward rate and the reverse rate for this mixture. [2]
  2. (b) State whether the mixture is at equilibrium and, if not, in which direction the net reaction proceeds. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) forward = 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹; reverse = 2.0 × 10⁻² mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹
    forward = 4.0 × 10⁻³ × 0.10 × 0.20 = 8.0 × 10⁻⁵; reverse = 0.20 × 0.10 = 2.0 × 10⁻² mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.
  2. (b) Not at equilibrium: the reverse rate is larger, so the net reaction proceeds in the reverse direction (C → A + B) until the forward and reverse rates are equal.
    1 mark: not equilibrium (rates unequal). 1 mark: net reverse direction.
Q160Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain, in terms of collisions, why the reverse reaction rate increases as the concentration of product increases in a reversible reaction at constant temperature. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) More product molecules per unit volume give more collisions between products per second; a larger fraction of these collisions can react, so the reverse rate increases until it equals the forward rate.
    1 mark: more product molecules per volume. 1 mark: more collisions per second between them. 1 mark: more successful collisions so reverse rate rises until balance.
Q161Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

For the equilibrium A(g) ⇌ B(g) at constant temperature, the equilibrium concentrations are [A] = 0.25 mol dm⁻³ and [B] = 0.75 mol dm⁻³.

  1. (a) Calculate Kc for this equilibrium. [2]
  2. (b) More B is added at constant temperature and a new equilibrium is established. Explain, in terms of the forward and reverse rates, why the equilibrium moves towards A, but Kc is unchanged. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kc = 3.0 (no units)
    Kc = [B] ÷ [A] = 0.75 ÷ 0.25 = 3.0. The units cancel because the expression is concentration over concentration.
  2. (b) Adding B increases the reverse rate at once, so the reverse rate is greater than the forward rate and the net reaction proceeds towards A until the two rates are equal again. Kc depends only on temperature, so it stays at 3.0.
    1 mark: the reverse rate rises first, so the net reaction moves towards A. 1 mark: Kc depends only on temperature, so it is unchanged.
Q162Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A reversible reaction has forward activation energy 80 kJ mol⁻¹ and reverse activation energy 110 kJ mol⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the enthalpy change, ΔH, of the forward reaction and state whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how a rise in temperature affects the position of this equilibrium, using the rates of forward and reverse reactions. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) ΔH = Ea(forward) − Ea(reverse) = 80 − 110 = −30 kJ mol⁻¹; exothermic.
    1 mark: correct difference of Ea values. 1 mark: negative, exothermic.
  2. (b) Raising temperature increases both rates, but the reverse reaction has the larger activation energy so its rate increases by the larger factor. The reverse rate now exceeds the forward rate, so the equilibrium moves to the left (towards reactants).
    1 mark: both rates rise. 1 mark: reverse Ea larger, so reverse rate increases more. 1 mark: equilibrium shifts left.
Q163Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

For the equilibrium H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g) at 700 K, the equilibrium concentrations are [H₂] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, [I₂] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³ and [HI] = 0.80 mol dm⁻³.

  1. (a) Calculate Kc for this equilibrium. [2]
  2. (b) State the units of Kc and explain your answer. [1]
  3. (c) State and explain the effect of increasing the pressure at constant temperature on the position of this equilibrium. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kc = 32
    Kc = [HI]² ÷ ([H₂][I₂]) = 0.80² ÷ (0.10 × 0.20) = 0.64 ÷ 0.020 = 32.
  2. (b) No units: there are 2 mol of gas on each side, so the units cancel.
    1 mark: the number of moles of gas on each side is equal, so the units of Kc cancel (both expressions have units (mol dm⁻³)⁰).
  3. (c) No change in position. Both sides contain 2 mol of gas, so the forward and reverse rates increase by the same factor and remain equal.
    1 mark: no shift, since there is no change in the number of gas molecules. 1 mark: the forward and reverse rates both increase by the same factor.
Q164Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Plan an experiment to show that, at equilibrium, the forward and reverse rates are equal but the concentrations are constant. Include the reaction you would choose, how you would start it, how you would measure concentrations over time, and how you would show that no net change occurs. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Choose a reversible reaction with a visible or measurable change (for example one with a coloured species or a measurable pressure change); start from reactants and from products separately; take readings of concentration (or absorbance/pressure) at intervals until constant; show the same final composition from both starting points; the constant composition with continuing reactions shows dynamic equilibrium.
    1 mark: suitable reversible reaction with measurable property. 1 mark: measurements over time. 1 mark: start from both sides to show same equilibrium. 1 mark: explanation that constant composition is consistent with equal forward and reverse rates.
Q165Rate & equilibrium (rate of forward/back reactions) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) At equilibrium, extra reactant is added to a reaction mixture. Describe how the rates of the forward and reverse reactions change with time until a new equilibrium is established. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The forward rate increases immediately, because the reactant concentration has increased. The reverse rate then increases gradually as more product forms. The forward rate falls back as reactants are used, and the two rates become equal again at a new equilibrium, which has a higher concentration of products than before the addition.
    1 mark: forward rate jumps up at once. 1 mark: reverse rate increases as product builds up. 1 mark: forward falls as reactant is used. 1 mark: rates become equal at a new equilibrium.
