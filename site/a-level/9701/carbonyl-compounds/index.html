Q11Nucleophilic addition (HCN) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What type of reaction occurs when ethanal reacts with HCN in the presence of KCN? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B nucleophilic addition
    CN⁻ (a nucleophile) attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon and the C=O π bond breaks; nothing is replaced. A is the typical reaction of alkenes, not carbonyls.
Q12Nucleophilic addition (HCN) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give the reagents and conditions for the reaction of propanal with HCN. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the mechanism of this reaction. In your answer, say where each curly arrow starts and ends, and give the intermediate. [4]
  3. (c) Name the product. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) HCN with a small amount of KCN (or NaCN), at 10–20 °C
    B1
  2. (b) Step 1: a curly arrow from the lone pair on the carbon of CN⁻ to the δ+ carbonyl carbon; a second arrow from the C=O π bond to the oxygen, forming the anion CH₃CH₂CH(O⁻)CN. Step 2: an arrow from a lone pair on O⁻ to the H of HCN, and an arrow from the H–C bond of HCN to its C, giving CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CN and regenerating CN⁻.
    B1 arrow from CN⁻ lone pair (on C) to carbonyl C. B1 arrow from C=O bond to O (dipole on C=O shown). B1 correct intermediate with negative charge on O. B1 O⁻ takes H⁺ from HCN (or H₂O), regenerating CN⁻.
  3. (c) 2-hydroxybutanenitrile
    B1

Q15Nucleophilic addition (HCN) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Lactic acid is used in the food industry. One laboratory route to it starts from ethanal.

  1. (a) Give the structural formula of the product when ethanal reacts with HCN. [1]
  2. (b) Give the reagent and condition to convert this product into lactic acid, CH₃CH(OH)COOH. [1]
  3. (c) Lactic acid made in this way is optically inactive, although it has a chiral centre. Explain why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃CH(OH)CN
    B1
  2. (b) Heat under reflux with dilute hydrochloric (or sulfuric) acid
    B1
  3. (c) The carbonyl group (and the C atom attacked) is planar; CN⁻ can attack from either side with equal probability; so equal amounts of both enantiomers form (a racemic mixture), whose effects on plane-polarised light cancel.
    B1 planar C=O. B1 attack equally from above and below. B1 racemic mixture / 50:50 enantiomers cancel.
Q16Nucleophilic addition (HCN) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) HCN is a weak acid. Explain why a little KCN is added to the reaction mixture. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the carbon atom of the carbonyl group is attacked by nucleophiles. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why alkenes do not undergo nucleophilic addition. [1]
  4. (d) Give one safety precaution needed for this reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) HCN is only slightly dissociated, so there are very few CN⁻ ions; KCN provides CN⁻ ions, which are the nucleophile needed to start the reaction.
    B1 HCN gives few CN⁻. B1 CN⁻ is the nucleophile.
  2. (b) Oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, so the C=O bond is polar and the carbon is δ+.
    B1
  3. (c) The C=C bond is non-polar and electron-rich, so it repels nucleophiles and attracts electrophiles instead.
    B1
  4. (d) Work in a fume cupboard because HCN is extremely toxic (or avoid acidifying KCN in the open laboratory).
    B1

  2. (b) Calculate the percentage by mass of propan-2-ol in the mixture. (Mr propan-2-ol 60.0; Mr CHI₃ 393.7) [3]

    B1

Q27Tri-iodomethane test · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

T is an unbranched alcohol, C₅H₁₂O, with a chiral centre. T gives a pale yellow precipitate with alkaline aqueous iodine. Oxidation of T with acidified dichromate gives U, which gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH but no change with Fehling's solution. The alkaline iodine reaction of T gives CHI₃ and the sodium salt V of a carboxylic acid.

  1. (a) Identify T, U and V, giving their structural formulae. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how the information shows that U is a ketone and that T is a secondary alcohol. [2]
  3. (c) Pentan-3-ol is also an unbranched secondary alcohol. Give two pieces of evidence from the data that rule it out. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) T pentan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃; U pentan-2-one, CH₃COCH₂CH₂CH₃; V CH₃CH₂CH₂COONa (sodium butanoate)
    B1 each. T must be unbranched, chiral and have CH₃CH(OH)–.
  2. (b) U gives an orange ppt with 2,4-DNPH (carbonyl) but does not reduce Fehling's (not an aldehyde); oxidation of an alcohol to a ketone shows T is secondary.
    B1 U ketone reasoning. B1 T secondary.
  3. (c) Pentan-3-ol has no chiral centre, and it does not give a positive tri-iodomethane test.
    B1 both.

  1. (a) Give the reagents for oxidising primary and secondary alcohols. [1]
  2. (b) Propanal is made from propan-1-ol. Explain why the product is distilled off as it forms, rather than heated under reflux. [2]
  3. (c) Name the organic product when propan-2-ol is heated under reflux with the same reagent, and explain why it is not oxidised further. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (K₂Cr₂O₇ with dilute H₂SO₄)
    B1
  2. (b) Propanal has a low boiling point and is removed immediately, so it is not oxidised further to propanoic acid, which would happen under reflux.
    B1 distil immediately. B1 prevents further oxidation to carboxylic acid.

    B1 propanone. B1 no H on C=O carbon / ketones resist oxidation.
Q30Carbonyl structure & reactions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State the shape around the carbonyl carbon atom and the approximate bond angle. [2]
  2. (b) Write an equation, using [H], for the reduction of butanone by NaBH₄ and name the product. [2]
  3. (c) But-3-en-2-one, CH₂=CHCOCH₃, is reduced with NaBH₄. Give the structural formula of the product and explain why the C=C bond is not affected. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Trigonal planar; 120°
    B1 each.
  2. (b) CH₃COCH₂CH₃ + 2[H] → CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃; butan-2-ol
    B1 equation. B1 name.
  3. (c) CH₂=CHCH(OH)CH₃ (but-3-en-2-ol). NaBH₄ provides H⁻, a nucleophile, which attacks the δ+ carbon of the polar C=O; the non-polar C=C does not attract nucleophiles.
    B1 product. B1 H⁻ nucleophile / C=C non-polar.
