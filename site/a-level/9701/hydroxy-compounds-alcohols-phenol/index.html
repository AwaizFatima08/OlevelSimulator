Q1Phenol: acidity & reactions · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of phenol with aqueous sodium hydroxide. [1]
  2. (b) Place ethanol, phenol and water in order of increasing acidity. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why phenol is more acidic than ethanol. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅O⁻Na⁺ + H₂O
    B1
  2. (b) ethanol < water < phenol
    B1
  3. (c) In the phenoxide ion the negative charge on oxygen is delocalised into the benzene ring (a p-orbital lone pair overlaps with the π system), stabilising the anion; in ethoxide the ethyl group donates electrons, intensifying the charge on O; so phenol ionises more.
    B1 phenoxide charge delocalised into ring; B1 ethoxide charge concentrated / alkyl group electron-donating; B1 more stable anion means equilibrium further to the right / more H⁺ released
Q2Phenol: acidity & reactions · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe what is seen when bromine water is added to aqueous phenol at room temperature. [2]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the reaction, naming the organic product. [2]
  3. (c) State the conditions needed for benzene to react with bromine. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Orange/brown bromine water is decolourised and a white precipitate forms.
    B1 decolourised; B1 white precipitate
  2. (b) C₆H₅OH + 3Br₂ → C₆H₂Br₃OH + 3HBr; 2,4,6-tribromophenol.
    B1 equation; B1 name
  3. (c) Liquid bromine (not bromine water) with a halogen carrier catalyst such as FeBr₃ / AlBr₃ / iron, at room temperature.
    B1
Q3Phenol: acidity & reactions · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which reagent reacts with phenol but not with ethanol?A sodium metalB aqueous sodium hydroxideC acidified potassium dichromate(VI)D aqueous sodium carbonate, forming CO₂ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B aqueous sodium hydroxide
    Phenol is acidic enough to react with NaOH(aq) to form sodium phenoxide; ethanol is not. Both react with sodium metal. Phenol is too weak an acid to release CO₂ from carbonate. Dichromate oxidises ethanol, not phenol.
Q4Phenol: acidity & reactions · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Phenol reacts with dilute nitric acid at room temperature. Name the two main organic products. [2]
  2. (b) Benzene needs a mixture of concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids at about 55 °C to be nitrated. Explain why phenol reacts under much milder conditions. [3]
  3. (c) Write an equation for the reaction of phenol with sodium metal. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2-nitrophenol and 4-nitrophenol.
    1 mark each
  2. (b) A lone pair on the oxygen of the OH group is delocalised into the ring; this increases the electron density of the ring (especially at the 2, 4 and 6 positions); the ring is attacked more easily by electrophiles.
    B1 O lone pair (p-orbital) overlaps / delocalised into π system; B1 higher electron density in ring; B1 more susceptible to electrophilic attack (ring activated)
  3. (c) 2C₆H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₆H₅ONa + H₂
    B1
Q5Phenol: acidity & reactions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which list shows the compounds in order of increasing acidity?A phenol, ethanol, water, ethanoic acidB ethanol, water, phenol, ethanoic acidC water, ethanol, ethanoic acid, phenolD ethanol, phenol, water, ethanoic acid [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B ethanol, water, phenol, ethanoic acid
    Ethanol is the weakest acid (alkyl group donates electrons); water next; phenol is stronger because phenoxide is stabilised by delocalisation; ethanoic acid is strongest (carboxylate charge spread over two O atoms). D puts phenol below water.

  3. (c) Explain why the reaction is carried out in alkaline solution. [1]

    B1 orange/yellow precipitate; B1 azo compound/dye
  2. (b) HO–C₆H₄–N=N–C₆H₅ with the N=N joined at the 4-position (para) to the OH group.
    B1 –N=N– link between two rings; B1 para to OH
  3. (c) Phenol forms the phenoxide ion, which has a more electron-rich ring and couples more readily (and dissolves).
    B1

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of 2,4,6-tribromophenol, in g, obtained. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (Mr: phenol = 94.0; 2,4,6-tribromophenol = 330.7) [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the minimum mass of bromine, Br₂, in g, needed to react with all of the phenol. (Mr Br₂ = 159.8) [2]

  1. (a) 14.1 g

  2. (b) 24.0 g
    M1 n(Br₂) = 3 × 0.0500 = 0.150 mol; A1 24.0 g
  3. (c) Its melting point is sharp and matches the data-book value (or it gives a single spot on a chromatogram).
    B1 any valid test
Q9Phenol: acidity & reactions · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

X, Y and Z are isomers with formula C₇H₈O. Each contains a benzene ring.X reacts with aqueous NaOH and with bromine water at room temperature forming C₇H₅Br₃O.Y does not react with aqueous NaOH but reacts with sodium, giving hydrogen.Z does not react with aqueous NaOH or with sodium.

  1. (a) Identify X, Y and Z, giving their structures and names. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why X gives a tribromo product but 4-methylphenol would give only a dibromo product. [2]
  3. (c) Write an equation for the reaction of Y with sodium. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) X: 3-methylphenol (CH₃C₆H₄OH, OH and CH₃ at 1,3); Y: phenylmethanol, C₆H₅CH₂OH; Z: methoxybenzene, C₆H₅OCH₃.
    1 mark each
  2. (b) Bromine substitutes at the 2, 4 and 6 positions relative to OH; in 3-methylphenol all three positions are free; in 4-methylphenol the 4-position is taken by CH₃, so only 2 and 6 can be substituted.
    B1 2,4,6 directing by OH; B1 4-position blocked in 4-methylphenol
  3. (c) 2C₆H₅CH₂OH + 2Na → 2C₆H₅CH₂ONa + H₂
    B1
Q10Phenol: acidity & reactions · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Phenol, used in some antiseptics, has Ka = 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol/dm³ at 298 K.

  1. (a) Calculate the pH of 0.100 mol/dm³ phenol. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the pH of 0.100 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid (Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ mol/dm³), to 2 decimal places. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why ethanoic acid is a stronger acid than phenol. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.44
    M1 [H⁺] = √(1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 0.100) = 3.606 × 10⁻⁶ mol/dm³; A1 pH = 5.44
  2. (b) 2.87
    B1 [H⁺] = 1.342 × 10⁻³; pH = 2.87
  3. (c) In the ethanoate ion the negative charge is delocalised over two electronegative oxygen atoms; in phenoxide it is spread into the ring of carbon atoms (less electronegative), so it is less stabilised.
    B1 carboxylate delocalised over two O atoms; B1 phenoxide charge partly on C atoms, less stable anion

  3. (c) Name the product formed if the oxidation is carried out with the alcohol in excess and the product distilled off immediately. Explain why distilling gives this product. [2]

Q12Alcohols: oxidation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks

Q14Alcohols: oxidation · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

A student heats propan-1-ol and drips in acidified sodium dichromate(VI) from a dropping funnel. The flask is fitted with a still-head, thermometer and condenser set for distillation. The receiving flask stands in ice.Boiling points: propanal 49 °C; propan-1-ol 97 °C; propanoic acid 141 °C.

  1. (a) The student wants to collect propanal. State the temperature range on the thermometer at the still-head at which she should collect the distillate. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the receiving flask is placed in ice. [1]
  3. (c) Describe a test, with the result, to show that the distillate contains an aldehyde. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the dichromate solution is added slowly from a dropping funnel to the warm alcohol. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) About 46–52 °C (around 49 °C).
    B1 range including 49 °C and below 97 °C
  2. (b) Propanal is volatile (low b.p.), so cooling reduces loss by evaporation.
    B1

Q17Alcohols: oxidation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Older roadside breath-testing devices used the oxidation of ethanol by acidified dichromate(VI).

    B1

  4. (d) Suggest why the reaction must be carried out in a fume cupboard. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + SOCl₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl + SO₂ + HCl
    B1
  2. (b) Both by-products (SO₂ and HCl) are gases, so the chloroalkane is easy to separate.
    B1
  3. (c) Substitution (nucleophilic substitution of OH by Cl).
    B1
  4. (d) SO₂ and HCl are toxic/corrosive gases.
    B1
Q24Alcohols: substitution & dehydration · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

W has formula C₃H₈O₂. 0.380 g of W reacts with excess PCl₅ and gives 240 cm³ of HCl gas at room temperature and pressure (1 mol of gas occupies 24.0 dm³).

  1. (a) Show that W contains two –OH groups. (Mr of W = 76.0) [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in cm³ at r.t.p., formed when the same mass of W reacts with excess sodium. [1]
  3. (c) Give the structures of the two possible diols. Describe a chemical test that would distinguish them, giving the result for each. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) n(W) = 0.380 ÷ 76.0 = 0.00500 mol; n(HCl) = 0.240 ÷ 24.0 = 0.0100 mol; ratio 1 : 2, so two OH groups (each OH gives one HCl).
    M1 both mole values; A1 ratio and conclusion
  2. (b) 120 cm³
    B1 2 OH → 1 H₂ per W: 0.00500 mol H₂ × 24 000 = 120 cm³
  3. (c) Propane-1,2-diol CH₃CH(OH)CH₂OH and propane-1,3-diol HOCH₂CH₂CH₂OH; warm with alkaline aqueous iodine: propane-1,2-diol gives a yellow precipitate (CHI₃) because it contains CH₃CH(OH)–; propane-1,3-diol gives no precipitate.
    B1 each structure; B1 test with both results
Q25Alcohols: substitution & dehydration · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

2-Methylbutan-2-ol is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid and the products are distilled.

  1. (a) Name the two alkenes formed from 2-methylbutan-2-ol and explain why neither shows cis/trans isomerism. [3]
  2. (b) In a second experiment, 10.0 g of cyclohexanol (Mr 100.0) is dehydrated and 5.74 g of cyclohexene (Mr 82.0) is collected. Calculate the percentage yield to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) The crude cyclohexene is shaken with sodium carbonate solution and then dried with anhydrous calcium chloride. State the purpose of each step. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2-methylbut-1-ene and 2-methylbut-2-ene; in each, one carbon of the C=C carries two identical groups (two H or two CH₃).
    B1 each name; B1 reason
  2. (b) 70.0%
    M1 theoretical = 0.100 × 82.0 = 8.20 g; A1 5.74 ÷ 8.20 × 100 = 70.0%
  3. (c) Sodium carbonate neutralises/removes acid impurity; CaCl₂ removes water.
    1 mark each
Q26Alcohols: reactions · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe two observations when a small piece of sodium is added to ethanol. [2]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the reaction and name the organic product. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the reaction is less vigorous than that of sodium with water. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bubbles (effervescence) of gas; sodium sinks/dissolves slowly (less vigorously than with water).
    1 mark each for any two of: bubbles; sodium disappears slowly; solution warms; colourless solution forms
  2. (b) 2C₂H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₂H₅ONa + H₂; sodium ethoxide.
    B1 equation; B1 name
  3. (c) Ethanol is a weaker acid than water: the ethyl group donates electrons, making the O–H bond less polar.
    B1
Q27Alcohols: reactions · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which alcohol gives a yellow precipitate when warmed with alkaline aqueous iodine?A methanolB propan-1-olC propan-2-olD 2-methylpropan-2-ol [1]

  1. (a) C propan-2-ol

    B1
Q29Alcohols: reactions · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in dm³ at r.t.p., formed when 0.92 g of ethanol reacts with excess sodium. (1 mol of gas occupies 24.0 dm³) [2]
  2. (b) 1.48 g of an unknown alcohol, ROH, gives 240 cm³ of hydrogen with excess sodium. Calculate the Mr of the alcohol and deduce its molecular formula. [2]
  3. (c) The alcohol gives a yellow precipitate with alkaline aqueous iodine. Identify it. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.240 dm³
    M1 n(ethanol) = 0.92 ÷ 46.0 = 0.0200 mol, n(H₂) = 0.0100; A1 0.240 dm³
  2. (b) Mr = 74; C₄H₉OH
    M1 n(H₂) = 0.0100, n(ROH) = 0.0200; A1 Mr = 1.48 ÷ 0.0200 = 74, C₄H₉OH (4×12 + 10×1 + 16 = 74)
  3. (c) Butan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃.
    B1 (only C₄ alcohol with CH₃CH(OH)– group)
Q30Alcohols: preparation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Ethanol for hand sanitisers in Pakistan is made mainly by fermenting molasses from sugar mills, but elsewhere it is also made from ethene.

  1. (a) Give the conditions for the industrial hydration of ethene to make ethanol. [2]
  2. (b) Write equations for the hydration of ethene and for the fermentation of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the atom economy of fermentation, to 3 significant figures. (Mr: glucose = 180.0, ethanol = 46.0) [2]
  4. (d) Name a laboratory reagent that reduces ethanal to ethanol. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Steam, phosphoric acid catalyst, about 300 °C and 6–7 MPa (60–70 atm).
    B1 steam and H₃PO₄ catalyst; B1 temperature and pressure
  2. (b) C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH; C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
    1 mark each
  3. (c) 51.1%
    M1 2 × 46.0 ÷ 180.0 × 100; A1 = 51.1% (hydration is 100%)
  4. (d) Sodium tetrahydridoborate, NaBH₄ (or LiAlH₄ in dry ether).
    B1
