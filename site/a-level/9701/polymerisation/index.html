Q1Addition polymers · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Water pipes and crates sold in Pakistani markets are often made from poly(propene) and poly(chloroethene).

  1. (a) Write the repeat unit of poly(propene). Use the form –CH₂–CHX– with X identified. [1]
  2. (b) Give the structural formula and name of the monomer used to make poly(chloroethene), PVC. [2]
  3. (c) State the type of polymerisation and why no other product forms. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–
    B1 continuation bonds shown at both ends.
  2. (b) CH₂=CHCl, chloroethene
    B1 formula; B1 name.
  3. (c) Addition polymerisation; the C=C π bonds open and monomers join with no atoms lost, so the polymer is the only product.
    B1 addition; B1 explanation.
Q2Addition polymers · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A polymer has the repeat unit –CH₂–CCl₂–. What is the monomer?A CH₂=CHClB CHCl=CHClC CH₂=CCl₂D CCl₂=CCl₂ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C CH₂=CCl₂
    Both Cl atoms are on the same carbon of the repeat unit, so the monomer is 1,1-dichloroethene. B would give –CHCl–CHCl–.
Q3Addition polymers · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give the structural formula of the monomer and the repeat unit of PTFE, poly(tetrafluoroethene). [2]
  2. (b) Explain why PTFE is very unreactive, making it suitable for non-stick cooking pans. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Monomer CF₂=CF₂; repeat unit –CF₂–CF₂–
    B1 each.
  2. (b) The C–F bonds are very strong (high bond energy) and the polymer is saturated (no C=C), so it resists attack by most chemicals.
    B1 strong C–F bonds; B1 saturated/non-polar overall, no reactive sites.
Q4Addition polymers · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Expanded polystyrene is used to pack electronic goods and to make disposable food boxes.

  1. (a) Give the name and structural formula of the monomer of polystyrene. [2]
  2. (b) Give the repeat unit of polystyrene. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why poly(alkene)s such as polystyrene are chemically unreactive. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) phenylethene (styrene), C₆H₅CH=CH₂
    B1 name; B1 formula.
  2. (b) –CH₂–CH(C₆H₅)–
    B1.
  3. (c) They are saturated (contain no C=C in the chain) and have strong non-polar C–C and C–H bonds.
    B1.
Q5Addition polymers · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A molecule of poly(ethene) has Mr = 280 000. Calculate the number of monomer units in the molecule. [Mr of C₂H₄ = 28.0] [1]
  2. (b) A chain of poly(chloroethene) contains 2000 monomer units. Calculate its Mr. [Mr of C₂H₃Cl = 62.5] [1]
  3. (c) Explain why a sample of poly(ethene) is described by an average Mr rather than a single value. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10000
    B1 280 000/28.0.
  2. (b) 125 000
    B1 2000 × 62.5.
  3. (c) Chains grow and stop at random, so the polymer contains chains of different lengths.
    B1.
Q6Addition polymers · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A city council plans to reduce waste from thin plastic shopping bags made of poly(ethene) and from PVC pipes.

  1. (a) Explain why poly(alkene)s cause problems in landfill sites. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why burning PVC waste is particularly harmful. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one advantage of recycling over incineration. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) They are non-biodegradable because they are saturated/non-polar with strong C–C and C–H bonds; microorganisms cannot break them down, so they persist for many years.
    B1 non-biodegradable; B1 reason (inert, no polar bonds for enzymes to attack).
  2. (b) Combustion releases toxic and acidic hydrogen chloride gas (and possibly toxic chlorinated compounds); HCl causes acid rain/respiratory damage.
    B1 HCl produced; B1 harmful effect.
  3. (c) Saves crude oil (raw material) and energy; no toxic gases released.
    B1.

Q9Addition polymers · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Draw (as a text formula) the repeat unit of the polymer formed from but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃. [1]
  2. (b) A sample of this polymer has an average Mr of 112 000. Calculate the average number of but-2-ene monomer units in each chain. [Mr of C₄H₈ = 56.0] [1]
  3. (c) State the atom economy of any addition polymerisation and explain your answer. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the polymer from but-2-ene is saturated even though the monomer is unsaturated. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) –CH(CH₃)–CH(CH₃)–
    B1.
  2. (b) 2000
    B1 112 000 ÷ 56.0 = 2000 (the repeat unit has the same formula as the monomer, ignoring end groups).
  3. (c) 100 %; all the atoms of the monomer end up in the polymer – it is the only product.
    B1 100 %; B1 only one product / no atoms lost.
  4. (d) The π bond of each C=C breaks to form new C–C single bonds to neighbouring monomers.
    B1.
Q10Addition polymers · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of poly(ethene), (C₂H₄)ₙ. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass, in kg, of carbon dioxide produced when 14.0 kg of poly(ethene) burns completely. [Mr: C₂H₄ 28.0; CO₂ 44.0] [2]
  3. (c) Give one reason why poly(ethene) waste should not be burned in an open fire. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (C₂H₄)ₙ + 3nO₂ → 2nCO₂ + 2nH₂O
    B1.
  2. (b) 44.0 kg
    M1 14 000/28.0 = 500 mol repeat units → 1000 mol CO₂; A1 × 44.0 = 44 000 g = 44.0 kg.
  3. (c) Incomplete combustion produces toxic carbon monoxide (and soot).
    B1.
Q11Addition polymers · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Perspex, a clear plastic used for shop signs, is an addition polymer made from methyl 2-methylpropenoate.

  1. (a) Give the structural formula of the monomer, methyl 2-methylpropenoate. [1]
  2. (b) Give the repeat unit of the polymer. [1]
  3. (c) The polymer is heated with aqueous sodium hydroxide. Describe the change to the side groups and name the small molecule released. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the polymer backbone survives this treatment while a polyester backbone would not. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₂=C(CH₃)COOCH₃
    B1.
  2. (b) –CH₂–C(CH₃)(COOCH₃)–
    B1.
  3. (c) The ester side groups are hydrolysed to sodium carboxylate groups (–COO⁻Na⁺); methanol is released. The main carbon chain is not broken.
    B1 –COOCH₃ → –COO⁻Na⁺; B1 methanol.
  4. (d) The backbone of the addition polymer contains only C–C bonds; in a polyester, ester links are in the main chain and are hydrolysed.
    B1.
Q12Addition polymers · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A waste incinerator burns PVC pipe offcuts.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of hydrogen chloride, at r.t.p., released when 1.00 kg of PVC is burned completely. Assume each repeat unit –CH₂–CHCl– gives one HCl. [Mr of C₂H₃Cl = 62.5; molar gas volume 24.0 dm³] [2]
  2. (b) The HCl is removed by passing the gases through powdered calcium carbonate. Write the equation and calculate the mass of calcium carbonate needed. [Mr CaCO₃ = 100.1] [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 384 dm³
    M1 1000/62.5 = 16.0 mol; A1 × 24.0 = 384 dm³.
  2. (b) CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂; 801 g
    B1 equation; M1 8.00 mol CaCO₃; A1 801 g.
Q13Addition polymers · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Prop-2-en-1-ol, CH₂=CHCH₂OH, can form an addition polymer. Give its repeat unit. [1]
  2. (b) Suggest why the addition polymer from (a) can dissolve in water, whereas poly(propene) cannot. [2]
  3. (c) Explain whether prop-2-en-1-ol alone could form a condensation polymer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) –CH₂–CH(CH₂OH)–
    B1.
  2. (b) Each repeat unit has an –OH group that forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules; poly(propene) has only non-polar C–H/C–C bonds with instantaneous dipole forces.
    B1 –OH hydrogen bonds with water; B1 poly(propene) non-polar.
  3. (c) No – it has only one functional group (–OH) able to take part in condensation; a condensation polymer needs two reacting groups per monomer (or two different monomers).
    B1 only one condensing group; B1 needs two functional groups.

Q16Condensation polymers (polyesters/polyamides) · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) Classify each linkage: –COO– and –CONH–. [2]

Q26Condensation polymers (polyesters/polyamides) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the complete hydrolysis of one repeat unit of nylon-6,6, –NH(CH₂)₆NHCO(CH₂)₄CO–, by hot concentrated hydrochloric acid. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the diamine is obtained as its salt. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why nylon clothing is damaged by spills of acid. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) –NH(CH₂)₆NHCO(CH₂)₄CO– + 2H₂O + 2HCl → [H₃N(CH₂)₆NH₃]²⁺ 2Cl⁻ + HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH
    B1 diacid and diammonium salt products; B1 balanced (2H₂O and 2HCl).
  2. (b) The amine groups are basic and are protonated by the excess acid.
    B1.
  3. (c) The amide links in the polymer chain are hydrolysed, breaking the chains and weakening the fibres.
    B1.
Q27Polymer properties & degradability · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why polyesters and polyamides are biodegradable while poly(alkene)s are not. [2]
  2. (b) Describe what is meant by a photodegradable polymer and give one structural feature that makes a polymer photodegradable. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Polyesters/polyamides contain polar ester/amide links that can be hydrolysed (by enzymes/water); poly(alkene)s have only non-polar C–C and C–H bonds that are not attacked.
    B1 ester/amide links hydrolysed; B1 poly(alkene)s non-polar/inert.
  2. (b) One that breaks down when exposed to light (UV); it contains C=O groups that absorb UV and break bonds nearby.
    B1 meaning; B1 C=O groups.
Q28Polymer properties & degradability · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why nylon-6,6 has a much higher melting point than poly(ethene) of similar chain length. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why low-density poly(ethene), with many side branches, is softer than high-density poly(ethene). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nylon chains form hydrogen bonds between N–H and C=O of neighbouring chains; poly(ethene) has only weaker instantaneous dipole–induced dipole forces, needing less energy to overcome.
    B1 hydrogen bonds in nylon; B1 only id–id forces in poly(ethene)/less energy.
  2. (b) Branches stop the chains packing closely, so intermolecular forces are weaker and chains slide past each other more easily.
    B1 packing; B1 weaker forces.
Q29Polymer properties & degradability · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A large city such as Karachi produces thousands of tonnes of plastic waste every day.

  1. (a) Give one advantage and one disadvantage of incinerating plastic waste. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why mixed plastic waste is difficult to recycle. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why a bag made of a polyester would break down faster in a compost heap than one made of poly(propene). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Advantage: energy released can generate electricity / reduces landfill volume. Disadvantage: releases CO₂ (greenhouse gas) and toxic gases such as HCl from PVC or CO.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Different polymers have different properties/melting points and must be sorted first.
    B1.
  3. (c) Ester links are hydrolysed by water/enzymes from microorganisms; poly(propene) has no such polar links.
    B1 hydrolysis of ester; B1 poly(propene) inert.
