Q10Phenylamine & azo dyes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why azo compounds such as 4-hydroxyazobenzene are coloured. [2]
  2. (b) Azo compounds are used as dyes for cotton and as acid–base indicators. Suggest how a change in pH can change the colour of an azo indicator. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the benzenediazonium ion is only a weak electrophile, so that it reacts with phenol but not with benzene. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The N=N group links the two benzene rings to give an extended delocalised system; this absorbs visible light (some wavelengths), and the remaining light is seen as the colour.
    B1 extended delocalisation through –N=N–; B1 absorbs in visible region.
  2. (b) Adding or removing H⁺ (e.g. on the N=N or –OH/–SO₃⁻ groups) changes the extent of delocalisation, so the wavelength absorbed changes.
    B1 protonation/deprotonation; B1 changes delocalisation/absorbed wavelength.
  3. (c) Its positive charge is delocalised over the ring and N=N group (stabilised); phenol's ring is activated by the lone pair on oxygen (more electron-rich), but benzene is not.
    B1 weak electrophile, charge delocalised; B1 phenol ring activated/electron-rich.
