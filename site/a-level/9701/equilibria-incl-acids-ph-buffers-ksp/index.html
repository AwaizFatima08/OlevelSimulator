Q1Kc & Kp calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Hydrogen and iodine react in a sealed flask: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). At 700 K the equilibrium concentrations are [H₂] = 0.0040 mol/dm³, [I₂] = 0.0025 mol/dm³ and [HI] = 0.0230 mol/dm³.

  1. (a) Write an expression for Kc for this reaction. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the value of Kc at this temperature. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why Kc for this reaction has no units. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kc = [HI]² / ([H₂][I₂])
    B1
  2. (b) 52.9
    M1 0.0230² / (0.0040 × 0.0025)A1 52.9
  3. (c) There are equal numbers of moles of gas (concentration terms) on each side, so the units cancel.
    B1
Q2Kc & Kp calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A student in Karachi mixes 1.00 mol of ethanoic acid with 1.20 mol of ethanol and a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid, then leaves the mixture in a sealed flask until equilibrium is reached:CH₃COOH(l) + C₂H₅OH(l) ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅(l) + H₂O(l)At equilibrium, 0.72 mol of ethyl ethanoate is present.

  1. (a) Calculate the amounts, in mol, of ethanoic acid, ethanol and water present at equilibrium. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate Kc. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the total volume of the mixture is not needed to calculate Kc for this reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ethanoic acid 0.28 mol; ethanol 0.48 mol; water 0.72 mol
    M1 0.72 mol of each reactant usedA1 all three amounts
  2. (b) 3.86
    M1 Kc = (0.72 × 0.72)/(0.28 × 0.48)A1 3.86
  3. (c) The volume terms cancel because there are equal numbers of moles on each side of the equation.
    B1
Q3Kc & Kp calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

1.00 mol of dinitrogen tetroxide is heated in a closed container: N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g). At equilibrium, 25.0% of the N₂O₄ has dissociated and the total pressure is 150 kPa.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of each gas at equilibrium and the mole fraction of each gas. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the partial pressure of each gas. [1]
  3. (c) Write an expression for Kp and calculate its value, giving its units. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) N₂O₄ 0.75 mol, NO₂ 0.50 mol; mole fractions 0.60 and 0.40
    M1 amountsA1 mole fractions (total 1.25 mol)
  2. (b) p(N₂O₄) = 90 kPa; p(NO₂) = 60 kPa
    B1 both
  3. (c) Kp = p(NO₂)²/p(N₂O₄) = 40.0 kPa
    M1 expressionA1 40.0 kPa
Q4Kc & Kp calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A mixture of nitrogen and hydrogen is allowed to reach equilibrium at a fixed temperature: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g). At equilibrium the mixture contains 2.40 mol N₂, 7.20 mol H₂ and 1.60 mol NH₃, and the total pressure is 2.00 × 10⁴ kPa.

  1. (a) Calculate the amounts of nitrogen and hydrogen that were present at the start. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the partial pressure of each gas at equilibrium. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate Kp and give its units. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) N₂ 3.20 mol; H₂ 9.60 mol
    M1 1.60 mol NH₃ formed needs 0.80 mol N₂ and 2.40 mol H₂A1 both
  2. (b) p(N₂) = 4.29 × 10³ kPa; p(H₂) = 1.29 × 10⁴ kPa; p(NH₃) = 2.86 × 10³ kPa
    M1 mole fractions (total 11.2 mol)A1 all three
  3. (c) Kp = 8.96 × 10⁻¹⁰ kPa⁻²
    M1 Kp = p(NH₃)² / (p(N₂) × p(H₂)³)A1 8.96 × 10⁻¹⁰ kPa⁻²

  1. (a) A 0.0267
    At equilibrium: PCl₅ 0.120 mol, PCl₃ 0.080 mol, Cl₂ 0.080 mol; concentrations 0.060, 0.040, 0.040 mol/dm³. Kc = 0.040 × 0.040 / 0.060 = 0.0267. B uses amounts instead of concentrations.
Q6pH, Ka, Kw & strong/weak acids · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Write an expression for the ionic product of water, Kw, and state its value at 298 K. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the pH of 0.0250 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the pH of 0.0150 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide solution at 298 K. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶
    B1
  2. (b) 1.60
    B1 −log 0.0250 = 1.60
  3. (c) 12.18
    M1 [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 0.0150 = 6.67 × 10⁻¹³A1 pH = 12.18
Q7pH, Ka, Kw & strong/weak acids · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Propanoic acid, used as a food preservative, has Ka = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵ mol/dm³ at 298 K.

  1. (a) Write an expression for the acid dissociation constant, Ka, of propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the pH of 0.100 mol/dm³ propanoic acid. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) State two assumptions you made in (b). [2]
  4. (d) Calculate pKa for propanoic acid. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ka = [CH₃CH₂COO⁻][H⁺] / [CH₃CH₂COOH]
    B1
  2. (b) 2.93
    M1 [H⁺] = √(1.35 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.100) = 1.16 × 10⁻³A1 pH = 2.93
  3. (c) [H⁺] = [CH₃CH₂COO⁻] (H⁺ from water ignored); the equilibrium concentration of the acid equals 0.100 (dissociation is negligible).
    B1 each
  4. (d) 4.87
    B1

  3. (c) Explain why methanoic acid is described as a weak acid. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.03 × 10⁻³ mol/dm³

  2. (b) 1.82 × 10⁻⁴ mol/dm³
    M1 Ka = [H⁺]² / 0.200A1 1.82 × 10⁻⁴
  3. (c) It only partially dissociates (ionises) in water.
    B1
Q9pH, Ka, Kw & strong/weak acids · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) 25.0 cm³ of 0.120 mol/dm³ HCl is mixed with 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH. Calculate the pH of the resulting solution. [3]
  2. (b) At 50 °C, Kw = 5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶. Calculate the pH of pure water at 50 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why pure water at 50 °C is neutral even though its pH is below 7. [1]
  4. (d) Use the data to deduce whether the dissociation of water is exothermic or endothermic. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.65
    M1 n(H⁺) = 3.00 × 10⁻³; n(OH⁻) = 2.00 × 10⁻³M1 excess H⁺ = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol in 45.0 cm³ → 0.0222 mol/dm³A1 pH = 1.65
  2. (b) 6.63
    M1 [H⁺] = √(5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴)A1 6.63
  3. (c) [H⁺] = [OH⁻] in pure water.
    B1
  4. (d) Endothermic: Kw increases as temperature increases.
    B1
Q10pH, Ka, Kw & strong/weak acids · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Calculate the pH of 0.00500 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid, assuming it is fully dissociated into 2H⁺ and SO₄²⁻. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the pH of 0.00500 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid (Ka = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ mol/dm³). [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the volume of 0.0100 mol/dm³ NaOH needed to neutralise 25.0 cm³ of each acid. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the volume of NaOH needed to neutralise the ethanoic acid depends on its full concentration, even though only a small fraction of the acid is dissociated. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.00
    B1 [H⁺] = 0.0100
  2. (b) 3.53
    M1 [H⁺] = √(1.74 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.00500) = 2.95 × 10⁻⁴A1 3.53
  3. (c) sulfuric acid 25.0 cm³; ethanoic acid 12.5 cm³
    B1 each
  4. (d) As OH⁻ removes H⁺, the equilibrium CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺ shifts right until all the acid has reacted.
    B1
Q11Solubility product (Ksp) & common ion effect · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
