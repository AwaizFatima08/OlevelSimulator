Q1Gibbs free energy & feasibility · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g); ΔH° = +178 kJ mol⁻¹; ΔS° = +161 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹

  1. (a) Calculate ΔG° at 298 K and state whether the reaction is feasible at this temperature. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the minimum temperature at which the reaction becomes feasible. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) +130 kJ mol⁻¹; not feasible
    M1 ΔG = 178 − 298 × 0.161 (ΔS in kJ); A1 value and conclusion.
  2. (b) 1110 K
    M1 ΔG = 0 → T = ΔH/ΔS = 178/0.161; A1 1110 K (1105.6 K).

  1. (a) Calculate the standard entropy change for N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g). [2]
  2. (b) Explain the sign of your answer. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) −198.4 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
    M1 ΔS = ΣS(products) − ΣS(reactants) = 2(192.5) − [191.6 + 3(130.6)]; A1 value with sign and units.
  2. (b) Negative: the number of gas molecules decreases (4 → 2), so fewer ways of arranging particles.
    B1.
Q27Entropy changes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Methane (natural gas) from the Sui gas field is burned in home cookers.S° / J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹: CH₄(g) 186.2; O₂(g) 205.0; CO₂(g) 213.6; H₂O(l) 69.9

  1. (a) Calculate ΔS° for the complete combustion of methane: CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l). [2]
  2. (b) Predict how ΔS° would differ if the water were formed as steam. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) −242.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
    M1 [213.6 + 2(69.9)] − [186.2 + 2(205.0)]; A1.
  2. (b) Less negative (≈ zero): 3 mol gas would give 3 mol gas, and gaseous water has much higher entropy than liquid water.
    B1 less negative; B1 reason.
Q28Entropy changes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Instant cold packs used by sports teams contain solid ammonium nitrate and a pouch of water.

  1. (a) Ammonium nitrate dissolves in water spontaneously although the process is endothermic. Explain this in terms of entropy. [2]
  2. (b) State the sign of the entropy change of the surroundings for this process and explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The ordered solid lattice breaks up into freely moving aqueous ions, so ΔS of the system is large and positive; TΔS is greater than ΔH, so ΔG is negative.
    B1 ΔS positive (ions dispersed); B1 TΔS > ΔH so ΔG < 0.
  2. (b) Negative – energy is absorbed from the surroundings (they cool), reducing their entropy.
    B1.
Q29Entropy changes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Place the following reactions in order of increasing ΔS, without calculation. Explain your order.W: 2NaHCO₃(s) → Na₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(g) + CO₂(g)X: C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)Y: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2SO₃(g)Z: 2H₂O₂(l) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g) [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Y < X < Z < W
    B1 Y lowest/negative – amount of gas decreases (3 mol → 2 mol); B1 X ≈ 0 – 1 mol gas → 1 mol gas; B1 Z forms 1 mol gas from liquids, W forms 2 mol gas from a solid, so W is largest.
Q30Entropy changes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why ΔG = 0 for the vaporisation of a liquid at its boiling point. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the entropy change of vaporisation of ethanol at its boiling point, 351 K, given ΔH_vap = +38.6 kJ mol⁻¹. [2]
  3. (c) For water, ΔH_vap = +40.7 kJ mol⁻¹ at 373 K. Calculate ΔS_vap and suggest why it is similar to that of ethanol. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) At the boiling point liquid and vapour are in equilibrium (neither direction is favoured).
    B1.
  2. (b) +110 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹
    M1 ΔS = ΔH/T = 38 600/351; A1.
  3. (c) +109 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; both involve changing 1 mol of liquid into 1 mol of gas, giving a similar increase in disorder.
    B1 value; B1 explanation.
