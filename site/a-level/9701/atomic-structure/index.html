Q1Ionisation energies · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which equation represents the first ionisation energy of chlorine? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Cl(g) → Cl⁺(g) + e⁻
    First ionisation energy is for one mole of gaseous atoms each losing one electron to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions. B starts from the molecule (it includes atomisation); C is electron affinity; D removes an electron from an anion.
Q2Ionisation energies · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term first ionisation energy. [2]
  2. (b) Write an equation, with state symbols, for the second ionisation energy of magnesium. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the second ionisation energy of magnesium is greater than its first ionisation energy. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The energy needed to remove one electron from each atom in one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions.
    M1 energy to remove one electron from each atom in 1 mole of atomsM2 atoms and ions are gaseous (1+ ions formed)
  2. (b) Mg⁺(g) → Mg²⁺(g) + e⁻
    B1 correct species and state symbols
  3. (c) The electron is removed from a positive ion; there is the same nuclear charge but fewer electrons, so less repulsion and the remaining electrons are held more tightly / the outer electron is closer to the nucleus.
    M1 electron removed from a positive ion / greater attraction (effective nuclear charge per electron greater)M2 same number of protons but fewer electrons so less shielding/repulsion; radius of ion smaller
Q3Ionisation energies · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State three factors that affect the size of the first ionisation energy of an atom. [3]
  2. (b) State whether sodium or potassium has the lower first ionisation energy and explain your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nuclear charge; distance of the outer electron from the nucleus (atomic radius); shielding by inner electrons. (Spin-pair repulsion also accepted.)
    B1 each for any three of: nuclear charge; atomic radius/distance; shielding; spin-pair repulsion / sub-shell
  2. (b) Potassium; its outer electron is in the 4th shell, further from the nucleus with more shielding, so it is less strongly attracted.
    B1 potassiumB1 outer electron further from nucleus / more shielding (outweighs larger nuclear charge)
Q4Ionisation energies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The first ionisation energies of the Period 3 elements, in kJ/mol, are:Na 496, Mg 738, Al 578, Si 787, P 1012, S 1000, Cl 1251, Ar 1521.

  1. (a) Explain why there is a general increase in first ionisation energy from sodium to argon. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the first ionisation energy of aluminium is lower than that of magnesium. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the first ionisation energy of sulfur is lower than that of phosphorus. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nuclear charge increases across the period while shielding stays roughly the same (electrons added to the same shell), so the outer electron is held more strongly / atomic radius decreases.
    M1 nuclear charge (number of protons) increasesM2 similar shielding / same shell, so greater attraction for outer electrons
  2. (b) In Al the electron is removed from a 3p orbital, which is higher in energy (further from the nucleus) and is shielded by the 3s electrons, so less energy is needed.
    M1 Al outer electron in 3p (Mg in 3s)M2 3p is higher in energy / further from nucleus / extra shielding by 3s²
  3. (c) In S the electron is removed from a 3p orbital containing a pair of electrons; repulsion between the paired electrons makes it easier to remove. P has three unpaired 3p electrons.
    M1 S has a paired electron in one 3p orbital (3p⁴); P is 3p³ with no pairsM2 spin-pair repulsion makes the paired electron easier to remove
Q5Ionisation energies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

The first six successive ionisation energies of element Y, in kJ/mol, are:578, 1817, 2745, 11 577, 14 842, 18 379.

  1. (a) Deduce the group of the Periodic Table that element Y belongs to. Explain your answer. [2]
  2. (b) Y is in Period 3. Identify Y and write the electronic configuration of the ion Y³⁺. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why each successive ionisation energy of Y is larger than the one before. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Group 13; the large jump is between the 3rd and 4th ionisation energies, so Y has 3 outer-shell electrons.
    M1 big jump between IE3 (2745) and IE4 (11 577)M2 three electrons in outer shell, so Group 13
  2. (b) Aluminium; Al³⁺ is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶
    B1 aluminiumB1 1s² 2s² 2p⁶
  3. (c) Each electron is removed from an increasingly positive ion, so there is less repulsion and greater attraction to the nucleus.
    B1 removing electron from more positive ion / same nuclear charge holding fewer electrons

Q7Ionisation energies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write the electronic configurations of nitrogen and oxygen atoms using orbital notation (e.g. 1s² …). [1]
  2. (b) The first ionisation energy of nitrogen is 1402 kJ/mol and that of oxygen is 1314 kJ/mol. Explain why oxygen's value is lower although it has more protons. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the energy, in kJ, needed to convert 0.500 g of gaseous nitrogen atoms into N⁺ ions. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [Ar: N, 14.0] [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) N 1s² 2s² 2p³; O 1s² 2s² 2p⁴
    B1 both correct
  2. (b) Oxygen's electron is removed from a 2p orbital that holds two electrons; the repulsion between the paired electrons makes it easier to remove. In nitrogen each 2p orbital holds one electron.
    M1 O has a pair of electrons in one 2p orbitalM2 spin-pair repulsion outweighs the extra nuclear charge
  3. (c) 50.1 kJ
    M1 n = 0.500/14.0 = 0.0357 molA1 0.0357 × 1402 = 50.1 kJ
Q8Ionisation energies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Ionisation energies in kJ/mol: magnesium 1st 738, 2nd 1451; calcium 1st 590, 2nd 1145.

  1. (a) Calculate the total energy needed to convert one mole of gaseous atoms into one mole of gaseous 2+ ions for (i) magnesium and (ii) calcium. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the difference between your two answers. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest how this difference relates to the reactions of magnesium and calcium with cold water. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 2189 kJ/mol (ii) 1735 kJ/mol
    B1 738 + 1451 = 2189B1 590 + 1145 = 1735
  2. (b) Calcium's outer electrons are in the 4th shell, further from the nucleus and shielded by more inner shells. Although Ca has more protons, the extra distance and shielding outweigh this, so attraction is weaker and less energy is needed.
    M1 Ca outer electrons in a higher shell / larger atomic radiusM2 more shielding by inner electronsM3 increase in nuclear charge is outweighed, so weaker attraction
  3. (c) Calcium reacts much more readily with cold water than magnesium because it loses its two outer electrons more easily.
    B1 Ca more reactive (reacts readily with cold water; Mg very slowly) because ions form more easily
Q9Ionisation energies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A student plots log₁₀(ionisation energy) against the number of the electron removed for all 19 electrons of a potassium atom.

  1. (a) Between which successive ionisation energies would you expect the three large jumps to occur? Explain. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why, between the jumps, the ionisation energies rise steadily rather than staying constant. [2]
  3. (c) State the sub-shell from which the 2nd electron is removed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Between the 1st and 2nd, between the 9th and 10th, and between the 17th and 18th, because potassium is 2,8,8,1: each jump marks the start of removing electrons from a shell closer to the nucleus.
    B1 1st→2ndB1 9th→10th and 17th→18thB1 each jump = removing from a new inner shell, closer to nucleus / less shielded
  2. (b) Each electron removed leaves an ion with more positive charge relative to the remaining electrons, so less repulsion and the ion shrinks; each next electron is attracted more strongly.
    M1 increasing positive charge on ion / fewer electrons for same nuclear chargeM2 less repulsion / smaller radius so stronger attraction
  3. (c) 3p
    B1 3p

Q13Particles, isotopes & ions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A beam of particles is passed through an electric field between two parallel charged plates in a vacuum.

  1. (a) Describe what happens to beams of protons, neutrons and electrons as they pass between the plates. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why electrons are deflected through a much larger angle than protons. [1]
  3. (c) A beam of ¹H⁺ ions is deflected through 12.0°. Assuming the angle is proportional to charge/mass, calculate the angle of deflection for a beam of ⁴He²⁺ ions under the same conditions. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Protons are deflected towards the negative plate; electrons are deflected towards the positive plate, through a much larger angle; neutrons pass straight through undeflected.
    B1 protons towards negative plateB1 electrons towards positive plate, larger angleB1 neutrons not deflected
  2. (b) Same size of charge but an electron's mass is about 1/1836 of a proton's, so its charge/mass ratio is much bigger.
    B1 deflection depends on charge/mass; electron much lighter
  3. (c) 6.0°
    M1 charge/mass for ⁴He²⁺ = 2/4 = 0.5 of that of ¹H⁺A1 12.0 × 0.5 = 6.0°

Q24Atomic & ionic radius · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why atomic radius decreases from sodium to chlorine across Period 3. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why atomic radius increases down Group 1 from lithium to caesium. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nuclear charge increases while electrons are added to the same shell with similar shielding, so outer electrons are pulled closer.
    M1 increasing nuclear charge / more protonsM2 same shell / similar shielding, so stronger attraction
  2. (b) Each element has an extra occupied shell, so outer electrons are further from the nucleus and more shielded.
    M1 more shellsM2 more shielding (outweighs increased nuclear charge)
