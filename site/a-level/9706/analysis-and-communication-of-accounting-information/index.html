Q1Performance evaluation and advice · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Kiran owns a clothing boutique in Hyderabad (Sindh). Her ratios are: 2024 2025Gross margin 38% 41%Profit margin 14% 10%Return on capital employed 22% 17%Current ratio 1.9 : 1 1.8 : 1Trade receivables turnover 34 days 29 days

  1. (a) Identify two ratios that show an improvement in 2025. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the profit margin fell although the gross margin rose. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two actions Kiran could take to improve her profit margin. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gross margin (rose from 38% to 41%) and the trade receivables turnover (fell from 34 to 29 days).
    1 mark each with direction stated.
  2. (b) Operating expenses (such as rent, wages or advertising) rose faster than gross profit, so a smaller share of revenue was left as profit.
    1 mark expenses rose; 1 mark faster than gross profit / as % of revenue.
  3. (c) Cut operating expenses such as advertising or energy; move to cheaper premises or renegotiate rent.
    1 mark each for any two sensible expense-control or revenue actions.
Q2Performance evaluation and advice · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Esi runs a homeware shop in Kumasi. For 2025: cost of sales $384,000; inventory 1 January 2025 $40,000; inventory 31 December 2025 $56,000. In 2024 the rate of inventory turnover was 12 times.

  1. (a) Calculate the rate of inventory turnover for 2025. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest two possible reasons why the rate of inventory turnover has fallen from 12 times. [2]
  3. (c) Advise Esi whether she should sell her slow-moving inventory at reduced prices. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.00 times
    Average inventory (40,000 + 56,000) ÷ 2 = 48,000 (M1); 384,000 ÷ 48,000 = 8.00 times (A1).
  2. (b) Sales have slowed (fewer customers); Esi bought too much inventory or some lines are out of fashion.
    1 mark each for any two of: lower demand/sales; overbuying/bulk purchases; slow-moving or obsolete lines; higher selling prices reduced sales; new competitor.
  3. (c) Selling at a discount releases cash and storage space and avoids obsolescence, but lowers the gross margin and may train customers to wait for sales; a justified decision.
    For: cash released, less risk of obsolescence/damage, lower storage cost, turnover improves. Against: lower gross margin, damages brand image, customers wait for discounts. 1 mark per point (max 3), 1 mark decision.
Q3Performance evaluation and advice · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Liang owns a bicycle shop in Kunming and has asked a bank for a five-year loan. At 31 December 2025: inventory $64,000; trade receivables $41,000; cash $3,000; current liabilities $72,000. Other information: Liang Industry averageReturn on capital employed 21% 15%Interest cover 6 times 5 timesCurrent ratio ? 1.80 : 1Liquid ratio ? 1.00 : 1

  1. (a) Calculate, to two decimal places, Liang's (i) current ratio and (ii) liquid (acid test) ratio. [4]
  2. (b) Advise the bank whether it should grant the loan. Justify your answer. [4]

Q4Performance evaluation and advice · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Tendai sells farm supplies in Harare. In 2025 he increased the credit period for customers from 30 to 60 days to win more business; his trade receivables turnover in 2024 was 38 days and the business now has a bank overdraft. For 2025: credit sales $730,000; credit purchases $511,000; trade receivables $122,000; trade payables $42,000.

  1. (a) Calculate for 2025, to the nearest whole day: (i) the trade receivables turnover, (ii) the trade payables turnover. [4]
  2. (b) Advise Tendai whether to continue offering 60 days' credit. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 61 days (ii) 30 days
    122,000 ÷ 730,000 × 365 (M1) = 61 days (A1). 42,000 ÷ 511,000 × 365 (M1) = 30 days (A1).
  2. (b) Longer credit raised sales but customers now pay 31 days after Tendai pays suppliers, which has caused the overdraft; he should probably return to shorter credit or offer cash discounts.
    For keeping: sales grew; competitive; customer loyalty. Against: receivables days 38 → 61 while payables 30, overdraft interest, higher risk of irrecoverable debts. 1 mark per point (max 3, both sides), 1 mark decision.

Q6Performance evaluation and advice · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Directors of Dune Solar Ltd review the last three years. For 2025: profit from operations $840,000; equity $4,200,000; non-current liabilities $1,400,000. 2023 2024 2025Return on capital employed 19% 17% ?Gearing 10% 18% 25%Current ratio 1.9 : 1 1.6 : 1 1.3 : 1

  1. (a) Calculate the return on capital employed for 2025, to two decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Discuss whether the company's financial position has improved over the three years. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15.00%
    Capital employed 4,200,000 + 1,400,000 = 5,600,000 (M1); 840,000 ÷ 5,600,000 × 100 = 15.00% (A1).
  2. (b) No: profitability has fallen (ROCE 19% → 15%), borrowing has risen (gearing 10% → 25%) and liquidity has weakened (current ratio 1.9 → 1.3), although gearing is still low and the borrowing may be funding growth.
    ROCE falling each year: less return per $ invested (1–2 marks). Gearing up: more risk, more interest, but still below 50% (1–2). Current ratio down: possible liquidity pressure, still above 1 (1–2). Balanced point: new investment may not yet have produced profit (1). Conclusion (1). Max 6.
Q7Performance evaluation and advice · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Meridian Furniture plc has two divisions. The directors can invest in only one of them. North $ South $Revenue 2,400,000 1,500,000Profit from operations 288,000 270,000Capital employed 1,600,000 1,800,000Profit margin 12.00% 18.00%

  1. (a) Calculate for each division, to two decimal places: (i) the asset turnover (revenue ÷ capital employed), (ii) the return on capital employed. [4]
  2. (b) Advise the directors which division should receive the investment. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) North: asset turnover 1.50 times, ROCE 18.00%. South: asset turnover 0.83 times, ROCE 15.00%.
    North 2,400,000 ÷ 1,600,000 = 1.50; 288,000 ÷ 1,600,000 = 18.00%. South 1,500,000 ÷ 1,800,000 = 0.83; 270,000 ÷ 1,800,000 = 15.00%. 1 mark each.
  2. (b) North earns the higher ROCE through efficient use of assets, though South has the better margin; a justified choice, usually North.
    North: higher ROCE 18% v 15%; uses assets more intensively (1.50 v 0.83). South: higher profit margin 18% v 12% – if extra capital raises its sales, it may gain more. Non-financial: market growth, competition, capacity. 1 mark per point (max 3, both sides), 1 mark decision.

Q9Performance evaluation and advice · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Divya runs an electrical wholesaler in Chennai. Her current assets are inventory $60,000, trade receivables $80,000 and cash $10,000. Current liabilities are trade payables $40,000 and a bank overdraft $60,000 (current ratio 1.50 : 1; liquid ratio 0.90 : 1). A debt factor offers to buy all her trade receivables for $76,000 cash. Divya would use the cash to repay the overdraft in full.

  1. (a) Calculate, to two decimal places, the current ratio and the liquid ratio after the proposal. [4]
  2. (b) Advise Divya whether to accept the factor's offer. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Current ratio 2.15 : 1; liquid ratio 0.65 : 1
    After: inventory 60,000 + cash (10,000 + 76,000 − 60,000) 26,000 = 86,000 current assets (M1); current liabilities 40,000 (M1); 86,000 : 40,000 = 2.15 : 1 (A1); 26,000 : 40,000 = 0.65 : 1 (A1).

    For: overdraft repaid, no overdraft interest, immediate cash, no irrecoverable debt risk. Against: cost 4,000 (5%); liquid ratio falls 0.90 → 0.65; customers dealt with by a third party; one-off – future receivables still slow. 1 mark per point (max 3), 1 mark decision.
Q10Investment and gearing ratios · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Kilimanjaro Tea Ltd: at 31 December 2024 long-term loans were $800,000 and equity was $1,200,000 (gearing 40.00%). At 31 December 2025 long-term loans were $500,000 and equity was $1,500,000. Gearing = non-current liabilities ÷ (equity + non-current liabilities) × 100.

  1. (a) Calculate the gearing ratio at 31 December 2025. [2]
  2. (b) State whether the company is high or low geared in 2025 and give one advantage to ordinary shareholders of the change since 2024. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25.00%
    500,000 ÷ (1,500,000 + 500,000) × 100 (M1) = 25.00% (A1).
  2. (b) Low geared (below 50%). Less interest has to be paid, so more profit is available for dividends and the risk of not paying a dividend is lower.
    1 mark low geared; 1 mark advantage (less interest/lower risk/more profit for dividends).

Q12Investment and gearing ratios · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Andes Coffee plc has 4,000,000 issued ordinary shares of $1 each and no preference shares. For the year: profit for the year $1,320,000; ordinary dividends paid $480,000. The market price of one share is $4.62.

  2. (b) Calculate the price earnings ratio. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the dividend cover. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate the dividend yield, to two decimal places. [2]

  2. (b) 50 days
    40 + 40 − 30 = 50 days.
  3. (c) $16,000
    New receivables = 584,000 ÷ 365 × 30 = 48,000 (M1); 64,000 − 48,000 = $16,000 (A1).
  4. (d) Customers may move to competitors that give longer credit, so sales fall.
    1 mark.
Q22Efficiency ratios · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Shifa Pharmacy, Rawalpindi, has an annual cost of sales of Rs 1,095,000 and holds average inventory of Rs 150,000. The owner wants to reduce inventory turnover to 35 days with no change in cost of sales.

  1. (a) Calculate the current inventory turnover in days. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the reduction in average inventory needed to reach 35 days. [2]
  3. (c) Holding inventory costs 12% of its value per year. Calculate the annual saving. [1]
  4. (d) State two risks to the pharmacy of holding less inventory. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 50 days
    150,000 ÷ 1,095,000 × 365 (M1) = 50 days (A1).
  2. (b) Rs 45,000
    Target inventory = 1,095,000 ÷ 365 × 35 = 105,000 (M1); 150,000 − 105,000 = Rs 45,000 (A1).
  3. (c) Rs 5,400
    12% × 45,000 = Rs 5,400.
  4. (d) Running out of medicines that patients need, losing customers to other pharmacies; losing bulk-buying discounts.
    1 mark each for any two of: stock-outs/lost sales; loss of customer goodwill (patients need medicines urgently); loss of bulk discounts; more frequent ordering costs; supplier delays.
Q23Profitability ratios · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A shop had revenue of $250,000 and cost of sales of $175,000. What was its gross margin?A 30.00%B 42.86%C 70.00%D 25.00% [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 30.00%
    Gross profit = 75,000; 75,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 30.00%. 42.86% is the mark-up (gross profit ÷ cost of sales); 70.00% is cost of sales as a percentage of revenue.
Q24Profitability ratios · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Tariq Electronics, Gujranwala, provides this information: 2024 $ 2025 $Revenue 480,000 560,000Gross profit 168,000 179,200Profit from operations 57,600 50,400Capital employed 320,000 360,000Ratios for 2024: gross margin 35.00%, profit margin 12.00%, return on capital employed 18.00%.

  1. (a) Calculate for 2025, to two decimal places: (i) the gross margin, (ii) the profit margin, (iii) the return on capital employed. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two possible reasons for the change in the gross margin. [2]
  3. (c) Comment on the change in the return on capital employed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 32.00% (ii) 9.00% (iii) 14.00%
    GM = 179,200 ÷ 560,000 × 100; PM = 50,400 ÷ 560,000 × 100; ROCE = 50,400 ÷ 360,000 × 100. 1 mark each.
  2. (b) Selling prices were cut to raise sales volume; the cost of buying goods rose and was not passed on.
    1 mark each for any two of: lower selling prices/discounts to customers; higher purchase prices; change in sales mix towards lower-margin goods; loss of trade discounts; more inventory losses/theft.
  3. (c) ROCE fell from 18.00% to 14.00%: each $1 invested earned less profit, because profit fell while extra capital of $40,000 was invested.
    1 mark identifies fall (with figures); 1 mark explains (lower profit and higher capital employed / less efficient use of resources).
Q25Profitability ratios · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Noor Cosmetics, Islamabad, had a gross margin of 40% and a profit from operations margin of 15% for the year. Its operating expenses were Rs 72,000. There was no other income.

  1. (a) Calculate the revenue for the year. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the cost of sales. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the mark-up, to two decimal places. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 288,000
    Gross profit − expenses = profit from operations: 0.40R − 72,000 = 0.15R (M1); 0.25R = 72,000 (M1); R = Rs 288,000 (A1).
  2. (b) Rs 172,800
    60% × 288,000 = Rs 172,800.
  3. (c) 66.67%
    Gross profit 115,200 ÷ cost of sales 172,800 × 100 (M1) = 66.67% (A1).
Q26Users of accounting information · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which user of a business's financial statements is most interested in its liquid (acid test) ratio?A A supplier deciding whether to offer 30 days' creditB A shareholder comparing dividend yieldsC The tax authority checking the profit figureD A competitor comparing gross margins [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A A supplier deciding whether to offer 30 days' credit
    A trade supplier wants to know whether the business can pay its short-term debts on time, which the liquid ratio measures. Shareholders are more interested in returns and dividends.
Q27Users of accounting information · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Bilal owns Sunrise Poultry Farm near Sheikhupura. He employs a farm manager and twelve workers and wants to borrow money from a bank to build a new shed.

  1. (a) Identify two internal users of the financial statements of Sunrise Poultry Farm. [2]
  2. (b) Explain what the bank would look for in the financial statements before lending to the farm. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the farm's employees are interested in its financial statements. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The owner (Bilal) and the farm manager/employees.
    1 mark each: owner; managers; employees (internal).
  2. (b) Whether the farm makes enough profit and cash to pay interest and repay the loan, and what assets could be offered as security.
    1 mark profitability/cash flow to repay; 1 mark security/gearing/existing debts.
  3. (c) To judge job security and whether the farm can afford pay rises.
    1 mark job security/continuity; 1 mark ability to pay wages/pay rises/bonuses.
Q28Liquidity ratios · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A business has current assets of $80,000 and current liabilities of $40,000. It then pays a trade payable $10,000 by cheque from a positive bank balance. What is the new current ratio?A 2.33 : 1B 2.00 : 1C 1.75 : 1D 1.67 : 1 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 2.33 : 1
    Both current assets and current liabilities fall by 10,000: 70,000 : 30,000 = 2.33 : 1. 1.75 : 1 reduces only the current assets.
Q29Liquidity ratios · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Grace runs a hardware store in Accra. At 31 December 2025: $Inventory 46,000Trade receivables 31,500Prepayments 2,500Cash in hand 1,200Trade payables 38,600Accrued expenses 2,800Bank overdraft 8,200

  1. (a) Calculate, to two decimal places: (i) the current ratio, (ii) the liquid (acid test) ratio. [4]
  2. (b) Comment on the liquidity of the business and suggest two ways to improve the liquid ratio. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 1.64 : 1 (ii) 0.71 : 1
    Current assets 81,200; current liabilities 49,600 (M1). Current ratio 81,200 : 49,600 = 1.64 : 1 (A1). Liquid assets 81,200 − 46,000 = 35,200 (M1); 35,200 : 49,600 = 0.71 : 1 (A1).
  2. (b) The liquid ratio is well below 1 : 1, so Grace cannot pay her current liabilities without selling inventory; she relies on an overdraft. She could collect debts faster, reduce inventory purchases, introduce capital or replace the overdraft with a long-term loan.
    Comment: 1 mark current ratio below a typical 2 : 1 / heavy reliance on inventory; 1 mark liquid ratio below 1 : 1 so difficulty paying debts as they fall due. Ways: 1 mark each for any two of: chase receivables/offer cash discounts; sell surplus inventory for cash; owner introduces cash; convert overdraft to long-term loan; sell unused non-current assets.
Q30Limitations of ratios · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Hira owns a shoe retailer with two shops in Karachi. She compares her ratios with those of a large shoe retailer in Madrid that publishes its financial statements online, to decide whether her business is doing well.

  1. (a) Explain three limitations of using ratio analysis to compare the two businesses. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Different accounting policies (e.g. depreciation methods, inventory valuation) make ratios not comparable; historical cost and different inflation and currency conditions in Pakistan and Spain distort figures; ratios ignore non-financial factors such as location, brand and staff skills.
    For each of three limitations: 1 mark identifying, 1 mark explaining in context. Accept: different policies; different year ends/seasonal timing; historical cost/inflation; different sizes and markets; ratios are based on past data; window dressing at the year end; non-financial factors ignored; no single 'correct' ratio.
