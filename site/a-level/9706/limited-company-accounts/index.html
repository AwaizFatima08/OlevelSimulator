Q1Statement of profit or loss and SOFP · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which item is shown in the equity section of a limited company's statement of financial position? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B share premium
    Share premium is a capital reserve and part of equity. Loan notes (A) are a non-current liability; dividends paid (D) appear in the statement of changes in equity as a deduction from retained earnings, not as a separate line in the SOFP.
Q2Statement of profit or loss and SOFP · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Shalimar Foods Limited provides the following for the year ended 31 March 2027: revenue $1,840,000; cost of sales $1,104,000; distribution costs $196,000; administrative expenses $248,000; finance costs $36,000; tax $64,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the gross profit. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit from operations. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the profit for the year. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $736,000
    1,840,000 − 1,104,000 = 736,000.
  2. (b) $292,000
    736,000 − 196,000 − 248,000 = 292,000. M1 A1.
  3. (c) $192,000
    292,000 − finance costs 36,000 = 256,000 profit before tax; − tax 64,000 = 192,000. M1 A1.
Q3Statement of profit or loss and SOFP · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State two items that appear in the statement of profit or loss of a limited company but not in that of a sole trader. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why dividends paid are not shown as an expense in a company's statement of profit or loss. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: tax (corporation tax / income tax on company profits); directors' remuneration; auditors' fees; loan note (debenture) interest as finance costs.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Dividends are an appropriation (distribution) of profit to the owners, not a cost of earning profit. They are deducted from retained earnings in the statement of changes in equity.
    1 mark: appropriation/distribution to owners, not an expense; 1 mark: shown in SOCE/retained earnings.

Q20Share issues (bonus and rights) · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 11 marks

Shandur Ceramics Limited has 3,000,000 ordinary shares of $1 each in issue, with a market price of $2.40. The directors are considering two options:Option 1: a bonus issue of 1 share for every 6 held, funded from the share premium account, which has a balance of $650,000.Option 2: a rights issue of 1 share for every 6 held at $1.80 per share.Ayesha owns 12,000 shares.

  1. (a) Calculate the cash the company would raise under Option 2, and the amount Ayesha would have to pay to take up all her rights. [3]
  2. (b) State the effect of each option on the company's (i) cash, (ii) share capital and (iii) total equity. [3]
  3. (c) The company needs $850,000 to modernise its kiln. Advise the directors which option they should choose. Justify your answer. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Company: $900,000; Ayesha: $3,600
    Rights 3,000,000 ÷ 6 = 500,000 × $1.80 = 900,000 (M1 A1). Ayesha 12,000 ÷ 6 = 2,000 × $1.80 = 3,600 (A1).
  2. (b) Option 1 (bonus): (i) no change; (ii) increases by $500,000; (iii) no change. Option 2 (rights): (i) increases by $900,000; (ii) increases by $500,000; (iii) increases by $900,000.
    1 mark per row (i), (ii), (iii) correct for both options.
  3. (c) Rights issue: raises 900,000, enough for the $850,000 kiln; no interest or repayment; existing holders keep their percentage if they take up rights. But shareholders must find cash, and it may depress the share price; any shortfall in take-up reduces funds. Bonus issue: costs shareholders nothing and may please them, makes shares more marketable, but raises no cash, so it does not meet the need. Decision: choose the rights issue (Option 2), as only it provides the finance required.
    Up to 4 marks for points on both options, using figures; 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q21Sources of finance · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State two differences between ordinary shares and loan notes (debentures). [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: ordinary shareholders are owners, loan note holders are lenders (creditors); dividends are paid only if declared and profits allow, interest must be paid at a fixed rate whether or not there is profit; ordinary shareholders usually vote, loan note holders do not; loan notes are repaid on a set date, ordinary shares are not normally repaid; on liquidation loan note holders are repaid before shareholders; interest is an expense in the SOPL, dividends are a distribution in the SOCE.
    2 marks per difference, clearly comparing both.

  1. (a) $35,000
    7% × 500,000 = 35,000.
  2. (b) Option A: $453,750; Option B: $480,000
    A: (640,000 − 35,000) × 75% = 453,750. B: 640,000 × 75% = 480,000.
  3. (c) Option A: 22.69 cents; Option B: 21.33 cents
    A: 453,750 ÷ 2,000,000 = 22.69c. B: 480,000 ÷ 2,250,000 = 21.33c.
  4. (d) Option A (loan notes): higher return per share (22.69c v 21.33c); no dilution of control; interest is fixed. But interest must be paid even in a bad year and the 500,000 must be repaid; gearing rises; security may be needed. Option B (shares): no compulsory payments or repayment; lower risk. But return per share falls and control is diluted. Decision with reason, e.g. choose loan notes if profits are stable, as the profit before interest is well above the interest cost (640,000 v 35,000).
    Up to 4 marks for points on both sides (using figures); 1 mark justified decision.
Q23Sources of finance · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 10 marks

Swat Valley Hotels Limited wants $2 million to build a new wing. Its gearing (non-current liabilities ÷ (equity + non-current liabilities)) is already 58%. Profits are seasonal and fell in each of the last two years after floods. The directors are considering: (1) a rights issue of ordinary shares; (2) a five-year bank loan at 11% interest secured on the hotel; (3) a sale and leaseback of its existing staff accommodation annex.

  1. (a) Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of each of the three sources of finance for Swat Valley Hotels Limited, and recommend which the directors should use. Justify your answer. [10]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rights issue: no interest or repayment, no security needed, keeps gearing low (already high at 58%); but shareholders may not take up the issue after two poor years; cost of issue; dividends expected later. Bank loan (5 years): quick, interest tax-deductible, owners keep control; but interest and repayments must be met from uncertain seasonal cash flow, raises gearing further, bank may require the hotel as security and may refuse given current gearing. Sale and leaseback of the annex: releases cash from existing assets quickly; leaseback means future lease payments and loss of the asset's future value. Recommendation: a rights issue (or mixed with a smaller loan) because gearing is already high and profits are volatile, so fixed interest increases risk.
    Level 3 (8–10): balanced discussion of all three sources applied to the hotel (gearing, seasonality, control), clear justified recommendation. Level 2 (4–7): advantages and disadvantages of at least two sources with some application, recommendation given. Level 1 (1–3): generic points about sources of finance, little application.
Q24Corrections to profit and reserves · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The bookkeeper of Kaghan Dairy Limited included dividends paid of $40,000 in administrative expenses. The draft profit for the year was $146,800.

  1. (a) Calculate the corrected profit for the year. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why your correction does not change the retained earnings at the end of the year. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $186,800
    146,800 + 40,000 = 186,800. M1 A1.

Q27Reserves and equity · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Distinguish between a capital reserve and a revenue reserve. [2]
  2. (b) Give two examples of each type of reserve. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A capital reserve arises from capital transactions (e.g. share premium, revaluation) and cannot be used to pay cash dividends. A revenue reserve arises from retained profits and can be distributed as dividends.
    1 mark each side.
  2. (b) Capital: share premium, revaluation reserve (also capital redemption reserve). Revenue: retained earnings, general reserve.
    1 mark for two capital, 1 mark for two revenue.
Q28Reserves and equity · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Lahore Leather Limited has ordinary share capital of $900,000 ($1 shares), share premium $180,000, general reserve $40,000 and retained earnings $95,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum cash dividend the company could legally pay from its reserves. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum bonus issue that could be made without reducing revenue reserves, and express it as a ratio of new shares to existing shares. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why a company might choose to keep profits in a general reserve rather than pay them as dividends. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $135,000
    Only revenue reserves can be distributed: retained earnings 95,000 + general reserve 40,000 = 135,000. Share premium is a capital reserve. M1 A1.
  2. (b) $180,000, i.e. 1 new share for every 5 held
    Share premium 180,000 ÷ share capital 900,000 = 1/5 → 1 for 5.
  3. (c) To retain cash in the business for expansion or replacement of assets; to show shareholders that this profit is not intended for distribution; to strengthen the SOFP for lenders.
    1 mark each for two valid reasons.
Q29Reserves and equity · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Saadat Hospitals Limited revalued its property at 31 December 2026. Land cost $260,000 and is now valued at $395,000. Buildings cost $480,000, with accumulated depreciation of $96,000, and are now valued at $520,000. There was no revaluation reserve before.

  1. (a) Calculate the balance on the revaluation reserve after the revaluation. [2]
  2. (b) Prepare the journal entries to record the revaluation of the buildings. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the revaluation reserve cannot be used to pay a cash dividend. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $271,000
    Land 395,000 − 260,000 = 135,000; buildings 520,000 − (480,000 − 96,000) = 136,000; total 271,000. M1 (uses carrying amount for buildings) A1.
  2. (b) Debit $ Credit $Buildings: accumulated depreciation 96,000 Buildings: cost/valuation 40,000 Revaluation reserve 136,000
    Eliminate accumulated depreciation 96,000 (1); increase buildings to valuation 40,000 (1); credit revaluation reserve 136,000 (1).
  3. (c) The gain is unrealised — no cash has been received and the assets have not been sold — so distributing it would pay out capital and could harm creditors.
    1 mark unrealised/no cash; 1 mark protects capital/creditors.
Q30Dividends · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Indus Motors Parts Limited had 1,600,000 ordinary shares of $1 each on 1 July 2026. During the year ended 30 June 2027:• August 2026: paid the final dividend for the previous year of 6 cents per share.• January 2027: bonus issue of 1 share for every 4 held. The bonus shares rank for any later dividends.• April 2027: paid an interim dividend of 3.5 cents per share.

  1. (a) Calculate the total dividends paid during the year ended 30 June 2027. [3]
  2. (b) Explain whether a company with a large retained earnings balance but a bank overdraft should pay a dividend. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $166,000
    Final for 2026: 1,600,000 × 6c = 96,000. After bonus 1 for 4: 2,000,000 shares; interim 2,000,000 × 3.5c = 70,000. Total 166,000. M1 M1 A1.
  2. (b) Legally it may pay, as dividends come from distributable profits (retained earnings). But dividends need cash; paying would increase the overdraft and interest, and could harm liquidity and ability to pay suppliers. It may be better to pay a smaller dividend or keep cash until liquidity improves, while considering shareholders' expectations.
    1 mark: legal — distributable reserves; 1 mark: cash/liquidity problem; 1 mark: conclusion/shareholder expectations.
