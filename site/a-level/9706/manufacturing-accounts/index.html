Q1Factory profit and unrealised profit · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Jade River Toys transfers completed goods from its factory to its warehouse at production cost plus 20%. The production cost of goods completed for the year was $300,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the transfer price of goods completed and the factory profit. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why a manufacturer may transfer goods to its trading account at a price above production cost. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Transfer price $360,000; factory profit $60,000
    20% × 300,000 = 60,000 factory profit (M1 A1); 300,000 + 60,000 = $360,000 (A1).
  2. (b) To compare the factory's cost with the price of buying the goods from outside, so the factory's efficiency (and the trading section's) can be judged separately; it can also be used to reward factory managers.
    1 mark compare with market/outside price; 1 mark measure factory performance separately/manager bonus.
Q2Factory profit and unrealised profit · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Falcon Paints transfers goods to its trading account at production cost plus 20%. Finished goods inventory at the year end, valued at transfer price, was $48,000. The provision for unrealised profit brought forward was $6,500.

  1. (a) Calculate the provision for unrealised profit at the year end. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the adjustment to the provision for the year and state its effect on profit. [2]
  3. (c) State the value of finished goods inventory in the statement of financial position. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $8,000
    48,000 × 20/120 (M1) = $8,000 (A1).
  2. (b) Increase of $1,500, which reduces profit.
    8,000 − 6,500 = 1,500 increase (M1); charged against profit (A1).
  3. (c) $40,000
    48,000 − 8,000 = $40,000 (production cost).
Q3Factory profit and unrealised profit · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Orchid Soaps, Ipoh, transfers its products from its factory to its shop at production cost plus a mark-up.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by 'unrealised profit' in a manufacturing business. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a provision for unrealised profit is needed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The factory profit added to goods that are still in finished goods inventory at the year end; it has not been earned because the goods have not been sold to customers.
    1 mark factory profit/mark-up in closing inventory; 1 mark not yet sold outside the business.
  2. (b) Without it, inventory would be shown above cost and profit would include profit the business has made from 'selling to itself', so profit and assets would be overstated.
    1 mark inventory must be at cost (not above); 1 mark profit/assets would be overstated (realisation/prudence).
Q4Factory profit and unrealised profit · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Sundarbans Honey processes and bottles honey in its own factory and sells it from its own warehouse.

  1. (a) Define (i) production cost of goods completed and (ii) transfer price. [2]
  2. (b) State where (i) the factory profit and (ii) the provision for unrealised profit are shown in the financial statements. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) The total factory cost (prime cost plus factory overheads, adjusted for work in progress) of goods finished in the period. (ii) The value at which finished goods are passed from the factory to the trading account (production cost plus factory profit, or market value).
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) (i) Added to the gross profit in the statement of profit or loss (transferred from the manufacturing account). (ii) The change is charged/credited in the statement of profit or loss; the balance is deducted from finished goods inventory in the statement of financial position.
    1 mark each.

  2. (b) State how the provision is shown in the statement of financial position. [1]
  3. (c) Name the accounting concept that says inventory should not be valued above cost, and explain it. [2]

Q8Factory profit and unrealised profit · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Q18Factory profit and unrealised profit · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Mosaic Tiles transfers goods from its factory at production cost plus 15% (the same rate as last year). For the year: production cost of goods completed $500,000; revenue $820,000; finished goods at transfer price $46,000 at the start and $69,000 at the end; administration and selling expenses $110,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit for the year of the business as a whole, using transfer prices. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit if all goods were valued at production cost, with no factory profit. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the two profit figures are the same. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $230,000
    Cost of sales 46,000 + 575,000 − 69,000 = 552,000; gross profit 268,000 (M1); factory profit 15% × 500,000 = 75,000 (B1); PUP 9,000 − 6,000 = 3,000 increase (B1); 268,000 − 110,000 + 75,000 − 3,000 = $230,000 (A1).

    Cost of sales at cost 40,000 + 500,000 − 60,000 = 480,000 (M1); 820,000 − 480,000 − 110,000 = $230,000 (A1).
  3. (c) Factory profit is only an internal transfer; the provision removes the part still in unsold inventory, so the only profit recognised is that realised by selling to customers.
    1 mark internal transfer/not earned from outside; 1 mark PUP removes unrealised element so totals agree.
Q19Factory profit and unrealised profit · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

The factory manager of Nile Valley Juices receives a bonus of 5% of factory profit. Goods produced this year cost $450,000 and would have cost $540,000 to buy from another manufacturer. The manager proposes that goods should be transferred at production cost plus 30% instead of at market price.

  1. (a) Calculate the factory profit (i) using the market price, (ii) using the proposed 30% mark-up. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a market-price transfer is a better measure of the factory's performance. [2]
  3. (c) Advise the directors which basis to use for the factory manager's bonus. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) $90,000 (ii) $135,000
    (i) 540,000 − 450,000 = 90,000; (ii) 30% × 450,000 = 135,000. 1 mark each.
  2. (b) It shows what the business would have paid an outside supplier, so the factory profit shows how much the factory saves by making the goods; a fixed mark-up gives a profit even if the factory is inefficient.
    1 mark comparison with buying in; 1 mark mark-up hides inefficiency/rewards cost increases.
  3. (c) Market price: the 30% mark-up would raise the bonus by $2,250 (5% of $45,000) without any improvement, and rises automatically if costs rise; market price rewards real cost savings, though market prices can be hard to find and may change.
    For market price: fair, motivates cost control, comparable with outside. Against: market price may be hard to obtain/volatile; manager cannot control it. For mark-up: simple, certain; but rewards higher costs. 1 mark per point (max 3), 1 mark decision.

Q23Factory profit and unrealised profit · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Ember Stoves transfers completed goods from its factory to its trading account at production cost plus a fixed mark-up. At the year end, finished goods inventory at transfer price was $57,500; in the statement of financial position it is shown at production cost of $50,000. The factory profit for the year was $72,000. Q29Manufacturing account · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Orbit Bicycles makes 12,000 gear units a year. The production cost of goods completed is $504,000, which includes $96,000 of fixed factory overheads that would still be paid if the gear units were bought in. An outside supplier offers to sell identical units for $39 each.

  1. (a) Calculate the production cost per unit. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the avoidable cost per unit if the factory stopped making the part. [2]
  3. (c) Advise the directors whether to buy the part from the supplier at $39 instead of making it. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $42
    504,000 ÷ 12,000 (M1) = $42 (A1).
  2. (b) $34
    504,000 − unavoidable fixed overheads 96,000 = 408,000 (M1); ÷ 12,000 = $34 (A1).
  3. (c) Keep making it: buying at $39 costs $5 a unit more than the $34 that would be saved, i.e. $60,000 a year worse, even though $39 is below the full cost of $42.
    For making: avoidable cost 34 < 39, saves 60,000 a year; control of quality and supply; keeps jobs. For buying: factory space could be used for something more profitable; supplier may be more reliable or innovative. 1 mark per point (max 3), 1 mark decision.

  2. (b) Explain why selling and distribution costs are not included in the cost of manufactured inventory. [2]
Show answer
