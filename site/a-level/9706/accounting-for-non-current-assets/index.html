Q2Depreciation methods and calculation · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Hamza Printers, Lahore, bought a printing machine on 1 January 2024 for Rs 1,250,000. Installation cost Rs 50,000. The machine is expected to last 5 years and then be sold for Rs 100,000. The straight-line method is used and the financial year ends on 31 December.

  1. (a) Calculate the annual depreciation charge. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the carrying amount of the machine at 31 December 2026. [2]
  3. (c) State the accounting concept applied when depreciation is charged, and explain how it is applied. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 240,000
    Cost = 1,250,000 + 50,000 installation = 1,300,000 (M1). (1,300,000 − 100,000) ÷ 5 = Rs 240,000 (A1).
  2. (b) Rs 580,000
    Three years' depreciation = 3 × 240,000 = 720,000 (M1). 1,300,000 − 720,000 = Rs 580,000 (A1).
  3. (c) Matching (accruals) concept: the cost of the machine is spread over the years in which it helps to earn revenue.
    1 mark for naming matching/accruals; 1 mark for explaining that the cost is charged against the revenue of the periods that benefit from the asset.

Q16Depreciation methods and calculation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Farhan is opening a furniture workshop in Sialkot and will apply for a bank loan after his first year. He buys a CNC machine for $90,000, expected to last 5 years with a residual value of $10,000. He can use the straight-line method or the reducing balance method at 35% per annum. Profit before depreciation for year 1 is expected to be $60,000. He wants to choose the method that shows the higher profit.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit for year 1 under each method. [2]
  2. (b) Advise Farhan which method he should use. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Straight-line $44,000; reducing balance $28,500
    SL: (90,000 − 10,000) ÷ 5 = 16,000; 60,000 − 16,000 = 44,000 (B1). RB: 35% × 90,000 = 31,500; 60,000 − 31,500 = 28,500 (B1).
