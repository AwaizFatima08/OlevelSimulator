Q7Cost drivers and ABC pricing · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The owner of a spice-grinding and packing business in Hyderabad has heard about activity based costing.

  1. (a) State two features of activity based costing. [2]
  2. (b) State two features of a business for which activity based costing is likely to be most useful. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: overheads are collected in cost pools by activity; each pool has its own cost driver; costs are charged to products according to their use of each activity; recognises that some costs are driven by batches or complexity, not volume.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any two: overheads are a large part of total cost; many different products or services; products differ greatly in complexity, batch size or support needed; a competitive market where accurate prices matter.
    1 mark each.

Kohinoor Paints sells 12,000 tins a year at $18 each to a hardware chain, delivered in 12 monthly deliveries. The chain now wants weekly deliveries (52 a year) with no change in total volume or price. ABC rates: delivery $140 per delivery; order processing $35 per order (one order per delivery); quality certificate $20 per delivery.

  1. (a) Calculate the annual activity cost of supplying the customer under the present arrangement. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the annual activity cost if the customer's request is accepted. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the increase in price per unit needed to keep the profit from this customer unchanged. [2]
  4. (d) Advise the company how to respond to the customer's request. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,340
    12 deliveries × (140 + 35 + 20) = 12 × 195. M1 A1.
  2. (b) $10,140
    52 × 195. M1 A1.
  3. (c) $0.65 per unit (price rises to $18.65)
    Extra cost 7,800 ÷ 12,000 units. M1 A1.
  4. (d) Accept only if the price rises by about $0.65 a unit (or a delivery charge of $195 per extra delivery is made); otherwise profit falls by $7,800. Consider the value of keeping a large customer and whether deliveries can be combined with other customers' routes.
    1 mark use of figures; 1 mark justified advice.
Q26ABC versus absorption costing · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Quetta Woollens absorbs all its production overheads using a single rate per direct labour hour. The finance director suggests changing to activity based costing.

  1. (a) Explain two advantages of activity based costing compared with traditional absorption costing. [4]
  2. (b) State one limitation of activity based costing. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two explained: more accurate product costs because overheads are charged according to what causes them; better pricing decisions, avoiding over-pricing high-volume and under-pricing low-volume products; identifies costly activities that can be controlled or reduced; better information for decisions about products and customers.
    1 mark each advantage, 1 mark each explanation.
  2. (b) Any one: expensive and time-consuming to set up and run; choice of cost drivers can be subjective; some costs (e.g. factory rent) have no clear driver and still have to be apportioned; still based on historical/budgeted data.
    1 mark.
Q27ABC versus absorption costing · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Sargodha Agro Tools makes Alpha (20,000 units, 1 direct labour hour each) and Beta (2,000 units, 2 direct labour hours each). Total overheads are $240,000, analysed as:Activity Cost $ Cost driver Alpha BetaSet-ups 100,000 Number of set-ups 10 40Order handling 60,000 Number of orders 50 100Machining 80,000 Machine hours 15,000 5,000

  1. (a) Calculate the overhead cost per unit of each product using a single absorption rate per direct labour hour. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the overhead cost per unit of each product using activity based costing. [4]
  3. (c) Explain why the two methods give such different results. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Alpha $10.00; Beta $20.00
    240,000 ÷ 24,000 hours = $10 per hour; Alpha 1 hour, Beta 2 hours. M1 A1.
  2. (b) Set-ups 100,000 ÷ 50 = $2,000 per set-up; Order handling 60,000 ÷ 150 = $400 per order; Machining 80,000 ÷ 20,000 = $4 per machine hourAlpha: 10 × 2,000 + 50 × 400 + 15,000 × 4 = $100,000; ÷ 20,000 units = $5.00 per unitBeta: 40 × 2,000 + 100 × 400 + 5,000 × 4 = $140,000; ÷ 2,000 units = $70.00 per unit
    1 mark set-up and order rates; 1 mark machining rate; 1 mark each per-unit figure (2).
  3. (c) Beta is made in small batches with many set-ups and orders, but uses few labour hours. Absorption on labour hours ignores this, so Beta is under-costed and high-volume Alpha over-costed.
    1 mark: low-volume/complex product uses more batch-level activities; 1 mark: labour hours do not reflect this.
Q28ABC versus absorption costing · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Tulip Cosmetics produced 10,000 units of a face cream and sold 8,000 units in its first year. There was no opening inventory. Production overhead per unit is $12 under traditional absorption costing and $9 under activity based costing.

  1. (a) Calculate the overhead included in the closing inventory under each method. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the effect on reported profit for the year of using ABC instead of absorption costing. [2]
  3. (c) State when the two methods would give the same profit. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Absorption $24,000; ABC $18,000
    Closing inventory 10,000 − 8,000 = 2,000 units; × $12 and × $9. 1 mark each.
  2. (b) Profit is $6,000 lower under ABC, because $3 less overhead per unit is carried forward in closing inventory, so more of the year's overhead is charged against this year's profit.
    1 mark amount; 1 mark explanation.
  3. (c) When there is no change in the level of inventory (production equals sales), since total overhead charged is the same.
    1 mark.
Q29ABC versus absorption costing · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Spice Route, Karachi, sells 50,000 packs of ground spices (direct cost $2.00 each, 0.1 machine hours each) and 4,000 gift hampers (direct cost $30.00 each, 0.25 machine hours each). Overheads of $180,000 are currently absorbed on machine hours. An ABC analysis shows:Activity Cost $ Cost driver Packs HampersSet-ups 72,000 Number of set-ups 12 36Packaging design 48,000 Design hours 20 60Machining 60,000 Machine hours 5,000 1,000

  1. (a) Calculate the overhead cost per unit of each product using the current machine-hour rate. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the overhead cost per unit of each product using activity based costing. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the selling price of each product under each method, using a mark-up of 30% on total cost. [2]
  4. (d) Discuss the consequences for the business of continuing to price using the machine-hour rate. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Packs $3.00; hampers $7.50
    180,000 ÷ 6,000 hours = $30; packs 0.1 hours, hampers 0.25 hours.
  2. (b) Set-ups 72,000 ÷ 48 = $1,500 per set-up; Packaging design 48,000 ÷ 80 = $600 per design hour; Machining 60,000 ÷ 6,000 = $10 per machine hourPacks: 12 × 1,500 + 20 × 600 + 5,000 × 10 = $80,000; ÷ 50,000 units = $1.60 per unitHampers: 36 × 1,500 + 60 × 600 + 1,000 × 10 = $100,000; ÷ 4,000 units = $25.00 per unit
    1 mark rates; 1 mark each per-unit figure.
  3. (c) Machine-hour rate: packs $6.50, hampers $48.75. ABC: packs $4.68, hampers $71.50
    1 mark each method.
  4. (d) Packs are over-priced ($6.50 against an ABC-based $4.68), so the business may lose sales of its main product to competitors. Hampers are under-priced ($48.75 against $71.50), so they may sell well but could be making a loss. Overall profit may fall and wrong products be promoted.
    1 mark packs over-priced/lost sales; 1 mark hampers under-priced/possible losses.
Q30ABC versus absorption costing · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Indus Packaging plc makes 40 product lines, from high-volume standard cartons to small custom orders that need special design and many machine set-ups. Overheads are 60% of total cost and are absorbed using a single direct labour hour rate. The company has recently lost two large contracts for standard cartons to competitors that charged lower prices, while custom orders have increased. A new ABC system would cost $45,000 to install.

  1. (a) Advise the directors whether they should replace absorption costing with activity based costing. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) For ABC: overheads are 60% of total cost, so how they are charged matters; the 40 product lines vary greatly in batch size and the custom orders need many set-ups and design hours, so labour-hour absorption is likely to distort costs; recent loss of high-volume contracts suggests these are over-priced; ABC would give better pricing and show which customers and products are profitable.Against ABC: the $45,000 system cost and staff time; drivers must be chosen and data collected continually; some costs (rent) still have to be apportioned; staff may resist change; benefits are not certain.Decision: introduce ABC (perhaps first in the custom-order area) because the potential to correct pricing outweighs the cost.
    Up to 2 marks for points for; up to 2 marks for points against (in context); 1 mark use of case data; 1 mark justified decision.
