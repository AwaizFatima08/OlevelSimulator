Q1Income and expenditure account · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The new treasurer of Al-Huda Community Sports Club, Quetta, has prepared only a receipts and payments account. Members have asked for an income and expenditure account instead.

  1. (a) Explain three differences between a receipts and payments account and an income and expenditure account. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) R&P is a summary of the cash book; I&E follows the accruals basis. R&P includes capital items such as buying equipment; I&E has revenue items only. R&P ends with a cash/bank balance (an asset); I&E ends with a surplus or deficit. I&E includes non-cash items such as depreciation.
    For each of three differences: 1 mark identifying, 1 mark developing. Accept: cash v accruals basis; capital and revenue items v revenue only; closing balance v surplus/deficit; no depreciation/accruals in R&P; R&P covers all years' amounts received (e.g. arrears) v I&E for the current year only.

Q3Income and expenditure account · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

[4]

Q19Club accounts principles and decisions · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A group of residents in Abbottabad are forming a hiking club and ask how its accounts will differ from those of a business.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a non-profit-making organisation. [2]

Q22Club accounts principles and decisions · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Show answer

Q23Club accounts principles and decisions · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) Explain how the going concern concept applies to the club. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the surplus if the treasurer follows the prudence concept for subscriptions in arrears. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the club should apply its policy on subscriptions in arrears consistently. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Accounts assume the club will continue; with membership falling by a third, if the club might close, assets would need to be shown at what they would sell for instead of their carrying amounts.

  2. (b) $3,350
    5,750 − 2,400 arrears not yet received (M1) = $3,350 (A1).
  3. (c) So that surpluses can be compared fairly from year to year; changing the policy could make the club look better or worse without any real change.
    1 mark comparability; 1 mark avoids manipulation/misleading members.

    2,400 ÷ 10 = 240 (M1); 240 − 200 = 40 more (A1).
  3. (c) Life membership brings in cash now for the new courts and secures members, but the club loses income from long-serving members (after 12 years they pay nothing) and must still provide services; a justified decision.
    For: immediate cash; loyal members; less collection effort/arrears; income spread $240 a year for 10 years. Against: members staying more than 12 years cost the club income; costs rise with inflation but the fee is fixed; club still serves them after the fee has been fully transferred. 1 mark per point (max 4), 1 decision.
Q25Club accounts principles and decisions · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Crescent Moon Cricket Club's café had takings of $18,000, food costs of $9,500 and wages of $7,200. The treasurer also charged the café $2,400 as its share of the clubhouse rent and insurance, which the club will pay whether or not the café stays open.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit or loss of the café after charging its share of general overheads. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the effect on the club's surplus if the café is closed. [2]
  3. (c) Advise the committee whether to close the café. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Loss $1,100
    18,000 − 9,500 − 7,200 = 1,300 (M1); 1,300 − 2,400 = loss $1,100 (A1).
  2. (b) The surplus would fall by $1,300.
    The 2,400 overheads would still be paid (M1); the club would lose the café's $1,300 surplus over its own costs (A1).
  3. (c) Keep the café: it covers its own costs and contributes $1,300 towards overheads, and it attracts members; but look at raising prices or cutting wages.
    For keeping: contributes 1,300; service to members helps retain them; may raise prices. For closing: shown as a loss; volunteer/staff effort; could use space for something more profitable (e.g. rent to a caterer). 1 mark per point (max 3), 1 mark decision consistent with (b).
Q26Accumulated fund and membership fees · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The treasurer of Riverside Tennis Club provides these balances at 1 January 2025: $Clubhouse 180,000Equipment 24,500Bar inventory 3,800Subscriptions in arrears 1,200Subscriptions in advance 900Bank 6,700Electricity accrued 450Bar suppliers (payables) 2,100

  1. (a) Calculate the accumulated fund of the club at 1 January 2025. [4]
  2. (b) Explain the meaning of the term 'accumulated fund'. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $212,750
    Assets: 180,000 + 24,500 + 3,800 + 1,200 + 6,700 = 216,200 (M1 A1). Liabilities: 900 + 450 + 2,100 = 3,450 (M1). Accumulated fund = 216,200 − 3,450 = $212,750 (A1).
  2. (b) It is the club's capital: the excess of its assets over its liabilities, built up from past surpluses (and capital receipts) that belong to the members.
    1 mark equivalent of capital / assets − liabilities; 1 mark built up from accumulated surpluses.
Q27Accumulated fund and membership fees · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Cedar Hill Chess Club started on 1 January 2025 with 240 members. The annual subscription is $150. By 31 December 2025, 12 members had not paid their 2025 subscription and 9 members had already paid their 2026 subscription.

  1. (a) Calculate the subscriptions income for 2025. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the cash received for subscriptions during 2025. [2]
  3. (c) State how the subscriptions in arrears and in advance are shown in the statement of financial position at 31 December 2025. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $36,000
    240 × $150 = $36,000.
  2. (b) $35,550
    36,000 − arrears 12 × 150 = 1,800 + advance 9 × 150 = 1,350 (M1); = $35,550 (A1).
  3. (c) Arrears $1,800: current asset. In advance $1,350: current liability.
    1 mark each.

Q29Accumulated fund and membership fees · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Highland Golf Society was formed on 1 January 2022. Life membership fees are credited to a life membership account and transferred to the income and expenditure account in equal amounts over 10 years, starting in the year the fee is received. Life membership fees received were: 2022 $9,000; 2023 $6,000; 2024 $12,000; 2025 $7,500.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount transferred from the life membership account to the income and expenditure account for 2025. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the balance on the life membership account at 31 December 2025. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why life membership fees are not treated as income in full in the year they are received. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $3,450
    All fees received 2022–2025 are still within their 10 years: 10% × (9,000 + 6,000 + 12,000 + 7,500) (M1) = $3,450 (A1).
  2. (b) $25,950
    Transfers: 2022 900; 2023 1,500; 2024 2,700; 2025 3,450 = 8,550 (M1). 34,500 − 8,550 = $25,950 (A1).
  3. (c) Matching: the fee pays for membership over many years, so it is spread over the years in which members use the club; taking it all at once would overstate the surplus in one year.
    1 mark matching/accruals; 1 mark fee covers future years / avoids distorting one year's surplus.
Q30Accumulated fund and membership fees · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Lakeside Rowing Club's year ends on 31 March. At 31 March 2026 its accumulated fund was $158,400. The surplus for the year was $12,600. During the year the club received a legacy (a gift in a former member's will) of $20,000, which was credited directly to the accumulated fund.

  1. (a) Calculate the accumulated fund at 1 April 2025. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the legacy was credited directly to the accumulated fund rather than to the income and expenditure account. [2]
  3. (c) State one item, other than a deficit, that would reduce the accumulated fund. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $125,800
    Closing fund = opening fund + surplus + legacy (M1). Opening = 158,400 − 12,600 − 20,000 (M1) = $125,800 (A1).
  2. (b) It is a one-off capital receipt, not a regular revenue income; including it would overstate the surplus from normal activities and mislead members about whether subscriptions cover running costs.
    1 mark capital/non-recurring receipt; 1 mark would distort the year's surplus.
  3. (c) A decrease in the value of assets with no change in liabilities, e.g. a downward revaluation of the clubhouse charged to the fund.
    1 mark for any valid item (e.g. downward revaluation; loss of an uninsured asset).
