Q1Investment decision and sensitivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A rice mill in Larkana is thinking of replacing an old husking machine.

  1. (a) State two non-financial factors a business should consider before replacing a machine. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one advantage of net present value over the payback method. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: effect on employees (training, job losses, morale); quality of output; environmental impact; reliability and after-sales service of the supplier; flexibility of the new machine.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) NPV considers all cash flows over the project's life and the time value of money, whereas payback ignores cash flows after the payback point.
    1 mark advantage; 1 mark explanation.
Q2Investment decision and sensitivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The manager of a cinema in Karachi has been told that a new projector has a positive net present value.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a positive net present value. [2]
  2. (b) State two limitations of the net present value method. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The present value of the future net cash inflows is greater than the initial cost; the project earns more than the cost of capital and increases the value of the business.
    1 mark PV of inflows > cost; 1 mark return above cost of capital.
  2. (b) Any two: cash flows are hard to forecast; choosing the right discount rate is difficult; ignores non-financial factors; harder to understand than payback; assumes the cost of capital stays the same.
    1 mark each.
Q3Investment decision and sensitivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The finance director of a cement company is appraising a new kiln.

  1. (a) Explain why a business uses its cost of capital as the discount rate. [2]
  2. (b) Explain what is meant by sensitivity analysis. [2]
  3. (c) State one limitation of sensitivity analysis. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is the return the business must pay to its providers of finance; a project must earn at least this return to add value.
    1 mark cost of finance; 1 mark minimum required return.
  2. (b) Testing how much one estimate (e.g. sales volume, cost, discount rate) can change before the NPV becomes zero, to show which estimates the decision depends on most.
    1 mark change one variable; 1 mark effect on NPV/decision.
  3. (c) Changes one variable at a time, whereas in reality several change together / gives no probability of the change happening.
    1 mark.

Q5Investment decision and sensitivity · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A project costing $100,000 is expected to give net cash inflows of $32,000 a year for five years. The cost of capital is 12% and the sum of the discount factors for years 1–5 at 12% is 3.605.

  1. (a) Calculate the net present value. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage by which the annual cash inflow could fall before the project became unprofitable, correct to two decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Explain what your answer to (b) shows. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 32,000 × 3.605 = 115,360; NPV = 115,360 − 100,000 = $15,360
    M1; A1.
  2. (b) 15,360 ÷ 115,360 × 100 = 13.31%
    M1 NPV ÷ PV of the inflows; A1.
  3. (c) If annual inflows fall by more than 13.31% (below about $27,739), the NPV becomes negative; a small margin means the project is risky.
    1 mark.

Q28Accounting rate of return · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Haroon Builders may buy a crane for $140,000. It will produce net cash inflows of $50,000 a year for four years and then be sold for $20,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the average annual profit. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the accounting rate of return on (i) the average investment and (ii) the initial investment, correct to two decimal places. [2]

Q29Payback period · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A school in Lahore must choose between two projects, each costing $48,000. Project X returns $12,000 a year for six years. Project Y returns $20,000, $18,000, $15,000 and $10,000 in years 1–4. Cash flows arise evenly through each year.

  1. (a) Calculate the payback period for Project X. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the payback period for Project Y in years and months. [3]
  3. (c) State one disadvantage of the payback method. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 48,000 ÷ 12,000 = 4 years
    1 mark.
  2. (b) After 2 years 38,000 recovered; 10,000 of year 3's 15,000 needed = 0.67 of a year ≈ 8 months. Payback = 2 years 8 months.
    M1 cumulative; M1 fraction; A1.
  3. (c) Ignores cash flows after payback / ignores the time value of money (unless discounted).
    1 mark.
Q30Payback period · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A bakery chain in Islamabad may open a new outlet costing $75,000. Net cash inflows are $28,000, $30,000, $26,000 and $20,000 in years 1–4, arising evenly through each year. The cost of capital is 10%.Discount factors at 10%: year 1 0.909; year 2 0.826; year 3 0.751; year 4 0.683

  1. (a) Calculate the simple payback period, correct to two decimal places of a year. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the discounted payback period, correct to two decimal places of a year. [4]
  3. (c) Explain why the discounted payback period is longer than the simple payback period. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) After 2 years 58,000; 17,000 ÷ 26,000 = 0.65; payback = 2.65 years
    M1; A1.
  2. (b) Present values: 25,452, 24,780, 19,526, 13,660. Cumulative after year 3: 69,758; still to recover 5,242; 5,242 ÷ 13,660 = 0.38. Discounted payback = 3.38 years
    M1 present values; M1 cumulative; M1 fraction of year 4; A1.
  3. (c) Discounting reduces the value of each future inflow, so it takes longer to recover the cost.
    1 mark.
