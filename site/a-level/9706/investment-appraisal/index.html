  1. (a) State two non-financial factors a business should consider before replacing a machine. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one advantage of net present value over the payback method. [2]

Q2Investment decision and sensitivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The manager of a cinema in Karachi has been told that a new projector has a positive net present value.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a positive net present value. [2]
  2. (b) State two limitations of the net present value method. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The present value of the future net cash inflows is greater than the initial cost; the project earns more than the cost of capital and increases the value of the business.
    1 mark PV of inflows > cost; 1 mark return above cost of capital.
  2. (b) Any two: cash flows are hard to forecast; choosing the right discount rate is difficult; ignores non-financial factors; harder to understand than payback; assumes the cost of capital stays the same.
    1 mark each.
Q3Investment decision and sensitivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The finance director of a cement company is appraising a new kiln.

  1. (a) Explain why a business uses its cost of capital as the discount rate. [2]
  2. (b) Explain what is meant by sensitivity analysis. [2]
  3. (c) State one limitation of sensitivity analysis. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is the return the business must pay to its providers of finance; a project must earn at least this return to add value.
    1 mark cost of finance; 1 mark minimum required return.
  2. (b) Testing how much one estimate (e.g. sales volume, cost, discount rate) can change before the NPV becomes zero, to show which estimates the decision depends on most.

    1 mark.

  1. (a) 41,000 × 3.791 − 140,000 = $15,431
