Q1Missing figures and statements from incomplete records · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Rukhsana does not keep full accounting records. Her business net assets were $25,000 at the start of the year and $31,400 at the end. During the year she took drawings of $8,200 and paid $3,000 of her own savings into the business bank account. What was her profit for the year? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C $11,600
    Profit = closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced = 31,400 − 25,000 + 8,200 − 3,000 = $11,600. D adds the capital introduced instead of deducting it; the extra $3,000 came from Rukhsana's savings, not from trading.
Q2Missing figures and statements from incomplete records · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) An electronics wholesaler in Karachi keeps incomplete records. Trade receivables were $7,420 at the start of the year and $8,950 at the end. During the year it received $61,300 from credit customers, allowed cash discounts of $1,150 and wrote off irrecoverable debts of $480. What were the credit sales for the year? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C $64,460
    Credit sales = receipts + discounts allowed + irrecoverable debts + closing receivables − opening receivables = 61,300 + 1,150 + 480 + 8,950 − 7,420 = $64,460. B ignores discounts allowed and irrecoverable debts, which also reduce what customers owe.

    M1 accrual adjustments; M1 remove owner's amount (drawings, not wages); A1.
  3. (c) The 35% margin is an average; if some goods were sold at a discount, damaged, stolen or sold at different margins, actual inventory will differ from the estimate.
    1 mark reason; 1 mark development.

Q27Mark-up and margin · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A shop in Karachi sells mobile phones at a mark-up of 20% and accessories at a mark-up of 60%. Last year it sold phones for $96,000 and accessories for $24,000. Opening inventory was $14,000 and purchases were $92,500. The inventory count at the year end found goods costing $10,300.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of sales for each product line. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the overall gross margin, correct to two decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the value of the inventory missing at the year end. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Phones 96,000 × 100/120 = $80,000; accessories 24,000 × 100/160 = $15,000
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Gross profit = 16,000 + 9,000 = 25,000; margin = 25,000 ÷ 120,000 × 100 = 20.83%
    M1 total gross profit; A1.
  3. (c) Expected inventory = 14,000 + 92,500 − 95,000 = 11,500; missing = 11,500 − 10,300 = $1,200
    M1 total cost of sales used; M1 expected inventory; A1.
Q28Full versus incomplete records · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Tariq sells second-hand books from a small shop in Lahore. He keeps a cash book and files his invoices, but has no ledgers.

  1. (a) State two reasons why a small trader might keep incomplete records rather than a full double-entry system. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two disadvantages to the trader of keeping incomplete records. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: lack of bookkeeping knowledge; saves time; saves the cost of a bookkeeper or software; the business is small with few transactions.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any two, developed: (1) no trial balance, so arithmetical errors are hard to find; (2) no detailed statement of profit or loss, so the trader cannot see which expenses are rising; (3) theft or loss of cash or goods may go unnoticed; (4) harder to get a bank loan or satisfy tax authorities without reliable statements.
    1 mark for each disadvantage and 1 mark for each explanation, max 2 disadvantages.

Q30Full versus incomplete records · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Idris owns a furniture workshop in Chiniot. Five years ago he had four customers who paid in cash; now he has 35 credit customers and six employees. He keeps a cash book only, and an accountant prepares his statements from statements of affairs each year. Last year cash differences of $1,800 could not be explained. Idris wants a bank loan of $40,000 to buy a new machine. A part-time bookkeeper would cost $3,000 per year.

  1. (a) Advise Idris whether he should change to a full double-entry bookkeeping system. Justify your answer. [7]
Show answer
  1. (a) A reasoned recommendation, e.g. Idris should change to full double entry (or computerised accounts): the business has grown, a bank loan is wanted and the cost is small compared with the benefits. Either decision accepted if justified.
    For (max 4): bank needs reliable statements for the $40,000 loan; full detail of expenses lets him control costs; control accounts and trial balance help find errors and fraud (cash differences of $1,800 last year); detailed receivables records help chase the 35 credit customers; accounting software can automate entries. Against (max 4): cost of a bookkeeper ($3,000 per year) or software and training; time taken from running the workshop; incomplete records have produced a profit figure so far; an accountant could still prepare statements from his records at year end. Decision with justification linked to the figures (1). Max 7: at most 5 for one-sided answers.
