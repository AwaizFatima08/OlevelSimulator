Q1Missing figures and statements from incomplete records · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Rukhsana does not keep full accounting records. Her business net assets were $25,000 at the start of the year and $31,400 at the end. During the year she took drawings of $8,200 and paid $3,000 of her own savings into the business bank account. What was her profit for the year? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C $11,600
    Profit = closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced = 31,400 − 25,000 + 8,200 − 3,000 = $11,600. D adds the capital introduced instead of deducting it; the extra $3,000 came from Rukhsana's savings, not from trading.
Q2Missing figures and statements from incomplete records · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) An electronics wholesaler in Karachi keeps incomplete records. Trade receivables were $7,420 at the start of the year and $8,950 at the end. During the year it received $61,300 from credit customers, allowed cash discounts of $1,150 and wrote off irrecoverable debts of $480. What were the credit sales for the year? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C $64,460
    Credit sales = receipts + discounts allowed + irrecoverable debts + closing receivables − opening receivables = 61,300 + 1,150 + 480 + 8,950 − 7,420 = $64,460. B ignores discounts allowed and irrecoverable debts, which also reduce what customers owe.

Conclusion: at present incomplete records (with a well-kept cash book, daily banking and regular inventory counts) are adequate; move to double entry/accounting software if he starts giving credit or opens more stalls.
    Level 3 (5–6): balanced, applied to Kashif's cash-only business, justified conclusion. Level 2 (3–4): points on both sides, limited application. Level 1 (1–2): simple statements.

Q61Full versus incomplete records · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Waqar owns a pharmacy in Bahawalpur with three employees. He keeps only a cash book and suspects one employee has been taking cash from sales.

  1. (a) Explain how a full double-entry system with control accounts and bank reconciliations would help Waqar detect the theft. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how, without a full system, his accountant could still estimate the amount stolen. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sales ledger control account would show what customers should have paid; differences from cash received would be investigated. A bank reconciliation each month compares the cash book with the bank statement, so receipts not banked would show. Separation of duties (recording vs handling cash) is possible with full records. Regular trial balances reveal irregular balances.
    B1 each valid point explained (up to 4).
  2. (b) Using the normal mark-up/margin: calculate cost of sales from opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory, apply the mark-up to find expected sales, and compare with takings recorded/banked; the difference estimates the loss.
    B1 margin/mark-up to estimate sales; B1 compare with recorded takings.

Q63Full versus incomplete records · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Yasir runs an auto-repair workshop in Mardan and keeps incomplete records. His capital was $35,000 at the start of the year and $39,200 at the end. During the year he brought his private car, valued at $6,000, into the business as a recovery vehicle. His accountant has reconstructed a profit of $18,600. Yasir says he took only $600 a month in cash for himself.

  1. (a) Calculate Yasir's total drawings for the year. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the drawings that Yasir cannot explain. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest two explanations for the difference, and state how the accountant could investigate one of them. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $20,400
    Closing capital = opening capital + capital introduced + profit − drawings (M1); 39,200 = 35,000 + 6,000 + 18,600 − drawings (M1); drawings 20,400 (A1).
  2. (b) $13,200
    20,400 − 600 × 12 = 13,200.
  3. (c) Explanations: goods taken for his family without recording; private bills paid from business cash/bank; cash stolen by staff; errors in the statement of affairs (e.g. inventory over/understated). Investigation: examine bank statements for private payments; do a margin check on sales; recount/re-value inventory.
    B1 each explanation (×2); B2 a suitable investigation linked to an explanation.
