Practice questions

Original questions written by HomiLabs in the style of the exam, with answers and mark schemes. They are not Cambridge past paper questions and have not been reviewed by Cambridge. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which feature applies to a sole trader?A The owner has limited liability.B The business is a separate legal entity from its owner.C The owner has unlimited liability for the business's debts.D The business must publish audited financial statements. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C The owner has unlimited liability for the business's debts.
    A sole trader is not a separate legal person, so the owner is personally liable for all business debts (unlimited liability). A and B describe limited companies.
Q2Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Areeba plans to open a small bakery in Multan as a sole trader.

  1. (a) State two advantages of operating as a sole trader. [2]
  2. (b) State two disadvantages of operating as a sole trader. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: owner keeps all the profit; full control and quick decisions; easy and cheap to set up; financial affairs are private (no need to publish accounts).
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any two: unlimited liability; limited capital (own savings and loans only); heavy workload / no one to share responsibility; business may end if the owner dies or is ill; harder to borrow.
    1 mark each.
Q3Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the term limited liability. [2]
  2. (b) Explain what is meant by saying a limited company is a separate legal entity. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shareholders can lose no more than the amount they have paid (or agreed to pay) for their shares; their personal assets cannot be used to pay the company's debts.
    1 mark: loss limited to investment in shares; 1 mark: personal assets protected.
  2. (b) The company exists in law separately from its owners: it can own assets, enter contracts, sue and be sued in its own name, and it continues if shareholders change or die.
    1 mark: separate person in law; 1 mark: example (contracts, own assets, continuity).
Q4Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between ownership and control in a limited company. [2]
  2. (b) Give two reasons why limited companies must publish annual financial statements. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shareholders own the company, but the directors (who may not be shareholders) manage and control it on their behalf.
    1 mark owners = shareholders; 1 mark control = directors/managers.
  2. (b) Because shareholders do not run the company, they need information to judge the directors' performance (stewardship); creditors and lenders need information because their claims are limited to the company's assets (limited liability).
    1 mark each.
Q5Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statements about a public limited company are correct?1 Its shares may be offered for sale to the general public.2 Its shareholders have unlimited liability.3 It is a separate legal entity from its shareholders.4 It must have only one owner.A 1 and 2B 1 and 3C 2 and 4D 3 and 4 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 1 and 3
    A public limited company can offer shares to the public (1) and is a separate legal entity (3). Shareholders' liability is limited to the amount unpaid on their shares, so 2 is wrong; it can have many shareholders, so 4 is wrong.
Q6Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State two differences between a private limited company and a public limited company. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest one reason why a family business might prefer to remain a private limited company. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: a private company cannot offer its shares to the general public, a public company can (and may be listed on a stock exchange); private company shares are usually transferred only with the directors' agreement, public company shares are freely traded; a public company usually needs a minimum share capital and stricter disclosure; a private company name ends in 'Limited/(Private) Limited', a public one in 'plc' or equivalent.
    2 marks per difference, comparing both types.
  2. (b) The family keeps control because shares cannot be sold to outsiders without approval, so there is no risk of takeover; also fewer disclosure requirements and lower costs.
    1 mark reason, 1 mark development.
Q7Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Usman has run a computer accessories shop in Hyderabad as a sole trader for six years. He wants to open a second branch and start selling online. His brother, who has IT skills, offers to invest $40,000 and become a partner.

  1. (a) Advise Usman whether he should form a partnership with his brother. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: extra capital ($40,000) to open the second branch; brother's IT and online-sales skills add expertise; workload and decision-making shared; cover during illness or holidays. Against: profits must be shared; unlimited liability continues — Usman becomes liable for his brother's business decisions; disagreements may arise, especially in a family; slower decisions; partnership may end on death or retirement. Decision: e.g. form the partnership with a written partnership agreement covering profit sharing, interest on capital and salaries, because the capital and skills are needed for the expansion.
    Up to 2 marks for advantages and up to 2 for disadvantages, applied to the case; 1 mark recommendation of a written agreement or other safeguard; 1 mark justified decision.
Q8Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Fahad, a sole trader, has closed his furniture business. It owes creditors $85,000. The business assets were sold for $52,000. Fahad has personal assets (savings and a car) worth $60,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount that Fahad must pay from his personal assets. [2]
  2. (b) Fahad's friend Nasir owned 30,000 $1 shares, fully paid at $1.20 each, in a limited company that collapsed with debts larger than its assets. State the maximum Nasir could lose and explain why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $33,000
    Debts 85,000 − business assets realised 52,000 = 33,000. M1 A1.
  2. (b) $36,000. Nasir has limited liability: his loss is limited to the amount paid for his shares (30,000 × $1.20); his personal assets cannot be claimed by the company's creditors.
    1 mark amount, 1 mark limited liability, 1 mark personal assets protected.
Q9Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Compare how owners withdraw profits from (i) a sole trader business and (ii) a limited company. [2]
  2. (b) Compare how the owners' capital is shown in the statement of financial position of a partnership and of a limited company. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) The sole trader takes drawings, recorded in the capital account. (ii) Shareholders receive dividends declared by the directors out of distributable profits; directors who work in the company also receive salaries treated as expenses.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Partnership: each partner has a capital account (often fixed) and a current account for profit shares, interest and drawings. Company: equity shows issued share capital and reserves (share premium, retained earnings and other reserves).
    1 mark each.
Q10Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) For each organisation, state whether it is a sole trader, a partnership, a private limited company, a public limited company or a non-profit organisation.(i) Three doctors share a clinic, profits and unlimited liability.(ii) A cricket club funded by members' subscriptions.(iii) A company whose shares are bought and sold on a stock exchange.(iv) A family firm whose shares can only be transferred with the directors' permission. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) partnership; (ii) non-profit organisation (club); (iii) public limited company; (iv) private limited company.
    1 mark each.
Q11Types of business organisation · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A hockey club in Sialkot is run for its members, not to make a profit. Which statement does it prepare instead of a statement of profit or loss, and what is the name of its equivalent of capital?A income and expenditure account; accumulated fundB receipts and payments account; retained earningsC statement of changes in equity; accumulated fundD income and expenditure account; current account [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A income and expenditure account; accumulated fund
    Non-profit organisations prepare an income and expenditure account (accruals basis) and their capital is the accumulated fund. A receipts and payments account (B) is only a summary of cash and does not replace the statement of profit or loss.
Q12Types of business organisation · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Zainab and Kiran are partners in an interior design business in Karachi. Turnover has tripled in three years and they have just won a contract to design the interior of a 200-room hotel, which will need expensive materials bought in advance. A cousin is willing to invest money but does not want to take part in running the business or have unlimited liability.

  1. (a) Advise Zainab and Kiran whether they should convert their partnership into a private limited company. Justify your answer. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) For conversion: limited liability protects their homes against claims (important given the large contract and its risks); the company is a separate legal entity, so it continues if a partner leaves; easier to raise finance — can issue shares to investors such as their cousin and use loan notes; may appear more credible to large clients. Against: set-up costs and legal formalities; must file accounts publicly (loss of privacy) and may require an audit; profits taxed as a company and owners paid through salaries and dividends; banks may still ask for personal guarantees, reducing the benefit of limited liability; shares in a private company cannot easily be sold. Decision: convert, because the growth in contract size and the need for outside capital make limited liability and access to share finance worth the extra cost.
    Level 3 (7–8): well-applied points on both sides and a justified decision. Level 2 (4–6): some applied points on both sides or one-sided with a decision. Level 1 (1–3): general features of partnerships/companies.
Q13Types of business organisation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Hassan bought 40,000 ordinary shares of $1 each in a private limited company at $1.25 per share, fully paid. Salman is a general partner in a firm of architects with one other partner.

  1. (a) State the maximum amount Hassan could lose as a shareholder, and the maximum Salman could lose as a partner. Explain the difference. [4]
  2. (b) Explain one way in which the law allows a partner to have limited liability, and the condition that applies. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hassan: $50,000 (40,000 × $1.25 paid). Salman: there is no limit — he could lose his capital and his personal assets, because partners have unlimited liability and are jointly liable for all the partnership's debts (including those caused by the other partner).
    1 mark Hassan's 50,000; 1 mark Salman unlimited; 1 mark limited liability explained; 1 mark joint liability/personal assets.
  2. (b) A limited partner (in a limited partnership / LLP structure) may have liability limited to their capital, provided there is at least one general partner with unlimited liability and the limited partner takes no part in managing the business (or the firm is registered as a limited liability partnership).
    1 mark limited partner/LLP; 1 mark condition (no management role / at least one general partner / registration).
Q14Capital and revenue expenditure · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which item is capital expenditure for a courier company?A fuel for the delivery vansB repainting the officeC purchase of a new delivery motorcycleD insurance of the vans [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C purchase of a new delivery motorcycle
    Buying a new motorcycle provides a non-current asset used for more than one year, so it is capital expenditure. Repainting (B) only maintains an existing asset, so it is revenue expenditure.
Q15Capital and revenue expenditure · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Bano Distributors bought and equipped a warehouse in Gujranwala.

  1. (a) Classify each item as capital expenditure or revenue expenditure.(i) Legal fees for buying a new warehouse(ii) Wages of the warehouse staff(iii) Installing air-conditioning in the warehouse for the first time(iv) Replacing a broken window pane in the warehouse [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) capital; (ii) revenue; (iii) capital; (iv) revenue.
    1 mark each.
Q16Capital and revenue expenditure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Nishat Weaving bought a weaving machine. The invoice and other costs were: price $86,000; delivery $2,400; installation $3,100; staff training on the machine $1,800; first-year maintenance contract $950; insurance for the first year $1,200.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount to be capitalised as the cost of the machine. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the revenue expenditure and state where it is shown. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $91,500
    Price 86,000 + delivery 2,400 + installation 3,100 = 91,500. Training, maintenance and insurance are revenue. M1 A1 + 1 for excluding all revenue items.
  2. (b) $3,950; shown as expenses in the statement of profit or loss.
    1,800 + 950 + 1,200 = 3,950.
Q17Capital and revenue expenditure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A school in Abbottabad built an extension to its building costing $40,000. The bookkeeper debited this to repairs and maintenance. Buildings are depreciated at 10% per year on cost, with a full year's charge in the year of acquisition.

  1. (a) Calculate the effect of the error on profit for the year. [2]
  2. (b) State the effect of the error on the statement of financial position. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Profit is understated by $36,000.
    The 40,000 should be removed from expenses (+40,000) and depreciation of 10% × 40,000 = 4,000 charged; net +36,000. M1 A1.
  2. (b) Non-current assets are understated by $36,000 (cost 40,000 less depreciation 4,000), and capital (via profit) is understated by the same amount.
    1 mark NCA; 1 mark capital/retained profit.
Q18Capital and revenue expenditure · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Royal Pharmacy spent the following on its building during the year:1 Replacing worn roof tiles: $6,8002 Adding a second floor: $95,0003 Repainting the shop front: $3,2004 Installing a new lift (not there before): $28,5005 Architect's fees for the second floor: $7,4006 Cleaning after the building work: $1,100

  1. (a) Calculate the total capital expenditure and the total revenue expenditure. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why item 1 is revenue expenditure while item 2 is capital expenditure. [2]
  3. (c) Distinguish between a capital receipt and a revenue receipt, giving one example of each. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Capital $130,900; revenue $11,100
    Capital: items 2, 4, 5 = 95,000 + 28,500 + 7,400 = 130,900. Revenue: items 1, 3, 6 = 6,800 + 3,200 + 1,100 = 11,100. 1 mark each correct classification group, 1 mark each total.
  2. (b) Replacing worn tiles restores the building to its original condition (maintenance), so it is an expense; adding a floor improves/enlarges the asset and increases its earning capacity for more than one year, so it is capital.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) Capital receipt: from sale of non-current assets, loans or capital introduced (e.g. proceeds of selling an old van). Revenue receipt: from normal trading (e.g. sales revenue, rent received, commission).
    1 mark each with example.
Q19Capital and revenue expenditure · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A trader records the cost of servicing a delivery van as an addition to the van's cost. What is the effect on the financial statements for the year, ignoring depreciation?A profit overstated; non-current assets overstatedB profit understated; non-current assets overstatedC profit overstated; non-current assets understatedD profit understated; non-current assets understated [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A profit overstated; non-current assets overstated
    Revenue expenditure treated as capital means an expense is missing from the statement of profit or loss, so profit is overstated, and the van is shown at too high a cost. D is the effect of the opposite error (capital expenditure treated as revenue).
Q20Double entry and books of prime entry · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the book of prime entry in which each transaction is first recorded.(i) An invoice received for goods bought on credit for resale(ii) A credit note sent to a customer for faulty goods returned(iii) A cheque paid to a supplier(iv) The purchase of office furniture on credit [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) purchases journal (day book); (ii) sales returns journal; (iii) cash book; (iv) the journal (general journal).
    1 mark each.
Q21Double entry and books of prime entry · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Zara, a wholesaler, sells goods with a list price of $6,400 on credit to Bilal Stores, allowing 15% trade discount.

  1. (a) State the amount at which the sale is recorded in Zara's books. [1]
  2. (b) For each transaction, state the account to be debited and the account to be credited in Zara's ledger.(i) the credit sale to Bilal Stores(ii) Bilal Stores returns goods with a net value of $340(iii) Bilal Stores pays the balance by bank transfer [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $5,440
    Trade discount is deducted before recording: 6,400 × 85% = 5,440.
  2. (b) (i) Dr Bilal Stores (trade receivable), Cr Sales $5,440. (ii) Dr Sales returns, Cr Bilal Stores $340. (iii) Dr Bank, Cr Bilal Stores $5,100.
    1 mark each double entry (amount in (iii) must be correct).
Q22Double entry and books of prime entry · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A business receives a credit note from a supplier for damaged goods it returned. In which book of prime entry is the credit note recorded?A purchases journalB purchases returns journalC sales returns journalD cash book [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B purchases returns journal
    Goods returned to a supplier are returns outwards (purchases returns), recorded from the credit note received. C would be used for a credit note sent to a customer.
Q23Double entry and books of prime entry · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why businesses use books of prime entry rather than posting every transaction straight to the ledger. [2]
  2. (b) Name the source document used to write up (i) the sales journal and (ii) the purchases returns journal. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) They group similar transactions (e.g. all credit sales) so that only totals need to be posted to the nominal ledger, saving time and reducing errors; they also provide a chronological record and audit trail back to source documents.
    1 mark each for two valid points.
  2. (b) (i) copies of sales invoices sent; (ii) credit notes received from suppliers.
    1 mark each.
Q24Double entry and books of prime entry · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Tahir runs an engineering supplies business.1 Bought a delivery van for $18,500 on credit from Rahim Motors.2 Brought his own car, valued at $7,200, into the business for business use.3 Sent three credit sales invoices: list price $2,400 less 10% trade discount; list price $5,600 less 20% trade discount; $3,150 with no discount.

  1. (a) Prepare journal entries, with narratives, for transactions 1 and 2. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the total of the sales journal for the three invoices in transaction 3, and state the double entry for posting the total. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Debit $ Credit $1 Delivery van 18,500 Rahim Motors (other payable) 18,500 Purchase of van on credit 2 Motor vehicles 7,200 Capital 7,200 Owner's car brought into the business
    1 mark each correct double entry with amounts (2); 1 mark each narrative (2). Accept 'non-current asset' for the van account.
  2. (b) $9,790; Dr each customer's account individually, Cr Sales account with the total $9,790.
    2,400 × 90% = 2,160 + 5,600 × 80% = 4,480 + 3,150 × 100% = 3,150; total 9,790 (2). Posting (1).
Q25Trial balance · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name four types of error that are not revealed by a trial balance, giving an example of each. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any four with examples: omission (invoice not recorded at all); commission (sale debited to the wrong customer); principle (repairs debited to a non-current asset account); original entry (wrong amount from source document in both accounts); complete reversal (debit and credit swapped); compensating errors (two errors cancel out).
    1 mark each type with a valid example.

Q27Trial balance · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which error will cause the totals of a trial balance to disagree?A A purchase invoice for $800 was not recorded.B Sales of $1,250 were credited to the rent received account.C Wages of $3,400 were debited to the wages account as $4,300 and credited to the bank as $3,400.D A payment to Saleem of $500 was debited to Salim's account. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C Wages of $3,400 were debited to the wages account as $4,300 and credited to the bank as $3,400.
    In C the debit (4,300) and credit (3,400) are unequal, so the trial balance does not agree (debit side $900 too high). A is an omission, B an error of commission/principle between income accounts and D an error of commission — in each case equal debits and credits remain.
Q28Accounting equation · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Imran owns a printing shop. At 30 June 2027 his non-current assets are $186,000, current assets $43,700, non-current liabilities (bank loan) $60,000 and current liabilities $27,450.

  1. (a) Calculate Imran's capital at 30 June 2027. [2]
  2. (b) State the accounting equation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $142,250
    Capital = assets − liabilities = (186,000 + 43,700) − (60,000 + 27,450) = 142,250. M1 A1.
  2. (b) Assets = Capital + Liabilities (or Assets − Liabilities = Capital).
    1 mark.

  1. (a) Show the effect of each transaction on assets, liabilities and capital, and state the closing totals. [6]

Q30Accounting equation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Junaid does not keep full double-entry records. At 1 January 2026 his business assets were $148,000 and liabilities $39,500. At 31 December 2026 assets were $176,400 and liabilities $44,200. During the year he withdrew $21,600 and paid in a legacy of $15,000 as extra capital.

  1. (a) Calculate the capital at the start and at the end of the year. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for the year. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why an increase in capital is not the same as profit. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Start $108,500; end $132,200
    148,000 − 39,500 = 108,500; 176,400 − 44,200 = 132,200.
  2. (b) $30,300
    Closing capital = opening + capital introduced + profit − drawings. Profit = 132,200 − 108,500 + 21,600 − 15,000 = 30,300. M1 add drawings, M1 deduct capital introduced, A1.
  3. (c) Capital also changes because of drawings (which reduce it) and extra capital introduced (which increases it); these are owner transactions, not business performance.
    1 mark drawings; 1 mark capital introduced.
