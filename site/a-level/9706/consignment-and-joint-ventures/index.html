Q1Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Two traders in Karachi, one selling dried fruit and one with a shop in Dubai, agree to work together for one shipment only and share the profit.

  1. (a) Explain the term 'joint venture'. [2]
  2. (b) State two differences between a joint venture and a partnership. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A short-term business arrangement in which two or more parties combine resources for a single, specific project and share the resulting profit or loss in an agreed ratio.
    1 mark: two or more parties for a specific/single project. 1 mark: temporary, sharing profit or loss in agreed ratio.
  2. (b) A joint venture ends when the project is completed, whereas a partnership continues indefinitely; joint venturers usually carry on their own separate businesses and there is usually no firm name or separate continuing set of books.
    1 mark each for any two: duration (temporary vs continuing); scope (one project vs ongoing business); venturers keep own businesses; no firm name / records often kept in venturers' own books.

Q4Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Sialkot Leather sends handbags to an agent in Doha. The agent sells them for a commission and returns the money. A competitor instead works with a Doha trader who shares the profit on the goods equally.

  1. (a) Identify which arrangement is a consignment and which is a joint venture. [1]
  2. (b) Explain two differences between a consignment and a joint venture. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sialkot Leather and its agent: consignment. The competitor and the Doha trader: joint venture.
    B1 both correct.
  2. (b) In a consignment the consignor owns the goods until they are sold and bears all the profit or loss; the agent earns only commission. In a joint venture the venturers are co-owners for the project and share profit or loss in an agreed ratio. Also, a consignee acts for the consignor as agent, whereas joint venturers are like temporary partners with equal standing.
    Up to 2 marks per difference: ownership of goods; who bears profit/loss (commission vs share of profit); relationship (principal–agent vs co-venturers).

Tariq and Omar form a joint venture to run a food and craft stall at a three-day Eid bazaar in Multan. They share profits and losses equally. Q12Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Hamid and Kiran agree a joint venture to buy and sell 150 hand-carved chairs from Sialkot. Each venturer keeps his or her own books and records his or her own transactions. Hamid sends chairs from his own inventory, costing $9,000, to the venture. He then pays Kiran an advance of $2,000 by bank transfer to cover local costs.

  1. (a) Debit: Joint venture with Kiran account $9,000. Credit: Inventory (goods at cost) $9,000.

  2. (b) Debit: Joint venture with Kiran account $2,000. Credit: Bank $2,000.

  3. (c) It is cash handed over to another venturer, not a cost of the venture. The costs are recorded in the memorandum account when Kiran actually incurs them.
    1 mark: the advance is a transfer between venturers, not a cost. 1 mark: costs appear only when they are incurred and paid for the venture.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit on the joint venture. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the cash Ghazal must pay Shehzad, and the cash Ghazal keeps for herself. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the unsold goods are credited at their agreed value of $3,000 and not at their cost of $5,000. [2]

Q59Joint ventures · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A joint venture takes over unsold goods at an agreed value of $12,000. Their net realisable value is $7,500.

  1. (a) Evaluate whether the memorandum joint venture account gives a fair view of the profit when unsold goods are taken over at an agreed value above their net realisable value. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Not a fair view. Goods taken over at 12,000 but worth 7,500 overstate profit by 4,500, and the overstatement is shared between the venturers. Prudence and the fair view point to net realisable value (7,500). However, the agreed value is a genuine agreement between venturers, and it may be accepted if documented and the recipient pays the agreed price. Balanced conclusion: profit should be reported at the lower value unless the agreement is clear and the valuation is reasonable.
    1–2 marks: identifies the valuation issue. 3–4 marks: explains the effect (profit overstated by 4,500, shared unfairly). 5–6 marks: evaluates both sides and reaches a supported conclusion, referring to prudence or net realisable value.
