Q1Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which of the following is a physical security measure for a computerised accounting system?A a password on each user accountB encryption of the data filesC a locked room for the serverD a firewall on the network [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C a locked room for the server
    A locked room physically prevents access to the hardware. A, B and D are software (logical) controls.
Q2Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the main purpose of making regular backup copies of accounting data?A to stop hackers from reading the dataB to allow the data to be restored if it is lost or corruptedC to check that entries have been input correctlyD to reduce the storage space needed [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B to allow the data to be restored if it is lost or corrupted
    A backup is a copy kept so that lost or damaged data can be recovered. It does not stop unauthorised reading (A, which needs encryption/passwords) or check accuracy (C).
Q3Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Fatima owns a clothes shop in Hyderabad and keeps her accounts by hand. She is thinking of buying accounting software.

  1. (a) State two advantages to Fatima of using a computerised accounting system. [2]
  2. (b) State two disadvantages to Fatima of using a computerised accounting system. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Records and reports (e.g. trial balance, statements to customers) are produced quickly; arithmetic is accurate and ledgers update automatically.
    1 mark each for any two: speed; accuracy of calculations; automatic posting to ledgers; up-to-date information; easy reports; less storage space.
  2. (b) Cost of hardware, software and training; risk of data loss through system failure, viruses or hacking.
    1 mark each for any two: cost; training time; risk of breakdown/data loss; security threats; input errors processed unnoticed.
Q4Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Ravi Motors, Karachi, keeps its customer and supplier records on a computerised accounting system.

  1. (a) Explain two methods the business could use to prevent unauthorised access to its accounting data. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Passwords: each user must enter a secret password, so only authorised staff can log in. Access levels: staff can see or change only the parts of the system they need, e.g. a sales clerk cannot open the payroll. (Also: encryption, biometric log-in, automatic log-out, firewalls.)
    Up to 2 marks per method (1 identify, 1 explain).
Q5Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A trading business in Lahore saves all its accounting data on one office computer.

  1. (a) State two ways the business could reduce the risk of losing its accounting data. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a backup copy should be stored away from the office. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Make regular backups; store a backup copy off-site or in the cloud.
    1 mark each: regular backups; off-site/cloud storage; antivirus software; uninterruptible power supply; fire-proof storage.
  2. (b) If the office suffers a fire, flood or theft, both the computer and an on-site backup could be destroyed; an off-site copy can still be used to restore the records.
    1 mark: risk of physical disaster/theft at the office. 1 mark: off-site copy allows restoration.
Q6Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The finance manager of a school in Islamabad prepares the annual budget on a spreadsheet.

  1. (a) Explain two ways a spreadsheet helps in preparing budgets. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Formulas recalculate totals automatically when any figure is changed, saving time and avoiding arithmetic errors. 'What-if' analysis lets the manager test different fee levels or salary increases and see the effect on the budget straight away. (Also: easy charts, copying layouts for each month, sharing.)
    Up to 2 marks per way (1 identify, 1 explain).
Q7Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The external auditor of Indus Foods Ltd asks whether its accounting software keeps an audit trail.

  1. (a) Explain the term 'audit trail'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why an audit trail is useful to the auditor. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A record kept by the system of every transaction and change, showing who made it and when, so that each entry can be traced from the source document to the financial statements.
    1 mark: record of transactions/changes with user and time. 1 mark: allows tracing source to final accounts.
  2. (b) The auditor can check that recorded figures are supported by source documents and can identify unauthorised or suspicious changes, which helps detect errors and fraud.
    1 mark: verify figures to evidence. 1 mark: detect unauthorised changes/fraud.
Q8Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A computerised accounting system needs both hardware and software.

  1. (a) Distinguish between hardware and software, giving one example of each. [2]
  2. (b) Name two reports that accounting software can produce automatically. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hardware is the physical equipment, e.g. a computer or printer; software is the programs that run on it, e.g. an accounting package.
    1 mark each, with example.
  2. (b) Trial balance; aged trade receivables report (also: statement of profit or loss, customer statements, VAT/tax return, inventory report).
    1 mark each.
Q9Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Sunrise Bakery, Peshawar, is introducing a computerised accounting system.

  1. (a) Explain two reasons why staff should be trained before the system is used. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) So that they enter transactions correctly and avoid input errors that would make the accounts wrong. So that they can use the system efficiently and obtain the reports needed, getting the expected time savings. (Also: understand security procedures such as passwords and backups; reduce resistance to change.)

Q10Computerised systems and data security · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The accounts department of a hospital has a payroll clerk, a purchases clerk and an accountant, all using one computerised system.

  1. (a) Explain the term 'access levels'. [2]
  2. (b) Give one example of how access levels could be used in this department. [1]
  3. (c) State one physical control that would protect the accounting system. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each user is allowed to view or change only the parts of the system that their job needs.
    1 mark: restricted rights per user. 1 mark: related to job role/view vs edit.
  2. (b) The purchases clerk cannot open or change payroll records.
    B1 any sensible example.
  3. (c) Lock the server room (also: CCTV, lockable cabinets for backups, fire protection).
    B1.
Q11Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A business's purchase system will not accept a single invoice above $10,000. A clerk keys in an invoice for $85,000 instead of $8,500.Which validation check will reject the entry?A format checkB range (limit) checkC presence checkD check digit [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B range (limit) check
    A range or limit check tests that a value lies within set limits. A format check only tests the pattern of characters, which 85,000 still matches; a presence check only tests that a value has been entered.
Q12Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which of the following is a disadvantage of a computerised accounting system compared with a manual system?A ledger balances are updated automaticallyB reports such as the trial balance can be produced quicklyC an input error may be repeated in every record that uses that dataD arithmetic errors in totalling the ledgers are eliminated [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C an input error may be repeated in every record that uses that data
    If wrong data is input, the system processes it faithfully and the error flows through all linked records ('garbage in, garbage out'). A, B and D are advantages.
Q13Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the purpose of an audit trail in a computerised accounting system?A to prevent staff from changing their passwordsB to allow each transaction to be traced from its source document through to the financial statementsC to make a copy of all files at the end of each dayD to stop viruses from entering the system [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B to allow each transaction to be traced from its source document through to the financial statements
    An audit trail records who entered or changed each transaction and when, so it can be traced. C describes a backup; D describes antivirus software.
Q14Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A wholesaler's clerks key in customer account codes (format: two letters followed by four digits, e.g. KH2041) and invoice amounts.

  1. (a) Name and describe three validation checks that would be suitable for this data. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Format check: tests that the account code is two letters followed by four digits. Existence (lookup) check: tests that the account code matches an existing customer. Range check: tests that the invoice amount is within set limits, e.g. above $0 and below the customer's credit limit. (Also: presence check that no field is left blank.)
    For each of three checks: 1 mark name, 1 mark description applied to the data.
Q15Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Zaitoon Traders uses an integrated accounting package in which the sales, purchases, inventory and nominal ledgers are linked.

  1. (a) Explain what happens in the system when a credit sales invoice is entered. [3]
  2. (b) State one benefit of the ledgers being integrated. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The customer's account in the sales ledger is debited; the sales account in the nominal ledger is credited and the sales ledger control (trade receivables) account is updated; the inventory records are reduced by the quantity sold.
    1 mark each: customer account debited; sales/control accounts updated in nominal ledger; inventory updated.
  2. (b) Each transaction is entered only once, so all records agree and are up to date.
    B1 (single entry, consistency, saves time, fewer posting errors).
Q16Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A garment exporter in Sialkot has received emails that appear to come from its bank, asking staff to confirm the company's online banking log-in details. Last month another firm in the city had its accounting files locked by ransomware.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by 'phishing' and suggest one way to protect the business against it. [2]
  2. (b) Explain what is meant by 'ransomware' and suggest one way to protect the business against it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Phishing is a fraudulent message pretending to be from a trusted organisation to trick staff into revealing passwords or bank details. Protection: train staff never to give log-in details by email and to check messages with the bank directly.
    1 mark explanation; 1 mark protection (training, spam filters, two-factor authentication).
  2. (b) Ransomware is malicious software that encrypts or locks files and demands payment to release them. Protection: keep regular backups stored separately so files can be restored without paying, and keep antivirus software and updates current.
    1 mark explanation; 1 mark protection (separate backups, antivirus, updates, not opening unknown attachments).
Q17Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

An online retailer in Karachi stores customers' names, addresses, phone numbers and payment records in its accounting system.

  1. (a) Explain two responsibilities the business has when holding this personal data. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Keep the data secure: use passwords, encryption and access controls so it cannot be stolen or misused. Use the data only for the purpose it was collected and keep it accurate and up to date, deleting it when no longer needed. (Also: obey data protection law, do not share without consent.)
    Up to 2 marks per responsibility (1 identify, 1 explain).
Q18Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The owner of a computerised business says, 'The computer never makes mistakes, so our accounts must be correct.'

  1. (a) Identify and explain two types of error that can still occur in a computerised accounting system. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Error of original entry: a wrong amount is keyed in from the invoice, and the system posts the wrong figure to both accounts. Error of commission: a payment is posted to the wrong supplier's account; debits still equal credits so the system does not detect it. (Also: error of omission, error of principle, compensating errors.)
    Up to 2 marks per error (1 identify, 1 explain why the system does not detect it).
Q19Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A pharmacy in Rawalpindi uses its accounting software to keep a running record of inventory. The software automatically produces a purchase order when inventory of an item falls to its reorder level. For one medicine, maximum daily usage is 120 packs and the maximum time for delivery is 5 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the reorder level for this medicine, using maximum usage × maximum lead time. [2]
  2. (b) Explain one benefit and one risk of the software producing purchase orders automatically. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 600 packs
    120 × 5 (M1) = 600 packs (A1).
  2. (b) Benefit: the pharmacy will not run out of medicines because orders are made on time without staff having to check shelves. Risk: if the recorded inventory is wrong (e.g. theft or input error), orders may be wrong, so physical counts are still needed.
    1 mark benefit; 1 mark risk.
Q20Computerised systems and data security · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Ravi Builders spent the following when introducing a computerised accounting system: $Computers and printers 9,000Accounting software (permanent licence) 3,000Installation of the network 600Staff training course 1,200Annual maintenance contract 800Temporary staff to key in old records 700

  1. (a) Calculate the total capital expenditure and the total revenue expenditure. [4]
  2. (b) Explain the effect on the profit for the year if the training course were treated as capital expenditure. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Capital $12,600; revenue $2,700.
    Capital: hardware 9,000, software 3,000, installation 600 (B1 B1) = 12,600. Revenue: training 1,200, maintenance 800, temporary staff 700 (B1) = 2,700 (B1).
  2. (b) Profit would be overstated by $1,200 this year (less any depreciation charged), and non-current assets would also be overstated.
    1 mark: profit overstated by 1,200. 1 mark: assets overstated / breaks the rule that training is revenue expenditure.
Q21Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A business uses an integrated computerised accounting system in which every entry must have equal debits and credits.Which error could still occur?A a sales ledger total that is added up incorrectlyB a debit entry with no corresponding credit entryC a trial balance that does not agreeD a sales invoice posted to the account of the wrong customer [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D a sales invoice posted to the account of the wrong customer
    Posting to the wrong customer is an error of commission: debits still equal credits, so the system cannot detect it. The software performs the arithmetic and enforces double entry, so A, B and C cannot occur.
Q22Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A batch of supplier invoices has a manually calculated batch total of $23,640. After the invoices were keyed in, the computer total was $23,910.Which single error would explain the difference?A an invoice for $4,580 was keyed in as $4,850B an invoice for $270 was not keyed inC an invoice for $4,850 was keyed in as $4,580D a credit note for $270 was keyed in as an invoice [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A an invoice for $4,580 was keyed in as $4,850
    The computer total is 270 too high (23,910 − 23,640). Keying 4,850 for 4,580 adds 270. B and C would make the total 270 too low; D would make it 540 too high.
Q23Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Noor Pharmacy, Quetta, is considering a computerised accounting system. Estimated costs and savings: $Software (one-off) 4,800Hardware (one-off) 7,200Staff training (one-off) 1,500Annual software licence 900Annual saving in part-time bookkeeper's wages 6,000

  1. (a) Calculate the total initial cost. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the net annual saving. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the payback period in years, correct to two decimal places. [2]
  4. (d) Advise the owner whether to introduce the system. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $13,500
    4,800 + 7,200 + 1,500 = 13,500 (B1).
  2. (b) $5,100
    6,000 − 900 = 5,100 (B1).
  3. (c) 2.65 years
    13,500 ÷ 5,100 (M1) = 2.65 years (A1).
  4. (d) For: the cost is recovered in under three years, after which $5,100 a year is saved; faster, more accurate records and up-to-date inventory and receivables information. Against: hardware may need replacing in about three to five years; staff must learn the system; risks of breakdown and data loss; savings are estimates. Decision: introduce the system, as payback is short compared with the system's likely life, but plan backups and training.
    1 mark: financial point using figures. 1 mark: non-financial benefit. 1 mark: drawback/risk. 1 mark: justified decision.
Q24Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Kaghan Supplies gives every customer a four-digit account number followed by a check digit. The check digit is calculated as follows: multiply the four digits by the weights 5, 4, 3 and 2 in order; add the results; divide by 11; the check digit is 11 minus the remainder. When an account number is entered, the computer multiplies the five digits by 5, 4, 3, 2 and 1 and accepts the number only if the total is exactly divisible by 11.

  1. (a) Calculate the check digit for account number 3725. [3]
  2. (b) A clerk keys in 37527 instead of 37257. Show that the computer will reject it. [2]
  3. (c) State the type of input error this check is designed to detect. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7 (full number 37257)
    3×5 + 7×4 + 2×3 + 5×2 = 59 (M1); 59 ÷ 11 remainder 4 (M1); 11 − 4 = 7 (A1).
  2. (b) Weighted total = 69, which is not divisible by 11, so the number is rejected.
    3×5 + 7×4 + 5×3 + 2×2 + 7×1 = 69 (M1); 69 ÷ 11 leaves remainder 3, so rejected (A1).
  3. (c) Transposition errors (digits swapped) and single-digit keying errors.
    B1.
Q25Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Margalla Engineering Ltd is replacing its old accounting software with a new system. The finance director must choose between direct changeover (switching completely on one day) and parallel running (running both systems together for three months).

  1. (a) Explain the term 'parallel running'. [1]
  2. (b) Advise the finance director which method to use. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Operating the old and new systems at the same time, with the same data, so their results can be compared before the old system is dropped.
    B1.
  2. (b) Parallel running: results from the new system can be checked against the old, and if the new system fails the old one is still available, so the risk of losing accounting records is low; but staff must enter everything twice, which is costly, slow and may cause mistakes. Direct changeover: cheap and quick, and staff concentrate on one system; but if the new system fails there is no fallback and records, payments to suppliers or payroll could be disrupted. Decision: for a company's main accounting records, use parallel running (perhaps for one month-end close only) because the cost of failure is greater than the extra staff time.
    Up to 2 marks for points on parallel running; up to 2 marks for points on direct changeover; 1 mark for applying to the company (size, importance of records, staff time); 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q26Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Bolan Distributors issues 2,400 sales invoices a month. Preparing an invoice by hand takes 6 minutes; with the proposed system it would take 1.5 minutes. Invoicing clerks are paid $9 an hour. The new system would cost $12,000 a year to rent and maintain.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of clerk hours saved each month. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the annual saving in clerks' wages. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the net annual saving after the cost of the system. [1]
  4. (d) Discuss non-financial factors the directors should consider before introducing the system. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 180 hours
    (6 − 1.5) × 2,400 = 10,800 minutes (M1); ÷ 60 = 180 hours (A1).
  2. (b) $19,440
    180 × 9 × 12 = 19,440 (B1).
  3. (c) $7,440
    19,440 − 12,000 = 7,440 (B1).
  4. (d) Staff may resist the change or fear job losses, affecting morale; staff need training and there may be early errors; faster, more accurate invoices may improve customer service and cash collection; dependence on the system creates risks of breakdown and data loss.
    1 mark each for up to three relevant factors, developed (staff morale/redundancy; training; customer service; security/reliability).
Q27Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

The accounting software of Chenab Wholesalers produced this aged trade receivables report at 31 March 2026:Age of debt $0–30 days 45,20031–60 days 21,00061–90 days 10,500Over 90 days 7,300Total 84,000The business creates a provision for doubtful debts of 2% on debts up to 30 days, 5% on 31–60 days, 10% on 61–90 days and 40% on debts over 90 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of trade receivables that are more than 60 days old. Give your answer to two decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the provision for doubtful debts at 31 March 2026. [3]
  3. (c) Explain how the computerised aged receivables report helps credit control. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 21.19%
    (10,500 + 7,300) ÷ 84,000 × 100 (M1) = 21.19% (A1).
  2. (b) $5,924
    904 + 1,050 (M1) + 1,050 + 2,920 (M1) = 5,924 (A1).
  3. (c) It is produced instantly and accurately at any time, so the credit controller can quickly see which customers are overdue and by how long, and chase them or stop further credit before debts become irrecoverable.
    1 mark: quick/accurate/up to date. 1 mark: identifies overdue customers for action.
Q28Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

At Khyber Steel Ltd one purchases clerk can set up new suppliers in the computerised purchase ledger, record invoices and authorise payments. Last year the clerk created a false supplier account and paid $18,000 into his own bank account before the fraud was discovered.

  1. (a) Explain two weaknesses in the control system that allowed the fraud. [2]
  2. (b) Recommend controls to prevent such a fraud happening again. Justify your recommendations. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) There was no segregation of duties: one person could create suppliers, record invoices and approve payments. Access levels were too wide and no one else reviewed new supplier accounts or payments.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Separate duties so that different staff create suppliers, record invoices and authorise payments, so fraud needs collusion. Set access levels so the clerk cannot add suppliers; new suppliers approved by a manager with checks on bank details. Review the audit trail and exception reports of new suppliers and payments regularly. Require two authorisations for payments above a set limit.
    Up to 2 marks per developed recommendation (control + why it works), max 4.
Q29Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Sadia & Co. Ltd introduced a computerised accounting system last year. A director says: 'Now that the software does all the double entry, we no longer need to prepare bank reconciliations or check control accounts.'

  1. (a) Evaluate the director's statement. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Partly true: the software keeps debits equal to credits, posts to ledgers and control accounts automatically and removes arithmetic errors, so some manual checks are no longer needed. However, the bank reconciliation compares the business's records with an independent source (the bank statement); it still finds unpresented cheques, bank charges, direct debits not entered, keying errors and fraud. Errors of commission, omission and original entry are not detected by the software. Control accounts and reconciliations are also internal controls that auditors expect. Conclusion: the statement is not justified; reconciliations should continue, although the software makes them quicker.
    Up to 2 marks for points supporting the statement; up to 3 marks for points against (independent check, errors not detected, fraud, audit expectations); 1 mark for a justified conclusion.
Q30Computerised systems and data security · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Rehman Electricals, a small business in Multan, is thinking of moving from accounting software on one office computer to a cloud-based accounting package paid for by monthly subscription.

  1. (a) Discuss whether the business should move to cloud-based accounting. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: data can be reached from anywhere, e.g. by the owner at home or the accountant; the provider makes automatic backups and updates; no large up-front cost; can link to online banking for automatic bank feeds. Against: depends on a reliable internet connection, which may be a problem in some areas; monthly fees continue for ever; data held by a third party raises security and confidentiality concerns; staff need retraining. Conclusion: move to the cloud if the internet is reliable and the provider has strong security, because backup and access benefits outweigh the subscription cost.
    Up to 3 marks for advantages and up to 2 marks for disadvantages applied to the business; 1 mark for a justified conclusion.
