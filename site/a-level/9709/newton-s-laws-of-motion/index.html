Q1Connected particles (pulley) · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Particles A and B, of masses 5 kg and 3 kg, are attached to the ends of a light inextensible string which passes over a fixed smooth pulley. The particles hang vertically and are released from rest.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 2.5 m s⁻², T = 37.5 N
    A: 50 − T = 5a; B: T − 30 = 3a (M1 A1). Adding: 20 = 8a, a = 2.5 m s⁻² (A1). T = 30 + 7.5 = 37.5 N (A1).
Q2Connected particles (pulley) · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Particles P and Q, of masses 0.4 kg and 0.6 kg, are connected by a light inextensible string passing over a fixed smooth pulley. Q is held 1.2 m above the floor with both parts of the string vertical and taut. Q is released from rest.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of Q and the tension in the string. [4]
  2. (b) Find the speed with which Q reaches the floor. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 2 m s⁻², T = 4.8 N
    Q: 6 − T = 0.6a; P: T − 4 = 0.4a (M1 A1). a = 2 m s⁻² (A1), T = 4.8 N (A1).
  2. (b) 2.19 m s⁻¹
    v² = 0 + 2 × 2 × 1.2 = 4.8 (M1). v = 2.19 m s⁻¹ (A1).
Q3Connected particles (pulley) · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block P of mass 2 kg rests on a smooth horizontal table. It is attached to a light inextensible string which passes over a small smooth pulley at the edge of the table. The other end of the string carries a particle Q of mass 0.5 kg, which hangs freely. The system is released from rest.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 2 m s⁻², T = 4 N
    P: T = 2a; Q: 5 − T = 0.5a (M1 A1). 5 = 2.5a, a = 2 m s⁻² (A1). T = 4 N (A1).
Q4Connected particles (pulley) · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Two particles, of masses 0.8 kg and 1.2 kg, are connected by a light inextensible string passing over a fixed smooth pulley, and released from rest with the string taut and both parts vertical.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string. [4]
  2. (b) Find the force exerted on the pulley by the string. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 2 m s⁻², T = 9.6 N
    12 − T = 1.2a, T − 8 = 0.8a (M1 A1). a = 4/2 = 2 m s⁻² (A1). T = 0.8 × 12 = 9.6 N (A1).
  2. (b) 19.2 N vertically downwards
    2T = 19.2 N (B1).
Q5Connected particles (pulley) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block of mass 3 kg lies on a rough horizontal table, where the coefficient of friction is 0.2. It is connected by a light inextensible string over a smooth pulley at the edge of the table to a particle of mass 2 kg hanging freely. The system is released from rest.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. [5]
  2. (b) Find the time taken for the block to move 0.7 m, assuming it does not reach the pulley. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 2.8 m s⁻², T = 14.4 N
    R = 30, F = 0.2 × 30 = 6 N (B1). Block: T − 6 = 3a; particle: 20 − T = 2a (M1 A1). a = 14/5 = 2.8 m s⁻² (A1). T = 2(10 − 2.8) = 14.4 N (A1).
  2. (b) 0.707 s
    0.7 = ½ × 2.8 × t² (M1). t = √0.5 = 0.707 s (A1).
Q6Connected particles (pulley) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Particles A and B, of masses 2 kg and 3 kg, are attached to the ends of a light inextensible string passing over a fixed smooth pulley. B is 1.5 m above the ground and A is on the ground, with the string taut. The system is released from rest. When B hits the ground it does not rebound. A does not reach the pulley.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the particles before B hits the ground. [3]
  2. (b) Find the speed of A when B hits the ground. [2]
  3. (c) Find the greatest height of A above the ground. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2 m s⁻²
    B: 30 − T = 3a; A: T − 20 = 2a (M1 A1). a = 10/5 = 2 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) √6 = 2.45 m s⁻¹
    v² = 2 × 2 × 1.5 = 6 (M1). v = 2.45 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 1.8 m
    After B lands the string is slack; A moves freely under gravity (M1). Extra height = 6/(2 × 10) = 0.3 m (A1). Greatest height 1.5 + 0.3 = 1.8 m (A1).
Q7Connected particles (pulley) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Two particles, of masses 1.5 kg and m kg, where m > 1.5, are connected by a light inextensible string over a fixed smooth pulley. When the system is released from rest, each particle has acceleration 2.5 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Find the tension in the string. [2]
  2. (b) Find the value of m. [2]
  3. (c) Find the magnitude of the force exerted by the string on the pulley. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18.75 N
    Lighter particle: T − 15 = 1.5 × 2.5 (M1). T = 18.75 N (A1).
  2. (b) m = 2.5
    10m − 18.75 = 2.5m (M1). m = 18.75/7.5 = 2.5 (A1).
  3. (c) 37.5 N
    2T = 37.5 N (B1).
Q8Connected particles (pulley) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle P of mass 4 kg lies on a smooth plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal. It is attached to a light inextensible string which runs parallel to a line of greatest slope, passes over a smooth pulley at the top of the plane, and carries a particle Q of mass 3 kg hanging vertically. The system is released from rest.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the particles. [4]
  2. (b) Find the tension in the string. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10/7 = 1.43 m s⁻²
    P: T − 40 sin 30° = 4a; Q: 30 − T = 3a (M1 A1 A1). 30 − 20 = 7a, a = 10/7 = 1.43 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 25.7 N
    T = 3(10 − 10/7) = 180/7 = 25.7 N (M1 A1).
Q9Connected particles (pulley) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block A of mass 5 kg is on a rough plane inclined at angle α to the horizontal, where tan α = 3/4. The coefficient of friction between A and the plane is 0.25. A is attached to a light inextensible string, parallel to a line of greatest slope, which passes over a smooth pulley at the top of the plane to a particle B of mass 6 kg hanging freely. The system is released from rest and B moves downwards.

  1. (a) Find the friction force acting on A. [2]
  2. (b) Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10 N
    R = 50 cos α = 50 × 0.8 = 40 N (M1). F = 0.25 × 40 = 10 N (A1).
  2. (b) a = 20/11 = 1.82 m s⁻², T = 49.1 N
    A: T − 50 × 0.6 − 10 = 5a; B: 60 − T = 6a (M1 A1). 20 = 11a, a = 1.82 m s⁻² (A1). T = 6(10 − 20/11) = 49.1 N (A1).
Q10Connected particles (pulley) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block of mass 4 kg rests on a rough horizontal table. It is connected by a light inextensible string over a smooth pulley at the edge of the table to a particle of mass 1 kg hanging freely. When the system is released from rest, the hanging particle descends 0.5 m in the first second.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the system. [2]
  2. (b) Find the tension in the string and the coefficient of friction between the block and the table. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 m s⁻²
    0.5 = ½a(1)² (M1), a = 1 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) T = 9 N, μ = 0.125
    Particle: 10 − T = 1 × 1, T = 9 N (M1 A1). Block: 9 − μ × 40 = 4 × 1 (M1), μ = 5/40 = 0.125 (A1).
Q11Connected particles (pulley) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block of mass 8 kg is on a rough plane inclined at angle θ to the horizontal, where sin θ = 0.28 and cos θ = 0.96. The coefficient of friction is 0.3. A light inextensible string attached to the block runs up the plane, parallel to a line of greatest slope, over a smooth pulley at the top, to a particle of mass m kg hanging freely.

  1. (a) Find the value of m for which the block is on the point of moving up the plane. [4]
  2. (b) Given that m = 6, find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) m = 4.544 (the block moves up the plane for any m greater than this)
    Limiting: mg = 8g sin θ + μ × 8g cos θ (M1 A1). m = 8 × 0.28 + 0.3 × 8 × 0.96 = 2.24 + 2.304 (M1) = 4.544 (A1).
  2. (b) a = 1.04 m s⁻², T = 53.8 N
    Block: T − 22.4 − 23.04 = 8a; particle: 60 − T = 6a (M1 A1). 14a = 14.56, a = 1.04 m s⁻² (A1). T = 6(10 − 1.04) = 53.76 N ≈ 53.8 N (A1).
Q12Connected particles (pulley) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block P of mass 2 kg is on a rough horizontal table, with coefficient of friction 0.4. A light inextensible string attached to P passes over a smooth pulley at the edge of the table and carries a particle Q of mass 3 kg, which hangs 0.9 m above the floor. The system is released from rest. Q does not rebound when it hits the floor, and P does not reach the pulley.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the system while Q is moving. [3]
  2. (b) Find the speed of P when Q hits the floor. [2]
  3. (c) Find the total distance travelled by P before it comes to rest. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.4 m s⁻²
    F = 0.4 × 20 = 8 N (B1). 30 − 8 = 5a (M1), a = 4.4 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 2.81 m s⁻¹
    v² = 2 × 4.4 × 0.9 = 7.92 (M1), v = 2.81 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 1.89 m
    After Q lands, P decelerates at μg = 4 m s⁻² (M1). Distance = 7.92/8 = 0.99 m (A1). Total 0.9 + 0.99 = 1.89 m (A1).
Q13Connected particles (pulley) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Particles A and B, of masses 3 kg and m kg (m > 3), are connected by a light inextensible string over a fixed smooth pulley and released from rest. After 1.5 s the particles are moving with speed 3 m s⁻¹. At this instant the string breaks. Neither particle hits the pulley or the ground in the motion described.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the particles before the string breaks, and hence find m. [4]
  2. (b) Find the time from the instant the string breaks until A returns to its starting position. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 2 m s⁻², m = 4.5
    a = 3/1.5 = 2 m s⁻² (B1). B: 10m − T = 2m; A: T − 30 = 6 (M1). T = 36, 8m = 36 (A1), m = 4.5 (A1).
  2. (b) 1.03 s
    A rose ½ × 2 × 1.5² = 2.25 m (B1). After the break, taking up as positive: −2.25 = 3t − 5t² (M1). 5t² − 3t − 2.25 = 0 (A1). t = (3 + √54)/10 = 1.03 s (A1).

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the car and trailer. [2]
  2. (b) Find the tension in the tow-bar. [2]

    2400 − 400 = 1600a (M1), a = 1.25 m s⁻² (A1).

Q15Connected particles (slope/towing) · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

On the Karachi–Hyderabad motorway, a truck of mass 3000 kg tows a broken-down car of mass 1000 kg with a light horizontal rope. The truck's driving force is 5000 N. The resistances are 800 N on the truck and 200 N on the car.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration. [2]
  2. (b) Find the tension in the rope. [2]
  3. (c) Find the time taken to increase speed from 8 m s⁻¹ to 20 m s⁻¹. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 m s⁻²
    5000 − 1000 = 4000a (M1), a = 1 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 1200 N
    Car: T − 200 = 1000 × 1 (M1), T = 1200 N (A1).
  3. (c) 12 s
    t = (20 − 8)/1 = 12 s (B1).
Q16Connected particles (slope/towing) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A car of mass 1400 kg tows a caravan of mass 600 kg along a horizontal road using a light rigid tow-bar. They are travelling at 20 m s⁻¹ when the car's brakes are applied, and they come to rest in 50 m with constant deceleration. The resistances to motion (not including braking) are 300 N on the car and 200 N on the caravan. The braking force acts on the car only.

  1. (a) Find the deceleration. [2]
  2. (b) Find the braking force. [2]
  3. (c) Find the force in the tow-bar, stating whether it is a tension or a thrust. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4 m s⁻²
    0 = 20² − 2 × a × 50 (M1), a = 4 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 7500 N
    Whole system: B + 300 + 200 = 2000 × 4 (M1), B = 7500 N (A1).
  3. (c) Thrust of 2200 N
    Caravan decelerates at 4 m s⁻², needs 2400 N backwards (M1). Resistance gives 200 N, so tow-bar pushes back with 2200 N (A1): a thrust (A1).
Q17Connected particles (slope/towing) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A car of mass 1000 kg tows a trailer of mass 250 kg up a straight hill inclined at angle α to the horizontal, where sin α = 0.05, using a light rigid tow-bar parallel to the road. The driving force is 2000 N. Resistances to motion are 200 N on the car and 80 N on the trailer.

Q18Connected particles (slope/towing) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Blocks A and B, of masses 3 kg and 2 kg, are on a rough plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal. They are connected by a light inextensible string parallel to a line of greatest slope, with B below A. A force of 50 N, acting up the plane parallel to a line of greatest slope, is applied to A. The coefficient of friction between each block and the plane is 0.2.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the blocks. [4]
  2. (b) Show that the tension in the string is 20 N. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.27 m s⁻²
    Friction: 0.2 × 30 cos 30° = 5.196 N on A, 0.2 × 20 cos 30° = 3.464 N on B (B1). System: 50 − 25 − 8.660 = 5a (M1 A1). a = 3.27 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) T = 20 N
    B: T − 20 sin 30° − 3.464 = 2a (M1). T = 10 + 3.464 + 2 × 3.2679 = 20 N (A1). (Because both blocks have the same μ and slope, T = 50 × 2/5 exactly.)
Q19Connected particles (slope/towing) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

A car of mass 900 kg tows a trailer of mass 300 kg along a straight horizontal road using a light rigid tow-bar. The car's driving force is 1800 N; the resistances are 150 N on the car and 90 N on the trailer, and these stay constant.

  1. (a) Find the acceleration and the tension in the tow-bar. [4]
  2. (b) When the speed is 15 m s⁻¹ the tow-bar breaks. Find the distance the trailer travels after this before coming to rest. [3]
  3. (c) Find the new acceleration of the car, assuming the driving force does not change. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 1.3 m s⁻², T = 480 N
    1800 − 240 = 1200a (M1), a = 1.3 m s⁻² (A1). Trailer: T − 90 = 300 × 1.3 (M1), T = 480 N (A1).
  2. (b) 375 m
    Trailer deceleration = 90/300 = 0.3 m s⁻² (M1 A1). 0 = 15² − 2 × 0.3 × s, s = 375 m (A1).
  3. (c) 1.83 m s⁻²
    (1800 − 150)/900 = 1.83 m s⁻² (B1).
Q20Connected particles (slope/towing) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A fixed wedge has two faces: face 1 is smooth and inclined at 30° to the horizontal, and face 2 is rough and inclined at 60° to the horizontal. The faces meet at a ridge where there is a small smooth pulley. Particle A, of mass 4 kg, is on face 1 and particle B, of mass 3 kg, is on face 2. They are connected by a light inextensible string over the pulley, each part parallel to a line of greatest slope. The coefficient of friction between B and face 2 is 0.2. The system is released from rest.

  1. (a) Show that B moves down face 2. [2]
  2. (b) Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Weight component of B down face 2 = 25.98 N > 20 N + friction 3 N
    B: 30 sin 60° = 25.98 N; A: 40 sin 30° = 20 N (M1). Even with maximum friction 0.2 × 30 cos 60° = 3 N opposing, 25.98 > 23, so B moves down (A1).
  2. (b) a = 0.426 m s⁻², T = 21.7 N
    B: 25.98 − 3 − T = 3a (M1 A1); A: T − 20 = 4a (A1). 7a = 2.981, a = 0.426 m s⁻² (A1). T = 20 + 4a = 21.7 N (A1).

Q22Newton's 2nd law on a slope · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle of mass 0.5 kg slides down a rough plane inclined at angle α to the horizontal, where tan α = 3/4. The coefficient of friction is 0.5. The particle starts from rest.

  1. (a) Find the normal reaction and the friction force. [2]
  2. (b) Find the acceleration and the distance travelled in the first 3 s. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = 4 N, F = 2 N
    R = 5 cos α = 5 × 0.8 = 4 N (B1). F = 0.5 × 4 = 2 N (B1).
  2. (b) a = 2 m s⁻², distance 9 m
    0.5a = 5 × 0.6 − 2 = 1 (M1), a = 2 m s⁻² (A1). s = ½ × 2 × 9 = 9 m (A1).
Q23Newton's 2nd law on a slope · Standard · Paper 4 style · 9 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle is projected up a line of greatest slope of a rough plane inclined at 25° to the horizontal, with initial speed 8 m s⁻¹. The coefficient of friction between the particle and the plane is 0.3.

  1. (a) Find the deceleration of the particle while it moves up the plane. [3]
  2. (b) Find the distance it travels up the plane before coming to instantaneous rest. [2]
  3. (c) Show that the particle then slides back down, and find its speed when it returns to its starting point. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.95 m s⁻²
    Friction acts down the plane: a = g sin 25° + 0.3g cos 25° (M1 A1) = 6.95 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 4.61 m
    0 = 64 − 2 × 6.9451 × s (M1), s = 4.61 m (A1).
  3. (c) tan 25° = 0.466 > 0.3, so it slides; speed 3.73 m s⁻¹
    tan 25° > μ, so the weight component exceeds limiting friction (B1). Down: a = 10(sin 25° − 0.3 cos 25°) = 1.5073 (M1 A1). v = √(2 × 1.5073 × 4.6076) = 3.73 m s⁻¹ (A1).

  1. (a) Find the acceleration of the block. [2]
  2. (b) Find the coefficient of friction between the block and the plane. [4]
  3. (c) State one assumption made in your model. [1]
Show answer

  2. (b) μ = 0.334

  1. (a) Find the normal reaction and the acceleration of the particle. [5]
  2. (b) When the particle's speed is 5 m s⁻¹ the horizontal force is removed. Find how much further the particle travels up the plane. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = 64.6 N, a = 3.95 m s⁻²
    R = 40 cos 30° + 60 sin 30° = 64.6 N (M1 A1). F = 0.25R = 16.16 N (B1). 4a = 60 cos 30° − 40 sin 30° − F (M1), a = 3.95 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 1.74 m
    Deceleration = 10(sin 30° + 0.25 cos 30°) = 7.165 m s⁻² (M1 A1). s = 25/(2 × 7.165) = 1.74 m (A1).

Take g = 10 m s⁻². An ice-hockey puck slides in a straight line on a horizontal rink. Its speed falls from 12 m s⁻¹ to 4 m s⁻¹ while it travels 40 m. The only horizontal force is friction.

  2. (b) Find the coefficient of friction. [2]
  3. (c) Find the time taken for this part of the motion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.6 m s⁻²

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A box of mass 20 kg is on a rough horizontal floor, with coefficient of friction 0.4. A force of magnitude P N is applied to the box, directed downwards at 30° below the horizontal.

  1. (a) Given that the box moves at constant speed, find P, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [4]
  2. (b) Find the acceleration of the box when P = 150. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = 120
    R = 200 + P sin 30° (B1). P cos 30° = 0.4(200 + 0.5P) (M1 A1). P = 80/(cos 30° − 0.2) = 120.1… = 120 (3 s.f.) (A1).
