Q1Integration of trig functions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find ∫ (3 cos 2x − 4 sin(½x)) dx. [3]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫₀^{π/3} (3 cos 2x − 4 sin(½x)) dx. Give your answer in the form a√3 + b. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (3/2) sin 2x + 8 cos(½x) + c
    B1 (3/2) sin 2x; B1 +8 cos(½x) (coefficient 8 and sign); B1 + c.
  2. (b) 19√3/4 − 8 (≈ 0.227)
    M1 substitute both limits into the result of (a); sin(2π/3) = √3/2, cos(π/6) = √3/2, cos 0 = 1; (3√3/4 + 4√3) − 8 = 19√3/4 − 8 A1.
Q2Integration of trig functions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Use the identity sec²x ≡ 1 + tan²x to express 2 tan²x + 1 in terms of sec²x. [1]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫₀^{π/4} (2 tan²x + 1) dx. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2 sec²x − 1
    B1 2(sec²x − 1) + 1 = 2 sec²x − 1.
  2. (b) 2 − π/4
    M1 integrate to 2 tan x − x; M1 apply limits; A1 2(1) − π/4 = 2 − π/4.
Q3Integration of trig functions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Express cos²3x in terms of cos 6x. [1]
  2. (b) Hence show that ∫₀^{π/12} cos²3x dx = π/24 + 1/12. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) cos²3x = ½(1 + cos 6x)
    B1 from cos 2A = 2cos²A − 1 with A = 3x.
  2. (b) π/24 + 1/12 (≈ 0.214)
    M1 integrate ½ + ½cos 6x; A1 x/2 + (1/12) sin 6x; M1 limits, sin(π/2) = 1; A1 π/24 + 1/12 shown.
Q4Integration of trig functions · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) By expanding sin(3x + x) and sin(3x − x), show that sin 3x cos x ≡ ½(sin 4x + sin 2x). [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫₀^{π/6} sin 3x cos x dx. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 sin(3x+x) + sin(3x−x) = 2 sin 3x cos x; A1 rearrange to the given result.
  2. (b) 5/16
    M1 integrate to ½(−¼ cos 4x − ½ cos 2x); A1 correct; M1 limits, cos(2π/3) = −½, cos(π/3) = ½; A1 ½[(1/8 − 1/4) − (−3/4)] = ½ × 5/8 = 5/16.
Q5Integration of trig functions · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Show that 1/(1 + cos 2x) ≡ ½ sec²x. [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫_{π/6}^{π/3} (1 + 2/(1 + cos 2x)) dx, giving your answer in the form aπ + b√3. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 use 1 + cos 2x = 2cos²x; A1 1/(2cos²x) = ½ sec²x.
  2. (b) π/6 + 2√3/3 (≈ 1.678)
    M1 integrand = 1 + sec²x; A1 x + tan x; M1 limits: (π/3 + √3) − (π/6 + 1/√3); A1 π/6 + 2√3/3.
Q6Curve from gradient · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A curve has gradient function dy/dx = 6x² − 4x + 1 and passes through the point (2, 9). Find the equation of the curve. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 2x³ − 2x² + x − 1
    M1 integrate (power of each term increased by 1); A1 2x³ − 2x² + x + c; M1 substitute (2, 9): 16 − 8 + 2 + c = 9; A1 c = −1.
Q7Curve from gradient · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) A curve passes through the point (4, 5) and its gradient is given by dy/dx = 3/√(2x + 1). Find the equation of the curve. [4]
  2. (b) Find the x-coordinate of the point where the curve crosses the x-axis. [2]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point (4, 5). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 3√(2x + 1) − 4
    M1 integrate (2x+1)^(−½) to (2x+1)^(½) ÷ (½ × 2); A1 3(2x+1)^(½) + c; M1 substitute (4, 5): 9 + c = 5; A1 c = −4.
  2. (b) x = 7/18
    M1 3√(2x+1) = 4 so 2x + 1 = 16/9; A1 x = 7/18.
  3. (c) y = x + 1
    M1 gradient = 3/√9 = 1; A1 y − 5 = 1(x − 4), y = x + 1.
Q8Curve from gradient · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) A curve has d²y/dx² = 12x − 6. The curve has a stationary point at (1, 2). Find dy/dx and hence find the equation of the curve. [5]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the other stationary point and determine the nature of each stationary point. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = 6x² − 6x; y = 2x³ − 3x² + 3
    M1 integrate: dy/dx = 6x² − 6x + c; A1 dy/dx = 0 at x = 1 gives c = 0; M1 integrate again: y = 2x³ − 3x² + k; M1 substitute (1, 2); A1 k = 3.
  2. (b) (0, 3) is a maximum; (1, 2) is a minimum
    B1 6x² − 6x = 0 gives x = 0, y = 3; M1 d²y/dx² at x = 0 is −6 < 0, maximum; A1 at x = 1, d²y/dx² = 6 > 0, minimum.
Q9Integration by parts · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the exact value of ∫₀¹ x e^{2x} dx. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) ¼(e² + 1) (≈ 2.097)
    M1 parts with u = x, dv/dx = e^{2x}; A1 ½x e^{2x} − ¼e^{2x}; M1 apply limits; A1 (½e² − ¼e²) − (−¼) = ¼(e² + 1).
Q10Integration by parts · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Show that ∫₁^e x² ln x dx = (2e³ + 1)/9. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2e³ + 1)/9 (≈ 4.575)
    M1 parts with u = ln x, dv/dx = x²; A1 (x³/3) ln x − ∫ x²/3 dx; A1 (x³/3) ln x − x³/9; M1 limits, ln e = 1, ln 1 = 0; A1 e³/3 − e³/9 + 1/9 = (2e³ + 1)/9.
Q11Integration by parts · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Use integration by parts twice to find the exact value of ∫₀^{π/2} x² cos x dx. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) π²/4 − 2 (≈ 0.467)
    M1 first parts: x² sin x − ∫ 2x sin x dx; A1; M1 second parts: ∫ 2x sin x dx = −2x cos x + 2 sin x; A1 x² sin x + 2x cos x − 2 sin x; M1 limits; A1 π²/4 − 2.
Q12Integration by substitution · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Use the substitution u = 2x − 1 to find the exact value of ∫₁⁵ x/√(2x − 1) dx. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16/3
    B1 x = (u+1)/2, dx = ½ du, limits u = 1 to 9; M1 integrand becomes ¼(u^{½} + u^{−½}); A1 ¼[(2/3)u^{3/2} + 2u^{½}]; M1 limits; A1 ¼(24 − 8/3) = 16/3.
Q13Integration by substitution · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Use the substitution u = 1 + x² to show that ∫₀^{√3} x³/√(1 + x²) dx = 4/3. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4/3
    B1 du = 2x dx and x² = u − 1; B1 limits 1 and 4; M1 integrand ½(u − 1)u^{−½} = ½(u^{½} − u^{−½}); A1 ½[(2/3)u^{3/2} − 2u^{½}]; M1 limits; A1 ½(4/3 + 4/3) = 4/3.
Q14Integration by substitution · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Use the substitution x = 2 sin θ to find the exact value of ∫₀¹ 1/(4 − x²)^{3/2} dx. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) √3/12 (≈ 0.144)
    B1 dx = 2cos θ dθ; M1 (4 − 4sin²θ)^{3/2} = 8cos³θ; A1 integrand ¼ sec²θ; B1 limits 0 and π/6; M1 integrate to ¼ tan θ; A1 ¼ × 1/√3 = √3/12.
Q15Area between curves · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find the x-coordinates of the points where the line y = x + 3 meets the curve y = x² − 2x + 3. [2]
  2. (b) Find the area of the region enclosed between the line and the curve. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 0 and x = 3
    M1 x² − 3x = 0; A1 x = 0, 3.
  2. (b) 9/2
    M1 ∫₀³ ((x + 3) − (x² − 2x + 3)) dx; A1 integrand 3x − x²; M1 [3x²/2 − x³/3]₀³; A1 27/2 − 9 = 9/2.
Q16Area between curves · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = 4√x and the line y = x meet at the origin O and at the point A. Find the coordinates of A. [2]
  2. (b) Find the area of the region enclosed by the curve and the line. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A(16, 16)
    M1 4√x = x gives √x = 4; A1 (16, 16).
  2. (b) 128/3 (≈ 42.7)
    M1 ∫₀¹⁶ (4x^{½} − x) dx; A1 (8/3)x^{3/2} − x²/2; M1 limits; A1 512/3 − 128 = 128/3.
Q17Area between curves · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = 4 − x² passes through P(1, 3). Find the equation of the normal to the curve at P. [3]
  2. (b) The normal meets the curve again at Q. Find the x-coordinate of Q. [2]
  3. (c) Find the area of the region enclosed by the curve and the normal. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = ½x + 5/2
    B1 dy/dx = −2x = −2 at P; M1 normal gradient ½; A1 y − 3 = ½(x − 1).
  2. (b) x = −3/2
    M1 4 − x² = ½x + 5/2 gives 2x² + x − 3 = 0; A1 (2x + 3)(x − 1) = 0, x = −3/2.
  3. (c) 125/48 (≈ 2.60)
    M1 ∫_{−3/2}^{1} (3/2 − x/2 − x²) dx; A1 [3x/2 − x²/4 − x³/3]; A1 11/12 − (−27/16) = 125/48.
Q18Integration (ln forms) · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Find the exact value of ∫₀¹ (4x + 2)/(x² + x + 1) dx, giving your answer as a single logarithm. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2 ln 3 = ln 9
    M1 recognise 4x + 2 = 2 × d/dx(x² + x + 1); A1 2 ln(x² + x + 1); A1 2 ln 3 − 2 ln 1 = ln 9.
Q19Integration (ln forms) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Show that (2x + 5)/(x + 1) can be written as 2 + 3/(x + 1). [1]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫₀² (2x + 5)/(x + 1) dx. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    B1 2(x + 1) + 3 = 2x + 5.
  2. (b) 4 + 3 ln 3 (≈ 7.30)
    M1 integrate to 2x + 3 ln(x + 1); A1; A1 (4 + 3 ln 3) − 0 = 4 + 3 ln 3.
Q20Integration of exponentials · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find ∫ (e^{2x} + 3e^{−x}) dx. [2]
  2. (b) Hence show that ∫₀^{ln 2} (e^{2x} + 3e^{−x}) dx = 3. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) ½e^{2x} − 3e^{−x} + c
    B1 ½e^{2x}; B1 −3e^{−x} (+ c).
  2. (b) 3
    M1 substitute ln 2: e^{2 ln 2} = 4, e^{−ln 2} = ½; A1 (2 − 3/2) − (½ − 3); A1 ½ + 5/2 = 3.
Q21Integration of exponentials · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Given that ∫₀^k 4e^{2x} dx = 10, find the exact value of k. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact value of ∫₀¹ (eˣ + 1)² dx. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = ½ ln 6 (≈ 0.896)
    M1 integrate to 2e^{2x}; M1 2e^{2k} − 2 = 10; A1 e^{2k} = 6, k = ½ ln 6.
  2. (b) ½e² + 2e − 3/2 (≈ 7.63)
    M1 expand e^{2x} + 2eˣ + 1; A1 ½e^{2x} + 2eˣ + x; M1 limits; A1 (½e² + 2e + 1) − (½ + 2) = ½e² + 2e − 3/2.
Q22Area / definite integrals · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate ∫₁⁴ (3√x − 2/x²) dx. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25/2 = 12.5
    M1 increase powers: 2x^{3/2} + 2x^{−1}; A1 each term correct (A1 A1 if separate); M1 limits (16 + ½) − (2 + 2); A1 12.5.
Q23Area / definite integrals · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find ∫ 4/(2x + 1)³ dx. [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the value of the improper integral ∫₁^∞ 4/(2x + 1)³ dx, explaining how the upper limit is dealt with. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) −1/(2x + 1)² + c
    M1 (2x + 1)^{−2} ÷ (−2 × 2) form; A1 −(2x + 1)^{−2} + c.
  2. (b) 1/9
    M1 as x → ∞, −1/(2x + 1)² → 0; M1 0 − (−1/9); A1 1/9.
Q24Volume of revolution · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The region bounded by the curve y = √(x + 1), the x-axis and the lines x = 0 and x = 3 is rotated through 360° about the x-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid formed. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15π/2
    M1 V = π∫₀³ (x + 1) dx; A1 π[x²/2 + x]; M1 limits; A1 π(9/2 + 3) = 15π/2.
Q25Volume of revolution · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The line y = 5 meets the curve y = x² + 1 at the points A and B. Find the x-coordinates of A and B. [1]
  2. (b) The region enclosed between the curve and the line is rotated through 360° about the x-axis. Find the exact volume of the solid formed. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = −2 and x = 2
    B1 x² = 4.
  2. (b) 1088π/15 (≈ 228)
    M1 V = π∫_{−2}^{2} (5² − (x² + 1)²) dx; M1 expand (x² + 1)² = x⁴ + 2x² + 1; A1 integrand 24 − 2x² − x⁴; A1 [24x − 2x³/3 − x⁵/5]; M1 limits (or 2 × from 0 to 2); A1 2π(48 − 16/3 − 32/5) = 1088π/15.
Q26Area under curve · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = x³ − 6x² + 8x crosses the x-axis at O, A and B. Find the x-coordinates of A and B. [2]
  2. (b) Find the total area of the regions enclosed between the curve and the x-axis from x = 0 to x = 4. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 2 and x = 4
    M1 x(x − 2)(x − 4) = 0; A1 2 and 4.
  2. (b) 8
    M1 integrate to x⁴/4 − 2x³ + 4x²; A1 ∫₀² = 4; A1 ∫₂⁴ = −4 (region below the axis); A1 total area 4 + 4 = 8.
Q27Area under curve · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find the area of the region bounded by the curve y = 9/(x + 1)², the x-axis and the lines x = 0 and x = 2. [3]
  2. (b) The line x = k divides this region into two parts of equal area. Find k. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6
    M1 integrate to −9/(x + 1); M1 limits −3 − (−9); A1 6.
  2. (b) k = ½
    M1 9 − 9/(k + 1) = 3 (half of 6); A1 9/(k + 1) = 6; A1 k = ½.
Q28Integration using partial fractions · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Express 5/((x − 1)(x + 4)) in partial fractions. [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫₂⁴ 5/((x − 1)(x + 4)) dx, giving your answer as a single logarithm. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1/(x − 1) − 1/(x + 4)
    M1 5 = A(x + 4) + B(x − 1); A1 A = 1, B = −1.
  2. (b) ln(9/4)
    M1 ln(x − 1) − ln(x + 4); M1 limits (ln 3 − ln 8) − (ln 1 − ln 6); A1 ln(18/8) = ln(9/4).
Q29Integration using partial fractions · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) Express (2x² + 3)/(x²(x + 1)) in partial fractions. [4]
  2. (b) Hence show that ∫₁² (2x² + 3)/(x²(x + 1)) dx = 3/2 + 5 ln 3 − 8 ln 2. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) −3/x + 3/x² + 5/(x + 1)
    B1 form A/x + B/x² + C/(x + 1); M1 2x² + 3 = Ax(x + 1) + B(x + 1) + Cx²; A1 B = 3, C = 5; A1 A = −3.
  2. (b) 3/2 + 5 ln 3 − 8 ln 2 (≈ 1.448)
    M1 integrate: −3 ln x − 3/x + 5 ln(x + 1); A1 (follow through); M1 limits: (−3 ln 2 − 3/2 + 5 ln 3) − (−3 + 5 ln 2); A1 3/2 + 5 ln 3 − 8 ln 2; A1 fully correct log work.
Q30Integration (ln and tan^-1 forms) · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) Hence find the exact value of ∫₀¹ (2x + 3)/(x² + 1) dx. [2]
Show answer

    M1 split into 2x/(x² + 1) + 3/(x² + 1); A1 ln(x² + 1); A1 3 tan⁻¹x + c.
  2. (b) ln 2 + 3π/4 (≈ 3.05)
    M1 limits, tan⁻¹1 = π/4; A1 ln 2 + 3π/4.
