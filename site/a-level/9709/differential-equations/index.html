  1. (a) The number of fish, N, in a pond at time t months is modelled by dN/dt = 0.0005N(800 − N). When t = 0, N = 100. Using partial fractions, solve the differential equation to show that N = 800/(1 + 7e^(−0.4t)). [6]

  3. (c) 7.61 months; N approaches 800
    M1 1 + 7e^(−0.4t) = 4/3. A1 t = (ln 21)/0.4 = 7.61. B1 N → 800.

Q56Forming a differential equation · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

A curve passes through (1, 2) and (4, 18). At each point (x, y) on the curve, the gradient is ky/x, for a constant k.

  1. (a) Solve the differential equation dy/dx = ky/x and show that y = 2x^k. [4]
  2. (b) Given also that y = 18 at x = 4, find the exact value of k. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 2x^k
    Separate: dy/y = k dx/x, so ln y = k ln x + C. With y = 2 at x = 1, C = ln 2, so y = 2x^k.
  2. (b) k = log₂3 ≈ 1.585
    2 × 4^k = 18, so 4^k = 9. Then 2^(2k) = 9, so 2k = log₂9 and k = log₂3 ≈ 1.585.
Q57Forming a differential equation · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

A curve passes through (1, 3). At each point (x, y) on the curve, dy/dx = 2y/x.

  1. (a) Show that y = 3x². [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the normal to the curve at the point where x = 1, in the form ax + by + c = 0. [3]
  3. (c) Find where this normal meets the x-axis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 3x²
    dy/dx = 2y/x gives dy/y = 2 dx/x, so y = Ax². At (1, 3), A = 3, so y = 3x².
  2. (b) x + 6y − 19 = 0
    dy/dx = 6x, so dy/dx = 6 at (1, 3). The normal has gradient −1/6: y − 3 = −1/6(x − 1), giving x + 6y − 19 = 0.
  3. (c) (19, 0)
    Put y = 0 in x + 6y − 19 = 0: x = 19.

  2. (b) Find the value of x when v = 1, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]

Q59Forming a differential equation · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 10 marks

A tank initially holds 100 litres of water containing 5 kg of salt. Brine containing 0.2 kg of salt per litre flows in at 3 litres per minute. The well-mixed mixture flows out at 2 litres per minute, so after t minutes the volume is (100 + t) litres. Let S kg be the amount of salt at time t.

  1. (a) Show that dS/dt = 0.6 − 2S/(100 + t). [3]
  2. (b) Show that S = 0.2(100 + t) − 150000/(100 + t)² satisfies the differential equation in part (a) and that S = 5 when t = 0. [5]
  3. (c) Find the amount of salt after 20 minutes, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) dS/dt = 0.6 − 2S/(100 + t)
    Salt in at 3 × 0.2 = 0.6 kg per minute. Salt out at 2 × S/(100 + t) kg per minute, since the volume is 100 + t litres.
  2. (b) Verified: dS/dt = 0.2 + 300000/(100 + t)³, and 0.6 − 2S/(100 + t) = 0.2 + 300000/(100 + t)³, so the equation holds; at t = 0, S = 5.
    Differentiate: dS/dt = 0.2 + 300000/(100 + t)³ (M1, chain rule on the quotient term). Substitute: 0.6 − 2S/(100 + t) = 0.6 − 0.4 + 300000/(100 + t)³ = 0.2 + 300000/(100 + t)³, which matches (A1). At t = 0: S = 20 − 15 = 5 (B1).
  3. (c) 13.6 kg
    S = 0.2 × 120 − 150000/14400 = 24 − 10.42 = 13.58, so 13.6 kg.
Q60Forming a differential equation · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 10 marks

A skydiver falls vertically. Her speed v m/s after t seconds satisfies dv/dt = 9.8 − 0.01v², and she starts from rest.

  1. (a) Show that the terminal velocity is √980 m/s, and find it correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Show that t = (50/√980) ln((√980 + v)/(√980 − v)) for 0 ≤ v < √980. [5]
  3. (c) Find the time taken for the speed to reach 20 m/s, correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 31.3 m/s
    Terminal velocity when dv/dt = 0: 9.8 = 0.01v², so v² = 980 and v = √980 = 31.3 m/s.
  2. (b) t = (50/√980) ln((√980 + v)/(√980 − v))
    dt/dv = 1/(9.8 − 0.01v²) = 100/(980 − v²) = (50/√980)[1/(√980 − v) + 1/(√980 + v)] (partial fractions). Integrating from v = 0 (t = 0) gives t = (50/√980) ln((√980 + v)/(√980 − v)).
  3. (c) 2.42 s
    Substitute v = 20: t = (50/31.305) ln(51.305/11.305) = 1.5972 × 1.5126 = 2.416, so t = 2.42 s (3 s.f.).
