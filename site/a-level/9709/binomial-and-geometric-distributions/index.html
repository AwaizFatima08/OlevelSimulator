Q1Binomial probabilities · Core · Paper 5 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) The random variable X has the distribution B(10, 0.2). Find P(X = 3). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.201
    M1 ¹⁰C₃ × 0.2³ × 0.8⁷; A1 0.201.
Q2Binomial probabilities · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

A packet contains 12 wheat seeds. Each seed germinates independently with probability 0.85.

  1. (a) Find the probability that at least 11 of the seeds germinate. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.443
    M1 binomial term with n = 12, p = 0.85; M1 P(11) + P(12) = 0.3012 + 0.1422; A1 0.443.
Q3Binomial probabilities · Core · Paper 5 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) The number of patients, X, out of 20 at a clinic in Peshawar who have been vaccinated against a disease has the distribution B(20, 0.35). Find the mean and variance of X. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mean 7, variance 4.55
    B1 np = 7; B1 npq = 4.55.
Q4Binomial probabilities · Core · Paper 5 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) A fair six-sided die is thrown 5 times. Find the probability of obtaining exactly two sixes. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.161
    M1 ⁵C₂ (1/6)² (5/6)³; A1 0.161 (= 625/3888).
Q5Binomial probabilities · Core · Paper 5 style · 2 marks

A quiz has 15 multiple-choice questions, each with 4 options, only one of which is correct. A student guesses every answer at random.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the student gets exactly 5 answers correct. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.165
    M1 ¹⁵C₅ (0.25)⁵ (0.75)¹⁰; A1 0.165.
Q6Binomial probabilities · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A box contains 7 mangoes. Each mango is ripe with probability 0.45.

  1. (a) State two conditions needed for the number of ripe mangoes in a box to be modelled by a binomial distribution. [2]
  2. (b) Assuming the model, find the probability that exactly 4 of the 7 mangoes are ripe. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fixed number of mangoes; each ripe independently with the same probability
    B1 independence; B1 constant probability (and fixed n, two outcomes).
  2. (b) 0.239
    M1 ⁷C₄ (0.45)⁴ (0.55)³; A1 0.239.
Q7Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

At a fruit market, 30% of apples in a large delivery are bruised. A shopper picks 8 apples at random.

  1. (a) Find the probability that more than 2 of the 8 apples are bruised. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.448
    M1 1 − [P(0) + P(1) + P(2)]; A1 correct binomial terms with n = 8, p = 0.3; A1 0.448.
Q8Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

In a town, 12% of shop customers pay using a mobile wallet. Customers are chosen at random.

  1. (a) Find the least number of customers that must be surveyed for the probability that at least one of them pays by mobile wallet to be greater than 0.95. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 24
    M1 1 − 0.88ⁿ > 0.95; M1 0.88ⁿ < 0.05, n > ln 0.05/ln 0.88 = 23.4; A1 n = 24.
Q9Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A batsman hits a boundary off any ball with probability 0.15, independently of other balls.

  1. (a) Find the probability that he hits exactly 2 boundaries in an over of 6 balls. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that he hits no boundary in an over. [1]
  3. (c) He faces 5 overs. Find the probability that there is at least one over in which he hits no boundary. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.176
    M1 ⁶C₂ × 0.15² × 0.85⁴; A1 0.176.
  2. (b) 0.377
    B1 0.85⁶ = 0.377.
  3. (c) 0.906
    M1 overs independent, B(5, 0.3771); M1 1 − (1 − 0.3771)⁵; A1 0.906.
Q10Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The random variable X ~ B(n, p) has mean 4.8 and variance 2.88. Find n and p. [3]
  2. (b) Find P(X = 5). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) p = 0.4, n = 12
    M1 np(1 − p)/np = 2.88/4.8; A1 1 − p = 0.6, p = 0.4; A1 n = 12.
  2. (b) 0.227
    M1 ¹²C₅ × 0.4⁵ × 0.6⁷; A1 0.227.
Q11Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A school bus in Islamabad is late on any school day with probability 0.1, independently. A school week has 5 days.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the bus is late on at most one day in a particular week. [3]
  2. (b) Find the probability that this happens in each of 4 consecutive weeks. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.919
    M1 B(5, 0.1); M1 0.9⁵ + 5(0.1)(0.9⁴); A1 0.919.
  2. (b) 0.712
    M1 0.91854⁴; A1 0.712.
Q12Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

It is known that 10% of adults are left-handed. A random sample of 25 adults is chosen.

  1. (a) Find the probability that between 2 and 4 inclusive of the 25 people are left-handed. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.631
    M1 B(25, 0.1); M1 P(2) + P(3) + P(4); A1 0.631.
Q13Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

In a district, 30% of households have solar panels. A random sample of 14 households is taken and X is the number with solar panels.

  1. (a) By calculating P(X = 3), P(X = 4) and P(X = 5), find the most likely number of households with solar panels in the sample. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4 (P = 0.194, 0.229, 0.196)
    M1 binomial terms with n = 14, p = 0.3; A1 P(3) = 0.194; A1 P(4) = 0.229, P(5) = 0.196; A1 most likely value 4.
Q14Binomial probabilities · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

On a certain train route, 25% of passengers travel without a ticket. An inspector checks 16 passengers chosen at random.

  1. (a) Find the expected number of passengers in the sample who travel without a ticket. [1]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the number of passengers without a ticket is greater than the expected number. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4
    B1 16 × 0.25 = 4.
  2. (b) 0.370
    M1 P(X > 4) = 1 − P(X ≤ 4); M1 at least 3 correct terms; A1 0.370.
Q15Binomial probabilities · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

LED bulbs are packed in boxes of 10. Each bulb is faulty with probability 0.05, independently. A box is accepted if it contains at most 1 faulty bulb.

  1. (a) Find the probability that a box is accepted. [3]
  2. (b) A shop receives 6 boxes. Find the probability that exactly 5 of them are accepted. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.914
    M1 P(X ≤ 1) with X ~ B(10, 0.05); A1 0.5987 + 0.3151; A1 0.914.
  2. (b) 0.329
    M1 B(6, 0.9139); M1 6 × 0.9139⁵ × 0.0861; A1 0.329.
Q16Binomial probabilities · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The random variable X ~ B(9, p), where 0 < p < 1. Given that P(X = 1) = P(X = 2), find p. [3]
  2. (b) Find P(X ≤ 2). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) p = 0.2
    M1 9pq⁸ = 36p²q⁷; A1 q = 4p; A1 p = 0.2.
  2. (b) 0.738
    M1 P(0) + P(1) + P(2) with p = 0.2; A1 0.738.
Q17Binomial probabilities · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A charity worker makes 6 phone calls. Each call is answered with probability 0.25, independently of other calls. X is the number of calls answered.

  1. (a) Find the probability that exactly 2 of the 6 calls are answered, given that at least one call is answered. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.361
    M1 P(X = 2) = 0.2966; M1 P(X ≥ 1) = 1 − 0.75⁶ = 0.8220; M1 conditional probability ratio; A1 0.361.
Q18Binomial probabilities · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Sara and Hina play a squash match. The first player to win 3 games wins the match. Sara wins any game with probability 0.6, independently of other games.

  1. (a) Explain why the probability that Sara wins the match is equal to P(X ≥ 3), where X ~ B(5, 0.6), even though the match may finish before 5 games are played. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that Sara wins the match. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Imagine all 5 games played: Sara wins the match exactly when she wins at least 3 of them
    B1 extending to 5 games does not change the winner; B1 so Sara wins iff she wins ≥ 3 of 5.
  2. (b) 0.683
    M1 P(3) + P(4) + P(5); A1 0.3456 + 0.2592 + 0.0778; A1 0.683.

Q21Geometric distribution · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) The random variable X ~ Geo(p) and E(X) = 2.5. Find p. [1]
  2. (b) Find P(X = 3). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.4
    B1 1/p = 2.5.
  2. (b) 0.144

Q22Geometric distribution · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

On a production line, each glass bottle is cracked with probability 0.15, independently. Bottles are inspected one at a time.

  1. (a) Find the probability that more than 6 bottles are inspected before a cracked one is found (that is, the first cracked bottle is the 7th or later). [2]
  2. (b) Find the expected number of bottles inspected up to and including the first cracked one. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.377
    M1 0.85⁶; A1 0.377.
  2. (b) 6.67
    B1 1/0.15 = 6.67.
Q23Geometric distribution · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

Bilal tries to throw a ball into a basket. Each attempt succeeds with probability 0.3, independently.

  1. (a) Find the smallest value of n such that the probability that Bilal succeeds on or before his nth attempt is greater than 0.9. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7
    M1 1 − 0.7ⁿ > 0.9; M1 0.7ⁿ < 0.1, n > 6.46; A1 n = 7.
Q24Geometric distribution · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A hockey player scores from a penalty corner with probability 0.35 on each attempt, independently.

  1. (a) Find the probability that her first goal is scored on her 3rd attempt. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that her second goal is scored on her 5th attempt. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.148
    M1 0.65² × 0.35; A1 0.148.
  2. (b) 0.135
    M1 exactly 1 goal in first 4 attempts: 4 × 0.35 × 0.65³; M1 multiply by 0.35; A1 0.135.
Q25Geometric distribution · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

A spinner lands on red with probability 0.25. It is spun until it lands on red, and X is the number of spins needed.

  1. (a) Find P(3 ≤ X ≤ 6). [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.385
    M1 P(X > 2) − P(X > 6) = 0.75² − 0.75⁶; A1 correct expression; A1 0.385.
Q26Geometric distribution · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A sales agent at a call centre makes calls one after another. Each call results in a sale with probability 0.12, independently.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the first successful call is after the 10th call. [2]
  2. (b) Find the expected number of calls up to and including the first successful call. [1]
  3. (c) Find the probability that the first successful call is the 4th call. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.279
    M1 0.88¹⁰; A1 0.279.
  2. (b) 8.33
    B1 1/0.12.
  3. (c) 0.0818
    M1 0.88³ × 0.12; A1 0.0818.
Q27Geometric distribution · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The random variable X ~ Geo(p). Given that P(X ≤ 2) = 0.51, find p. [3]
  2. (b) Find P(X > 4). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) p = 0.3
    M1 1 − q² = 0.51; A1 q = 0.7; A1 p = 0.3.
  2. (b) 0.240
    M1 0.7⁴; A1 0.2401.
Q28Geometric distribution · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

X ~ Geo(p).

  1. (a) Show that the probability that the first success occurs on an odd-numbered trial is p/(1 − q²), where q = 1 − p. [3]
  2. (b) Evaluate this probability when p = 0.4. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 p + q²p + q⁴p + …; M1 geometric series with ratio q²; A1 sum = p/(1 − q²).
  2. (b) 0.625
    B1 0.4/0.64 = 0.625.
Q29Geometric distribution · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

Ali and Zara take turns to throw a fair die, with Ali throwing first. The first person to throw a six wins.

  1. (a) Find the probability that Ali wins the game. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6/11
    M1 Ali wins on throw 1, 3, 5, …: 1/6 + (5/6)²(1/6) + …; M1 geometric series ratio 25/36; A1 (1/6)/(11/36); A1 6/11.
Q30Geometric distribution · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

An old vending machine accepts each coin inserted with probability 0.3, independently.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the third time the machine accepts a coin is on the 7th coin inserted. [3]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the first acceptance happens on or before the 3rd coin. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0972
    M1 exactly 2 accepted in the first 6: ⁶C₂ × 0.3² × 0.7⁴; M1 × 0.3; A1 0.0972.
  2. (b) 0.657
    M1 1 − 0.7³; A1 0.657.
