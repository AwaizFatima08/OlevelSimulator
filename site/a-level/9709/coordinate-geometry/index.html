Q1Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The point P(5, 3) lies on the circle (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 25. Find the gradient of the radius through P. [1]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P, giving your answer in the form ax + by = c. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4/3
    B1 (3 − (−1)) ÷ (5 − 2) = 4/3.
  2. (b) 3x + 4y = 27
    M1 tangent gradient = −3/4 (negative reciprocal). M1 y − 3 = −¾(x − 5). A1 3x + 4y = 27.
Q2Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The circle C has equation x² + y² − 6x − 4y − 12 = 0. Find the centre and radius of C, and show that the point A(7, 5) lies on C. [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the normal to C at A. [2]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the tangent to C at A. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Centre (3, 2), radius 5; (7 − 3)² + (5 − 2)² = 16 + 9 = 25
    M1 (x − 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25. A1 centre (3, 2), r = 5. B1 substitution of (7, 5) gives 25.
  2. (b) 3x − 4y = 1
    M1 the normal passes through the centre: gradient (5 − 2)/(7 − 3) = 3/4. A1 3x − 4y = 1.
  3. (c) 4x + 3y = 43
    M1 gradient −4/3 through (7, 5). A1 4x + 3y = 43.
Q3Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The normals to a circle at the points A(1, 4) and B(3, −2) have equations y = 2x + 2 and x + 2y = −1 respectively. Find the coordinates of the centre of the circle. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the circle. [2]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at B. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (−1, 0)
    M1 solve simultaneously: x + 2(2x + 2) = −1. A1 x = −1, y = 0.
  2. (b) (x + 1)² + y² = 20
    M1 r² = (1 + 1)² + 4² = 20 (or using B: 16 + 4). A1 (x + 1)² + y² = 20.
  3. (c) y = 2x − 8
    M1 tangent ⟂ normal (gradient −½), so tangent gradient 2. A1 y + 2 = 2(x − 3), y = 2x − 8.
Q4Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The points A(5, 5) and B(7, −1) lie on the circle x² + y² = 50. Find the equations of the tangents to the circle at A and at B. [4]
  2. (b) The tangents meet at T. Find the coordinates of T and show that TA = TB. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) At A: x + y = 10; at B: 7x − y = 50
    M1 gradient OA = 1 so tangent gradient −1. A1 x + y = 10. M1 gradient OB = −1/7 so tangent gradient 7. A1 7x − y = 50.
  2. (b) T(7.5, 2.5); TA = TB = √12.5 = (5√2)/2
    M1 solve simultaneously. A1 T(7.5, 2.5). A1 TA² = 2.5² + 2.5² = 12.5 and TB² = 0.5² + 3.5² = 12.5.
Q5Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The point P(6, 7) lies on the circle (x − 4)² + (y − 3)² = 20. Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P. [3]
  2. (b) The tangent meets the x-axis at Q and the y-axis at R. Find the area of triangle OQR, where O is the origin. [2]
  3. (c) Find the exact length PQ. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x + 2y = 20
    M1 radius gradient (7 − 3)/(6 − 4) = 2. M1 tangent gradient −½. A1 y − 7 = −½(x − 6), x + 2y = 20.
  2. (b) 100
    M1 Q(20, 0), R(0, 10). A1 area = ½ × 20 × 10 = 100.
  3. (c) 7√5
    M1 PQ = √(14² + 7²) = √245. A1 7√5.
Q6Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A circle has equation (x + 1)² + (y − 2)² = 10. Show that the point T(5, 4) lies outside the circle. [2]
  2. (b) A tangent is drawn from T to touch the circle at S. Find the exact length TS. [2]
  3. (c) Find the area of the kite formed by T, the centre C and the two points of contact of the tangents from T. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Distance from centre (−1, 2) to T is √40 > √10
    M1 (5 + 1)² + (4 − 2)² = 40. A1 40 > 10 so outside.
  2. (b) √30
    M1 angle CST = 90°, so TS² = CT² − r² = 40 − 10. A1 TS = √30.
  3. (c) √300 = 10√3 ≈ 17.3
    M1 two right-angled triangles each of area ½ × √10 × √30. A1 √300 = 10√3.
Q7Tangent / normal to circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Lines of gradient 2 are tangents to the circle x² + y² = 20. By considering the equation y = 2x + c, find the two possible values of c. [4]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the points of contact. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) c = 10 or c = −10
    M1 substitute: x² + (2x + c)² = 20 → 5x² + 4cx + c² − 20 = 0. M1 discriminant = 0: 16c² − 20(c² − 20) = 0. A1 c² = 100. A1 c = ±10.
  2. (b) (−4, 2) and (4, −2)
    M1 x = −4c/10 (repeated root). A1 (−4, 2) for c = 10 and (4, −2) for c = −10.
Q8Tangent / normal to circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The line y = mx passes through the origin and is a tangent to the circle (x − 6)² + (y − 2)² = 4. Show that 32m² − 24m = 0. [4]
  2. (b) Hence find the equations of the two tangents from the origin to the circle and the coordinates of the point of contact on the tangent that is not an axis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Substituting gives (1 + m²)x² − (12 + 4m)x + 36 = 0; discriminant (12 + 4m)² − 144(1 + m²) = 0 simplifies to 32m² − 24m = 0 (after dividing by −4)
    M1 substitute y = mx and expand: (1 + m²)x² − (12 + 4m)x + 36 = 0. M1 use b² − 4ac = 0. A1 (12 + 4m)² = 144(1 + m²). A1 −128m² + 96m = 0, i.e. 32m² − 24m = 0.
  2. (b) y = 0 and y = ¾x; point of contact (4.8, 3.6)
    A1 m = 0 or m = ¾. M1 for m = ¾: (25/16)x² − 15x + 36 = 0 → x = 4.8. A1 (4.8, 3.6).

Q10Line-circle intersection / tangency · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Show that the line 3x + 4y = 25 is a tangent to the circle x² + y² = 25, and find the point of contact. [4]
  2. (b) Write down the equation of the tangent to the same circle at the point (−3, −4). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Substitution gives x² − 6x + 9 = 0, a repeated root; point of contact (3, 4)

  2. (b) 3x + 4y = −25
    B1 (by symmetry through the origin).

Q12Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find the values of k for which the line y = kx is a tangent to the circle (x − 4)² + y² = 8. [4]
  2. (b) State the set of values of k for which the line meets the circle at two distinct points. [1]
  3. (c) For k = 1, find the point of contact. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 1 or k = −1
    M1 substitute: (1 + k²)x² − 8x + 8 = 0. M1 discriminant: 64 − 32(1 + k²) = 0. A1 k² = 1. A1 k = ±1.
  2. (b) −1 < k < 1
    B1 64 − 32(1 + k²) > 0.
  3. (c) (2, 2)
    B1 2x² − 8x + 8 = 0 → x = 2.
Q13Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The line x + y = k meets the circle x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0. Show that the x-coordinates of the points of intersection satisfy 2x² − (2k + 2)x + k² − 2k − 20 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Find the exact values of k for which the line is a tangent to the circle. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Substituting y = k − x and simplifying gives 2x² − (2k + 2)x + (k² − 2k − 20) = 0
    M1 substitute y = k − x and expand. A1 correct simplification to the given equation.
  2. (b) k = 3 ± 5√2
    M1 discriminant: (2k + 2)² − 8(k² − 2k − 20) = 0. A1 k² − 6k − 41 = 0. A1 k = 3 ± √50 = 3 ± 5√2.
Q14Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The line y = x − 1 meets the circle (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 10 at the points A and B. Find the coordinates of A and B. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the midpoint M of AB and show that CM is perpendicular to AB, where C is the centre of the circle. [2]
  3. (c) Find the exact perpendicular distance from C to the line AB. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (0, −1) and (4, 3)
    M1 (x − 1)² + (x − 3)² = 10. A1 2x² − 8x = 0. A1 (0, −1), (4, 3).
  2. (b) M(2, 1); gradient CM = −1, gradient AB = 1, product −1
    B1 M(2, 1). B1 gradients −1 and 1 so perpendicular.
  3. (c) √2
    B1 CM = √(1 + 1) = √2.
Q15Line-circle intersection / tangency · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The line y = x + c and the circle (x − 5)² + (y + 2)² = 18 are given. Show that the x-coordinates of any points of intersection satisfy 2x² + (2c − 6)x + c² + 4c + 11 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Find the set of values of c for which the line does not meet the circle. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Substitution and expansion give 2x² + (2c − 6)x + (c² + 4c + 11) = 0
    M1 substitute and expand (x − 5)² + (x + c + 2)² = 18. A1 given equation.
  2. (b) c < −13 or c > −1
    M1 discriminant < 0: (2c − 6)² − 8(c² + 4c + 11) < 0. A1 −4c² − 56c − 52 < 0, i.e. c² + 14c + 13 > 0. M1 critical values −13, −1. A1 c < −13 or c > −1.

Q17Equation of circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

  2. (b) Determine whether the point (6, 2) lies on, inside or outside the circle. [2]

Q20Equation of circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) The points P(1, 1), Q(7, 1) and R(1, 9) lie on a circle. Show that angle QPR = 90° and explain why QR is a diameter of the circle. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the circle. [3]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at R, giving your answer in the form ax + by + c = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) PQ is horizontal and PR is vertical, so angle QPR = 90°; the angle in a semicircle is 90°, so QR is a diameter
    B1 PQ along y = 1 and PR along x = 1 (or gradients 0 and undefined), so perpendicular. B1 angle in a semicircle / converse, so QR is a diameter.
  2. (b) (x − 4)² + (y − 5)² = 25
    B1 centre = midpoint of QR = (4, 5). M1 radius = ½√(6² + 8²) = 5. A1 (x − 4)² + (y − 5)² = 25.
  3. (c) 3x − 4y + 33 = 0
    M1 gradient of radius from (4, 5) to R(1, 9) = −4/3. M1 tangent gradient 3/4 through R. A1 3x − 4y + 33 = 0.
Q21Equation of circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

    M1 solve 2x − 3 = 6 − x → centre (3, 3). M1 r² = (3 − 2)² + 3² = 10. A1 (x − 3)² + (y − 3)² = 10.

Q23Equation of circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

  3. (c) Find the distance between the centres of the two circles, giving your answer in exact form. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Touching both axes means the centre is a distance r from each axis, so the centre is (r, r)
    B1 centre at distance r from both axes, so (r, r).
  2. (b) (x − 1)² + (y − 1)² = 1 or (x − 5)² + (y − 5)² = 25
    M1 (2 − r)² + (1 − r)² = r². A1 r² − 6r + 5 = 0. M1 r = 1 or r = 5. A1 both equations.
  3. (c) 4√2
    M1 distance between (1, 1) and (5, 5) = √(16 + 16). A1 4√2.
Q24Equation of circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

  3. (c) Find the equation of the common tangent to the two circles at this point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Distance between centres = √(6² + 8²) = 10 = 3 + 7, so they touch externally

  2. (b) (2.8, 4.4)
    M1 point is 3/10 of the way from (1, 2) to (7, 10). M1 (1 + 0.3 × 6, 2 + 0.3 × 8). A1 (2.8, 4.4).
  3. (c) 3x + 4y = 26 (y − 4.4 = −¾(x − 2.8))
    M1 gradient of line of centres 4/3, so tangent gradient −3/4. A1 3x + 4y = 26.

  3. (c) Hence find the area of triangle ABC. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x − 2y + 3 = 0 (y = ½x + 3/2)
    M1 gradient ½. A1 x − 2y + 3 = 0.
  2. (b) F(5, 4)
    M1 line through C with gradient −2: y = −2x + 14. M1 solve with AB. A1 (5, 4).
  3. (c) 20
    M1 ½ × AB × CF = ½ × 4√5 × 2√5. A1 20.

Q29Lines / perpendicular bisector · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) ABCD is a rhombus. A is (−1, 2) and C is (7, 6). Find the equation of the diagonal BD. [3]
  2. (b) B lies on the x-axis. Find the coordinates of B and D. [2]
  3. (c) Find the area of the rhombus and show that ABCD is in fact a square. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −2x + 10
    B1 midpoint of AC (3, 4). B1 gradient AC = ½, so BD gradient −2. A1 y = −2x + 10.
  2. (b) B(5, 0), D(1, 8)
    B1 B(5, 0). B1 D = 2M − B = (1, 8).
  3. (c) Area 40; AC = BD = √80 (diagonals equal and perpendicular), so it is a square
    M1 AC = √80, BD = √80. A1 area ½ × √80 × √80 = 40. A1 equal perpendicular bisecting diagonals ⇒ square.

  1. (a) The line l has equation y = 2x + 1. Find the coordinates of the foot of the perpendicular from P(6, 3) to l. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the reflection of P in l, and the exact perpendicular distance from P to l. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2, 5)
    M1 gradient −½ through P: y = −½x + 6. M1 solve 2x + 1 = −½x + 6. A1 (2, 5).
