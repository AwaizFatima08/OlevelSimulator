Q1Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The point P(5, 3) lies on the circle (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 25. Find the gradient of the radius through P. [1]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P, giving your answer in the form ax + by = c. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4/3
    B1 (3 − (−1)) ÷ (5 − 2) = 4/3.
  2. (b) 3x + 4y = 27
    M1 tangent gradient = −3/4 (negative reciprocal). M1 y − 3 = −¾(x − 5). A1 3x + 4y = 27.
Q2Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The circle C has equation x² + y² − 6x − 4y − 12 = 0. Find the centre and radius of C, and show that the point A(7, 5) lies on C. [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the normal to C at A. [2]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the tangent to C at A. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Centre (3, 2), radius 5; (7 − 3)² + (5 − 2)² = 16 + 9 = 25
    M1 (x − 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25. A1 centre (3, 2), r = 5. B1 substitution of (7, 5) gives 25.
  2. (b) 3x − 4y = 1
    M1 the normal passes through the centre: gradient (5 − 2)/(7 − 3) = 3/4. A1 3x − 4y = 1.
  3. (c) 4x + 3y = 43
    M1 gradient −4/3 through (7, 5). A1 4x + 3y = 43.
Q3Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The normals to a circle at the points A(1, 4) and B(3, −2) have equations y = 2x + 2 and x + 2y = −1 respectively. Find the coordinates of the centre of the circle. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the circle. [2]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at B. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (−1, 0)
    M1 solve simultaneously: x + 2(2x + 2) = −1. A1 x = −1, y = 0.
  2. (b) (x + 1)² + y² = 20
    M1 r² = (1 + 1)² + 4² = 20 (or using B: 16 + 4). A1 (x + 1)² + y² = 20.
  3. (c) y = 2x − 8
    M1 tangent ⟂ normal (gradient −½), so tangent gradient 2. A1 y + 2 = 2(x − 3), y = 2x − 8.
Q4Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

The circle C has equation x² + y² − 4x + 6y − 12 = 0. The point P(5, 1) lies on C.

  1. (a) Find the coordinates of the centre of C and its radius. [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to C at P, giving your answer in the form ax + by + c = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Centre (2, −3), radius 5
    Complete the squares: (x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 4 + 9 + 12 = 25. Centre (2, −3), radius 5.
  2. (b) 3x + 4y − 19 = 0
    Gradient of radius from (2, −3) to (5, 1) is 4/3, so the tangent has gradient −3/4. y − 1 = −3/4(x − 5), giving 3x + 4y − 19 = 0.
Q5Tangent / normal to circle · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A circle C has centre (−1, 3) and radius √13. The point Q(1, 0) lies on C.

  1. (a) Show that Q lies on C. [1]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the normal to C at Q, in the form ax + by + c = 0. [3]
  3. (c) Find the coordinates of the point where the normal meets the y-axis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Q lies on C
    Distance² from (−1, 3) to (1, 0) is (1 + 1)² + (0 − 3)² = 4 + 9 = 13 = r², so Q lies on C.
  2. (b) 3x + 2y − 3 = 0
    The normal passes through the centre (−1, 3) and Q(1, 0). Its gradient is (0 − 3)/(1 + 1) = −3/2, so y = −3/2(x − 1), i.e. 3x + 2y − 3 = 0.
  3. (c) (0, 3/2)
    Put x = 0 in 3x + 2y − 3 = 0: 2y = 3, so y = 3/2.
Q6Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The points A(5, 5) and B(7, −1) lie on the circle x² + y² = 50. Find the equations of the tangents to the circle at A and at B. [4]
  2. (b) The tangents meet at T. Find the coordinates of T and show that TA = TB. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) At A: x + y = 10; at B: 7x − y = 50
    M1 gradient OA = 1 so tangent gradient −1. A1 x + y = 10. M1 gradient OB = −1/7 so tangent gradient 7. A1 7x − y = 50.
  2. (b) T(7.5, 2.5); TA = TB = √12.5 = (5√2)/2
    M1 solve simultaneously. A1 T(7.5, 2.5). A1 TA² = 2.5² + 2.5² = 12.5 and TB² = 0.5² + 3.5² = 12.5.
Q7Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The point P(6, 7) lies on the circle (x − 4)² + (y − 3)² = 20. Find the equation of the tangent to the circle at P. [3]
  2. (b) The tangent meets the x-axis at Q and the y-axis at R. Find the area of triangle OQR, where O is the origin. [2]
  3. (c) Find the exact length PQ. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x + 2y = 20
    M1 radius gradient (7 − 3)/(6 − 4) = 2. M1 tangent gradient −½. A1 y − 7 = −½(x − 6), x + 2y = 20.
  2. (b) 100
    M1 Q(20, 0), R(0, 10). A1 area = ½ × 20 × 10 = 100.
  3. (c) 7√5
    M1 PQ = √(14² + 7²) = √245. A1 7√5.
Q8Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A circle has equation (x + 1)² + (y − 2)² = 10. Show that the point T(5, 4) lies outside the circle. [2]
  2. (b) A tangent is drawn from T to touch the circle at S. Find the exact length TS. [2]
  3. (c) Find the area of the kite formed by T, the centre C and the two points of contact of the tangents from T. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Distance from centre (−1, 2) to T is √40 > √10
    M1 (5 + 1)² + (4 − 2)² = 40. A1 40 > 10 so outside.
  2. (b) √30
    M1 angle CST = 90°, so TS² = CT² − r² = 40 − 10. A1 TS = √30.
  3. (c) √300 = 10√3 ≈ 17.3
    M1 two right-angled triangles each of area ½ × √10 × √30. A1 √300 = 10√3.
Q9Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

The circle C has equation (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 25. The point A(15, −1) lies outside C.

  1. (a) Find the length of the tangent from A to C, giving an exact answer. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equations of the two tangents from A to C. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12
    AC² = (15 − 2)² + (−1 + 1)² = 169. Tangent length² = 169 − 25 = 144, so the length is 12.
  2. (b) 5x − 12y − 87 = 0 and 5x + 12y − 63 = 0
    A line through A is y + 1 = m(x − 15), i.e. mx − y − 15m − 1 = 0. Distance from (2, −1) equals 5: |2m + 1 − 15m − 1|/√(m² + 1) = 5, so 169m² = 25(m² + 1), 144m² = 25, m = ±5/12. Tangents: y + 1 = ±5/12(x − 15), giving 5x − 12y − 87 = 0 and 5x + 12y − 63 = 0.
Q10Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

The circle C has equation x² + y² − 6x + 2y = 0. The point P(6, −2) lies on C.

  1. (a) Show that P lies on C, and find the equation of the tangent to C at P, in the form y = mx + c. [4]
  2. (b) Find the x-coordinate of the point where the tangent meets the x-axis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 3x − 20
    P: 36 + 4 − 36 − 4 = 0, so P lies on C. Centre (3, −1). Gradient of radius = (−2 + 1)/(6 − 3) = −1/3, so the tangent has gradient 3. y + 2 = 3(x − 6), so y = 3x − 20.
  2. (b) 20/3
    Put y = 0: 3x = 20, so x = 20/3.
Q11Tangent / normal to circle · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The circle C has equation x² + y² = 25.

  1. (a) Find the equations of the two tangents to C that have gradient 3/4. [4]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the points where these tangents touch C. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3x − 4y + 25 = 0 and 3x − 4y − 25 = 0
    A tangent with gradient 3/4 is y = 3x/4 + c, i.e. 3x − 4y + 4c = 0. Distance from the origin is |4c|/5 = 5, so 4c = ±25.
  2. (b) (−3, 4) and (3, −4)
    The point of contact is the foot of the perpendicular from O. For 3x − 4y + 25 = 0 it is (−3, 4); for 3x − 4y − 25 = 0 it is (3, −4). Both lie on C since 9 + 16 = 25.
Q12Tangent / normal to circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Lines of gradient 2 are tangents to the circle x² + y² = 20. By considering the equation y = 2x + c, find the two possible values of c. [4]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the points of contact. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) c = 10 or c = −10
    M1 substitute: x² + (2x + c)² = 20 → 5x² + 4cx + c² − 20 = 0. M1 discriminant = 0: 16c² − 20(c² − 20) = 0. A1 c² = 100. A1 c = ±10.
  2. (b) (−4, 2) and (4, −2)
    M1 x = −4c/10 (repeated root). A1 (−4, 2) for c = 10 and (4, −2) for c = −10.
Q13Tangent / normal to circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The line y = mx passes through the origin and is a tangent to the circle (x − 6)² + (y − 2)² = 4. Show that 32m² − 24m = 0. [4]
  2. (b) Hence find the equations of the two tangents from the origin to the circle and the coordinates of the point of contact on the tangent that is not an axis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Substituting gives (1 + m²)x² − (12 + 4m)x + 36 = 0; discriminant (12 + 4m)² − 144(1 + m²) = 0 simplifies to 32m² − 24m = 0 (after dividing by −4)
    M1 substitute y = mx and expand: (1 + m²)x² − (12 + 4m)x + 36 = 0. M1 use b² − 4ac = 0. A1 (12 + 4m)² = 144(1 + m²). A1 −128m² + 96m = 0, i.e. 32m² − 24m = 0.
  2. (b) y = 0 and y = ¾x; point of contact (4.8, 3.6)
    A1 m = 0 or m = ¾. M1 for m = ¾: (25/16)x² − 15x + 36 = 0 → x = 4.8. A1 (4.8, 3.6).
Q14Tangent / normal to circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

A circle C has its centre on the line y = 2x. C touches the x-axis at (3, 0).

  1. (a) Find the equation of C. [4]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the points on C where the tangent is parallel to the y-axis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x − 3)² + (y − 6)² = 36
    The circle touches the x-axis at (3, 0), so its centre is directly above, at (3, r) for radius r. The centre lies on y = 2x, so r = 6 and the centre is (3, 6). Equation (x − 3)² + (y − 6)² = 36.
  2. (b) (−3, 6) and (9, 6)
    The tangent is vertical where the radius is horizontal, so y = 6 and x = 3 ± 6, giving (−3, 6) and (9, 6).
Q15Tangent / normal to circle · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The circle C has equation x² + y² = 10.

  1. (a) Find the coordinates of the two points on C where the tangent has gradient −3. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact distance between the two tangents with gradient −3. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (3, 1) and (−3, −1)
    The radius to the point has gradient 1/3, so y = x/3. Substituting: x² + x²/9 = 10, so x² = 9, x = ±3 and y = ±1.
  2. (b) 2√10
    The tangents are 3x + y − 10 = 0 at (3, 1) and 3x + y + 10 = 0 at (−3, −1). Distance between parallel lines = |−10 − 10|/√(3² + 1²) = 20/√10 = 2√10.

Show answer
  1. (a) Substitution gives x² − 6x + 9 = 0, a repeated root; point of contact (3, 4)

Q19Line-circle intersection / tangency · Core · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The line y = 2x − 1 meets the circle x² + y² = 13 at the points A and B.

  1. (a) Find the coordinates of A and B, the points where the line meets the circle. [4]
  2. (b) Find the exact length of the chord AB. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A(2, 3) and B(−6/5, −17/5)
    Substitute y = 2x − 1: x² + (2x − 1)² = 13, so 5x² − 4x − 12 = 0 and (x − 2)(5x + 6) = 0. x = 2 gives y = 3; x = −6/5 gives y = −17/5.
  2. (b) 16√5/5
    Difference of coordinates (16/5, 32/5). Length = √((16/5)² + (32/5)²) = √(1280/25) = (16/5)√5.
Q20Line-circle intersection / tangency · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

The circle C has equation (x − 1)² + (y + 2)² = 25.

  1. (a) Show that the line 3x + 4y = 20 is a tangent to C. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the point of contact. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Distance from centre = 5 = radius
    Centre (1, −2), radius 5. Distance = |3(1) + 4(−2) − 20|/√(3² + 4²) = |−25|/5 = 5, which equals the radius, so the line is a tangent.
  2. (b) (4, 2)
    The point (1 + 3t, −2 + 4t) lies on the line when 3(1 + 3t) + 4(−2 + 4t) = 20, so 25t = 25 and t = 1. The point is (4, 2).
Q21Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find the values of k for which the line y = kx is a tangent to the circle (x − 4)² + y² = 8. [4]
  2. (b) State the set of values of k for which the line meets the circle at two distinct points. [1]
  3. (c) For k = 1, find the point of contact. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 1 or k = −1
    M1 substitute: (1 + k²)x² − 8x + 8 = 0. M1 discriminant: 64 − 32(1 + k²) = 0. A1 k² = 1. A1 k = ±1.
  2. (b) −1 < k < 1
    B1 64 − 32(1 + k²) > 0.
  3. (c) (2, 2)
    B1 2x² − 8x + 8 = 0 → x = 2.

  1. (a) The line x + y = k meets the circle x² + y² − 4x − 2y − 20 = 0. Show that the x-coordinates of the points of intersection satisfy 2x² − (2k + 2)x + k² − 2k − 20 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Find the exact values of k for which the line is a tangent to the circle. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Substituting y = k − x and simplifying gives 2x² − (2k + 2)x + (k² − 2k − 20) = 0
    M1 substitute y = k − x and expand. A1 correct simplification to the given equation.
  2. (b) k = 3 ± 5√2
    M1 discriminant: (2k + 2)² − 8(k² − 2k − 20) = 0. A1 k² − 6k − 41 = 0. A1 k = 3 ± √50 = 3 ± 5√2.
Q23Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The line y = x − 1 meets the circle (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 10 at the points A and B. Find the coordinates of A and B. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the midpoint M of AB and show that CM is perpendicular to AB, where C is the centre of the circle. [2]

Show answer
  1. (a) (0, −1) and (4, 3)
    M1 (x − 1)² + (x − 3)² = 10. A1 2x² − 8x = 0. A1 (0, −1), (4, 3).
  2. (b) M(2, 1); gradient CM = −1, gradient AB = 1, product −1
    B1 M(2, 1). B1 gradients −1 and 1 so perpendicular.
  3. (c) √2
    B1 CM = √(1 + 1) = √2.
Q24Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The circle C has equation (x − 3)² + y² = 9.

  1. (a) Find the value of k for which the line y = kx + 1 is a tangent to C. [4]
  2. (b) Find the set of values of k for which the line meets C at two distinct points. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 4/3
    The line is kx − y + 1 = 0. Distance from (3, 0) = |3k + 1|/√(k² + 1) = 3, so (3k + 1)² = 9(k² + 1). This gives 6k + 1 = 9 and k = 4/3.
  2. (b) k < 4/3
    Two distinct points need distance < 3: (3k + 1)² < 9(k² + 1), so 6k < 8 and k < 4/3.
Q25Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The circle C has equation x² + y² − 2x − 4y − 20 = 0. The line x − 2y + 5 = 0 meets C at A and B.

  1. (a) Find the coordinates of A and B, the points where the line meets the circle. [4]
  2. (b) Find the exact length of AB. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A(5, 5) and B(−3.8, 0.6)
    Substitute x = 2y − 5: 5y² − 28y + 15 = 0, so (y − 5)(5y − 3) = 0. y = 5 gives x = 5; y = 3/5 gives x = −19/5.
  2. (b) 22√5/5
    Difference (44/5, 22/5). Length = √((44/5)² + (22/5)²) = (22/5)√5.
Q26Line-circle intersection / tangency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

The circle C has equation x² + y² = 5.

  1. (a) Find the values of c for which the line y = 2x + c is a tangent to the circle x² + y² = 5. [4]
  2. (b) For c = 5, find the coordinates of the point of contact. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) c = 5 or c = −5
    The line is 2x − y + c = 0. Distance from the origin is |c|/√5 = √5, so |c| = 5.
  2. (b) (−2, 1)

    Midpoint of BC is (7, 3). Gradient of BC = 6/(−6) = −1, so the perpendicular gradient is 1. y − 3 = x − 7, giving x − y − 4 = 0.
  2. (b) (5, 1)
    The perpendicular bisector of AB is x = 5. Substituting into x − y − 4 = 0 gives y = 1.
  3. (c) √26
    Radius = distance from (5, 1) to A(0, 0) = √(25 + 1) = √26.
Q60Lines / perpendicular bisector · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

A line passes through (2, 5) with gradient m.

  1. (a) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of the segment joining (−1, 2) and (3, 4), in the form ax + by + c = 0. [3]
  2. (b) Find the value of m for which the line through (2, 5) with gradient m meets this perpendicular bisector on the y-axis. [2]
  3. (c) Find the value of m for which the line through (2, 5) with gradient m is perpendicular to the bisector. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2x + y − 5 = 0
    Midpoint (1, 3). Gradient of the segment = 2/4 = 1/2, so the perpendicular has gradient −2. y − 3 = −2(x − 1), i.e. 2x + y − 5 = 0.
  2. (b) m = 0
    The bisector meets the y-axis at (0, 5). The line y = m(x − 2) + 5 passes through (0, 5) when −2m + 5 = 5, so m = 0.
  3. (c) m = 1/2
    Perpendicular gradients multiply to −1: m × (−2) = −1, so m = 1/2.
