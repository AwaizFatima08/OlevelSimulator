Q1Conditional probability · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Events A and B are such that P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.15. Find P(A | B) and P(B | A). [3]
  2. (b) Find P(A ∪ B). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) P(A | B) = 0.3, P(B | A) = 0.375
    P(A | B) = 0.15/0.5 = 0.3 M1 A1; P(B | A) = 0.15/0.4 = 0.375 A1
  2. (b) 0.75
    0.4 + 0.5 − 0.15 = 0.75 B1

Q7Conditional probability · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A card is chosen at random from 20 cards numbered 1 to 20. A is the event 'the number is a multiple of 3', B is the event 'the number is even' and C is the event 'the number is greater than 15'. Find P(A | B) and hence show that A and B are independent. [3]
  2. (b) Determine whether B and C are independent, justifying your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P(A | B) = 3/10 = P(A), so independent
    Even numbers: 10, multiples of 3 among them: 6, 12, 18 M1; P(A | B) = 3/10 A1; P(A) = 6/20 = 3/10, equal so independent A1
  2. (b) Not independent: P(C | B) = 3/10 but P(C) = 1/4
    Even numbers greater than 15: 16, 18, 20, so P(C | B) = 3/10 M1; P(C) = 5/20 = 1/4 ≠ 3/10, not independent A1

Q10Conditional probability · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Events A and B are such that P(B) = 0.4, P(A | B) = 0.25 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7. Find P(A ∩ B) and P(A). [3]
  2. (b) Find P(A′ | B′). [2]
  3. (c) State, with a reason, whether A and B are independent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P(A ∩ B) = 0.1, P(A) = 0.4
    P(A ∩ B) = 0.25 × 0.4 = 0.1 B1; 0.7 = P(A) + 0.4 − 0.1 M1; P(A) = 0.4 A1
  2. (b) 0.5
    P(A′ ∩ B′) = 1 − 0.7 = 0.3 M1; 0.3/0.6 = 0.5 A1
  3. (c) Not independent, since P(A) × P(B) = 0.16 ≠ 0.1 = P(A ∩ B)
    P(A)P(B) = 0.16 M1; ≠ 0.1 so not independent A1

Q21Combined events · Standard · Paper 5 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) For events A and B, P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7. Find P(A ∩ B) and the probability that exactly one of A and B occurs. [3]

Q23Combined events · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) A fair six-sided die is thrown twice. Event A is 'the total is 7', event B is 'the first throw is a 4' and event C is 'the product of the two scores is even'. Show that A and B are independent. [3]
  2. (b) Find P(C) and P(A ∩ C), and determine whether A and C are independent. [4]
  3. (c) Find P(A ∪ C). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) P(A ∩ B) = 1/36 = P(A)P(B)
    P(A) = 6/36 = 1/6, P(B) = 1/6 B1; P(A ∩ B) = P(4 then 3) = 1/36 B1; = (1/6)(1/6), so independent B1
  2. (b) P(C) = 3/4; P(A ∩ C) = 1/6; not independent
    P(C) = 1 − (1/2)(1/2) = 3/4 B1; a total of 7 needs one odd and one even score, so its product is always even: P(A ∩ C) = 1/6 M1 A1; (1/6)(3/4) = 1/8 ≠ 1/6, not independent A1
  3. (c) 3/4
    Since A ⊂ C, P(A ∪ C) = P(C) = 3/4 B1

Q27Probability without replacement · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A bag contains n red sweets and 4 green sweets. Two sweets are taken at random without replacement. The probability that both are red is ⅓. Show that n² − 5n − 6 = 0 and hence find n. [4]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the two sweets are of different colours. [2]
  3. (c) A third sweet is now taken from the bag. Given that the first two sweets were of different colours, find the probability that the third sweet is red. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) n = 6
    n(n − 1)/((n + 4)(n + 3)) = 1/3 M1; 3n² − 3n = n² + 7n + 12 A1; 2n² − 10n − 12 = 0 ⇒ n² − 5n − 6 = 0 A1; (n − 6)(n + 1) = 0, n = 6 A1
  2. (b) 8/15
    2 × (6/10)(4/9) M1; = 8/15 A1
  3. (c) 5/8
    After one red and one green are removed: 5 red out of 8 B1
Q28Two-way tables & independence · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A café records the drink chosen by 150 customers. Of the 75 customers under 30, 30 chose tea and 45 chose coffee. Of the 75 customers aged 30 or over, 50 chose tea and 25 chose coffee. A customer is chosen at random. Find the probability that the customer chose tea. [1]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the customer chose tea, given that the customer is under 30. [1]
  3. (c) State, with a reason, whether the events 'chose tea' and 'under 30' are independent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8/15
    80/150 = 8/15 B1
  2. (b) 2/5
    30/75 = 2/5 B1
  3. (c) Not independent, since P(tea | under 30) = 2/5 ≠ 8/15 = P(tea)
    Compare 2/5 with 8/15 M1; not equal so not independent A1
Q29Two-way tables & independence · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) In a college in Peshawar there are 200 first-year students: 120 girls and 80 boys. Of the girls, 72 study science and the rest study arts. Of the boys, 48 study science and the rest study arts. Copy and complete a two-way table for this information. [1]
  2. (b) A student is chosen at random. Show that the events 'the student is a girl' and 'the student studies science' are independent. [3]
  3. (c) Find the probability that the student is a boy, given that the student studies arts. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Girls: science 72, arts 48; boys: science 48, arts 32; totals: science 120, arts 80
    All entries correct B1
  2. (b) P(G ∩ S) = 0.36 = 0.6 × 0.6
    P(G) = 120/200 = 0.6, P(S) = 120/200 = 0.6 B1; P(G ∩ S) = 72/200 = 0.36 B1; 0.6 × 0.6 = 0.36, so independent B1
  3. (c) 2/5
    32/80 = 2/5 B1
Q30Two-way tables & independence · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The members of a gym in Karachi are classified as follows. Male, morning: 24; male, evening: x; female, morning: 36; female, evening: 54. The events 'a randomly chosen member is male' and 'a randomly chosen member attends in the morning' are independent. Find x. [4]
  2. (b) Find the probability that a randomly chosen member who attends in the evening is female. [1]
  3. (c) Two members are chosen at random without replacement. Find the probability that both attend in the morning. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 36
    P(male ∩ morning) = 24/N, P(male) = (24 + x)/N, P(morning) = 60/N with N = 114 + x B1; 24/N = (24 + x)(60)/N² M1; 24(114 + x) = 60(24 + x) A1; 36x = 1296, x = 36 A1
  2. (b) 3/5
    54/90 = 3/5 B1
  3. (c) 118/745 (≈ 0.158)
    (60/150)(59/149) M1; = 118/745 A1
