Q4Conditional probability · Core · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Events X and Y satisfy P(X) = 0.7, P(Y) = 0.25 and P(X | Y) = 0.6. Find P(X ∩ Y). [2]
  2. (b) Find P(X ∪ Y). [2]
  3. (c) State, giving a reason, whether X and Y are independent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.15
    P(X ∩ Y) = P(X | Y) × P(Y) = 0.6 × 0.25 = 0.15 (M1, A1).
  2. (b) 0.8
    P(X ∪ Y) = 0.7 + 0.25 − 0.15 = 0.8 (M1, A1).
  3. (c) Not independent: P(X) × P(Y) = 0.175, which is not 0.15
    Independent events need P(X ∩ Y) = P(X) × P(Y) (M1). 0.7 × 0.25 = 0.175 ≠ 0.15, so they are not independent (A1).

Q9Conditional probability · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A card is chosen at random from 20 cards numbered 1 to 20. A is the event 'the number is a multiple of 3', B is the event 'the number is even' and C is the event 'the number is greater than 15'. Find P(A | B) and hence show that A and B are independent. [3]
  2. (b) Determine whether B and C are independent, justifying your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P(A | B) = 3/10 = P(A), so independent
    Even numbers: 10, multiples of 3 among them: 6, 12, 18 M1; P(A | B) = 3/10 A1; P(A) = 6/20 = 3/10, equal so independent A1
  2. (b) Not independent: P(C | B) = 3/10 but P(C) = 1/4
    Even numbers greater than 15: 16, 18, 20, so P(C | B) = 3/10 M1; P(C) = 5/20 = 1/4 ≠ 3/10, not independent A1

Show answer
  1. (a) 1/6
    Automatic petrol cars = 50 − 30 = 20, so P(automatic | petrol) = 20/120 = 1/6 (M1, A1).

Show answer
  1. (a) P(A ∩ B) = 0.45 + 0.2 − 0.56 = 0.09 = 0.45 × 0.2

  3. (c) State, with a reason, whether passing the test is independent of gender. [2]

  1. (a) Boys passed 13, boys failed 9, girls failed 6
    Boys passed = 25 − 12 = 13 (M1). Boys failed = 22 − 13 = 9 and girls failed = 18 − 12 = 6 (A1).

Show answer
  1. (a) P(A) = 0.4 and P(B) = 0.5
    P(B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(A | B) = 0.2/0.4 = 0.5 (M1, A1). P(A) = 0.2/0.5 = 0.4 (A1).
  2. (b) P(A) × P(B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2 = P(A ∩ B)
    The product P(A) × P(B) = 0.2 equals P(A ∩ B) (M1), so the events are independent (A1).
Q69Two-way tables & independence · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) Find P(A | B) and state whether A and B are independent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.4
    P(A ∩ B) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2 (M1). P(B) = P(A ∪ B) − P(A) + P(A ∩ B) = 0.6 − 0.4 + 0.2 = 0.4 (M1, A1).
  2. (b) P(A | B) = 0.5; not independent since 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.16 ≠ 0.2
    P(A | B) = 0.2/0.4 = 0.5 (M1). P(A) × P(B) = 0.16 ≠ 0.2 = P(A ∩ B), so not independent (A1).

  2. (b) Find P(A′ ∩ B′). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2/7
    P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A)P(B), so 0.5 = 0.3 + 0.7P(B) (M1). 0.7P(B) = 0.2, so P(B) = 2/7 (M1, A1).

Show answer
  1. (a) P(A ∩ B) = 2/5 + 1/2 − 7/10 = 1/5
