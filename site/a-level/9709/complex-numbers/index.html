Q1Argand loci · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe fully the locus of points in an Argand diagram representing complex numbers z that satisfy |z − 3 + 2i| = 4. [2]
  2. (b) Find the greatest value of |z| for points on this locus. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A circle with centre (3, −2), i.e. the point 3 − 2i, and radius 4.
    B1 circle centre 3 − 2i; B1 radius 4.
  2. (b) √13 + 4 ≈ 7.61
    M1 distance from origin to centre = √(3² + 2²) = √13; A1 √13 + 4 = 7.61.
Q2Argand loci · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z = x + iy satisfies |z − 1| = |z + 3i|. Describe the locus of z geometrically. [1]
  2. (b) Find the Cartesian equation of the locus in the form ax + by + c = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The perpendicular bisector of the line joining the points 1 and −3i (i.e. (1, 0) and (0, −3)).
    B1.
  2. (b) x + 3y + 4 = 0
    M1 (x − 1)² + y² = x² + (y + 3)²; M1 expand and simplify: −2x + 1 = 6y + 9; A1 x + 3y + 4 = 0.
Q3Argand loci · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The locus of z is given by arg(z + 2) = π/3. Find a Cartesian equation for the locus, stating any restriction on x. [2]
  2. (b) Determine whether the point representing 1 + 3√3 i lies on the locus. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = √3(x + 2), x > −2
    M1 tan(π/3) = y/(x + 2); A1 with x > −2.
  2. (b) Yes: arg(3 + 3√3 i) = tan⁻¹(√3) = π/3.
    M1 z + 2 = 3 + 3√3 i; A1 argument is π/3, so yes.
Q4Argand loci · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z satisfies both |z| = 5 and |z − 6| = 5. Explain why Re z = 3. [1]
  2. (b) Find the two possible values of z. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) z is equidistant from 0 and 6, so it lies on the perpendicular bisector x = 3.
    B1.
  2. (b) z = 3 + 4i or z = 3 − 4i
    M1 3² + y² = 25; A1 y = ±4.
Q5Argand loci · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z satisfies |z − 4 − 3i| = 2. Find the least value of |z|. [2]
  2. (b) Find the greatest value of arg z for points on this locus. Give your answer in radians correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3
    M1 |4 + 3i| = 5; A1 5 − 2 = 3.
  2. (b) 1.06 rad
    B1 arg(4 + 3i) = tan⁻¹(3/4) = 0.6435; M1 angle between the line to the centre and the tangent = sin⁻¹(2/5) = 0.4115; A1 0.6435 + 0.4115 = 1.055 ≈ 1.06.
Q6Argand loci · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The region R in an Argand diagram consists of points z satisfying both |z − 2 − 3i| ≤ 3 and |z| ≥ |z − 4|. Describe R in words. [2]
  2. (b) Find the greatest value of |z| for points in R. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) State the least value of Re z for points in R. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The part of the disc with centre 2 + 3i and radius 3 that lies on or to the right of the line Re z = 2 (the perpendicular bisector of 0 and 4).
    B1 disc centre (2, 3), radius 3; B1 |z| ≥ |z − 4| ⇔ Re z ≥ 2, so R is the right-hand half of the disc.
  2. (b) √13 + 3 ≈ 6.61
    M1 farthest point lies on the line through O and the centre (it has Re z = 2 + 6/√13 > 2, so it is in R); A1 √13 + 3 = 6.61.
  3. (c) 2
    B1.
Q7Argand loci · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z = x + iy satisfies |z − 4i| = |z − 6|. Find the Cartesian equation of the locus of z. [3]
  2. (b) Find the least value of |z| for points on this locus, giving your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3x − 2y = 5
    M1 x² + (y − 4)² = (x − 6)² + y²; M1 −8y + 16 = −12x + 36; A1 3x − 2y = 5.
  2. (b) 5/√13 ≈ 1.39
    M1 perpendicular distance from O to 3x − 2y − 5 = 0 is 5/√(9 + 4); A1 1.39.
Q8Argand loci · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The locus L₁ is given by arg(z − 1 − i) = ¼π and the locus L₂ by |z − 5 − i| = 3. Describe each locus. [2]
  2. (b) Find, in exact form, the complex numbers represented by the points where L₁ meets L₂. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) L₁: half-line from the point 1 + i (excluded) at angle π/4 to the positive real direction. L₂: circle centre 5 + i, radius 3.
    B1 half-line from (1, 1) at 45°; B1 circle.
  2. (b) (3 + √2/2) + (3 + √2/2)i and (3 − √2/2) + (3 − √2/2)i
    M1 points on L₁: (1 + t) + (1 + t)i, t > 0; M1 (t − 4)² + t² = 9 ⇒ 2t² − 8t + 7 = 0; A1 t = 2 ± √2/2 (both positive, so both points lie on the half-line); A1 both complex numbers.
Q9Argand loci · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z satisfies |z + 2 − i| = 1. Show that the locus of z touches the real axis, and state the point of contact. [2]
  2. (b) Find the least and greatest values of arg z for points on the locus, where −π < arg z ≤ π. Give the least value in radians correct to 3 significant figures. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Centre −2 + i is 1 unit from the real axis, equal to the radius; contact at z = −2.
    B1 centre (−2, 1), radius 1; B1 distance to real axis = 1, contact at (−2, 0).
  2. (b) Least ≈ 2.21 rad (exactly π − 2 tan⁻¹(½)); greatest = π
    B1 greatest is π, at the point of contact −2; M1 arg(−2 + i) = π − tan⁻¹(½) = 2.678; M1 subtract sin⁻¹(1/√5) = 0.4636 for the other tangent; A1 2.214 ≈ 2.21.
Q10Argand loci · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The region R consists of points z satisfying |z − 3i| ≤ 2 and Re z ≥ 1. Find the least value of arg z for points in R. Give your answer in radians correct to 3 significant figures, and show that the point where it occurs is in R. [4]
  2. (b) Find the greatest value of |z| for points in R. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) ≈ 0.841 rad (π/2 − sin⁻¹(2/3)); at the tangent point Re z = 2√5/3 ≈ 1.49 ≥ 1
    M1 tangent from O to circle centre 3i, radius 2; M1 angle = π/2 − sin⁻¹(2/3); A1 0.841; B1 tangent length √(9 − 4) = √5, so Re z = √5 cos(π/2 − sin⁻¹(2/3)) = √5 × 2/3 = 2√5/3 ≈ 1.49 ≥ 1.
  2. (b) √(13 + 6√3) ≈ 4.84
    M1 on the circle x² + (y − 3)² = 4 we have |z|² = 6y − 5, so maximise y subject to x ≥ 1; M1 x = 1 gives y = 3 + √3; A1 |z| = √(13 + 6√3) = 4.84.

Q14Complex arithmetic & equations · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the complex number z satisfying 2z + 3iz* = 7 + 8i, where z* is the complex conjugate of z. Give your answer in the form x + iy. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) z = 2 + i
    M1 substitute z = x + iy, z* = x − iy; M1 2x + 2iy + 3ix + 3y; M1 equate real and imaginary parts: 2x + 3y = 7, 3x + 2y = 8; A1 x = 2; A1 y = 1.
Q15Complex arithmetic & equations · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The complex number u is given by u = (7 + i)/(1 − i). Express u in the form x + iy, showing all your working. [2]
  2. (b) Find u² in the form x + iy. [1]
  3. (c) Given that u² + pu + q = 0, where p and q are real, find p and q. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) u = 3 + 4i
    M1 multiply by (1 + i)/(1 + i); A1 (6 + 8i)/2 = 3 + 4i.
  2. (b) −7 + 24i
    B1.
  3. (c) p = −6, q = 25
    M1 −7 + 24i + 3p + 4pi + q = 0; M1 imaginary: 24 + 4p = 0 ⇒ p = −6; A1 real: −7 − 18 + q = 0 ⇒ q = 25.
Q16Complex arithmetic & equations · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The complex number w = a + 3i, where a is real, satisfies |w − 2| = 5. Find the two possible values of a. [3]
  2. (b) For the value of a with a > 2, find w² and arg(w²), giving the argument in radians correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 6 or a = −2
    M1 (a − 2)² + 9 = 25; M1 (a − 2)² = 16; A1 a = 6 or −2.
  2. (b) w² = 27 + 36i; arg(w²) ≈ 0.927 rad
    B1 w² = 36 − 9 + 36i = 27 + 36i; M1 tan⁻¹(36/27); A1 0.927.
Q17Complex arithmetic & equations · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Solve the simultaneous equations(1 + i)z + w = 1 + 2i2z + iw = 3 + 6i,giving z and w in the form x + iy. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) z = 1 + 2i, w = 2 − i
    M1 eliminate w: multiply first by i: (i − 1)z + iw = i − 2; M1 subtract from second: (3 − i)z = 5 + 5i; M1 z = (5 + 5i)/(3 − i) = (5 + 5i)(3 + i)/10; A1 z = 1 + 2i; M1 w = 1 + 2i − (1 + i)(1 + 2i); A1 w = 2 − i.
Q18Complex arithmetic & equations · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the complex numbers z which satisfy the equation zz* + 2iz = 12 + 6i, where z* is the complex conjugate of z. [5]
Show answer

    M1 put z = x + iy: x² + y² + 2ix − 2y = 12 + 6i; M1 equate parts: 2x = 6, x² + y² − 2y = 12; A1 x = 3; M1 y² − 2y − 3 = 0; A1 y = 3 or −1.
Q19Modulus-argument / exponential form · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z is given by z = −1 + √3 i. Find the modulus and argument of z, giving the argument as an exact multiple of π. [2]
  2. (b) Write z in the form re^{iθ} and hence find z³. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) |z| = 2, arg z = 2π/3
    B1 modulus; B1 argument (second quadrant).
  2. (b) z = 2e^{2πi/3}; z³ = 8e^{2πi} = 8
    B1 exponential form; B1 z³ = 8.
Q20Modulus-argument / exponential form · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The complex numbers z and w are given by z = 2e^{iπ/6} and w = 3e^{−iπ/4}. Find zw and z/w, each in the form re^{iθ} with r > 0 and −π < θ ≤ π. [3]
  2. (b) Express zw in the form x + iy, giving x and y correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) zw = 6e^{−iπ/12}; z/w = (2/3)e^{5iπ/12}
    M1 multiply moduli, add arguments; A1 zw; A1 z/w (divide moduli, subtract arguments).
  2. (b) 5.80 − 1.55i
    M1 6cos(π/12) − 6i sin(π/12); A1 5.80 − 1.55i.
Q21Modulus-argument / exponential form · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z is given by z = 4e^{iπ/3}. Find z³, giving your answer in the form x + iy. [2]
  2. (b) Find the smallest positive integer n for which zⁿ is real and positive, and state the value of zⁿ in this case. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) z³ = 64e^{iπ} = −64
    M1 64e^{iπ}; A1 −64.
  2. (b) n = 6, z⁶ = 4096
    M1 nπ/3 must be a multiple of 2π; A1 n = 6, z⁶ = 4⁶ = 4096.
Q22Modulus-argument / exponential form · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The complex numbers u and v are given by u = 1 + i and v = √3 − i. Find the modulus and argument of each of u and v. [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the modulus and argument of u/v. [2]
  3. (c) By writing u/v in the form x + iy, show that tan(5π/12) = 2 + √3. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) |u| = √2, arg u = π/4; |v| = 2, arg v = −π/6
    B1 u; B1 v.
  2. (b) |u/v| = √2/2, arg(u/v) = 5π/12
    B1 modulus; B1 argument π/4 − (−π/6).
  3. (c) u/v = ((√3 − 1) + (√3 + 1)i)/4, so tan(5π/12) = (√3 + 1)/(√3 − 1) = 2 + √3
    M1 multiply by (√3 + i)/(√3 + i); A1 correct x + iy; A1 rationalise (√3 + 1)²/2 = (4 + 2√3)/2 = 2 + √3.
Q23Modulus-argument / exponential form · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Given that z = e^{iθ}, where 0 < θ < π, show that 1 + z = 2cos(½θ)e^{iθ/2}. [3]
  2. (b) Hence state the modulus and argument of 1 + z when θ = 2π/3. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 + e^{iθ} = e^{iθ/2}(e^{−iθ/2} + e^{iθ/2}) = 2cos(½θ)e^{iθ/2}
    M1 factor out e^{iθ/2}; M1 e^{iα} + e^{−iα} = 2cos α; A1 shown.
  2. (b) |1 + z| = 1, arg(1 + z) = π/3
    B1 2cos(π/3) = 1; B1 argument π/3.
Q24Square roots of complex numbers · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the two square roots of 8i, giving your answers in the form x + iy, where x and y are real. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2 + 2i and −2 − 2i
    M1 (x + iy)² = 8i: x² − y² = 0, 2xy = 8; M1 x = y (since xy > 0), x² = 4; A1 2 + 2i; A1 −2 − 2i.
Q25Square roots of complex numbers · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) 3 − 2i and −3 + 2i

Q26Square roots of complex numbers · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) State the modulus of each square root and verify that its square equals |24 + 10i|. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5 + i and −5 − i

  2. (b) |5 + i| = √26 and (√26)² = 26 = |24 + 10i|
    B1.
Q27Square roots of complex numbers · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Find the square roots of −3 + 4i. [4]
  2. (b) Hence solve the quadratic equation z² − 3z + (3 − i) = 0, giving your answers in the form x + iy. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 + 2i and −1 − 2i
    M1 x² − y² = −3, 2xy = 4; M1 x⁴ + 3x² − 4 = 0; A1 x² = 1; A1 ±(1 + 2i).
  2. (b) z = 2 + i or z = 1 − i
    M1 discriminant 9 − 4(3 − i) = −3 + 4i; M1 z = (3 ± (1 + 2i))/2; A1 2 + i and 1 − i.
Q28Polynomial with complex roots · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The polynomial p(z) is defined by p(z) = z³ − 5z² + 17z − 13. Show that z = 1 is a root of p(z) = 0. [1]
  2. (b) Hence find the other two roots of p(z) = 0 in the form x + iy. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) p(1) = 1 − 5 + 17 − 13 = 0
    B1.
  2. (b) z = 2 + 3i and z = 2 − 3i
    M1 divide by (z − 1): z² − 4z + 13; A1 quadratic factor; M1 solve: (4 ± √(−36))/2; A1 2 ± 3i.
Q29Polynomial with complex roots · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Verify that z = 1 + 2i is a root of the equation z⁴ − 2z³ + 6z² − 2z + 5 = 0. [3]
  2. (b) Hence find the other three roots of the equation. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Substitution gives 0 (z² = −3 + 4i, z³ = −11 − 2i, z⁴ = −7 − 24i)
    B1 z² and z³ (or z⁴) correct; M1 substitute; A1 sum equals 0.
  2. (b) 1 − 2i, i and −i
    B1 1 − 2i (conjugate, real coefficients); M1 quadratic factor z² − 2z + 5; M1 divide: z² + 1; A1 ±i.
Q30Polynomial with complex roots · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The equation z³ + az² + bz + 10 = 0, where a and b are real, has a root 1 + 3i. Write down a second root and find the quadratic factor of z³ + az² + bz + 10 with these two roots. [3]
  2. (b) Find the values of a and b and the third root. [4]
Show answer
