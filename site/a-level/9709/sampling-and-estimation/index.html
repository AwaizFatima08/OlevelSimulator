Q1Confidence intervals · Core · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A machine fills bags of rice. The masses of the bags have standard deviation 4.5 g. A random sample of 50 bags has mean mass 502.3 g. Calculate a 95% confidence interval for the population mean mass, giving the end-points correct to 4 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why it was not necessary to assume that the masses are normally distributed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (501.1, 503.5)
    M1 502.3 ± z × 4.5/√50. B1 z = 1.96. A1 (501.1, 503.5).
  2. (b) The sample is large (n = 50), so by the Central Limit Theorem the sample mean is approximately normally distributed
    B1 n large, CLT applies.
Q2Confidence intervals · Core · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The times, x minutes, taken by a random sample of 40 customers to be served at a bank in Faisalabad are summarised by Σx = 1236 and Σx² = 38 900. Calculate unbiased estimates of the population mean and variance. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate a 90% confidence interval for the population mean time. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) mean 30.9; variance 18.1
    B1 x̄ = 30.9. M1 s² = (38 900 − 1236²/40)/39. A1 18.1.
  2. (b) (29.79, 32.01)
    M1 30.9 ± z√(s²/40). B1 z = 1.645. A1 (29.79, 32.01).

    B1 either reason.

Q5Confidence intervals · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The lifetimes of a type of LED bulb have standard deviation 12 hundred hours. A 99% confidence interval for the mean lifetime is to be found from a random sample of n bulbs. Find the smallest value of n for which the width of the interval is less than 5 hundred hours. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) n = 153
    M1 width = 2 × z × 12/√n. B1 z = 2.576. M1 √n > 2 × 2.576 × 12/5 = 12.36. A1 n > 152.9 so n = 153.

Q11Confidence intervals · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A random sample of n values is taken from a normal population with known standard deviation 4. A 95% confidence interval for the population mean is to have a width of at most 1.0. Find the smallest value of n. [3]
  2. (b) A student says, 'There is a 95% probability that the population mean lies in this confidence interval.' Explain why this statement is incorrect. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) n = 246
    Width = 2 × 1.96 × 4/√n ≤ 1 (M1); √n ≥ 15.68 (M1); n ≥ 245.9, so the smallest value is 246 (A1)
  2. (b) The population mean is a fixed value, so it either lies in the interval or it does not; the 95% describes the proportion of intervals, constructed this way from repeated samples, that would contain the mean
    The mean μ is fixed, not random (B1); the 95% is a long-run proportion of intervals containing μ (B2)

Q13Confidence intervals · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A mobile network claims that 40% of its customers use its streaming service. In a random sample of 450 customers, 150 use the service. Calculate an approximate 90% confidence interval for the proportion of customers who use the service. [4]
  2. (b) Comment on the network's claim. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate an approximate 99% confidence interval from the same data and state whether it leads to the same conclusion about the claim. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (0.297, 0.370)
    B1 p̂ = 1/3. M1 p̂ ± z√(p̂(1 − p̂)/450). B1 z = 1.645. A1 (0.297, 0.370).
  2. (b) 0.4 lies outside the interval, so the evidence suggests the claim is too high
    B1 0.4 not in the interval, claim doubtful.
  3. (c) (0.276, 0.391); yes, 0.4 still lies outside the interval, so the claim still appears too high
    M1 1/3 ± 2.576√((1/3)(2/3)/450). A1 (0.276, 0.391) and 0.4 not in the interval, same conclusion.

Q26Unbiased estimates / sample mean · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A random sample of 8 values gives an unbiased estimate of the population variance of 3.2. Find the value of Σ(x − x̄)², and the estimate that would be obtained by dividing Σ(x − x̄)² by 8 instead of 7. [3]

Show answer
  1. (a) Σ(x − x̄)² = 22.4; the estimate is 2.8

  2. (b) Dividing by n − 1 gives an unbiased estimate because one degree of freedom is lost by using x̄; dividing by n gives an estimate that underestimates the population variance on average
    n − 1 corrects for using the sample mean, giving an unbiased estimate (B1); dividing by n underestimates on average (B1)

Q28Unbiased estimates / sample mean · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) Explain why dividing by n tends to underestimate the population variance. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 19.2

  2. (b) Deviations are measured from the sample mean, which is the value that minimises Σ(x − a)²; so Σ(x − x̄)² is on average smaller than Σ(x − μ)², and dividing by n − 1 corrects this
