Q1Confidence intervals · Core · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A machine fills bags of rice. The masses of the bags have standard deviation 4.5 g. A random sample of 50 bags has mean mass 502.3 g. Calculate a 95% confidence interval for the population mean mass, giving the end-points correct to 4 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why it was not necessary to assume that the masses are normally distributed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (501.1, 503.5)
    M1 502.3 ± z × 4.5/√50. B1 z = 1.96. A1 (501.1, 503.5).
  2. (b) The sample is large (n = 50), so by the Central Limit Theorem the sample mean is approximately normally distributed
    B1 n large, CLT applies.
Q2Confidence intervals · Core · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The times, x minutes, taken by a random sample of 40 customers to be served at a bank in Faisalabad are summarised by Σx = 1236 and Σx² = 38 900. Calculate unbiased estimates of the population mean and variance. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate a 90% confidence interval for the population mean time. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) mean 30.9; variance 18.1
    B1 x̄ = 30.9. M1 s² = (38 900 − 1236²/40)/39. A1 18.1.
  2. (b) (29.79, 32.01)
    M1 30.9 ± z√(s²/40). B1 z = 1.645. A1 (29.79, 32.01).

Q12Confidence intervals · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A mobile network claims that 40% of its customers use its streaming service. In a random sample of 450 customers, 150 use the service. Calculate an approximate 90% confidence interval for the proportion of customers who use the service. [4]
  2. (b) Comment on the network's claim. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate an approximate 99% confidence interval from the same data and state whether it leads to the same conclusion about the claim. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (0.297, 0.370)
    B1 p̂ = 1/3. M1 p̂ ± z√(p̂(1 − p̂)/450). B1 z = 1.645. A1 (0.297, 0.370).
  2. (b) 0.4 lies outside the interval, so the evidence suggests the claim is too high
    B1 0.4 not in the interval, claim doubtful.
  3. (c) (0.276, 0.391); yes, 0.4 still lies outside the interval, so the claim still appears too high
    M1 1/3 ± 2.576√((1/3)(2/3)/450). A1 (0.276, 0.391) and 0.4 not in the interval, same conclusion.

Q21Unbiased estimates / sample mean · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student found the variance of a random sample of 25 values by dividing Σ(x − x̄)² by 25, obtaining 18.4. Find an unbiased estimate of the population variance. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why dividing by n tends to underestimate the population variance. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 19.2
    M1 multiply by 25/24. A1 19.2.
  2. (b) Deviations are measured from the sample mean, which is the value that minimises Σ(x − a)²; so Σ(x − x̄)² is on average smaller than Σ(x − μ)², and dividing by n − 1 corrects this
    B1 deviations from x̄ rather than μ. B1 Σ(x − x̄)² ≤ Σ(x − μ)², so dividing by n gives values that are too small on average.

Q23Central Limit Theorem · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The number of phone calls received per day by a helpline follows a Poisson distribution with mean 3.6. The numbers of calls on 40 randomly chosen days are recorded. Q27Central Limit Theorem · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The continuous random variable X has probability density function f(x) = 3x²/8 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2, and f(x) = 0 otherwise. Show that E(X) = 1.5 and Var(X) = 0.15. [3]

Show answer
  1. (a) E(X) = ∫3x³/8 dx = 1.5; E(X²) = ∫3x⁴/8 dx = 2.4; Var(X) = 2.4 − 2.25 = 0.15
    B1 E(X) = 1.5. M1 E(X²) = 2.4. A1 Var = 0.15.
  2. (b) 0.207
    M1 X̄ ~ N(1.5, 0.15/40) by CLT. M1 z = −0.05/√0.00375 = -0.816. A1 0.207.
