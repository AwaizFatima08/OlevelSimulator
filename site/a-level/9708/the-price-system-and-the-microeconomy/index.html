Q1Market equilibrium and interrelated markets · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Printers and ink cartridges are complements. The price of printers falls sharply. What is most likely to happen in the market for ink cartridges? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B price rises and quantity bought rises
    Cheaper printers → more printers bought → demand for cartridges shifts right → higher equilibrium price and quantity. A is wrong: it describes an increase in supply, not demand. D ignores the link between complements.
Q2Market equilibrium and interrelated markets · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

The daily market for tomatoes in Karachi is described by:Qd = 240 − 4PQs = −30 + 5Pwhere P is the price in Rs per kg and Q is in thousand kg per day.

  1. (a) Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity. [2]
  2. (b) The city government fixes the price at Rs 24 per kg. Calculate the excess demand at this price. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how, without price control, the price mechanism would remove a shortage in this market. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = Rs 30 per kg, Q = 120 thousand kg per day
    M1 240 − 4P = −30 + 5P → 9P = 270. A1 P = Rs 30, Q = 240 − 120 = 120 thousand kg.
  2. (b) Excess demand = 54 thousand kg per day
    M1 Qd = 240 − 96 = 144; Qs = −30 + 120 = 90. A1 excess demand = 54 thousand kg.
  3. (c) Buyers compete and bid the price up; higher price contracts quantity demanded and extends quantity supplied until the market clears at Rs 30.
    1 mark each: shortage leads buyers/sellers to raise price (signal); higher price gives incentive to sellers → extension of supply (movement along supply); higher price rations → contraction of demand; process continues until Qd = Qs at equilibrium.

Q4Market equilibrium and interrelated markets · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The weekly market for bicycles in Hanoi is described by Qd = 900 − 3P and Qs = 100 + 5P, where P is the price in US$ and Q is the number of bicycles (invented data).

  1. (a) Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity. [2]
  2. (b) A new duty on imported bicycle parts changes supply to Qs = 20 + 5P. Calculate the new equilibrium price and quantity. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate total spending on bicycles before and after the change, and explain why spending rose even though fewer bicycles were sold. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = $100, Q = 600 bicycles per week
    M1 900 − 3P = 100 + 5P, so 8P = 800. A1 P = 100, Q = 600.
  2. (b) P = $110, Q = 570 bicycles per week
    M1 900 − 3P = 20 + 5P, so 8P = 880. A1 P = 110, Q = 570.
  3. (c) $60,000 → $62,700 a week (a rise of $2,700). The price rose by 10% but quantity fell by only 5%, so demand is price inelastic over this range.
    1 both totals (600 × 100 and 570 × 110); 1 percentage price rise larger than percentage fall in quantity (inelastic demand).

Q7Market equilibrium and interrelated markets · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Invented data. In 2025 floods cut a South Asian country's cotton crop by 28%. The world price of raw cotton rose from 78 US cents per pound in 2024 to 96 US cents per pound in 2025.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage change in the world price of cotton between 2024 and 2025. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain, with the help of a demand and supply diagram, the likely effect of the rise in the cotton price on the market for polyester fibre. [3]
  3. (c) Cottonseed oil is produced from the seeds left after cotton is ginned. Consider the effect of the smaller cotton crop on the market for cottonseed oil. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) +23.1%
    M1 (96 − 78) ÷ 78 × 100. A1 23.1% (increase).
  2. (b) Polyester is a substitute: demand for polyester shifts right, raising its equilibrium price and quantity.
    1 polyester and cotton are substitutes (competitive demand); 1 demand curve for polyester shifts right (diagram with labelled axes P, Q and D1→D2); 1 equilibrium price and quantity both rise.
  3. (c) Joint supply: a smaller crop reduces supply of cottonseed, so supply of oil shifts left, price rises and quantity falls; the size depends on stocks, imports and PED.
    1 joint supply identified; 1 supply of cottonseed oil shifts left → price up, quantity down; 1 consideration, e.g. effect smaller if stocks held or imports of other edible oils available, or if demand for the oil is price elastic as other cooking oils substitute.
Q8Market equilibrium and interrelated markets · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the market for a good is linked to the markets for its complements and for goods produced in joint supply with it, and consider whether a fall in the price of a good will always change the equilibrium in a related market. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) Complements: lower price of good A raises demand for complement B (P and Q of B rise). Joint supply: changes in output of A change supply of its by-product. A related market changes only if the price fall causes a change in quantity of A and the link is strong.
    Mark bands (8): knowledge and understanding up to 3, analysis up to 3, evaluation up to 2. Knowledge: definitions of complements (joint demand), joint supply, a related market. Analysis: price of A falls → extension of demand for A → demand for complement shifts right (diagram); if price of A falls because of lower demand, output of A falls → supply of joint product shifts left → its price rises (diagram). Evaluation: effect depends on how strong the link is (cross elasticity), on the PED of A (if inelastic, little extra quantity), on whether the fall in price is caused by demand or supply, and on time; a weakly related good will hardly change. Judgement: not always, and the size varies.

Q10Market equilibrium and interrelated markets · Standard · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

Invented data. A government rule requires petrol to contain 10% ethanol, which is made from molasses. Molasses is a by-product of crushing sugar cane to make white sugar. In one year the price of molasses rose from Rs 18,000 to Rs 27,000 per tonne.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage change in the price of molasses. [2]
  2. (b) Molasses and white sugar are in joint supply. Explain the likely effect of the higher price of molasses on the market for white sugar. [4]
  3. (c) Assess whether households in Pakistan will gain from the ethanol-blending rule. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) +50%
    M1 (27,000 − 18,000) ÷ 18,000 × 100. A1 50%.
  2. (b) Mills earn more from each tonne of cane crushed, so they buy and crush more cane; this increases the supply of white sugar (supply shifts right), lowering the price of sugar and raising the quantity traded.
    1 joint supply: both come from crushing cane; 1 higher molasses price makes crushing more profitable; 1 supply of sugar shifts right; 1 sugar price falls, quantity rises.
  3. (c) Possibly: cheaper sugar via joint supply, but the size depends on how much extra cane can be grown (PES of cane), the PED for sugar, possible higher fuel costs, and whether cane land replaces food crops. Judgement needed.
    Up to 4 for analysis: lower sugar price; extra cane raises demand for land/inputs; cane price may rise; fuel price effect of ethanol blend. Up to 2 for evaluation: depends on supply response of cane in the short run, share of sugar in household budgets; reasoned conclusion.

Q12Market equilibrium and interrelated markets · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

The Punjab government is considering a subsidy for each electric motorcycle sold in Lahore. Most motorcycles in the city currently run on petrol.

  1. (a) Identify the relationship between electric motorcycles and electricity, and between electric motorcycles and petrol motorcycles. [2]
  2. (b) Assess whether a government subsidy for electric motorcycles in Lahore will reduce the price of petrol. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Electric motorcycles and electricity: complements (joint demand). Electric and petrol motorcycles: substitutes (competitive demand).
    1 mark for each correct relationship.
  2. (b) Possibly a small fall in petrol demand, but the effect on the petrol price is likely to be small: Pakistan's petrol price depends on world oil prices, the exchange rate, taxes and government pricing, and motorcycles are only part of petrol demand.
