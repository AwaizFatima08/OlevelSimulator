Q1Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State four reasons why bacteria are useful in biotechnology. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Reproduce rapidly; can make complex molecules; few ethical concerns over their use and manipulation; have plasmids (can be genetically modified easily); can be grown in fermenters cheaply.
    1 mark each, max 4.
Q2Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A baker in Peshawar mixes flour, water, sugar and yeast to make dough for naan.

  1. (a) Write the word equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the dough rises when left in a warm place. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest what happens to the ethanol when the naan is baked. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide
    B1 ethanol; B1 carbon dioxide (with glucose as reactant).
  2. (b) Yeast respires and produces carbon dioxide gas; bubbles of gas are trapped in the dough, making it expand.
    B1 carbon dioxide produced; B1 gas trapped in dough.
  3. (c) It evaporates (because of the high temperature).
    B1.
Q3Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A factory in Swat makes apple juice.

  1. (a) Explain how the enzyme pectinase is used in the production of fruit juice. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pectinase breaks down pectin in plant cell walls; cells break open more easily, so more juice is released; the juice is clearer (less cloudy).
    B1 breaks down pectin; B1 cell walls; B1 more juice / clearer juice.
Q4Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is the enzyme lactase used to produce? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C lactose-free milk
    Lactase breaks down lactose into glucose and galactose, so the milk can be drunk by people who are lactose intolerant. Clear fruit juice is made using pectinase.
Q5Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Yeast is used to produce a biofuel from sugar cane. Which product of yeast respiration is used as the fuel? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B ethanol
    Anaerobic respiration in yeast produces ethanol, which can be burned as a fuel. Lactic acid is made by anaerobic respiration in muscles, not yeast.
Q6Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In a biogas digester, anaerobic bacteria break down cow dung. Which gas is the main fuel produced? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B methane
    Biogas is mainly methane, which burns as a fuel. Carbon dioxide is a minor part of biogas and is not the fuel; oxygen is absent because the digester is anaerobic; hydrogen sulphide is only a small contaminant.
Q7Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a biogas digester on a farm produces methane from cow dung. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cow dung is put into a sealed digester with water. Anaerobic bacteria decompose the dung in the absence of oxygen, releasing methane (and carbon dioxide). The gas is collected at the top and used as a fuel for cooking or heating.
    1 mark: anaerobic bacteria decompose the dung. 1 mark: no oxygen present (sealed digester). 1 mark: methane is collected and used as fuel.
Q8Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Mycoprotein is a food made by growing a fungus in a fermenter on glucose syrup. State two advantages of mycoprotein compared with meat. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: it grows rapidly in a fermenter; it needs much less land and water than farming animals; no animals are killed; it is high in protein and low in fat.
    1 mark for each advantage, up to 2.
Q9Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Papaya contains the enzyme papain, a protease. Explain how papain could be used to make meat more tender. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Papain is a protease: it breaks down the proteins in the muscle fibres and connective tissue, so the meat becomes softer.
    1 mark: papain is a protease that digests proteins. 1 mark: the muscle and connective tissue proteins are broken down, so the meat softens.
Q10Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the enzyme amylase can be used to convert starch into a maltose syrup. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Amylase is added to a starch solution at its optimum temperature and pH. It breaks the starch down into maltose, so the solution becomes a maltose syrup. The enzyme is then removed or inactivated, and the syrup is filtered.
    1 mark: amylase breaks starch down into maltose. 1 mark: used at optimum temperature and pH. 1 mark: the enzyme is removed or inactivated and the syrup is purified.
Q11Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 6 style · 3 marks

A student leaves a slice of bread in a sealed bag at room temperature (25 °C) and another slice in a sealed bag in a fridge (4 °C). After 5 days, mould is visible on the slice at 25 °C only.

  1. (a) Predict how the amount of mould on the two slices would compare after 5 days. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the difference between the two slices. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) More mould on the slice at 25 °C; little or none on the slice at 4 °C.
    1 mark: more mould at 25 °C than at 4 °C.
  2. (b) Mould grows by enzyme-controlled reactions that work faster at warm temperatures, which are closer to the optimum for the fungus; at 4 °C the enzymes work slowly, so the mould grows very little.
    1 mark: enzymes work faster at 25 °C / closer to the optimum. 1 mark: at 4 °C enzyme activity and growth are very slow.
Q12Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why sugar is added to the warm water in which dried yeast is rehydrated before being mixed into bread dough. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the water should be warm but not hot. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The sugar is a respiratory substrate, providing food for the yeast so that it can start to respire and produce carbon dioxide.
    1 mark: sugar is a food/respiratory substrate for the yeast.
  2. (b) Warm water activates the yeast's enzymes, so it respires quickly; very hot water would denature the enzymes and kill the yeast.
    1 mark: warm water gives an optimum temperature for enzymes. 1 mark: hot water denatures the enzymes/kills the yeast.
Q13Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

Equal samples of bread dough were placed in measuring cylinders at different temperatures. Each started at 50 cm³. The volume was read after 30 minutes.Temperature / °C : volume after 30 min / cm³20 : 6230 : 8440 : 9860 : 51

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate of increase in volume, in cm³ per minute, at each temperature. Give your answers to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the result at 60 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest how the student could find the optimum temperature more accurately. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20 °C: 0.40; 30 °C: 1.13; 40 °C: 1.60; 60 °C: 0.03
    (final − 50) ÷ 30 (M1); all four correct (A1): 0.40, 1.13, 1.60, 0.03.
  2. (b) Yeast enzymes are denatured (or yeast cells killed) at high temperature; so little or no respiration / carbon dioxide produced.
    B1 enzymes denatured; B1 little respiration / CO₂.
  3. (c) Repeat with smaller temperature intervals between 30 °C and 60 °C (e.g. 35, 40, 45, 50, 55 °C).
    B1.
Q14Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the enzymes in biological washing powders remove stains of curry and egg from clothes. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why biological washing powders are less effective in very hot water. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lipases break down fats / oils in curry into fatty acids and glycerol; proteases break down proteins in egg into amino acids; the products are small and soluble, so they wash away in water.
    B1 lipase / fats; B1 protease / protein; B1 soluble products washed away.
  2. (b) The enzymes are denatured at high temperatures; the shape of the active site changes so substrates no longer fit.
    B1 denatured; B1 active site changes shape / substrate does not fit.
Q15Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

  1. (a) The rate of stain removal can be calculated as 1 ÷ time. Calculate the rate at 40 °C, in s⁻¹. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) State the temperature at which the washing powder worked best and explain the result at 60 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one way of making the end point of this experiment easier to judge. [1]

  1. (a) 0.00625 s⁻¹

  2. (b) 40 °C; at 60 °C the enzymes are (partly) denatured so the stain takes much longer to remove.
    B1 40 °C; B1 denatured at 60 °C.
  3. (c) Compare each cloth with a clean, unstained piece of the same cloth / use a light sensor or colorimeter / place cloth against a white background.
    B1.
Q16Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why each of the following is controlled in an industrial fermenter producing insulin from GM bacteria:(i) temperature(ii) pH(iii) oxygen supply(iv) nutrient supply [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the fermenter is cleaned with steam before use. [2]
Show answer

Q17Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Why does a fermenter have a stirrer? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C to keep the contents evenly mixed so that nutrients, oxygen and temperature are uniform
    Stirring keeps the microorganisms, nutrients, oxygen and temperature evenly distributed, so all the culture grows under the same conditions. Stirring does not produce ethanol, sterilise the medium or remove waste.
Q18Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Yoghurt (dahi) is made by adding a starter culture of bacteria to warm milk. Explain why the milk is heated before the bacteria are added. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the mixture is left in a warm place to set. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Heating kills other microorganisms that would compete with the starter bacteria or spoil the dahi, so the desired bacteria grow alone.
    1 mark: kills competing or contaminating microorganisms. 1 mark: the starter culture grows without competition / the product is safe.
  2. (b) The warm temperature is close to the optimum for the bacteria's enzymes, so the bacteria grow and respire quickly, producing lactic acid that makes the milk set.
    1 mark: warm temperature is the optimum for bacterial enzymes / growth. 1 mark: bacteria produce acid (lactic acid) that makes the milk thicken.

  1. (a) The enzymes can be reused many times, lowering cost; the product is not contaminated with enzyme, so it is easier to purify.
    1 mark: enzymes can be reused (lower cost). 1 mark: product is free of enzyme / easier to separate.

Q21Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Yeast ferments glucose to ethanol in a sealed fermenter. Relative molecular mass: glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) = 180; ethanol (C₂H₅OH) = 46. Equation: C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of glucose in 2000 g of glucose. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the theoretical mass of ethanol that could be produced from 2000 g of glucose (100 % yield). Give your answer to the nearest gram. [2]
  3. (c) The actual yield is 85 % of the theoretical yield. Calculate the actual mass of ethanol produced. Give your answer to the nearest gram. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 11.1 mol
    1 mark: 2000 ÷ 180 = 11.1 mol.
  2. (b) 1022 g
    1 mark: 11.1 mol glucose gives 22.2 mol ethanol (2 mol per mole). 1 mark: 22.2 × 46 = 1022 g.
  3. (c) 869 g
    1 mark: 1022 × 0.85 = 869 g.
Q22Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the enzymes in germinating barley help to make beer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Amylase in the germinating barley (malt) converts the starch into maltose. Yeast then ferments the maltose to ethanol and carbon dioxide anaerobically.
    1 mark: amylase converts starch into sugar (maltose). 1 mark: yeast can ferment maltose. 1 mark: anaerobic respiration produces ethanol and carbon dioxide.
Q23Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 7 marks

Samples of 100 g of apple pulp were mixed with different volumes of pectinase and left at 40 °C for 20 minutes, then filtered.Pectinase / cm³ : volume of juice / cm³0.0 : 121.0 : 212.0 : 27, 29, 18, 283.0 : 304.0 : 30.5

  1. (a) Identify the anomalous result for 2.0 cm³ of pectinase and calculate the mean volume of juice without it. [2]
  2. (b) Using your mean, calculate the percentage increase in juice volume with 2.0 cm³ of pectinase compared with no pectinase. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [2]

  2. (b) 133 %

    B1.
  4. (d) Any two of: mass of apple pulp; temperature; time; type of apple; total volume (add water to make volumes equal); filter paper / funnel type.
    1 mark each.
Q24Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Penicillin is made by a fungus grown in a fermenter.Day : penicillin concentration / g per dm³0 : 02 : 0.54 : 4.16 : 8.98 : 10.2

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate of penicillin production between day 2 and day 6, in g/dm³ per day. [2]
  2. (b) The fermenter has a water jacket through which cold water is pumped. Explain why. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the production rate between day 6 and day 8 is lower than between day 2 and day 6. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.1 g/dm³ per day
    (8.9 − 0.5) ÷ (6 − 2) (M1) = 2.1 (A1).
  2. (b) The fungus respires and releases heat energy; cooling stops the temperature rising above the optimum, which would denature enzymes / kill the fungus.
    B1 respiration releases heat; B1 prevents temperature rising / denaturing enzymes.
  3. (c) Nutrients (e.g. glucose) run low; waste products build up / pH changes; so fewer cells are active.
    B1 nutrients used up; B1 waste builds up.
Q25Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how the concentration of sugar affects the rate of carbon dioxide production by yeast. Include apparatus, method, variables to control, how results are recorded and how a conclusion is reached. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Yeast suspension + sugar solutions of different concentrations; collect CO₂ in a gas syringe / inverted measuring cylinder or count bubbles for a fixed time; constant temperature water-bath; repeats; calculate rate.
    1 mark each, max 6: at least five sugar concentrations (e.g. 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 %); same volume and concentration of yeast suspension; same volume of sugar solution; water-bath at a fixed temperature (e.g. 35 °C, thermostatically controlled); allow time to equilibrate; measure CO₂ volume in a gas syringe (or count bubbles / measure balloon) over a fixed time; repeat three times and calculate mean; rate = volume ÷ time; table of concentration against volume of CO₂; graph and describe trend for conclusion.
Q26Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A farm grows 65 tonnes of sugar cane per hectare on 12 hectares. Each tonne of cane can yield 72 dm³ of ethanol.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of ethanol that could be produced from the farm's sugar cane. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the sugar from cane is converted into ethanol. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one disadvantage of using large areas of land to grow sugar cane for biofuel. [1]
Show answer

Q40Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Show answer

Q41Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by 'sticky ends'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the same restriction enzyme must be used to cut both the human DNA and the plasmid. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Short lengths of single-stranded DNA left at the cut ends after a staggered cut by a restriction enzyme; their exposed bases can pair with complementary bases.
    B1 single-stranded overhang; B1 unpaired bases that can pair with complementary bases.
  2. (b) The enzyme cuts at a specific base sequence and leaves the same sticky ends; so the sticky ends of the gene and plasmid are complementary and can pair up.
    B1 same sequence cut / same sticky ends; B1 complementary so they join.
