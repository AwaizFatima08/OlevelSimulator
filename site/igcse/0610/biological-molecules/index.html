Q1Food tests · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student wants to test four separate food samples for (i) starch, (ii) reducing sugar, (iii) protein and (iv) fat. For each nutrient, name the reagent used and state the colour seen when the nutrient is present. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Starch: iodine solution → blue-black. Reducing sugar: Benedict's solution (heated) → green / yellow / orange / brick-red. Protein: biuret solution → purple (violet). Fat: ethanol (then water) → milky white emulsion.
    1 mark per nutrient for the correct reagent AND the correct positive colour (4 × B1). Accept 'cloudy white' for the emulsion test; do not accept 'pink' for biuret.
Q2Food tests · Core · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student in Multan squeezes a ripe mango to obtain some juice. She wants to find out whether the juice contains reducing sugar.

  1. (a) Describe how the student should carry out the Benedict's test on a sample of the juice. [3]
  2. (b) The juice turned from blue to brick-red. State what this shows. [1]
  3. (c) State one safety precaution the student should take during this test. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Put about 2 cm³ of juice in a test-tube, add an equal volume of Benedict's solution, heat in a hot water-bath (about 80 °C) for a few minutes and record the colour.
    B1 add Benedict's solution to the juice; B1 heat in a (hot / boiling) water-bath; B1 heat for a stated time (e.g. 2–5 minutes) / observe colour change. Do not credit 'heat over a Bunsen flame' alone.
  2. (b) The juice contains a reducing sugar (a large amount).
    B1 reducing sugar present; accept 'high concentration of reducing sugar'.
  3. (c) Wear eye protection (goggles) / point the test-tube away from people / use a test-tube holder.
    B1 any one sensible precaution linked to heating.

  2. (b) The student adds iodine solution to the sugar solution. State the colour seen and what this shows. [2]

Q5Food tests · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A breakfast in Lahore consists of a paratha fried in ghee, a boiled egg and a glass of lassi.

  1. (a) Describe how the emulsion test could be used to show that the paratha contains fat. [3]
  2. (b) Name the reagent used to show that the egg contains protein and state the positive result. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Crush / cut the paratha and shake it with ethanol; pour the ethanol (liquid) into a tube of water; a milky white emulsion shows fat.
    B1 shake (crushed) food with ethanol; B1 add the ethanol to water (or add water); B1 cloudy / milky white = fat present.
  2. (b) Biuret solution; the colour changes from blue to purple.
    B1 biuret; B1 purple / violet / lilac.
Q6Food tests · Core · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

A student mixes some grated cheese with water to make a cheese solution for a protein test.

  1. (a) Describe how the biuret test is carried out on the cheese solution. [2]
  2. (b) The student also sets up a tube containing distilled water instead of cheese solution, and adds the same reagents. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Add (a few drops of) dilute sodium hydroxide and then (dilute) copper(II) sulfate solution — or add biuret solution — and shake; no heating.
    B1 add biuret reagent / sodium hydroxide + copper sulfate; B1 shake / mix and observe (no heating needed).
  2. (b) It is a control; it shows the colour of a negative result (blue), so any purple in the test tube must be due to protein in the cheese.
    B1 control / comparison; B1 shows the colour without protein / shows the reagent alone does not turn purple.

A student heats 2 cm³ of each of five glucose solutions with 2 cm³ of Benedict's solution for 5 minutes in a boiling water-bath. She does the same with 2 cm³ of guava juice.Glucose concentration / % : colour after 5 minutes0 : blue0.5 : green1.0 : yellow2.0 : orange4.0 : brick-redguava juice : yellow-orange

  4. (d) Explain why the student kept all the tubes in the same water-bath and recorded the colour after the same time. [2]

Q9Food tests · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student wants to show that roasted groundnuts (peanuts) contain fat.

  1. (a) Explain why the test-tube used for the emulsion test must be dry when the crushed nuts are first shaken with ethanol. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a milky white colour appears when the ethanol is poured into water. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the ethanol should not be heated with a Bunsen burner. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fat does not dissolve in water; any water present would form the emulsion too early / stop the fat dissolving in the ethanol, so the result would be unclear.
    B1 fat dissolves in ethanol (not in water); B1 water would interfere / give a false or unclear result.
  2. (b) The fat that was dissolved in ethanol comes out of solution because fat is insoluble in water; tiny droplets of fat are suspended (an emulsion), which scatter light and look cloudy.
    B1 fat insoluble in water / comes out of solution; B1 forms tiny droplets / emulsion.
  3. (c) Ethanol is flammable.
    B1.

Q15Food tests · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

DCPIP is a blue solution that is decolourised by vitamin C. A student finds the volume of each liquid that must be added, drop by drop, to decolourise 1.0 cm³ of DCPIP.Vitamin C solution, 1.0 mg/cm³ : 2.5 cm³Fresh lemon juice : 4.0 cm³Lemon juice boiled for 10 minutes : 6.4 cm³

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of vitamin C needed to decolourise 1 cm³ of the DCPIP solution. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of vitamin C, in mg/cm³, in (i) the fresh lemon juice and (ii) the boiled lemon juice. Give (ii) to 2 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage decrease in vitamin C concentration caused by boiling. [2]
  4. (d) State why a larger volume of boiled juice was needed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.5 mg
    1.0 mg/cm³ × 2.5 cm³ = 2.5 mg (B1).
  2. (b) (i) 0.625 mg/cm³; (ii) 0.39 mg/cm³
    (i) 2.5 ÷ 4.0 = 0.625 (B1); (ii) 2.5 ÷ 6.4 = 0.3906 → 0.39 (B1).
  3. (c) 37.5 %
    (0.625 − 0.3906) ÷ 0.625 × 100 (M1) = 37.5 % (A1). Accept 37.6 % if the rounded value 0.39 is used.
  4. (d) Boiling destroys (breaks down) some of the vitamin C, so the juice has less vitamin C per cm³.
    B1.
Q16Food tests · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A length of Visking tubing (a partially permeable membrane) is filled with a mixture of starch suspension and glucose solution. The tubing is rinsed and placed in a tube of distilled water.

  1. (a) Predict the results of an iodine test and a Benedict's test on the water outside the tubing after 30 minutes. Explain your predictions. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest what this model shows about digestion in the small intestine. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Iodine: stays orange-brown — no starch, because starch molecules are too large to pass through the tubing. Benedict's: green/yellow/orange/red — reducing sugar present, because glucose molecules are small enough to diffuse out through the tubing.
    B1 iodine negative; B1 starch molecules too large; B1 Benedict's positive; B1 glucose small, diffuses through (partially permeable) tubing.
  2. (b) Large insoluble molecules like starch must be broken down into small soluble molecules (e.g. glucose) before they can be absorbed through the gut wall into the blood.
    B1 large molecules cannot be absorbed; B1 must be digested into small soluble molecules first.
Q17Food tests · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to compare the vitamin C content of freshly squeezed orange juice with orange juice that has been kept in an open glass at room temperature for 24 hours. Include the apparatus, method, variables to control, how results are recorded and how a conclusion is reached. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) DCPIP titration: same volume of DCPIP; add each juice drop by drop from a burette/syringe until decolourised; record volume; repeat three times; less volume = more vitamin C.
    1 mark each, max 6: use DCPIP (blue, decolourised by vitamin C); fixed volume of DCPIP (e.g. 1 cm³) in a test-tube; add juice from burette / graduated pipette / syringe drop by drop, shaking; record volume of juice when blue colour disappears; same oranges / same juice divided into two portions; same temperature / same concentration of DCPIP; repeat at least three times and calculate a mean; a smaller volume of juice needed means more vitamin C; table with headings 'volume of juice / cm³' for each juice; safety: eye protection.
Q18Food tests · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student times how long it takes for Benedict's solution to show the first colour change (from blue to green) when heated with glucose solutions in a water-bath at 90 °C.Glucose concentration / % : time / s0.2 : 1500.4 : 950.6 : 700.8 : 521.0 : 40

  1. (a) Describe the trend in the results. [1]
  2. (b) A sample of sugar-cane juice, diluted in the same way, took 80 s to show the first colour change. Assuming the change is linear between the two nearest readings, estimate the glucose concentration of the diluted juice. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why judging the 'first colour change' is a source of error and suggest how to reduce it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) As glucose concentration increases, the time for the first colour change decreases (falling more slowly at higher concentrations).
    B1.
  2. (b) 0.52 %
    80 s lies between 0.4 % (95 s) and 0.6 % (70 s); 0.4 + (95 − 80)/(95 − 70) × 0.2 (M1) = 0.52 % (A1).
  3. (c) The change is gradual and judged by eye, so it is subjective; compare against a reference tube / white background or use a colorimeter.
