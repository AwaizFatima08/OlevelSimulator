Q1Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A arachnids
    Arachnids have two body parts (cephalothorax and abdomen), four pairs of legs and no antennae. Insects have three pairs of legs and three body parts; crustaceans have more than four pairs of legs and two pairs of antennae.
Q2Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A wildlife survey in the Margalla Hills records a common leopard and a rose-ringed parakeet.

  1. (a) State three features that all mammals have but birds do not have. [3]
  2. (b) State two features that birds have that mammals do not have. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fur / hair on the skin; mammary glands (produce milk); external ears (pinnae).
    1 mark each for any three of: fur/hair; mammary glands / feed young on milk; external ear (pinna); accept: give birth to live young (most), diaphragm, different types of teeth. Do not accept 'warm-blooded' (birds are too).
  2. (b) Feathers; a beak (and lay hard-shelled eggs).
    1 mark each for any two of: feathers; beak; wings (forelimbs as wings); lay eggs with hard shells.
Q3Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the group of arthropods to which an organism with a body in three parts (head, thorax and abdomen), three pairs of legs and one pair of antennae belongs. [1]
  2. (b) A woodlouse has seven pairs of legs and two pairs of antennae. Name its arthropod group. [1]
  3. (c) A centipede has a long body made of many similar segments, each with one pair of legs. Name its arthropod group. [1]
  4. (d) State one feature that all arthropods share. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Insects
    B1 insects.
  2. (b) Crustaceans
    B1 crustaceans.
  3. (c) Myriapods
    B1 myriapods.
  4. (d) Jointed legs / an exoskeleton / a segmented body.
    B1 any one of: jointed legs (limbs); exoskeleton; segmented body.
Q4Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the kingdom to which each organism belongs: (i) Chlorella, a single-celled organism with a nucleus and chloroplasts that lives in ponds; (ii) a bacterium that lives in soil; (iii) a mushroom. [3]
  2. (b) State one feature found in all prokaryote cells that is not found in protoctist cells. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Protoctists; (ii) Prokaryotes; (iii) Fungi.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) No nucleus – DNA is a circular chromosome free in the cytoplasm (plasmids may be present).
    B1 no nucleus / circular DNA / plasmids. Do not accept 'cell wall' (some protoctists have walls).
Q5Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature is found in protoctists but NOT in bacteria? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C a nucleus surrounded by a nuclear envelope
    Protoctists are eukaryotes, so their genetic material is inside a nucleus with a nuclear envelope; bacteria are prokaryotes and have no nucleus. A cell membrane and ribosomes are found in both groups. Circular DNA lying free in the cytoplasm is a bacterial feature, not a protoctist one.
Q6Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature is shared by organisms in the fungus kingdom and the prokaryote kingdom but NOT by organisms in the plant kingdom? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B cell walls are not made of cellulose
    Fungi and prokaryotes both have cell walls, but neither wall is made of cellulose, whereas plant walls are cellulose. A is wrong because plants also have cell walls; prokaryotes have no nucleus (C).
Q7Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A frog and a monitor lizard are both vertebrates. Describe two differences between the skin and eggs of amphibians and reptiles. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why amphibians usually need to return to water or very damp places to breed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Skin: amphibians have moist, smooth skin without scales; reptiles have dry skin covered in scales. Eggs: amphibians lay eggs without shells (in water / jelly); reptiles lay eggs with soft, leathery shells (on land).
    B1 amphibian skin moist / no scales; B1 reptile skin dry / scaly; B1 amphibian eggs no shell / laid in water; B1 reptile eggs leathery / rubbery shell / laid on land. Comparative pairs needed for full marks.
  2. (b) Their eggs have no shell, so they would dry out (lose water) on land; the eggs must be fertilised and develop in water.
    B1 eggs lack a shell / waterproof covering so dry out; accept larvae (tadpoles) live in water.
Q8Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A farmer in Sindh grows wheat and maize. Both are monocotyledons. State two features of the leaves or flowers that show they are monocotyledons. [2]
  2. (b) Mustard and chickpea plants are dicotyledons. Give two ways their leaves or seeds differ from those of monocotyledons. [2]
  3. (c) Ferns are also in the plant kingdom. State how the reproduction of ferns differs from that of flowering plants. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Leaves have parallel veins; flower parts are in threes (or multiples of three).
    1 mark each for any two of: parallel veins; long narrow leaves; flower parts in threes/multiples of three; seed has one cotyledon.
  2. (b) Dicot leaves have a branching network of veins (not parallel); dicot seeds have two cotyledons (not one).
    1 mark each for any two comparative points: net/branched veins vs parallel; two cotyledons vs one; flower parts in fours or fives vs threes; leaves usually broad vs narrow.
  3. (c) Ferns do not produce flowers or seeds; they reproduce using spores.
    B1 spores (no flowers / no seeds).
Q9Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

In a practical lesson a student is given four preserved vertebrate specimens, labelled P, Q, R and S, and the following notes about each.

  1. (a) Specimen P has scales, gills and fins. Specimen Q has moist skin with no scales and four limbs. Name the vertebrate group of P and of Q. [2]
  2. (b) Specimen R has dry scaly skin and lays leathery eggs. Specimen S has feathers and a beak. Name the groups of R and S. [2]
  3. (c) State one feature that the student should look for, using a hand lens, to confirm that a preserved specimen is a mammal rather than a bird. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) P: fish. Q: amphibian.
    B1 P fish; B1 Q amphibian.
  2. (b) R: reptile. S: bird.
    B1 R reptile; B1 S bird.
  3. (c) Hair / fur on the skin (or external ears, mammary glands / nipples).
    B1 hair/fur; accept pinna, nipples/mammary glands.
Q10Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Complete the comparison: the number of pairs of legs in (i) an insect, (ii) an arachnid. [2]
  2. (b) A student observes an animal on a beach in Gwadar. It has a hard, chalky exoskeleton, ten jointed legs, two pairs of antennae and compound eyes on stalks. Identify its group and give two reasons for your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 3 pairs; (ii) 4 pairs.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Crustacean: it has more than four pairs of legs (five pairs) and two pairs of antennae (also calcified exoskeleton).
    B1 crustacean; B1 more than 4 pairs of legs / 5 pairs; B1 two pairs of antennae; accept chalky (calcium carbonate) exoskeleton as one reason.
Q11Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which kingdom contains organisms whose cell walls are made of chitin? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Fungi
    Fungal cell walls are made of chitin. Plant cell walls are cellulose and bacterial cell walls are peptidoglycan. Chitin also forms insect exoskeletons, but insects are animals, not fungi.
Q12Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement about viruses is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B They contain genetic material inside a protein coat and can only reproduce inside living cells.
    A virus is a protein coat around genetic material (DNA or RNA); it has no cell structures, so it reproduces only inside host cells. Viruses are not placed in any of the five kingdoms (D) and have no ribosomes or membrane of their own.
Q13Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Yeast is placed in the fungus kingdom even though it is single-celled. State two features of fungal cells that yeast shows. [2]
  2. (b) Most fungi, such as bread mould, are made of thread-like structures. Name these structures. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why fungi are not classified as plants, even though both have cell walls and do not move from place to place. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A nucleus; a cell wall that is not made of cellulose; (stores carbohydrate as glycogen; no chloroplasts).
    1 mark each for any two of: has a nucleus; cell wall not made of cellulose (accept chitin); no chloroplasts / does not photosynthesise; stores glycogen.
  2. (b) Hyphae (forming a mycelium).
