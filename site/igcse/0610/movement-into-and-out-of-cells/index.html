Q1Osmosis & water potential · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement describes osmosis? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B the net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential through a partially permeable membrane
    Osmosis is the net movement of water from higher to lower water potential (dilute to concentrated solution) across a partially permeable membrane. A reverses the direction; C describes solutes, not water; D describes active transport.
Q2Osmosis & water potential · Core · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Define the term osmosis. [2]
  2. (b) A strip of onion epidermis is placed in a concentrated salt solution. Describe how the cells will appear after 15 minutes under a light microscope. [2]
  3. (c) The onion strip is then moved into distilled water. Predict and explain what happens to the cells. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential to a region of lower water potential, through a partially permeable membrane.
    1 mark: net movement of water (molecules) from higher to lower water potential / dilute to concentrated solution; 1 mark: through a partially permeable membrane.
  2. (b) The vacuole/cytoplasm shrinks and the cell membrane pulls away from the cell wall; the cells are plasmolysed.
    1 mark each for any two: vacuole smaller; membrane/cytoplasm pulled away from cell wall; cell wall unchanged in shape; cells described as plasmolysed.
  3. (c) Water enters the cells by osmosis because the distilled water has a higher water potential than the cell sap; the vacuole swells and the cells become turgid; the cell wall stops them bursting.
    1 mark each: water enters by osmosis; water potential of water higher than cell contents; cells become turgid / membrane pushed against wall (cell wall prevents bursting).
Q3Osmosis & water potential · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

  1. (a) Explain how the salt helps to stop bacteria and fungi growing on the vegetables. [3]
  2. (b) The vegetables become softer and shrink in the salty mixture. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The salt gives a solution with a very low water potential; water leaves the bacterial and fungal cells by osmosis; without enough water their cells cannot carry out metabolic reactions / they die or cannot reproduce.

  2. (b) Water leaves the vegetable cells by osmosis into the salty mixture; the cells lose turgor / become flaccid or plasmolysed.
    1 mark: water leaves vegetable cells by osmosis; 1 mark: cells lose turgor / become flaccid.

Q7Osmosis & water potential · Standard · Paper 6 style · 10 marks

A student cut five potato cylinders, recorded their masses and placed each in a different sucrose solution for 40 minutes at 25 °C. The cylinders were then blotted dry and reweighed.Concentration (mol/dm³) | initial mass (g) | final mass (g)0.0 | 2.50 | 2.780.2 | 2.48 | 2.620.4 | 2.52 | 2.470.6 | 2.46 | 2.300.8 | 2.50 | 2.21

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage change in mass for the cylinders in 0.4 mol/dm³ and in 0.8 mol/dm³ sucrose solution. Give each answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) The percentage changes for 0.0, 0.2 and 0.6 mol/dm³ are +11.2 %, +5.6 % and −6.5 %. Use the results to estimate the concentration of sucrose solution that would cause no change in mass. Show your reasoning. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the cylinders in 0.8 mol/dm³ sucrose solution lost mass. [3]
  4. (d) Why did the student calculate percentage change in mass rather than using change in mass? [1]
  5. (e) State two variables that the student should keep constant in this investigation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.4 mol/dm³: −2.0 %; 0.8 mol/dm³: −11.6 %
    (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100. 0.4: (2.47 − 2.52) ÷ 2.52 × 100 = −2.0 %; 0.8: (2.21 − 2.50) ÷ 2.50 × 100 = −11.6 %. 1 mark each; the negative sign is needed.
  2. (b) about 0.35 mol/dm³ (accept 0.33–0.38)
    M1: the change in mass is zero between 0.2 (gain, +5.6 %) and 0.4 mol/dm³ (loss, −2.0 %); M2: interpolating, 0.2 + 0.2 × 5.6 ÷ 7.6 ≈ 0.35 mol/dm³ (or read from where the line crosses 0 % on a graph).

    1 mark each: solution has lower water potential than cell contents / cell sap; water moves out by osmosis; through a partially permeable (cell) membrane.
  4. (d) The starting masses of the cylinders were different, so percentage change allows a fair comparison.
    B1: initial masses were not all the same / to compare fairly.
  5. (e) Any two of: size/surface area of the cylinders; time in the solution; temperature; volume of solution; type/source of potato.
    1 mark each for any two valid controlled variables. Do not accept 'mass' alone.

Q10Osmosis & water potential · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by turgor pressure. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why a plant cell placed in pure water does not burst. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how turgor pressure helps a young spinach plant to stay upright, and what happens when the plant is short of water. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The pressure of the cell contents (vacuole/cytoplasm) pushing outwards against the cell wall when water enters by osmosis.
    B1: outward pressure of cell contents on cell wall.
  2. (b) Water enters by osmosis and the cell swells, but the strong, inelastic cell wall resists expansion; the wall pushes back, so no more water enters / the cell becomes turgid but does not burst.
    1 mark: cell wall strong / rigid / made of cellulose; 1 mark: resists further expansion / cell becomes turgid.
  3. (c) Turgid cells press against one another and support the stem and leaves; when water is lost, cells lose turgor and become flaccid; the plant wilts / leaves droop.
    1 mark each: turgid cells give support / keep tissues firm; water loss → cells flaccid / turgor lost; plant wilts.

Q12Osmosis & water potential · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Compare the water potential of the soil water with that of the root hair cell sap when the plant is absorbing water. [1]
  2. (b) Explain how a high concentration of sugars and ions in the vacuole of a root hair cell helps the plant to take up water. [2]
  3. (c) A gardener adds far too much dry fertiliser around the roots of a chilli plant. After two days the leaves are wilted. Suggest an explanation. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The soil water has a higher water potential than the cell sap.
    B1.
  2. (b) The solutes lower the water potential of the cell sap below that of the soil water, maintaining a water potential gradient so water enters by osmosis.
    1 mark: lowers water potential of cell; 1 mark: water moves in by osmosis down the gradient.
  3. (c) The fertiliser dissolves in the soil water and lowers its water potential below that of the root hair cells; water leaves the roots by osmosis (or stops entering); less water reaches the leaves and leaf cells lose turgor, so the plant wilts.
    1 mark each: soil water potential lowered / now lower than cells; water lost from roots by osmosis; leaf cells flaccid / plant wilts.
Q13Osmosis & water potential · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

On a farm in Sindh, irrigation water with a high salt content was used for several seasons. Salt built up in the soil, and young wheat plants wilted even though the soil was wet.

  1. (a) Explain, in terms of water potential, why the wheat plants wilted. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why flushing the field with plenty of fresh canal water before the next crop helps the new seedlings. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The salt raised the solute concentration of the soil water, so its water potential became lower than that of the root hair cells. Water left the root hair cells by osmosis (or could no longer enter), so less water reached the leaves; leaf cells lost water, became flaccid and the plant wilted.
    1 mark each for any four: soil water now has lower water potential than root cell sap; water moves out of / does not enter roots by osmosis; through partially permeable membrane; less water carried in xylem to leaves; leaf cells lose turgor / become flaccid; so leaves wilt.
  2. (b) Fresh water dissolves and washes the salt down out of the root zone, so the soil water has a higher water potential than the root cells and water can enter the roots by osmosis.
    1 mark: salt washed away / diluted; 1 mark: soil water potential higher than root cells so water enters by osmosis.

Q16Osmosis & water potential · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A patient in a hospital in Karachi who is dehydrated after severe diarrhoea is given a drip of saline (a salt solution of about 0.9 %) directly into a vein.

  1. (a) If its water potential matches the plasma, there is no net movement of water into or out of the blood cells by osmosis, so the cells keep their normal shape and size and can work properly.

  3. (c) Respiration is slower at low temperature because enzymes work more slowly, so less energy is available for active transport.

Show answer
  1. (a) A uptake of nitrate ions from soil into root hair cells
    Nitrate ions are usually more concentrated inside root hair cells than in soil water, so they are taken up against the gradient by active transport using carrier proteins. Oxygen moves by diffusion (C) and water enters by osmosis (D).

  1. (a) A Less oxygen reduced aerobic respiration in the root cells, so less energy was available for active transport of potassium ions.

Q25Active transport · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Copy and complete the comparison of three transport processes. For each process state: (i) whether it needs energy from respiration, (ii) the direction of movement in relation to the concentration gradient, (iii) whether it needs a membrane.Processes: diffusion, osmosis, active transport. [6]
  2. (b) A root cell takes up sodium ions by active transport. Suggest why the rate of uptake stops increasing at high external sodium ion concentrations, even with plenty of oxygen. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Diffusion: no energy from respiration (uses kinetic energy of particles); down the concentration gradient; no membrane needed. Osmosis: no energy from respiration; water moves from higher to lower water potential (down its gradient); needs a partially permeable membrane. Active transport: needs energy from respiration; against the concentration gradient (lower to higher); needs a membrane with carrier proteins.
    1 mark per correct pair of entries, six marks in total: diffusion energy + direction; diffusion membrane; osmosis energy + direction; osmosis partially permeable membrane; active transport energy; active transport against gradient + carrier proteins.
  2. (b) All the carrier proteins are in use (saturated), so the number of carriers limits the rate.
    1 mark: carrier proteins all occupied / limited number of carriers; 1 mark: carriers become the limiting factor.

Q27Diffusion · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Define diffusion. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why oxygen moves from the air in the alveoli into the blood. [2]
  3. (c) State two features of the alveoli that increase the rate of diffusion. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration (down a concentration gradient), as a result of their random movement.
    1 mark: net movement from higher to lower concentration / down gradient; 1 mark: due to random movement of particles.
  2. (b) The oxygen concentration in the alveoli is higher than in the blood arriving at the lungs, so oxygen diffuses down its concentration gradient into the blood.
    1 mark: higher concentration in alveoli than blood; 1 mark: diffusion down gradient.

    1 mark each.
Q28Diffusion · Standard · Paper 6 style · 8 marks

Cubes of pink agar (containing an alkali and an indicator) were cut with sides of 1 cm, 2 cm and 3 cm and placed in dilute hydrochloric acid. The indicator turns colourless when acid reaches it.Side length (cm) | time to become completely colourless (min)1 | 3.52 | 13.03 | 29.5

  1. (a) Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. [3]
  2. (b) Describe the relationship between surface area to volume ratio and the time for the cube to become colourless. [1]
  3. (c) Explain the results. [2]
  4. (d) Use your answers to explain why a large multicellular animal needs a transport system. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 cm cube: surface area 6 cm², volume 1 cm³, ratio 6:1; 2 cm cube: 24 cm², 8 cm³, ratio 3:1; 3 cm cube: 54 cm², 27 cm³, ratio 2:1
    1 mark each: SA = 6 × side², V = side³. 6/1; 24/8 = 3; 54/27 = 2.
  2. (b) The smaller the surface area to volume ratio, the longer the time to become colourless.
    B1.
  3. (c) In smaller cubes there is more surface for the acid to diffuse in through, relative to the volume, and a shorter distance to the centre, so the acid reaches all parts faster.
    1 mark: larger surface area relative to volume; 1 mark: shorter diffusion distance to centre.
  4. (d) Its surface area to volume ratio is small and the distances to cells inside are large, so diffusion alone through the body surface would be too slow to supply cells.
    1 mark: small SA:V / long distances; 1 mark: diffusion too slow, so a transport system (blood) is needed.
Q29Diffusion · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) During the day carbon dioxide diffuses from the air into a leaf. Describe the concentration gradient that causes this. [2]
  2. (b) State and explain three factors that increase the rate of diffusion of a gas into a cell. [3]
  3. (c) The smell of frying pakoras reaches people across a room even when there is no wind. Explain how. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Photosynthesis uses carbon dioxide in the mesophyll cells, so the concentration in the air spaces is lower than in the outside air; CO₂ diffuses in through the stomata down this gradient.
    1 mark: concentration lower inside leaf because used in photosynthesis; 1 mark: diffuses in through stomata from higher to lower concentration.
  2. (b) Any three of: larger surface area – more area for particles to cross; steeper concentration gradient – more net movement; higher temperature – more kinetic energy; shorter distance – particles have less far to travel.
    1 mark each for factor with explanation, any three.
  3. (c) Molecules of the smell move at random and spread from a high concentration near the pan to the lower concentration across the room – net movement by diffusion.
    1 mark: random movement of molecules; 1 mark: from higher to lower concentration (diffusion).
Q30Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 8 marks

A student placed one crystal of potassium manganate(VII) in the centre of each of three Petri dishes of still water at 10 °C, 30 °C and 50 °C. After 15 minutes the distance the purple colour had spread from the crystal was measured.Temperature (°C): 10, 30, 50Distance (mm): 12, 21, 33

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate of spread of the purple colour at 30 °C, in mm per minute. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the effect of temperature on the results. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why it is difficult to measure the distance the colour has spread, and suggest an improvement. [2]
  4. (d) State why the Petri dishes must not be moved during the experiment. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.4 mm/min
    M1: 21 ÷ 15; A1: 1.4 mm/min.
  2. (b) Higher temperature gives the particles more kinetic energy, so they move faster at random; the net movement down the concentration gradient is faster, so the colour spreads further in 15 minutes.
    1 mark: description (higher temperature, greater distance, quote data); 1 mark: more kinetic energy / faster random movement; 1 mark: faster diffusion.
  3. (c) The edge of the colour is not sharp / fades gradually, so the end point is a matter of judgement. Improvement: compare against a colour standard / use a white tile and grid behind the dish / have the same person judge each time.
    1 mark: difficulty; 1 mark: matching improvement.
  4. (d) Moving the water would mix the dye by currents (convection/stirring), not by diffusion alone.
    B1.
