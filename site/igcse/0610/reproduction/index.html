Q1Human reproductive systems & gametes · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name the structure in the human reproductive systems that:(i) produces sperm(ii) carries sperm from the testis to the urethra(iii) secretes fluid containing nutrients for sperm (name one gland)(iv) releases eggs(v) is where the fetus develops [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) testis; (ii) sperm duct; (iii) prostate gland (accept seminal vesicle); (iv) ovary; (v) uterus
    1 mark each.
Q2Human reproductive systems & gametes · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In the human male, which tube carries both urine and semen out of the body (at different times)? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B urethra
    The urethra runs through the penis and carries urine from the bladder and semen during ejaculation. The ureter (C) carries urine from kidney to bladder only; the sperm duct (A) carries sperm only.
Q3Human reproductive systems & gametes · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State the function of each structure in the male reproductive system.(i) scrotum (ii) sperm duct (iii) prostate gland (iv) penis [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) holds the testes outside the body (at a lower temperature) (ii) carries sperm from the testis to the urethra (iii) produces fluid that is added to sperm to make semen (iv) passes semen into the vagina during sexual intercourse (and urine out)
    1 mark each.
Q4Human reproductive systems & gametes · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the structures, in order, through which an egg cell passes from where it is released to where an embryo implants. [2]
  2. (b) State where sperm are deposited during sexual intercourse. [1]
  3. (c) Name the ring of muscle at the lower end of the uterus. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ovary → oviduct → uterus
    B1 oviduct; B1 uterus (in correct order after ovary).
  2. (b) the vagina (near the cervix)
    B1 vagina.
  3. (c) cervix
    B1 cervix.
Q5Human reproductive systems & gametes · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe the function of each part of a human egg cell.(i) jelly coat (ii) cytoplasm (iii) nucleus [3]
  2. (b) State where egg cells are produced. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) protects the egg / changes after fertilisation so that only one sperm can enter (ii) contains energy stores for the early embryo (iii) contains the genetic material (23 chromosomes) from the mother
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) the ovaries
    B1 ovary.
Q6Human reproductive systems & gametes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Where does fertilisation normally take place in humans? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C oviduct
    Sperm meet the egg in the oviduct (fallopian tube); the embryo then moves to the uterus to implant. D is the main distractor: the uterus is where implantation, not fertilisation, occurs.
Q7Human reproductive systems & gametes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a sperm cell is adapted for its function. [3]
  2. (b) Describe two ways an egg cell is adapted for its function. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Flagellum for swimming; many mitochondria to release energy (by respiration) for movement; acrosome containing enzymes to digest through the jelly coat of the egg; haploid nucleus.
    1 mark each for any three features with function.
  2. (b) Energy stores (cytoplasm with nutrients) for the early embryo; jelly coat that changes after fertilisation so only one sperm can enter; haploid nucleus.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q8Human reproductive systems & gametes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) List, in order, the structures that sperm pass through from where they are made to where fertilisation takes place. [3]
  2. (b) State the role of the cervix during birth. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) testis → sperm duct → urethra → (vagina) → cervix → uterus → oviduct
    3 marks for all in correct order; 2 marks for one error; 1 mark for two errors.
  2. (b) It dilates / widens to let the baby pass from the uterus into the vagina.
    B1 dilates / widens.
Q9Human reproductive systems & gametes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A fertility clinic tested semen samples from two men.Man A: 60 million sperm per cm³, volume 3.0 cm³Man B: 4 million sperm per cm³, volume 2.5 cm³

  1. (a) Calculate the total number of sperm in one sample for each man. Give your answers in millions. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate how many times more sperm there are in man A's sample. [1]
  3. (c) Only one sperm fertilises an egg. Suggest why man B is less likely to father a child. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) man A 180 million; man B 10 million
    B1 60 × 3.0 = 180; B1 4 × 2.5 = 10.
  2. (b) 18 times
    B1 180 ÷ 10 = 18.
  3. (c) Most sperm die or are lost on the way through the uterus and oviducts and only a few reach the egg; with a low count it is likely that none reach the egg; many sperm may be needed to break through the layers around the egg.
    B1 most sperm do not reach the oviduct / die on the way; B1 fewer sperm → lower chance any reach / penetrate the egg.
Q10Human reproductive systems & gametes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) In a vasectomy, a small section of each sperm duct is cut and the ends are tied. Explain why the man can no longer father children. [2]
  2. (b) After a vasectomy, the man still produces semen. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sperm made in the testes cannot pass along the sperm ducts to the urethra, so the semen contains no sperm and no egg can be fertilised.
    B1 sperm cannot leave the testes / reach the urethra; B1 no sperm in semen so no fertilisation.
  2. (b) Most of the fluid in semen comes from glands such as the prostate gland, which join the pathway after the cut, so fluid is still released.
    B1 fluid made by prostate gland (and other glands); B1 these add fluid beyond / below the cut point.
Q11Human reproductive systems & gametes · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student looked at a stained sample of bull semen with a microscope.

  1. (a) The image of one whole sperm measured 55 mm long at a magnification of ×1000. Calculate the actual length of the sperm in µm. [2]
  2. (b) The student counted the sperm in five squares of a counting slide: 12, 15, 9, 14, 10. Calculate the mean number per square. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the student counted sperm in five squares instead of one. [1]
  4. (d) State one feature of the sperm that the student would see that helps it swim to the egg. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 55 µm
    M1 55 ÷ 1000 = 0.055 mm; A1 × 1000 = 55 µm.
  2. (b) 12
    B1 60 ÷ 5 = 12.
  3. (c) The sperm are not spread evenly, so a mean of several squares is more representative / reliable.
    B1 more representative / reliable mean.
  4. (d) a long flagellum (tail)
