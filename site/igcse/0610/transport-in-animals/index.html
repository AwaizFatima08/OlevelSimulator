Q1Circulatory systems & the heart · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A fish has a single circulation and a mammal has a double circulation. Explain the difference between these two systems. [2]
  2. (b) State one advantage of a double circulation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Single: blood passes through the heart once for each complete circuit of the body (heart → gills → body → heart). Double: blood passes through the heart twice for each circuit (once to the lungs, once to the rest of the body).
    B1 single = once through heart per circuit; B1 double = twice (pulmonary and systemic circuits).
  2. (b) Blood can be pumped to the body at a higher pressure (after returning from the lungs), so oxygenated blood reaches tissues faster.
    B1; accept keeps oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate.
Q2Circulatory systems & the heart · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Put these structures in the order in which a red blood cell passes through them, starting at the vena cava:left atrium, aorta, right ventricle, lungs, pulmonary vein, right atrium, left ventricle, pulmonary artery [3]
  2. (b) State where in this pathway the blood becomes oxygenated. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta
    B3 all correct; B2 one structure out of place; B1 the right side (RA → RV → pulmonary artery) or the left side correct.
  2. (b) In the lungs (capillaries around the alveoli).
    B1.
Q3Circulatory systems & the heart · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which blood vessel carries oxygenated blood into the left atrium? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C pulmonary vein
    The pulmonary vein brings oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium. The pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart to the lungs.

Q6Circulatory systems & the heart · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than the wall of the right ventricle. [2]
  2. (b) State the function of the septum. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The left ventricle pumps blood all round the body, which needs a higher pressure; the right ventricle only pumps blood to the nearby lungs (low pressure protects lung capillaries). Thicker wall = more muscle = stronger contraction.
    B1 left pumps to whole body / further; B1 needs higher pressure / more muscle for stronger contraction.
  2. (b) Separates oxygenated blood (left side) from deoxygenated blood (right side), so they do not mix.
    B1.
Q7Circulatory systems & the heart · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the valves between the atria and the ventricles and state their function. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the semilunar valves at the base of the aorta and pulmonary artery help the heart to work efficiently. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Atrioventricular valves (bicuspid on the left, tricuspid on the right); they prevent blood flowing back into the atria when the ventricles contract.
    B1 name; B1 prevent backflow into the atria.
  2. (b) They close when the ventricles relax, so blood in the arteries cannot flow back into the ventricles; this keeps blood moving in one direction.
    B1 close when ventricles relax / pressure in artery higher; B1 prevent backflow into ventricles / one-way flow.

  2. (b) Name the hormone that increases heart rate before the match begins. [1]

  2. (b) Adrenaline.
    B1.

Q12Circulatory systems & the heart · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

A student counts her pulse for 15 seconds at one-minute intervals after running. Her resting rate is 72 beats per minute.Time after running / min : beats in 15 s0 : 311 : 272 : 243 : 214 : 305 : 186 : 18

  1. (a) Convert each count into a pulse rate in beats per minute. [2]
  2. (b) Identify the anomalous result and suggest one reason for it. [2]
  3. (c) Using the results, state how long it took for the pulse rate to return to its resting value (72 beats/min). [1]
  4. (d) Explain why counting for 15 seconds and multiplying by 4 is less accurate than counting for a full minute. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0 min: 124, 1 min: 108, 2 min: 96, 3 min: 84, 4 min: 120, 5 min: 72, 6 min: 72
    Multiply each 15-second count by 4 (M1); all correct (A1).
  2. (b) 4 minutes (120 beats/min); e.g. the student miscounted / moved or talked / lost the pulse / counted for longer than 15 s.
    B1 4 min reading; B1 plausible reason.
  3. (c) 5 minutes.
    B1.
  4. (d) Any counting error is multiplied by 4 (a miss of one beat gives an error of 4 beats/min).
    B1.
Q13Circulatory systems & the heart · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Cardiac output is the volume of blood pumped by one ventricle per minute.cardiac output = heart rate × stroke volumeA trained athlete from Karachi has a resting heart rate of 52 beats/min and a stroke volume of 96 cm³. A student who does not train has a resting heart rate of 75 beats/min and a stroke volume of 66 cm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the cardiac output of each person in dm³ per minute. Give your answers to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the athlete has a lower resting heart rate but almost the same cardiac output. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Athlete: 4.99 dm³/min; other student: 4.95 dm³/min
    52 × 96 = 4992 cm³ (M1); 75 × 66 = 4950 cm³ (A1); ÷ 1000 → 4.99 and 4.95 (A1).
  2. (b) Training has made the heart muscle stronger / larger, so each beat pumps more blood (larger stroke volume); fewer beats are needed to deliver the same volume of blood.
    B1 stronger / larger heart muscle; B1 larger stroke volume so fewer beats needed.
Q14Circulatory systems & the heart · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Some babies are born with a hole in the septum between the left and right ventricles. In some of these babies, deoxygenated blood passes through the hole from the right ventricle into the left ventricle.

  1. (a) Explain how this affects the oxygen concentration of the blood in the aorta. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest two symptoms a baby with this condition might show. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle mixes with oxygenated blood in the left ventricle; so blood in the aorta has a lower oxygen concentration than normal.
    B1 mixing of oxygenated and deoxygenated blood; B1 lower oxygen in aorta / to body.
  2. (b) Any two of: tiredness / lack of energy; breathlessness; slow growth / poor feeding; bluish skin or lips.
    1 mark each, linked to less oxygen reaching tissues.

Q16Circulatory systems & the heart · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how the length of time spent exercising affects a person's heart rate. Include what you would measure, how you would make it a fair test, how you would record the results and how you would reach a conclusion. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Measure resting pulse; do the same exercise (e.g. step-ups at a fixed rate) for 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 minutes; measure pulse immediately afterwards for 1 minute; rest until pulse returns to resting before the next trial; repeat and calculate means; plot pulse against time.
    1 mark each, max 6: measure resting pulse first; independent variable – exercise times (at least 4 values); same type and intensity of exercise (e.g. step height and rate set by a metronome); measure pulse at wrist/neck for 60 s (or with a pulse monitor) immediately after; rest until pulse returns to resting rate between trials; same person / same conditions (same time of day, no food or drink); repeat each time and calculate a mean; table: exercise time / min against pulse rate / beats per min; conclusion from graph trend; safety: stop if dizzy.
Q17Blood vessels · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Compare arteries, veins and capillaries by stating for each: the relative thickness of the wall, the size of the lumen, and whether valves are present. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Arteries: thick muscular elastic wall, narrow lumen, no valves (except at base of aorta/pulmonary artery). Veins: thin wall, wide (large) lumen, valves present. Capillaries: wall one cell thick, very narrow lumen, no valves.
    B1 per vessel with all three features correct.

Q19Blood vessels · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why blood flowing out of a cut artery spurts in time with the heartbeat. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each contraction of the ventricles pushes blood into the aorta at high pressure. The pressure rises and falls with each heartbeat, and the elastic artery wall stretches and recoils, so the blood leaves in spurts.
    1 mark: ventricles contract and push blood into arteries at high pressure. 1 mark: pressure rises and falls with each heartbeat. 1 mark: elastic artery wall stretches and recoils, so blood flows out in spurts.
Q20Blood vessels · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the elastic walls of the aorta help to maintain blood flow between heartbeats. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) During ventricular contraction (systole) the aorta wall stretches to take the blood. During relaxation (diastole) the elastic wall recoils, pushing the blood forward so that the flow and pressure are maintained and the surges from the heart are smoothed out.
    1 mark: walls stretch when blood enters during systole. 1 mark: elastic recoil during diastole. 1 mark: pushes blood forward, so flow is maintained. 1 mark: smooths out the surges of pressure.
Q21Blood vessels · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Name the blood vessel that: (i) carries deoxygenated blood from the right ventricle to the lungs; (ii) carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the left atrium; (iii) carries blood from the kidney to the vena cava. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) pulmonary artery; (ii) pulmonary vein; (iii) renal vein
    1 mark each: pulmonary artery; pulmonary vein; renal vein.
Q22Blood vessels · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the structure of a capillary is related to its function. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the structure of an artery is related to its function. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why veins have valves. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Wall is one cell thick so diffusion distance is short; substances (oxygen, glucose) diffuse quickly into tissues / narrow lumen brings red cells close to tissue cells.
    B1 thin wall / one cell thick; B1 short diffusion distance / exchange of materials.
  2. (b) Thick muscular and elastic wall withstands high pressure from the heart; elastic tissue stretches and recoils to maintain pressure / smooth flow.
    B1 thick wall withstands high pressure; B1 elastic recoil.
  3. (c) Blood in veins is at low pressure; valves prevent backflow (keep blood moving towards the heart).
    B1.

Q24Blood vessels · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which blood vessel has the thickest muscular and elastic wall? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C aorta
    The aorta carries blood at high pressure from the left ventricle, so its wall has thick layers of elastic tissue and muscle. The vena cava, pulmonary vein and hepatic portal vein carry blood at lower pressure and have thinner walls.
Q25Blood vessels · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Veins have a wider lumen than arteries, yet the blood pressure in veins is much lower. Explain why. [4]
