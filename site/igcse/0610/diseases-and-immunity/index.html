Q1Defences, antibodies & immunity · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which cells produce antibodies? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A lymphocytes
    Lymphocytes make antibodies. Phagocytes (B) engulf and digest pathogens; they do not make antibodies.
Q2Defences, antibodies & immunity · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State three ways in which the human body stops pathogens from entering it. [3]
  2. (b) Name the type of white blood cell that engulfs and digests pathogens. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three of: skin as a barrier; hairs in the nose trap particles; mucus traps pathogens in the airways; hydrochloric acid in the stomach kills pathogens; blood clotting seals wounds.
    1 mark each for any three mechanical / chemical barriers.
  2. (b) Phagocyte
    B1 phagocyte.
Q3Defences, antibodies & immunity · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State the difference between an antigen and an antibody. [2]
  2. (b) State why an antibody against the measles virus does not protect against the cholera bacterium. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) An antigen is a molecule (on the surface of a pathogen) that triggers an immune response; an antibody is a protein made by lymphocytes that binds to a specific antigen.
    B1 antigen – on pathogen / foreign, causes response; B1 antibody – protein made by lymphocytes / binds antigen.
  2. (b) Antibodies are specific; the antibody's shape is complementary only to the measles antigen.
    B1 specificity / different antigen shapes.
Q4Defences, antibodies & immunity · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how mucus and cilia in the trachea help to protect the body from pathogens. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why people whose cilia are damaged by smoke get more chest infections. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Goblet cells make mucus, which traps pathogens and dust; cilia beat / move the mucus up to the throat; where it is swallowed (and pathogens killed by stomach acid).
    1 mark each: mucus traps pathogens; cilia beat / waft; mucus moved upwards to throat / swallowed.
  2. (b) Mucus and trapped pathogens are not removed, so pathogens stay in the lungs and can multiply.
    B1 mucus / pathogens not removed.

  2. (b) State two ways a person can gain active immunity. [2]

    1 mark each.

Show answer

(a) Vaccine contains weakened / harmless measles pathogen with its antigens; antigens stimulate lymphocytes; lymphocytes produce specific antibodies; memory cells are produced; on later infection memory cells give a faster, larger antibody response, destroying the pathogen before symptoms develop.

Q12Defences, antibodies & immunity · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A person who has had measles rarely catches it again, but people can catch influenza (flu) many times. Suggest an explanation. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why a new flu vaccine is often needed each year. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Measles memory cells remain and recognise the same antigens on reinfection; the flu virus mutates and changes its antigens often; so memory cells / antibodies from earlier infections no longer fit the new antigens.
    1 mark each: memory cells give long-term immunity to measles; flu virus changes antigens (mutation / new strains); existing antibodies / memory cells specific to old antigen shape.
  2. (b) New strains have different antigens, so the vaccine must contain the new antigens.
    B1 antigens change / new strains.
Q13Defences, antibodies & immunity · Standard · Paper 6 style · 7 marks

A student pressed a finger onto three sterile agar plates: (1) before washing, (2) after washing with water only, (3) after washing with soap and water. The plates were incubated for 3 days and the bacterial colonies were counted.unwashed: 184 colonieswater only: 112 coloniessoap and water: 23 colonies

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage reduction in the number of colonies when hands were washed with soap and water, compared with unwashed hands. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Write a conclusion for this investigation. [2]
  3. (c) State two safety precautions the student should take with the plates after they have been touched. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the investigation should be repeated with several students. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 87.5 %
    (184 − 23) ÷ 184 × 100 (M1) = 87.5 % (A1).
  2. (b) Washing hands reduces the number of bacteria transferred; washing with soap and water reduces them much more (87.5 %) than water alone (39.1 %).
    B1 washing reduces colonies; B1 soap more effective than water alone (data optional).
  3. (c) Seal the lid with tape and do not open it; incubate at about 25 °C, not 37 °C (to avoid growing human pathogens); dispose of plates by autoclaving; wash hands afterwards.
    1 mark each for any two.
  4. (d) Different people's hands carry different numbers of bacteria, so results from one person may not be representative; to find a mean / spot anomalies.
    B1 representative / identify anomalous results.

Q16Defences, antibodies & immunity · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

The concentration of antibodies against a virus was measured in a person's blood. The person was exposed to the virus on day 0 and again on day 30.day: 0, 5, 10, 15, 20, 30, 33, 37, 42, 50antibody concentration / arbitrary units: 0, 2, 12, 20, 9, 3, 40, 160, 120, 70

  1. (a) 8 times
    160 ÷ 20 = 8 (B1).

Q17Defences, antibodies & immunity · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

In a polio vaccination campaign in one district of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa, 45 120 of the 48 000 children under five years old received the vaccine.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of children in the district who were vaccinated. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why vaccinating a high percentage of children also protects the children who were not vaccinated. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why health workers aim to vaccinate every child, even in a district where 94 % are already vaccinated. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 94.0 %
    45 120 ÷ 48 000 × 100 (M1) = 94.0 % (A1).
  2. (b) Most people are immune, so the virus cannot pass from person to person; fewer infected people to spread it; chance of an unvaccinated child meeting an infected person is very low (herd immunity).
    1 mark each: few people can be infected / carry the virus; transmission chain broken / spread reduced; unvaccinated child unlikely to come into contact with pathogen; idea of herd / community immunity.
  3. (c) Any remaining unvaccinated group allows the virus to survive and spread / outbreaks can start among the unvaccinated; aim to eradicate the disease.
    Any one sensible suggestion.
Q18Defences, antibodies & immunity · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how blood clotting acts as a defence against disease. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why a person whose blood does not clot properly is more likely to suffer infections after an injury. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) When skin is cut, platelets cause the soluble protein fibrinogen to be converted to insoluble fibrin; fibrin forms a mesh of fibres that traps red blood cells; the clot seals the wound and stops pathogens entering.
    1 mark each: fibrinogen (soluble) changed to fibrin (insoluble); mesh / network traps cells; seals wound so pathogens cannot enter (also limits blood loss).
  2. (b) Wound stays open for longer so more pathogens can enter.
    B1 open wound / entry point for pathogens.

    1 mark each for two sensible reasons.
Q20Defences, antibodies & immunity · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A man in Hyderabad is bitten by a dog that may carry the rabies virus. On the first day, doctors inject him with ready-made rabies antibodies and also give the first dose of rabies vaccine. More vaccine doses are given over the next two weeks.

  1. (a) Explain why the injection of ready-made antibodies is given on the first day. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the vaccine is also given. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It gives immediate (passive) protection, because the person's own lymphocytes would take days / weeks to make enough antibodies; rabies virus could reach the nervous system before that.
    B1 immediate protection / antibodies act at once; B1 own immune response too slow.
  2. (b) The injected antibodies are broken down after a few weeks and no memory cells are made; the vaccine stimulates the person's own lymphocytes to make antibodies and memory cells, giving long-term (active) immunity.
    B1 passive immunity short-lived / no memory cells; B1 vaccine gives active immunity / memory cells.

Q27Pathogens & transmission · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain why viruses can reproduce only inside living cells. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Viruses have no cell structure and no metabolism of their own, so they cannot make new copies without the enzymes and ribosomes of a host cell. They use the host cell's machinery to make new virus particles.
    1 mark: viruses have no cell machinery of their own (no ribosomes or enzymes). 1 mark: they use the host cell's machinery to make copies.
Q28Pathogens & transmission · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks

  1. (a) The pathogen can still be present in the person's body or secretions, and it can be passed to others through contact, droplets or contaminated food before symptoms appear or while they have none.
    1 mark: the pathogen is present in the body/secretions without symptoms. 1 mark: it can be transmitted to others.
Q29Pathogens & transmission · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between direct and indirect transmission of a pathogen. Give one example of each. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Direct transmission: the pathogen passes from one host to another by contact (e.g. touching, coughing droplets, sexual contact). Indirect transmission: the pathogen passes via a carrier such as food, water, an object or a vector (e.g. cholera from water, malaria from a mosquito).
    1 mark: direct transmission by contact. 1 mark: indirect transmission via a carrier. 1 mark: a correct example for each.

Q32Pathogens & transmission · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why HIV is not spread by a handshake or by sharing a meal. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why HIV is classed as a virus and not as a bacterium. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) HIV is spread only through transfer of body fluids such as blood or semen; it does not survive for long outside the body and is not transferred by casual contact.
    1 mark: needs transfer of blood or body fluids. 1 mark: it cannot survive outside the body for long.
  2. (b) A virus is not a cell: it is genetic material in a protein coat. It can only reproduce inside host cells, using their machinery. A bacterium is a single cell that can reproduce on its own.
    1 mark: a virus is not a cell (genetic material in a protein coat). 1 mark: viruses can only reproduce inside host cells.

    1 mark for each sensible method, up to 2.
  3. (c) Completing the course kills all the bacteria and reduces the chance of partial treatment selecting resistant bacteria / of the infection returning.
    1 mark: all bacteria must be killed to prevent recurrence or resistance.

  1. (a) If most people use nets and repellent, fewer mosquitoes are infected, so there are fewer sources of the parasite; this reduces the chance that an uninfected mosquito feeds on an infected person and then bites another person.

Q38Pathogens & transmission · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Show answer

Two bags made of Visking tubing were set up.Bag A was filled with distilled water and placed in concentrated salt solution; bag B was filled with dilute salt solution and placed in distilled water.   1. (a) bag A: −15.0 %; bag B: +3.3 %

Q44Cholera · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how cholera bacteria can spread through a town's drinking water supply. Give three steps. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1) An infected person passes Vibrio cholerae in faeces, which reach a river or well through sewage. 2) The water is contaminated and is drunk by other people. 3) The bacteria reach the small intestine of the new host and multiply there, producing toxin.
    1 mark for each correct step in order: faeces with bacteria enter the water supply; people drink the contaminated water; bacteria multiply in the small intestine.

Show answer
  1. (a) The solution is too dilute: each litre contains half the salts and glucose needed, so less sodium and water is absorbed from the gut, and the patient may not be rehydrated properly.
    1 mark: the solution is too dilute and contains less salt and glucose. 1 mark: less sodium and water is absorbed, so rehydration is less effective.

Q49Cholera · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The cholera toxin causes chloride ions to be secreted into the small intestine. Which row describes the effect on water potential and water movement? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D water potential of gut contents: decreases; water moves: out of the cells into the gut
    Chloride ions lower the water potential of the gut contents, so water leaves the cells (and blood) by osmosis into the lumen, causing watery diarrhoea. B is the common error of linking low water potential with water entering the cells.
Q50Cholera · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the toxin produced by the cholera bacterium leads to diarrhoea and dehydration. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bacterium multiplies in the small intestine and releases a toxin; toxin causes secretion of chloride ions into the lumen; this lowers the water potential of the gut contents; water moves out of the cells / blood into the lumen by osmosis; large volume of watery faeces (diarrhoea); loss of water (and ions) from blood and tissues causes dehydration.
    1 mark each, max 5: toxin; small intestine; chloride ions secreted into lumen; water potential of lumen lowered; water moves out by osmosis; diarrhoea; loss of water and ions from blood → dehydration.
Q51Cholera · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why cases of cholera often increase after floods, such as those in Sindh. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest three measures that could reduce the spread of cholera in a flooded area. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Floodwater mixes with untreated sewage, which contains the cholera bacteria; people drink or wash food with this contaminated water.
    B1 sewage / faeces mixes with drinking water; B1 people drink contaminated water.
  2. (b) Any three: boil drinking water; chlorinate / treat water supplies; provide bottled or tanker water; build safe toilets / proper sewage disposal; wash hands with soap; cook food thoroughly and protect it from flies; vaccination.
    1 mark each for any three.
Q52Cholera · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Where in the body do cholera bacteria multiply and produce their toxin? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B the small intestine
    Cholera bacteria colonise the lining of the small intestine, where the toxin is made and acts on the cells. Stomach acid kills most bacteria, the bacteria do not multiply in the blood, and they do not colonise the large intestine in this way.
Q53Cholera · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) Active immunity. Antibody levels and memory cells fall over time, so a booster dose is needed to stimulate the memory cells again and restore a rapid response.
    1 mark: active immunity. 1 mark: antibody levels fall over time. 1 mark: a booster re-stimulates memory cells / restores protection.
Q54Cholera · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) Soap removes bacteria from the skin, so fewer cholera bacteria are transferred from hands to food or water and then to the mouth. Soap also breaks down the grease and faecal material that bacteria cling to.
    1 mark: removes bacteria from hands. 1 mark: prevents transfer to food or water. 1 mark: soap breaks down the material bacteria stick to / kills bacteria on hands.

  1. (a) Explain why she prepares a plate with no sample added. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the plates should be incubated at 37 °C rather than at room temperature. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is a control: if bacteria grow on it, the agar, the plate or her technique is contaminated.

  2. (b) 37 °C is close to human body temperature, the temperature at which pathogens that infect humans grow best (enzymes work at their optimum).

  2. (b) Explain why a patient's faeces should be disinfected before it is disposed of. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cholera bacteria in faeces or vomit could be transferred to the health worker's hands and then to other patients or to food, so gloves and hand washing prevent spread.
    1 mark: bacteria are transferred from the patient to the worker's hands. 1 mark: they could then infect other people (prevention of transfer).

    1 mark: kills the bacteria. 1 mark: prevents contamination of water or soil and further infection.
Q57Cholera · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) Calculate the total volume of fluid the patient lost in 16 hours. Give your answer in dm³. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why oral rehydration solution contains water, salts and glucose. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why antibiotics alone, without rehydration, may not save a patient with severe cholera. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.2 dm³
    450 × 16 = 7200 cm³ (M1); ÷ 1000 = 7.2 dm³ (A1).
  2. (b) Water replaces the water lost in diarrhoea; salts (e.g. sodium and chloride ions) replace the ions lost; glucose provides energy and helps the absorption of salts and water from the intestine.
    1 mark each: water replaces lost water; salts / ions replace lost ions; glucose for energy / helps ion (and water) absorption.
  3. (c) Death is caused by dehydration; antibiotics kill bacteria but do not replace the lost water and ions quickly enough.
    B1 dehydration / fluid loss must be treated.

Q59Cholera · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

In a refugee camp after flooding, the number of new cholera cases was recorded each day: day 1: 2; day 2: 4; day 3: 8; day 4: 16; day 5: 32; day 6: 64.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in the number of cases from day 2 to day 4. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the number of cases increased so quickly. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two measures the camp could take to slow the spread. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 300 %
    1 mark: (16 − 4) = 12 more cases. 1 mark: 12 ÷ 4 × 100 = 300 %.
  2. (b) Each infected person passes the bacteria to others through contaminated water and food, and the bacteria multiply in the gut, so the number of cases doubles each day.
    1 mark: infected people contaminate water or food that others consume. 1 mark: cases double each day because the bacteria spread and multiply repeatedly (exponential growth).
  3. (c) Any two: provide clean chlorinated water; separate and treat faeces (latrines); hand washing with soap; treat patients with ORS and antibiotics; isolate cases.
    1 mark for each sensible measure, up to 2.
Q60Cholera · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A patient with cholera is treated with antibiotics and oral rehydration solution (ORS). Explain why both treatments are needed, and why antibiotics alone do not stop the patient's symptoms quickly. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Antibiotics kill the bacteria, so no more toxin is produced and the infection is shortened. But toxin already made continues to cause water loss until it is cleared, so symptoms continue. ORS replaces the water and salts already lost, which prevents dehydration and shock while the body recovers.
    1 mark: antibiotics kill the bacteria / stop toxin production. 1 mark: toxin already made continues to act, so symptoms do not stop at once. 1 mark: ORS replaces the water and salts lost. 1 mark: ORS prevents dehydration and shock while the body recovers.
