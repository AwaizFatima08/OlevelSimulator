Q1Chemical digestion & enzymes · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which enzyme begins the digestion of protein in the stomach?A amylaseB lipaseC maltaseD pepsin [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D pepsin
    Pepsin is the protease secreted in the stomach and works at acidic pH. Amylase digests starch, not protein.

Q3Chemical digestion & enzymes · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

Lipase is added to full-cream milk containing a few drops of an indicator that is pink above pH 8.3 and colourless below it. A little sodium carbonate solution is first added so the milk starts pink. The time taken for the pink colour to disappear is recorded at four temperatures.temperature / °C | time for pink colour to disappear / s15 | 42025 | 21035 | 11055 | 600

  1. (a) Explain why the pink colour disappears. [2]
  2. (b) State the temperature at which lipase was most active in these results. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the rate of reaction at 25 °C as 1000 ÷ time. Give your answer to one decimal place. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) lipase digests fat in the milk to fatty acids (and glycerol); the fatty acids lower the pH below 8.3
    B1 fats broken down to fatty acids; B1 acids lower the pH.
  2. (b) 35 °C
    B1.
  3. (c) 4.8 (arbitrary units)
    B1 1000 ÷ 210 = 4.8.
Q4Chemical digestion & enzymes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which row shows the places where starch is digested and the product of amylase action?A mouth and stomach; glucoseB mouth and small intestine; maltoseC stomach and small intestine; maltoseD mouth and small intestine; glucose [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B mouth and small intestine; maltose
    Salivary amylase acts in the mouth and pancreatic amylase in the small intestine (duodenum); amylase produces maltose, which maltase then breaks down to glucose. Glucose is the product of maltase, not amylase; amylase does not act in the acid stomach.

Q6Chemical digestion & enzymes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A student mixes salivary amylase with starch solution at different pH values at 37 °C. A drop of the mixture is tested with iodine solution every 15 s until it no longer turns blue-black. The results are:pH | time for starch to disappear / s4 | 3006 | 757 | 608 | 10010 | 600The rate of reaction can be found as 1000 ÷ time. Calculate the rate at pH 7 and at pH 4. Give each answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) State the optimum pH of this amylase from these results. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the starch takes 600 s to disappear at pH 10. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) pH 7: 16.7; pH 4: 3.3
    B1 1000 ÷ 60 = 16.7; B1 1000 ÷ 300 = 3.3.
  2. (b) pH 7
    B1.
  3. (c) pH far from the optimum changes the shape of the active site of amylase; fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form so digestion is slow
    B1 active site changes shape / some enzyme molecules denatured; B1 starch fits less often / fewer enzyme–substrate complexes.

Q8Chemical digestion & enzymes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State two functions of the hydrochloric acid secreted in the stomach. [2]
  2. (b) Pepsin stops working when the stomach contents reach the duodenum. Explain why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) kills pathogens / harmful microorganisms in food; gives the acidic pH that is optimum for pepsin
    B1 kills bacteria / pathogens; B1 provides optimum (low) pH for enzymes (pepsin).
  2. (b) bile (alkaline) neutralises the acid so pH rises; pepsin is far from its optimum pH; the shape of its active site changes so substrate no longer fits
    B1 bile / alkaline secretion neutralises acid, pH rises; B1 pepsin has a low optimum pH; B1 active site changes shape / enzyme denatured / no enzyme–substrate complexes form.
Q9Chemical digestion & enzymes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why proteins in a meal of chicken karahi must be digested before they can be used by body cells. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) proteins are large insoluble molecules that cannot pass through the wall of the small intestine into the blood; they are broken down to small soluble amino acids that can be absorbed; cells then use the amino acids to build their own proteins
    B1 proteins are large / insoluble; B1 cannot be absorbed (cannot cross the epithelium / membranes); B1 amino acids are small and soluble so can be absorbed and then used to make new proteins.
Q10Chemical digestion & enzymes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) In some people with cystic fibrosis, thick mucus blocks the duct that carries pancreatic juice to the duodenum. Suggest and explain two effects of this on digestion. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) less amylase, protease (trypsin) and lipase reach the duodenum, so starch, protein and fat are less digested; pancreatic juice is alkaline, so acid from the stomach is less neutralised and enzymes in the small intestine are not at their optimum pH
    Two effects, each with explanation (2 × 2): less fat digested because less lipase (fatty food passes out in faeces); less protein / starch digested because less trypsin / amylase; fewer nutrients absorbed so poor growth / weight loss; contents of duodenum stay acidic so intestinal enzymes work slowly.
Q11Physical digestion, teeth & bile · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the effect of bile on fats in the small intestine?A It breaks fats down into fatty acids and glycerol.B It emulsifies fats into small droplets.C It absorbs fats into the lacteals.D It produces lipase to digest fats. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B It emulsifies fats into small droplets.
    Bile emulsifies fat, breaking large drops into small droplets and increasing their surface area for lipase. It contains no enzymes, so A is wrong: that is the action of lipase.

Q13Physical digestion, teeth & bile · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Q14Physical digestion, teeth & bile · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the stomach carries out physical digestion. [2]
  2. (b) A cube of boiled potato with sides of 1 cm is cut into 8 equal cubes, each with sides of 0.5 cm. Calculate the total surface area before and after cutting. [2]
  3. (c) Use your answer to (b) to explain why physical digestion speeds up chemical digestion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) muscles in the stomach wall contract to churn the food, mixing it with gastric juice and breaking it into smaller pieces
    B1 muscle contractions / churning; B1 breaks food up / mixes with juices.
  2. (b) before 6 cm²; after 12 cm²
    B1 6 × 1 × 1 = 6 cm²; B1 8 × 6 × 0.5 × 0.5 = 12 cm².
  3. (c) the surface area doubles, so more food is exposed to enzymes at once
    B1.
Q15Physical digestion, teeth & bile · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Q18Absorption, villi & assimilation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks

Q19Absorption, villi & assimilation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) In coeliac disease, eating gluten damages the lining of the small intestine and the villi become flattened. In one patient the absorptive surface area fell from 32 m² to 4 m². Calculate the percentage decrease in surface area. Give your answer to two significant figures. [2]

  2. (b) smaller surface area means less glucose, amino acids and fats absorbed into the blood; less energy and fewer nutrients available, so body stores are used
    B1 less absorption of digested food; B1 less energy / materials, so fat or protein stores are used up.

Q22Digestive system organs & processes · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks

Q30Balanced diet & deficiencies · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) 100 g of guava contains 228 mg of vitamin C. A teenager needs 65 mg of vitamin C each day. Calculate the mass of guava, in g, that provides the daily need. Give your answer to two significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Name the deficiency disease caused by a long-term lack of vitamin C. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why rickets is more common in children who rarely go outdoors and drink little milk. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 29 g
    M1 65 ÷ 228 × 100 (= 28.5…); A1 29 g.
  2. (b) scurvy
    B1.
  3. (c) little sunlight on skin so less vitamin D made; little calcium (from milk) and vitamin D needed to absorb calcium, so bones are soft / weak
    B1 vitamin D made in skin in sunlight / milk supplies calcium (and vitamin D); B1 calcium needed for strong bones, vitamin D helps calcium absorption.
