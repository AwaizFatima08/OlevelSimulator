Q1Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Two plants that are heterozygous for flower colour (Rr) are crossed. Red (R) is dominant to white (r). What is the expected ratio of red to white offspring? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 3 : 1
    Rr × Rr gives RR, Rr, Rr, rr: three red to one white. 1 : 1 (A) is the result of a test cross Rr × rr.
Q2Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State the meaning of (i) homozygous and (ii) heterozygous. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why two heterozygous individuals do not 'breed true' for a characteristic. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Having two identical alleles of a particular gene; (ii) having two different alleles of a particular gene.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Each can pass on either allele, so offspring can be homozygous dominant, heterozygous or homozygous recessive – some offspring show a different phenotype from the parents.
    B1 gametes carry different alleles; B1 offspring can be recessive / different phenotype.
Q3Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Two parents with the genotypes Bb and bb are crossed. What is the probability that an offspring has the genotype bb? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 50%
    A cross of Bb × bb gives Bb and bb in a 1:1 ratio, so half the offspring are bb (50%). A 25% answer would be correct for Bb × Bb, not this cross.
Q4Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which cross produces offspring in a 1:1 phenotypic ratio? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C Aa × aa
    Aa × aa gives Aa and aa in a 1:1 ratio, so the phenotypes (dominant and recessive) also appear in a 1:1 ratio. Aa × Aa gives 3:1 and AA × aa gives all Aa, while AA × AA gives all AA.
Q5Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a recessive allele can be carried by an individual without showing in the phenotype. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A heterozygote has one dominant allele and one recessive allele. The dominant allele is expressed and masks the recessive allele, so the recessive trait does not show. The recessive allele can still be passed on to the next generation.
    1 mark: heterozygote has one dominant and one recessive allele. 1 mark: dominant allele is expressed and masks the recessive. 1 mark: recessive allele can still be passed on.
Q6Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define genotype and phenotype, using pea seed shape as an example. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Genotype: the alleles an organism carries (e.g. Rr). Phenotype: the observable characteristic produced (e.g. round seeds).
    1 mark: genotype is the allele combination. 1 mark: example Rr. 1 mark: phenotype is the observable feature. 1 mark: example round seeds.
Q7Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a farmer could use a test cross to find out whether a black-coated goat is homozygous (BB) or heterozygous (Bb). Black (B) is dominant to brown (b). [3]
  2. (b) Draw a genetic diagram for the cross if the black goat is heterozygous. State the expected ratio of offspring phenotypes. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cross the black goat with a brown goat (bb, homozygous recessive); if any offspring are brown, the black goat is Bb; if all offspring (in a large number) are black, it is probably BB.
    B1 cross with homozygous recessive / brown goat; B1 any brown offspring → Bb; B1 all black → BB.
  2. (b) Bb × bb; gametes B, b and b; offspring Bb (black), bb (brown); ratio 1 black : 1 brown.
    B1 parental genotypes and gametes; B1 offspring genotypes; B1 1 : 1 black : brown.
Q8Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

A student in Multan grew 810 pea seeds from a cross between two tall plants. 612 plants were tall and 198 were dwarf.

  1. (a) Calculate the ratio of tall to dwarf plants, to the nearest whole number : 1. [2]
  2. (b) State the genotypes of the parent plants. Use T for tall and t for dwarf. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the ratio is not exactly the expected ratio. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3 : 1
    M1 612 ÷ 198 = 3.09; A1 3 : 1.
  2. (b) Tt × Tt
    B1 both Tt.
  3. (c) Fertilisation is random / chance; sample is too small to give the exact ratio.
    B1 random fusion of gametes / chance.
Q9Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a pedigree diagram can show whether a trait is dominant or recessive. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) If two unaffected parents have an affected child, the trait must be recessive, because both parents must carry the recessive allele. A dominant trait appears in every generation and an affected person has at least one affected parent. A recessive trait can skip generations.
    1 mark: unaffected parents with an affected child shows a recessive trait. 1 mark: dominant trait appears in every generation. 1 mark: affected child of unaffected parents must be recessive. 1 mark: recessive traits can skip a generation.

  1. (a) Free earlobes (E) are dominant to attached earlobes (e). Two parents who are both heterozygous (Ee) have children. (i) State the genotype ratio of the children. (ii) State the phenotype ratio of the children. [4]

    (i) 2 marks: 1 mark for the three genotypes EE, Ee and ee; 1 mark for the ratio 1:2:1. (ii) 2 marks: 1 mark for the two phenotypes; 1 mark for the ratio 3:1.

Q12Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A family pedigree is described: Persons 1 (father) and 2 (mother) are both unaffected. They have three children: person 3 (son, unaffected), person 4 (daughter, unaffected) and person 5 (son, has cystic fibrosis).

  1. (a) Cystic fibrosis is caused by a recessive allele (f). Use the pedigree to state the genotypes of persons 1 and 2. Explain your answer. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the probability that the next child of persons 1 and 2 will have cystic fibrosis. [1]
  3. (c) Person 4 is unaffected. Calculate the probability that person 4 is a carrier (Ff). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both Ff. They are unaffected, so each has at least one F; their child 5 has cystic fibrosis (ff) and received one f from each parent.
    B1 both Ff; B1 unaffected so have dominant allele; B1 affected child must have received f from each.
  2. (b) 0.25
    B1 1/4 = 0.25.
  3. (c) 2/3 (0.67)
    M1 unaffected offspring of Ff × Ff are FF, Ff, Ff (ff excluded); A1 2/3.

Q14Monohybrid crosses & pedigrees · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) In goats, black coat (B) is dominant to white coat (b). A black goat is crossed with a white goat. The kids are 9 black and 8 white. State the genotype of the black parent, with a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of white kids. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why this cross is a test cross and what the result shows. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bb, because the white kids (bb) must receive a b allele from the black parent, and the 1:1 ratio is typical of a heterozygote.
    1 mark: Bb. 1 mark: white kids are bb so the black parent must carry b; roughly equal numbers show it is heterozygous.
  2. (b) 47.1%
    1 mark: 8 ÷ 17 seen. 1 mark: 47.1%.
  3. (c) The white (recessive) parent is used to test the black parent. A roughly 1:1 ratio of black to white kids shows that the black parent is heterozygous (Bb).
    1 mark: crossed with a homozygous recessive parent. 1 mark: 1:1 ratio shows heterozygous black parent.
