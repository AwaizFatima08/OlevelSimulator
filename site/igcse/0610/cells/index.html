Q1Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which structure is found in both plant cells and animal cells but NOT in bacterial cells? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C mitochondrion
    Bacteria have a cell membrane, a cell wall and ribosomes, but no mitochondria. Plant and animal cells both have mitochondria. Cell wall is wrong because animal cells lack one.
Q2Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the cell structure that matches each description.(i) the site of aerobic respiration(ii) the site of protein synthesis(iii) controls the movement of substances into and out of the cell(iv) contains the chromosomes [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) mitochondrion (ii) ribosome (iii) cell membrane (iv) nucleus
    B1 each. Do not accept cell wall for (iii).
Q3Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Complete the sentences about plant cells using suitable words.The cell wall is made of ______ and stops the cell bursting. The large permanent vacuole contains ______. Chloroplasts absorb ______ energy for photosynthesis. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) cellulose; cell sap; light
    B1 each.
Q4Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which structure controls which substances enter and leave a cell? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B cell membrane
    The cell membrane is partially permeable and controls movement in and out. The cell wall is fully permeable and gives support; the nucleus controls cell activities; ribosomes make proteins.
Q5Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a(i)) Name the organelle that contains chlorophyll and is the site of photosynthesis. [1]
  2. (a(ii)) Name the structure, made of cellulose, that surrounds a plant cell and gives it support. [1]
  3. (a(iii)) Name the jelly-like region of the cell in which most of the chemical reactions take place. [1]
  4. (a(iv)) Name the organelle that is the site of aerobic respiration. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a(i)) chloroplast
    Chloroplasts contain chlorophyll and carry out photosynthesis.
  2. (a(ii)) cell wall
    Plant cell walls are made of cellulose and support the cell.
  3. (a(iii)) cytoplasm
    Most chemical reactions take place in the cytoplasm.
  4. (a(iv)) mitochondrion (mitochondria)
    Aerobic respiration releases energy in the mitochondria.
Q6Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A student views a plant cell and sees a large central sac filled with cell sap. Name this structure. [1]
  2. (b) State two functions of the cell sap in the vacuole. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) vacuole
    The vacuole is a sac of cell sap surrounded by a membrane (tonoplast).
  2. (b) Any two: keeps the cell firm (turgid) by pressing the cell membrane against the cell wall; stores sugars, amino acids, mineral ions or pigments.
    1 mark each for any two functions listed.
Q7Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Match each structure to its function. Structures: 1 cell membrane, 2 cell wall, 3 nucleus. Functions: A gives support and stops the cell bursting when it takes in water. B controls the activities of the cell, including the production of proteins. C is the site of most chemical reactions in the cell. D controls which substances enter and leave the cell.Give the letter for each: (i) cell membrane (ii) cell wall (iii) nucleus. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) D; (ii) A; (iii) B
    1 mark each: membrane controls entry and exit (D); cell wall gives support (A); nucleus controls activities (B). C refers to the cytoplasm, which is not asked for here.
Q8Cell structure & organelles · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Compare mitochondria and chloroplasts. State two features that they have in common. [2]
  2. (b) State one structural difference between a mitochondrion and a chloroplast. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: both have a double membrane; both contain their own DNA and ribosomes; both are involved in energy transfer.
    1 mark each for any two features listed.
  2. (b) A chloroplast contains thylakoid membranes stacked into grana and chlorophyll; a mitochondrion has inner membrane folds (cristae).
    1 mark for a correct difference (chlorophyll/grana/thylakoids versus cristae).
Q9Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which features are present in a bacterial cell?1 cell wall2 plasmids3 nucleus4 ribosomes [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 1, 2 and 4
    Bacteria have a cell wall, plasmids, ribosomes and circular DNA that is not enclosed in a nucleus. Any option including 3 is wrong; 1 and 4 only omits plasmids.
Q10Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State three structural differences between a palisade mesophyll cell and a liver cell. [3]
  2. (b) Liver cells contain many mitochondria. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) palisade cell has a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts and a large permanent vacuole; liver cell has none of these
    1 mark each for any three: cell wall (cellulose) present / absent; chloroplasts present / absent; large permanent vacuole (with cell sap) present / absent or only small vacuoles; regular shape / irregular shape.
  2. (b) liver cells have a high rate of metabolism, so they need a lot of energy released by aerobic respiration in mitochondria
    B1 mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration; B1 high metabolic rate / many energy-requiring reactions (e.g. protein synthesis, active transport) so much energy must be released. Do not credit 'produce energy'.
Q11Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State two structures that are found in both a bacterial cell and a plant cell. [2]
  2. (b) Name one structure found in a bacterial cell that is not found in a plant cell. [1]
  3. (c) Describe how the main genetic material of a bacterial cell differs from that of a plant cell. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) any two of: cell wall, cell membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes
    1 mark each for any two. (Not nucleus, not chloroplast, not mitochondrion.)
  2. (b) plasmid
    B1.
  3. (c) bacterial DNA is a single circular chromosome / loop free in the cytoplasm, not enclosed in a nucleus
    B1 circular DNA; B1 not in a nucleus / no nuclear membrane.
Q12Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A student peels a thin layer of epidermis from the inside of an onion bulb, places it on a slide in a drop of iodine solution and adds a coverslip. State why iodine solution is added. [1]
  2. (b) Name two structures the student would see in the onion cells with a light microscope. [2]
  3. (c) The student notices that the onion cells have no chloroplasts. Explain why. [2]
  4. (d) State why the coverslip should be lowered slowly at an angle. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) to stain the cells / make the nucleus and cell outline visible
    B1.
  2. (b) any two of: cell wall, nucleus, cytoplasm, vacuole
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) the bulb grows underground / receives no light, so the cells do not photosynthesise and do not need chloroplasts
    B1 no light reaches these cells; B1 chloroplasts are for photosynthesis, which they do not carry out.
  4. (d) to avoid trapping air bubbles
    B1.
Q13Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature is present in both a bacterial cell and a plant cell, but NOT in an animal cell? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B cell wall
    Animal cells have no cell wall; bacterial cells (peptidoglycan) and plant cells (cellulose) both have one. Bacteria have no nucleus or mitochondria, and ribosomes are found in all three cell types.
Q14Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a mitochondrion. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the structure of a mitochondrion is adapted for aerobic respiration. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A double membrane (outer and inner membrane); the inner membrane is folded into cristae; the space inside (matrix) contains enzymes.
    1 mark for double membrane. 1 mark for inner membrane folded into cristae (matrix with enzymes may also be credited).
  2. (b) The folds (cristae) increase the surface area for the enzymes and carrier molecules that release energy; the matrix contains enzymes for respiration, so more energy (ATP) is released.
    1 mark: folds increase surface area. 1 mark: enzymes/carriers on the membrane and matrix allow more aerobic respiration.
Q15Cell structure & organelles · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student scrapes the inside of her cheek with a cotton bud and spreads the cells on a slide. She adds a drop of methylene blue stain and a coverslip, then draws a few cells under the microscope. Each cell she draws has a nucleus, cytoplasm and a cell membrane. She draws no cell wall, no chloroplasts and no large vacuole.

  1. (a) Give one reason why the cheek cells have no chloroplasts. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the nucleus appears darker than the cytoplasm after staining with methylene blue. [2]
  3. (c) State two structures that are found in both a human cheek cell and a plant cell. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cheek cells are not photosynthetic: they are inside the body and do not receive light, so they do not need chloroplasts.
    1 mark: cheek cells do not photosynthesise (no light / not a green, photosynthetic tissue).
  2. (b) The stain binds to the DNA (chromosomes) in the nucleus, so the nucleus takes up more stain and appears darker.
    1 mark: the stain binds to DNA / chromosomes in the nucleus. 1 mark: the nucleus takes up more stain, so it looks darker than the cytoplasm.
  3. (c) Any two: cell membrane; cytoplasm; nucleus; mitochondria; ribosomes.
    1 mark for each correct structure, up to 2.
Q16Cell structure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
