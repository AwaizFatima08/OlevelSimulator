Q1Narrative: story with a given title · Core · Paper 2 style · 40 marks
  1. (a) Write a story with the title 'The Key'.Write about 350 to 450 words. Up to 16 marks are available for the content and structure of your answer, and up to 24 marks for the style and accuracy of your writing (exam total 40). [40]
Show answer
  1. (a) Model plan: a girl clearing her late grandfather's room finds a small brass key taped under a drawer. Development: she searches the house, tries locks; tension with her mother who wants the room emptied quickly. Climax: the key opens a tin box in the garden shed containing letters he wrote to her each birthday but never sent. Resolution: she reads one a year. Possible opening: 'The key was smaller than my little finger and colder than it had any right to be.'
    Content ideas: the key can be literal or symbolic (key to a house, a secret, a solution, a new life); build mystery around what it opens; give the character a motive and an emotional stake; limit time span and characters. A story needs a credible character, a clear shape (opening, development, climax, resolution) and an ending that feels earned; avoid clichéd 'it was all a dream' endings. Content and structure (16): Level 6 (15–16) compelling, consistently well-developed and complex; Level 5 (12–14) effective, well-developed, secure structure; Level 4 (9–11) relevant, some development, clear structure; Level 3 (6–8) straightforward, some details, mostly organised; Level 2 (3–5) simple, brief or repetitive; Level 1 (1–2) little relevant content. Style and accuracy (24): Level 6 (21–24) precise, well-chosen vocabulary and varied sentences for deliberate effect, almost error-free; Level 5 (17–20) wide vocabulary, varied sentences, few errors; Level 4 (13–16) some range, mostly accurate; Level 3 (9–12) plain but clear, errors in complex structures; Level 2 (5–8) limited vocabulary, frequent errors; Level 1 (1–4) errors impede meaning. Exam total 40; on this site the task is scored out of 30 (multiply the exam total by 0.75).
