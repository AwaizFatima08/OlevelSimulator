Q21Kinetic particle theory & changes of state (incl. gas P/V/T) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

In many Pakistani homes drinking water is kept in an unglazed clay pot (matka). Water slowly seeps through the clay and evaporates from the outer surface.

  1. (a) Explain, in terms of the energy of the particles, why water kept in the pot becomes cooler than the surrounding air. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the pot cools the water better on a dry, windy day than on a humid, still day. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water particles have a range of energies; the most energetic particles escape (evaporate) from the outer surface; the average kinetic energy of the particles left behind falls, so the temperature falls.
    B1 evaporation from the surface; B1 particles with the most energy escape; B1 average energy (temperature) of remaining water decreases.
  2. (b) Wind carries away water vapour (and dry air has little water vapour), so evaporation continues faster and more cooling occurs.
    B1 faster evaporation; B1 vapour removed / less water vapour in the air.

    B1 steam condenses on skin; B1 condensation releases (extra) energy.

  2. (b) Calculate the distance of the white solid from the hydrochloric acid end. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the white solid forms closer to the hydrochloric acid end. [2]
  4. (d) State one safety precaution for this experiment and give a reason. [1]
  5. (e) Suggest why both pieces of cotton wool must be put into the tube at the same time. [1]

  2. (b) 100 − 59 = 41 cm
    B1
  3. (c) Ammonia has a lower Mr (17) than hydrogen chloride (36.5), so its molecules move faster and diffuse further in the same time.

Q29Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

Four identical Petri dishes of jelly (agar) were kept in water baths at different temperatures. A drop of coloured dye was placed in a small hole in the centre of each. The time taken for the colour to reach a circle 15 mm from the hole was recorded.Temperature/°C: 20, 30, 40, 50Time/min: 84, 61, 49, 35

  1. (a) Calculate the rate of diffusion, in mm per minute, at 20 °C and at 50 °C. Give your answers to 2 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the relationship between temperature and rate of diffusion shown by the results. [1]
  3. (c) Explain this relationship in terms of particles. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest one source of error in this experiment and one way of improving the experiment to reduce it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20 °C: 15 ÷ 84 = 0.18 mm/min; 50 °C: 15 ÷ 35 = 0.43 mm/min
    B1 each.
  2. (b) The higher the temperature, the faster the rate of diffusion (shorter time).
    B1
  3. (c) At higher temperature particles have more kinetic energy and move faster, so they spread out more quickly.
    B1 more kinetic energy; B1 move faster.
  4. (d) Judging exactly when the colour reaches the mark is difficult / temperature of water bath may change. Improvement: repeat and average / use a thermostatically controlled water bath / use a white background.
    B1 source of error; B1 matching improvement.
Q30Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A gas jar of air is placed upside down on a gas jar containing a few drops of liquid bromine. The brown vapour takes about 20 minutes to spread evenly through both jars. When the experiment is repeated with the air removed from the jars first, the brown colour fills both jars in less than a second.

  1. (a) Explain why diffusion is much faster in gases than in liquids at the same temperature. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the brown colour fills the lower jar almost at once when the air has been pumped out first. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gas particles are far apart and move quickly with few collisions; liquid particles are close together and keep colliding with neighbours, which slows their spreading.
    B1 gas particles far apart / move freely; B1 liquid particles close together / more collisions.
  2. (b) There are no air particles for the bromine particles to collide with, so they move in straight lines at high speed and fill the space immediately.
    B1 no air particles / no collisions; B1 bromine particles travel freely (fast).
