Q1Water treatment & tests for water · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Why is chlorine added to the domestic water supply? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C to kill microbes
    Chlorination kills harmful microbes (bacteria). Carbon removes tastes and odours; sedimentation and filtration remove solids.

River water is treated before it is supplied to homes in Rawalpindi.

  1. (a) Name the stages used to remove insoluble solids from the water. [2]
  2. (b) Name the substance used to remove tastes and odours. [1]
  3. (c) State the purpose of chlorination. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sedimentation and filtration
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Carbon (activated charcoal)
    B1
  3. (c) To kill microbes (bacteria)
    B1
Q3Water treatment & tests for water · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A student pours muddy water from a pond through a homemade filter: a plastic bottle containing layers of gravel, sand and powdered charcoal.

  1. (a) Describe how the appearance of the water changes after passing through the filter. [1]
  2. (b) State the purpose of the layer of charcoal in the filter. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the filtered water is still not safe to drink. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest a simple way to make the filtered water safe to drink at home. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It changes from cloudy/muddy to clear (colourless).
    B1
  2. (b) To remove tastes and odours (and some coloured substances).
    B1
  3. (c) It may still contain microbes (and dissolved harmful substances).
    B1
  4. (d) Boil it (or add a chlorine tablet).
    B1

Q5Water treatment & tests for water · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Water from the Indus is treated before it is piped to homes.

  1. (a) Put these stages in the order used at the treatment works: chlorination, filtration, sedimentation, treatment with carbon. [2]
  2. (b) Match each stage to its purpose: (i) sedimentation, (ii) filtration, (iii) carbon, (iv) chlorination. [4]
