Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins, plastics) · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the type of polymerisation used to make poly(ethene) from ethene. [1]
  2. (b) Give the repeat unit of poly(ethene) as a structural formula. [1]
  3. (c) Give two problems caused by the disposal of poly(ethene) shopping bags. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) addition polymerisation
  2. (b) –CH₂–CH₂–
    Accept a written description: two carbon atoms joined by a single bond, each with two H atoms, with continuation bonds at both ends.
  3. (c) Any two of: non-biodegradable, so they stay in landfill for a long time; litter and harm to animals (e.g. in rivers and seas); burning releases toxic gases / greenhouse gases.
    1 mark each.
Q2Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins, plastics) · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Suggest why plastic bottles found on a beach in Karachi can stay there for hundreds of years. [1]
  2. (b) Give one disadvantage of burning waste plastics. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest one way of reducing the amount of plastic waste. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Plastics are non-biodegradable (not broken down by microorganisms).
  2. (b) It releases toxic gases / carbon dioxide (greenhouse gas).
  3. (c) Recycling (e.g. PET bottles) / reusing / using less plastic packaging.
Q3Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins, plastics) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Poly(chloroethene) is used to make water pipes. What is its monomer?A CH₃CH₂ClB CH₂=CHClC CH₂Cl–CH₂ClD CCl₂=CCl₂ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B CH₂=CHCl
    An addition polymer is made from an alkene; chloroethene is CH₂=CHCl. A has no C=C double bond, so it cannot undergo addition polymerisation.
Q4Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins, plastics) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Poly(propene) is used to make ropes for fishing boats in Gwadar. It is made from propene, CH₃CH=CH₂.

  1. (a) Give the structural formula of the repeat unit of poly(propene). [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the relative formula mass of one repeat unit. [Ar: C = 12, H = 1] [1]
  3. (c) A molecule of poly(propene) has a relative molecular mass of 84 000. Calculate the number of repeat units in the molecule. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–
  2. (b) 42
    C₃H₆: 3 × 12 + 6 = 42.
  3. (c) 2000
    84 000 ÷ 42.
Q5Polymers (addition, condensation, proteins, plastics) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Lentils (daal) are a good source of protein.

  1. (a) Name the monomers from which proteins are made. [1]
  2. (b) Proteins are natural polyamides. Name the type of polymerisation that forms them. [1]
  3. (c) Give the name of the small molecule released each time two monomers join. [1]
  4. (d) State the name of the synthetic polymer that has the same linkage as proteins. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) amino acids
  2. (b) condensation polymerisation
  3. (c) water
  4. (d) nylon
