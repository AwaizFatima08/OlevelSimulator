Q1Group VII · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give the colour and physical state at room temperature of: (i) chlorine, (ii) bromine, (iii) iodine. [3]
  2. (b) Describe the trend in density down Group VII. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) pale yellow-green gas; (ii) red-brown liquid; (iii) grey-black solid
    1 mark each (colour and state).
  2. (b) Density increases down the group.
Q2Group VII · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give the electronic configuration of a chlorine atom. [1]
  2. (b) State the charge on the ion formed by chlorine, and explain why it has this charge. [2]
  3. (c) Give the formulae of the compounds formed when chlorine reacts with (i) magnesium and (ii) hydrogen. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2,8,7
  2. (b) 1−; the atom gains one electron to get a full outer shell.
    B1 1− (Cl⁻); B1 gains one electron.
  3. (c) (i) MgCl₂; (ii) HCl
    1 mark each.
Q3Group VII · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which halogen is a liquid at room temperature and pressure? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C bromine
    Bromine is a red-brown liquid. Fluorine and chlorine are gases, and iodine is a solid.
Q4Group VII · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State the colour of the solution produced when chlorine is bubbled into aqueous potassium bromide. [1]
  2. (b) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Orange (bromine is formed).
    1 mark.
  2. (b) Cl₂(aq) + 2Br⁻(aq) → 2Cl⁻(aq) + Br₂(aq)
    1 mark: correct ions and products. 1 mark: balanced.
Q5Group VII · Core · Paper 5 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) State one safety precaution when working with chlorine water, and explain why it is needed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Work in a fume cupboard (or with good ventilation). Chlorine is toxic and irritates the lungs and eyes.
    1 mark: precaution (fume cupboard, eye protection). 1 mark: chlorine is toxic or irritant.
Q6Group VII · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which mixture of aqueous solutions reacts to produce a halogen? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C chlorine and potassium bromide
    A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from its halide: chlorine displaces bromine. In A bromine is less reactive than chlorine, so no reaction.
Q7Group VII · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Chlorine water is added to aqueous potassium iodide.

  1. (a) State the colour change seen. [1]
  2. (b) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. [2]
  3. (c) Identify the species that is oxidised. Explain your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Colourless to brown (orange-brown).
  2. (b) Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂
    B1 species; B1 balanced.
  3. (c) Iodide ions; they lose electrons (oxidation number goes from −1 to 0).
    B1 I⁻; B1 lose electrons.
Q8Group VII · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

Three solutions A, B and C each contain one sodium halide. A student adds dilute nitric acid and then aqueous silver nitrate to each, and in separate tests adds chlorine water.solution A: white precipitate; no change with chlorine watersolution B: yellow precipitate; solution turns brown with chlorine watersolution C: cream precipitate; solution turns orange with chlorine water

  1. (a) Identify the halide ion in each solution. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why chlorine water causes no visible change with solution A. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why dilute nitric acid is added before the aqueous silver nitrate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: chloride (Cl⁻); B: iodide (I⁻); C: bromide (Br⁻)
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Chlorine cannot displace chloride (no reaction) — the halogen and halide are the same.
  3. (c) To remove (react with) carbonate ions, which would also give a precipitate.
Q9Group VII · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the reactivity of the halogens with hydrogen changes down Group VII. [2]
  2. (b) Name the product formed when chlorine reacts with hydrogen. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Reactivity decreases down the group. Fluorine reacts explosively in the dark, chlorine reacts with hydrogen in light or heat, and iodine reacts only slowly and needs heating.
    1 mark: decreases down the group. 1 mark: correct examples (fluorine explosive, iodine slow).
  2. (b) Hydrogen chloride (HCl)
    1 mark.
Q10Group VII · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Results of mixing halogen solutions with halide solutions (in test tubes): chlorine water + potassium bromide: orange; chlorine water + potassium iodide: brown; bromine water + potassium iodide: brown; bromine water + sodium chloride: no change; iodine solution + potassium bromide: no change.

  1. (a) Place chlorine, bromine and iodine in order of reactivity, most reactive first. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why bromine water and sodium chloride solution show no change. [2]
  3. (c) Predict the result when chlorine water is added to sodium fluoride solution. Explain your answer. [2]
Show answer
