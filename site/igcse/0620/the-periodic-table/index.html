Practice questions

Original questions written by HomiLabs in the style of the exam, with answers and mark schemes. They are not Cambridge past paper questions and have not been reviewed by Cambridge. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Group VII · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give the colour and physical state at room temperature of: (i) chlorine, (ii) bromine, (iii) iodine. [3]
  2. (b) Describe the trend in density down Group VII. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) pale yellow-green gas; (ii) red-brown liquid; (iii) grey-black solid
    1 mark each (colour and state).
  2. (b) Density increases down the group.
Q2Group VII · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give the electronic configuration of a chlorine atom. [1]
  2. (b) State the charge on the ion formed by chlorine, and explain why it has this charge. [2]
  3. (c) Give the formulae of the compounds formed when chlorine reacts with (i) magnesium and (ii) hydrogen. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2,8,7
  2. (b) 1−; the atom gains one electron to get a full outer shell.
    B1 1− (Cl⁻); B1 gains one electron.
  3. (c) (i) MgCl₂; (ii) HCl
    1 mark each.
Q3Group VII · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which mixture of aqueous solutions reacts to produce a halogen? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C chlorine and potassium bromide
    A more reactive halogen displaces a less reactive one from its halide: chlorine displaces bromine. In A bromine is less reactive than chlorine, so no reaction.
Q4Group VII · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Chlorine water is added to aqueous potassium iodide.

  1. (a) State the colour change seen. [1]
  2. (b) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. [2]
  3. (c) Identify the species that is oxidised. Explain your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Colourless to brown (orange-brown).
  2. (b) Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂
    B1 species; B1 balanced.
  3. (c) Iodide ions; they lose electrons (oxidation number goes from −1 to 0).
    B1 I⁻; B1 lose electrons.
Q5Group VII · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

Three solutions A, B and C each contain one sodium halide. A student adds dilute nitric acid and then aqueous silver nitrate to each, and in separate tests adds chlorine water.solution A: white precipitate; no change with chlorine watersolution B: yellow precipitate; solution turns brown with chlorine watersolution C: cream precipitate; solution turns orange with chlorine water

  1. (a) Identify the halide ion in each solution. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why chlorine water causes no visible change with solution A. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why dilute nitric acid is added before the aqueous silver nitrate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: chloride (Cl⁻); B: iodide (I⁻); C: bromide (Br⁻)
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Chlorine cannot displace chloride (no reaction) — the halogen and halide are the same.
  3. (c) To remove (react with) carbonate ions, which would also give a precipitate.
Q6Group VII · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Astatine is below iodine in Group VII. Predict its physical state and colour at room temperature. [2]
  2. (b) Predict whether bromine would react with aqueous sodium astatide. Give a reason. [2]
  3. (c) Explain, in terms of electrons, why the halogens become less reactive down the group. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) solid; black
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Yes; bromine is more reactive than astatine, so it displaces astatine.
    B1 yes; B1 bromine more reactive.
  3. (c) Atoms get larger with more shells; the outer shell is further from the nucleus (more shielding), so the attraction for an incoming electron is weaker and an electron is gained less easily.
    B1 more shells / larger atoms; B1 weaker attraction of nucleus for outer electrons; B1 less easily gains an electron.
Q7Group VII · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Melting points of the halogens:fluorine −220 °C, chlorine −101 °C, bromine −7 °C, iodine 114 °C, astatine ?

  1. (a) Describe the trend in the melting points of the halogens. [1]
  2. (b) Predict the melting point of astatine. [1]
  3. (c) Explain the trend in melting points in terms of forces. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Melting point increases down the group.
  2. (b) Any value from 200 °C to 320 °C
    The melting point rises by roughly 100–120 °C per element, so about 220–240 °C is expected; accept 200–320 °C.
  3. (c) Molecules get larger down the group, so the intermolecular forces get stronger and more energy is needed to overcome them.
    B1 larger molecules; B1 stronger intermolecular forces.
Q8Transition elements · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which property is typical of transition elements? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C they form coloured compounds
    Transition elements form coloured compounds, have high densities and high melting points. D describes Group I metals.
Q9Transition elements · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State three properties of transition elements that are not shown by Group I metals. [3]
  2. (b) Name the transition element used as the catalyst in the Haber process. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three of: high density; high melting point; form coloured compounds; act as catalysts (elements or compounds); have variable oxidation numbers.
    1 mark each for any three.
  2. (b) iron
