Q1Electrolysis products & electrode equations · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Molten lead(II) bromide is electrolysed using graphite electrodes. What is formed at each electrode?A cathode: lead; anode: bromineB cathode: bromine; anode: leadC cathode: hydrogen; anode: bromineD cathode: lead; anode: oxygen [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A cathode: lead; anode: bromine
    Positive Pb²⁺ ions move to the cathode and gain electrons to form lead; Br⁻ ions move to the anode and lose electrons to form bromine. B swaps the electrodes; there is no water, so no hydrogen or oxygen can form.

  3. (c) Explain why solid zinc chloride does not conduct electricity but molten zinc chloride does. [2]
  4. (d) Name the products at the cathode and at the anode when molten zinc chloride is electrolysed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The decomposition of an ionic compound, when molten or in aqueous solution, by the passage of an electric current.
    B1 decomposition/breakdown of ionic compound (molten or aqueous); B1 by electricity.
  2. (b) Anode
    B1
  3. (c) In the solid the ions are fixed in a lattice and cannot move; when molten the ions are free to move and carry charge.
    B1 solid: ions cannot move; B1 molten: ions mobile.
  4. (d) Cathode: zinc; anode: chlorine
    B1 each.

Q16Electrolysis products & electrode equations · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Show answer

    B1
  2. (b) It gains 0.48 g.
    B1 copper atoms lost from anode = copper deposited on cathode.

  4. (d) Copper ions are formed at the anode at the same rate as they are removed at the cathode, so the concentration of Cu²⁺ stays the same.
    B1 Cu²⁺ made at anode; B1 removed at cathode at same rate.

Q30Electroplating & industrial electrolysis · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Aluminium is extracted by the electrolysis of aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite, using carbon electrodes. Ar: Al = 27, O = 16.

  1. (a) Explain why the aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite. [2]
  2. (b) Write the half-equation for the reaction at the cathode. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbon anodes must be replaced regularly. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate the maximum mass of aluminium, in tonnes, that can be obtained from 1.02 tonnes of aluminium oxide. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It lowers the operating temperature (aluminium oxide has a very high melting point), so less energy is needed; the solution also conducts better.
    B1 lower temperature / melting point of electrolyte; B1 saves energy/cost.
  2. (b) Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
    B1
  3. (c) Oxygen is formed at the anode; it reacts with the hot carbon to form carbon dioxide, so the anodes burn away.
    B1 oxygen reacts with carbon; B1 CO₂ forms.
  4. (d) 0.54 tonnes
    M1 Mr Al₂O₃ = 102, contains 54 of Al; A1 1.02 × 54/102 = 0.54 t.
