Q2Salt preparation, solubility & hydrated salts · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A student makes crystals of hydrated copper(II) sulfate by adding excess copper(II) oxide to warm dilute sulfuric acid.

  1. (a) Write the word equation for the reaction. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why copper(II) oxide is added in excess. [1]
  3. (c) Describe how the excess copper(II) oxide is removed. [1]
  4. (d) Describe how crystals of hydrated copper(II) sulfate are then obtained from the filtrate. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) copper(II) oxide + sulfuric acid → copper(II) sulfate + water
    B1
  2. (b) To make sure all the acid has reacted (so the salt is not contaminated with acid).
    B1
  3. (c) Filtration; it stays on the filter paper as the residue.
    B1
  4. (d) Heat the filtrate to evaporate some water until the crystallisation point (crystals form on a cold glass rod); leave to cool so crystals form; filter off the crystals and dry them between filter papers.
    B1 heat/evaporate to crystallisation point; B1 cool; B1 filter and dry.

  3. (c) State how the student can tell that the reaction has finished. [1]

    B1

Potassium nitrate is used in fertilisers. It can be made from aqueous potassium hydroxide and dilute nitric acid.

  1. (a) Explain why potassium nitrate cannot be made by adding an excess of a solid to the acid and filtering. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how a titration is used to make a pure solution of potassium nitrate. [4]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the reaction. [1]
Show answer

  3. (c) KOH + HNO₃ → KNO₃ + H₂O
    B1
Q8Salt preparation, solubility & hydrated salts · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

A student heats hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·xH₂O, in a crucible, cools and reweighs it, and repeats until the mass is constant.Mass of empty crucible = 21.40 gMass of crucible + hydrated salt = 26.32 gMass of crucible + salt after heating to constant mass = 23.80 g

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of hydrated salt used and the mass of water lost. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage by mass of water in the hydrated salt. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [1]
  3. (c) Use Mr MgSO₄ = 120 and Mr H₂O = 18 to find x. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the student heated the crucible to constant mass. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hydrated salt 4.92 g; water 2.52 g
    B1 26.32 − 21.40 = 4.92 g; B1 4.92 − 2.40 = 2.52 g.
  2. (b) 51.2%
    B1 2.52 ÷ 4.92 × 100 = 51.2%.
  3. (c) x = 7
    M1 mol MgSO₄ = 2.40 ÷ 120 = 0.0200, mol H₂O = 2.52 ÷ 18 = 0.140; A1 ratio 7.
  4. (d) To make sure all the water of crystallisation had been driven off.
    B1

  1. (a) Which method is most suitable for preparing a sample of silver chloride?A titration of an acid with an alkaliB adding excess metal to an acid and filteringC mixing two aqueous solutions to form a precipitateD adding excess carbonate to an acid and filtering [1]

Q14Salt preparation, solubility & hydrated salts · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ aqueous sodium hydroxide is exactly neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of dilute sulfuric acid. Mr: Na₂SO₄ = 142.

  1. (a) Write the equation for the reaction. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in mol/dm³. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of sodium sulfate in the neutral solution. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O
    B1
  2. (b) 0.125 mol/dm³
    M1 mol NaOH = 0.0250 × 0.200 = 0.00500; M1 mol H₂SO₄ = 0.00250; A1 ÷ 0.0200 = 0.125 mol/dm³.
  3. (c) 0.355 g
    M1 mol Na₂SO₄ = 0.00250; A1 × 142 = 0.355 g.
Q15Salt preparation, solubility & hydrated salts · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

Washing soda crystals are sold in markets as a cleaning agent.

  1. (a) Plan an experiment to find the value of x in washing soda, Na₂CO₃·xH₂O, a hydrated salt. Include how you would use your results. You may use common laboratory apparatus. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Weigh crucible, add salt, heat to constant mass, calculate moles ratio.
    1 mark each, max 6: weigh an empty crucible; add a known mass of washing soda (e.g. 3 g) and reweigh; heat (gently, then strongly) with a Bunsen burner on a pipe-clay triangle; cool (in a desiccator) and reweigh; repeat heating, cooling and weighing until the mass is constant; mass of water = loss in mass; mol Na₂CO₃ = mass of residue ÷ 106 and mol H₂O = mass lost ÷ 18; x = mol H₂O ÷ mol Na₂CO₃.

Name the products formed when dilute hydrochloric acid reacts with each substance. For (a) and (b) also give one observation.

  1. (a) magnesium [2]
  2. (b) calcium carbonate [2]
  3. (c) sodium hydroxide solution [1]

  4. (d) Explain why farmers should not spread calcium hydroxide on soil at the same time as an ammonium fertiliser. [2]

Aluminium oxide is described as an amphoteric oxide.

  3. (c) Describe what you would do to show that aluminium oxide also behaves as an acidic oxide, and what you would see. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the oxide of a metal such as magnesium does not dissolve in aqueous sodium hydroxide. [1]

Q28Strong/weak acids & pH · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A student has 0.10 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid and 0.10 mol/dm³ ethanoic acid.

  1. (a) Suggest which acid has the higher pH. [1]
  2. (b) Explain the difference in pH in terms of ions. [2]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the dissociation of ethanoic acid in water. [2]
  4. (d) Magnesium ribbon is added to each acid. State how the rate of reaction compares. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ethanoic acid
    B1
  2. (b) Hydrochloric acid is a strong acid and is fully dissociated into ions; ethanoic acid is weak and only partially dissociated, so its H⁺ ion concentration is lower.
    B1 strong = fully dissociated; B1 weak = partially, lower [H⁺].
  3. (c) CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺
    B1 ions; B1 reversible arrow.
  4. (d) Faster with hydrochloric acid (more H⁺ ions per unit volume).
    B1
Q29Strong/weak acids & pH · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which solution has the lowest pH?A 0.1 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acidB 0.1 mol/dm³ ethanoic acidC 0.01 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acidD 0.1 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 0.1 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid
    Lowest pH = highest H⁺ concentration. HCl is fully dissociated; at 0.1 mol/dm³ it has more H⁺ than the same concentration of weak ethanoic acid (partly dissociated) and than the more dilute HCl. NaOH is alkaline.
Q30Strong/weak acids & pH · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Three acids, P, Q and R, all at 0.50 mol/dm³ and in excess, each react with 0.12 g of magnesium ribbon.Acid: P, Q, RpH: 0.3, 2.5, 0.3Volume of hydrogen after 60 s / cm³: 92, 21, 95

  1. (a) Which acids are strong? Explain your choice using the data. [2]
  2. (b) Explain, in terms of particles, why acid Q gives less gas in 60 s. [2]
  3. (c) All the magnesium eventually reacts in each acid. Explain why the final volume of hydrogen is the same in each case. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Acids P and R; they have the lowest pH and give the most hydrogen in 60 s, showing a higher concentration of H⁺ ions.
    B1 P and R; B1 reason from data.
  2. (b) Q is weak/partially dissociated, so there is a lower concentration of H⁺ ions, so fewer collisions per unit time between H⁺ ions and magnesium.
    B1 lower [H⁺]; B1 less frequent collisions.
  3. (c) The acid is in excess and the same mass of magnesium is used, so the same number of moles of hydrogen is formed (as Q dissociates further as H⁺ is used up).
    B1 same amount of Mg / Mg limiting; B1 same moles of H₂.
