Q1Solving cubic equations · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks
  1. (a) Show that x = 2 is a root of the equation x³ − 3x² − 4x + 12 = 0. [1]
  2. (b) Hence solve x³ − 3x² − 4x + 12 = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    8 − 12 − 8 + 12 = 0 B1
  2. (b) x = 2, −2, 3
    Divide by (x − 2): x² − x − 6 M1 A1; (x + 2)(x − 3) → x = −2, 3 A1
Q2Solving cubic equations · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Solve 2x³ + 3x² − 11x − 6 = 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 2, −½, −3
    Trial: f(2) = 16 + 12 − 22 − 6 = 0, so (x − 2) is a factor M1 A1; quotient 2x² + 7x + 3 = (2x + 1)(x + 3) M1; x = 2, −½, −3 A1
Q3Solving cubic equations · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Find the exact roots of 4x³ − 13x − 6 = 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 2, −½, −3/2
    f(2) = 32 − 26 − 6 = 0 M1 A1; 4x³ − 13x − 6 = (x − 2)(4x² + 8x + 3) M1; (2x + 1)(2x + 3) → x = −½, −3/2 A1
Q4Solving cubic equations · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Solve x³ − 4x² − 2x + 8 = 0, giving the roots in exact form. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 4, √2, −√2
    x²(x − 4) − 2(x − 4) M1; (x − 4)(x² − 2) = 0 A1; x = 4 A1; x = ±√2 A1
Q5Solving cubic equations · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the x-coordinates of the points where the curve y = 2x³ − 5x² − 4x + 3 crosses the x-axis. [4]
  2. (b) State the coordinates of the point where the curve crosses the y-axis. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 3, ½, −1
    f(3) = 54 − 45 − 12 + 3 = 0 M1 A1; (x − 3)(2x² + x − 1) M1; (2x − 1)(x + 1) → x = ½, −1 A1
  2. (b) (0, 3)
    B1
Q6Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) Factorise 3x³ − 10x² + 9x − 2 completely. [3]
  2. (b) Hence solve 3y⁶ − 10y⁴ + 9y² − 2 = 0, giving exact answers. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x − 1)(3x − 1)(x − 2)
    f(1) = 0 so (x − 1) is a factor B1; quotient 3x² − 7x + 2 M1; (3x − 1)(x − 2) A1
  2. (b) y = ±1, ±1/√3 (= ±√3/3), ±√2
    Put x = y² M1; y² = 1, ⅓, 2 A1; y = ±1, ±√3/3, ±√2 A1
Q7Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Given that x = 3 is a root of x³ − 5x² + 5x + 3 = 0, find the other two roots in the form a ± √b. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 1 + √2, 1 − √2
    (x − 3)(x² − 2x − 1) M1 A1; x = (2 ± √8)/2 M1; = 1 ± √2 A1
Q8Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

The curve y = x³ − 2x² − 3x + 5 and the line y = 2x − 1 intersect at three points.

  1. (a) Show that the x-coordinates of the points of intersection satisfy x³ − 2x² − 5x + 6 = 0. [1]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the three points of intersection. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    x³ − 2x² − 3x + 5 = 2x − 1 → x³ − 2x² − 5x + 6 = 0 B1
  2. (b) (1, 1), (−2, −5), (3, 5)
    f(1) = 0 B1; (x − 1)(x² − x − 6) M1 A1; x = −2, 3 A1; y-values from y = 2x − 1 A1
Q9Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks

A storage box in a Karachi warehouse is a cuboid with edges x cm, (x + 2) cm and (x − 1) cm. Its volume is 72 cm³.

  1. (a) Show that x³ + x² − 2x − 72 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Show that x = 4 is the only real solution of this equation, and state the dimensions of the box. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    x(x + 2)(x − 1) = x³ + x² − 2x M1; = 72 A1
  2. (b) x = 4; box is 4 cm × 6 cm × 3 cm
    f(4) = 64 + 16 − 8 − 72 = 0 B1; (x − 4)(x² + 5x + 18) M1 A1; discriminant 25 − 72 = −47 < 0 so no other real roots; 4, 6, 3 cm A1
Q10Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) Factorise x³ − 3x² − 9x − 5 completely. [3]
  2. (b) Hence state the roots of x³ − 3x² − 9x − 5 = 0, and describe what happens to the curve y = x³ − 3x² − 9x − 5 at x = −1. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x + 1)²(x − 5)
    f(−1) = −1 − 3 + 9 − 5 = 0 B1; quotient x² − 4x − 5 M1; (x + 1)(x − 5) so (x + 1)²(x − 5) A1
  2. (b) x = −1 (repeated), x = 5; the curve touches the x-axis at (−1, 0)
    B1 roots; B1 touches (repeated root)
Q11Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find the coordinates of the points where the curve y = x³ + 2x² meets the line y = 5x + 6. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) (−1, 1), (2, 16), (−3, −9)
    x³ + 2x² − 5x − 6 = 0 M1; f(−1) = 0 B1; (x + 1)(x² + x − 6) M1; (x + 1)(x − 2)(x + 3) A1; x = −1, 2, −3 A1; y = 1, 16, −9 A1
Q12Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks

A small business in Faisalabad models its monthly profit, P thousand rupees, t months after opening by P = −t³ + 9t² − 23t + 15, for 0 ≤ t ≤ 6.

  1. (a) Show that P = 0 when t = 1. [1]
  2. (b) Find the other values of t for which P = 0. [3]
  3. (c) Find the profit when t = 4, and state whether the business is making a profit or a loss at that time. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    −1 + 9 − 23 + 15 = 0 B1
  2. (b) t = 3, t = 5
    −(t − 1)(t² − 8t + 15) M1 A1; (t − 3)(t − 5) → t = 3, 5 A1
  3. (c) P = 3, a profit of 3000 rupees
    −64 + 144 − 92 + 15 = 3 M1 A1
Q13Solving cubic equations · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Solve 6x³ + 7x² − x − 2 = 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = −1, −⅔, ½
    f(−1) = −6 + 7 + 1 − 2 = 0 M1 A1; quotient 6x² + x − 2 = (3x + 2)(2x − 1) M1; x = −1, −⅔, ½ A1
Q14Solving cubic equations · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) Factorise 2x³ − x² − 8x + 4 completely. [3]
  2. (b) Hence solve 2e³ʸ − e²ʸ − 8eʸ + 4 = 0, giving your answers in exact form. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x − 2)(x + 2)(2x − 1)
    x²(2x − 1) − 4(2x − 1) M1; (2x − 1)(x² − 4) A1; (2x − 1)(x − 2)(x + 2) A1
  2. (b) y = ln 2, y = −ln 2 (= ln ½)
    eʸ = 2, ½ or −2 M1; eʸ = −2 has no solution B1; y = ln 2, ln ½ A1
Q15Solving cubic equations · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) Show that (x − 1) is a factor of p(x) = x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 and factorise p(x) completely. [3]
  2. (b) Hence solve 8ᵘ − 6(4ᵘ) + 11(2ᵘ) − 6 = 0, giving your answers in exact form. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x − 1)(x − 2)(x − 3)
    p(1) = 1 − 6 + 11 − 6 = 0 B1; quotient x² − 5x + 6 M1; (x − 2)(x − 3) A1
  2. (b) u = 0, u = 1, u = log₂ 3
    Put x = 2ᵘ, so 8ᵘ = x³ and 4ᵘ = x² M1; 2ᵘ = 1, 2 or 3 A1; u = 0, 1, log₂ 3 A1
Q16Solving cubic equations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 7 marks

The point P(1, −2) lies on the curve y = x³ − 4x + 1.

  1. (a) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at P. [3]
  2. (b) The tangent meets the curve again at Q. Find the coordinates of Q. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −x − 1
    dy/dx = 3x² − 4 M1; at x = 1 gradient −1 A1; y + 2 = −(x − 1) → y = −x − 1 A1
  2. (b) Q(−2, 1)
    x³ − 4x + 1 = −x − 1 → x³ − 3x + 2 = 0 M1; (x − 1)²(x + 2) = 0 M1 A1 (repeated root at P); Q(−2, 1) A1
Q17Solving cubic equations · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.By first solving 2t³ − 3t² − 3t + 2 = 0, solve the equation 2(ln x)³ − 3(ln x)² − 3 ln x + 2 = 0, giving your answers in exact form. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = e², x = √e (= e^½), x = 1/e
    f(2) = 16 − 12 − 6 + 2 = 0 M1 A1; (t − 2)(2t² + t − 1) = (t − 2)(2t − 1)(t + 1) M1 A1; ln x = 2, ½, −1 M1; x = e², e^½, e⁻¹ A1

Q20Factor and remainder theorems · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

f(x) = 4x³ − 8x² + kx + 3, where k is a constant. (2x + 1) is a factor of f(x).

  1. (a) Show that k = 1. [2]
  2. (b) Factorise f(x) completely. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    f(−½) = −½ − 2 − k/2 + 3 = 0 M1; k = 1 A1
  2. (b) (2x + 1)(2x − 3)(x − 1)
    Quotient 2x² − 5x + 3 M1 A1; (2x − 3)(x − 1) A1
Q21Factor and remainder theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks

p(x) = x³ + ax² + bx − 12, where a and b are constants. (x − 2) is a factor of p(x), and when p(x) is divided by (x + 1) the remainder is −18.

  1. (a) Find the values of a and b. [4]
  2. (b) Show that the equation p(x) = 0 has only one real root. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = −1, b = 4
    p(2) = 0: 8 + 4a + 2b − 12 = 0 → 2a + b = 2 M1; p(−1) = −18: −1 + a − b − 12 = −18 → a − b = −5 M1; solve A1 A1
  2. (b) Shown: p(x) = (x − 2)(x² + x + 6), discriminant −23 < 0
    Quotient x² + x + 6 M1 A1; 1 − 24 = −23 < 0 so only x = 2 A1
Q22Factor and remainder theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks

f(x) = 2x³ + px² + qx + 6. (x + 2) is a factor of f(x), and the remainder when f(x) is divided by (x − 1) is −6.

  1. (a) Find the values of p and q. [4]
  2. (b) Factorise f(x) completely. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) p = −3, q = −11
    f(−2) = 0: −16 + 4p − 2q + 6 = 0 → 2p − q = 5 M1; f(1) = −6: 2 + p + q + 6 = −6 → p + q = −14 M1; p = −3 A1, q = −11 A1
  2. (b) (x + 2)(2x − 1)(x − 3)
    Divide by (x + 2): 2x² − 7x + 3 M1 A1; (2x − 1)(x − 3) A1
Q23Factor and remainder theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) Show that (2x − 1) is a factor of 2x³ + 5x² − 13x + 5. [2]
  2. (b) Hence solve 2x³ + 5x² − 13x + 5 = 0, giving the irrational roots in exact form. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    f(½) = ¼ + 5/4 − 13/2 + 5 M1; = 0 A1
  2. (b) x = ½, x = (−3 ± √29)/2
    Quotient x² + 3x − 5 M1 A1; x = (−3 ± √(9 + 20))/2 M1 A1
Q24Factor and remainder theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

f(x) = ax³ + bx² + 3x − 2, where a and b are constants. When f(x) is divided by (x − 1) the remainder is 5, and when f(x) is divided by (x + 2) the remainder is −16.

  1. (a) Find the values of a and b. [4]
  2. (b) Find the remainder when f(x) is divided by (2x − 1). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 2, b = 2
    a + b + 1 = 5 → a + b = 4 M1; −8a + 4b − 8 = −16 → 2a − b = 2 M1; a = 2, b = 2 A1 A1
  2. (b) ¼
    f(½) = 2/8 + 2/4 + 3/2 − 2 M1; = ¼ A1
Q25Factor and remainder theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) Both (x − 1) and (x − 3) are factors of x³ + ax² + 11x + b. Find the values of a and b, and the third factor. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = −6, b = −6; third factor (x − 2)
    1 + a + 11 + b = 0 → a + b = −12 M1; 27 + 9a + 33 + b = 0 → 9a + b = −60 M1; a = −6, b = −6 A1 A1; third factor: constant term −6 = (−1)(−3)(−c) gives (x − 2) A1
Q26Factor and remainder theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

f(x) = x³ + ax² − 4x + 2. The remainder when f(x) is divided by (x + 1) is three times the remainder when f(x) is divided by (x − 1).

  1. (a) Find the value of a. [3]
  2. (b) Find the remainder when f(x) is divided by (x + 2). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 4
    f(−1) = a + 5, f(1) = a − 1 B1; a + 5 = 3(a − 1) M1; a = 4 A1
  2. (b) 18
    f(−2) = −8 + 16 + 8 + 2 M1; = 18 A1
Q27Factor and remainder theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) (x + 1) and (2x − 1) are both factors of 2x³ + ax² + bx − 3. Find the values of a and b and hence write the cubic as a product of three linear factors. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 7, b = 2; (x + 1)(2x − 1)(x + 3)
    f(−1) = 0: −2 + a − b − 3 = 0 → a − b = 5 M1; f(½) = 0: ¼ + a/4 + b/2 − 3 = 0 → a + 2b = 11 M1; a = 7, b = 2 A1; comparing constants gives third factor (x + 3) M1 A1
Q28Factor and remainder theorems · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks

p(x) = 3x³ + ax² + bx + c. The remainder when p(x) is divided by x is −4. (x − 1) is a factor of p(x). The remainder when p(x) is divided by (x + 1) is −6.

  1. (a) Find the values of a, b and c. [4]
  2. (b) Show that x = 1 is the only real solution of p(x) = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 1, b = 0, c = −4
    Divide by x means p(0) = c = −4 B1; p(1) = 0: a + b = 1 M1; p(−1) = −6: a − b = 1 M1; a = 1, b = 0 A1
  2. (b) Shown
    p(x) = (x − 1)(3x² + 4x + 4) M1 A1; discriminant 16 − 48 < 0 A1
Q29Factor and remainder theorems · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 9 marks

f(x) = x³ + kx² − 2kx − 8, where k is a constant. When f(x) is divided by (x − k) the remainder is 28.

  1. (a) Show that k³ − k² − 18 = 0. [2]
  2. (b) Show that k = 3 is the only real value of k. [3]
  3. (c) Using k = 3, show that (x + 1) is a factor of f(x) and hence solve f(x) = 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    f(k) = k³ + k³ − 2k² − 8 = 28 M1; 2k³ − 2k² − 36 = 0 → k³ − k² − 18 = 0 A1
  2. (b) Shown
    k = 3: 27 − 9 − 18 = 0 B1; (k − 3)(k² + 2k + 6) M1; discriminant 4 − 24 < 0 A1
  3. (c) x = −1, −4, 2
    f(x) = x³ + 3x² − 6x − 8; f(−1) = −1 + 3 + 6 − 8 = 0 B1; quotient x² + 2x − 8 M1 A1; (x + 4)(x − 2) → x = −1, −4, 2 A1
Q30Factor and remainder theorems · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 8 marks

g(x) = x³ + ax² + bx − 6. g(x) leaves the same remainder when divided by (x − 1) as when divided by (x + 2). Also, (x + 3) is a factor of g(x).

  1. (a) Show that a − b = 3. [2]
  2. (b) Find the values of a and b. [3]
  3. (c) Factorise g(x) completely and state the common remainder. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    g(1) = a + b − 5, g(−2) = 4a − 2b − 14 M1; equate: 3a − 3b = 9 → a − b = 3 A1
  2. (b) a = 4, b = 1
    g(−3) = 0: −27 + 9a − 3b − 6 = 0 → 3a − b = 11 M1; solve with a − b = 3 M1; a = 4, b = 1 A1
  3. (c) (x + 3)(x + 2)(x − 1); remainder 0
    Quotient x² + x − 2 M1 A1; (x + 2)(x − 1), so g(1) = g(−2) = 0 A1
