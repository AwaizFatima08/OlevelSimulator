  1. (a) Calculate the temperature rise of each bottle over the 50 minutes. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the black bottle heats up more. [1]
  3. (c) Both temperatures stop rising towards the end. Explain why each bottle reaches a steady temperature. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) black: 18 °C; white: 8 °C
    B1 each: 48 − 30 = 18 °C; 38 − 30 = 8 °C.
  2. (b) The black surface is a better absorber of infrared radiation.
    B1.
  3. (c) As the bottle gets hotter it emits (and conducts/convects) energy faster; when the rate of losing energy equals the rate of absorbing radiation, the temperature stays constant.
    B1 rate of energy loss increases with temperature; B1 steady when energy in = energy out.
Q60Thermal energy transfer · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) On a cold morning, a metal bench and a wooden bench in a park are both at 5 °C. Explain why the metal bench feels much colder to touch. [3]
  2. (b) Explain, in terms of particles and electrons, why metals are good thermal conductors. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Metal is a good thermal conductor, wood is a poor conductor; the metal conducts energy away from the hand quickly, so the skin cools faster; both are at the same temperature.
    B1 same temperature; B1 metal good conductor, wood insulator; B1 energy conducted from hand faster so skin cools.
  2. (b) Metals have free (delocalised) electrons that move through the metal and carry energy quickly, as well as vibrating lattice particles passing energy on.
    B1 free electrons carry energy; B1 in addition to lattice vibrations.
