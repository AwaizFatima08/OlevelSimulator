Q1Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why places on the Earth have day and night, and state how long one cycle takes. [2]
  2. (b) State the time taken for the Earth to orbit the Sun once. [1]
  3. (c) State the approximate time taken for the Moon to orbit the Earth once. [1]
  4. (d) Name the force that keeps the Moon in orbit around the Earth. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) the Earth rotates (spins) on its axis, so each place faces towards and then away from the Sun; one rotation takes about 24 hours
    rotation on axis (B1); 24 hours (B1)
  2. (b) about 365 days (one year)
    B1
  3. (c) about one month (27–29 days)
    B1
  4. (d) gravitational force (gravity) of the Earth
    B1
Q2Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which list shows four planets in order of increasing distance from the Sun? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars
    The inner planets in order from the Sun are Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars. D is the same list in order of decreasing distance.
Q3Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Lahore is in the northern hemisphere. Its summers are hot and its winters are cool.

  1. (a) Explain why the Earth has seasons. [2]
  2. (b) State the month in which Lahore has its summer, and explain why it is warmer then. [2]
  3. (c) State what the season is in Australia when it is summer in Lahore. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) the Earth's axis is tilted relative to the plane of its orbit; as the Earth orbits the Sun, each hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun for part of the year and away from it for another part
    tilted axis (B1); hemisphere tilted towards/away from Sun at different times of year (B1)
  2. (b) around June (June–August); the northern hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, so the Sun is higher in the sky, its radiation is more concentrated, and days are longer
    June (B1); any one valid reason (B1)
  3. (c) winter
    B1

  1. (a) List the types of object found in the Solar System. [3]
  2. (b) Name the two largest planets, which are gas giants. [1]
  3. (c) State what is meant by a natural satellite and give an example. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) one star (the Sun); eight planets; minor planets such as dwarf planets (e.g. Pluto) and asteroids; moons (natural satellites); comets
    1 mark each for any three types
  2. (b) Jupiter and Saturn
    B1
  3. (c) a natural body that orbits a planet, e.g. the Moon
    B1
Q5Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The Earth orbits the Sun in an approximately circular orbit of radius 1.5 × 10¹¹ m. One orbit takes 365 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the orbital speed of the Earth in m/s. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) State the direction of the gravitational force on the Earth due to the Sun. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.0 × 10⁴ m/s
    T = 365 × 24 × 3600 = 3.15 × 10⁷ s (C1); v = 2πr/T (C1) = 2π × 1.5 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3.15 × 10⁷ = 3.0 × 10⁴ m/s (A1)
  2. (b) towards the centre of the Sun
    B1
Q6Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The Moon orbits the Earth in an approximately circular orbit of radius 3.84 × 10⁸ m, taking 27.3 days for one orbit.

  1. (a) Calculate the orbital speed of the Moon. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why we see the Moon go through phases, such as crescent and full moon, during each orbit. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.0 × 10³ m/s
    T = 27.3 × 24 × 3600 = 2.36 × 10⁶ s (C1); v = 2πr/T (C1) = 1.0 × 10³ m/s (A1)
  2. (b) the Moon does not give out its own light but reflects sunlight; as it orbits the Earth we see different amounts of its sunlit half
    reflects light from the Sun (B1); different portion of lit half visible as it orbits (B1)
Q7Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark

Data for four planets:Mercury: mean distance from Sun 58 million km, orbital period 88 daysVenus: 108 million km, 225 daysMars: 228 million km, 687 daysJupiter: 778 million km, 4330 days

  1. (a) Which statement is supported by the data? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The orbital speed decreases as the distance from the Sun increases.
    Using v = 2πr/T: Mercury 48 km/s, Venus 35 km/s, Mars 24 km/s, Jupiter 13 km/s. D is wrong: Jupiter is 13 times further than Mercury but its period is about 49 times longer.
Q8Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

The four planets closest to the Sun are small and rocky. The four outer planets are much larger and are made mostly of gases.

  1. (a) Name the model that describes how the Solar System formed from a cloud of gas and dust. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how the planets formed according to this model. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the inner planets are rocky while the outer planets are mainly gaseous. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) the accretion model
    B1
  2. (b) a rotating cloud of interstellar gas and dust collapsed under gravity; most material formed the Sun at the centre and the rest formed a flat spinning disc; within the disc, particles collided and stuck together (accreted) under gravity to form larger bodies, eventually planets
    collapse of cloud under gravity (B1); disc around the Sun (B1); particles accrete into planets (B1)
  3. (c) near the Sun the temperature was too high for light substances (gases, ices) to condense, so only rocky/metallic material remained to form planets; further out it was cold enough for gases and ices to collect, forming large gas giants
    high temperature near Sun leaves only rock/metal (B1); colder further out so gases collect (B1)
Q9Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

An astronaut has a mass of 72 kg. The gravitational field strength at the surface of Mars is 3.7 N/kg and at the cloud tops of Jupiter it is 24.8 N/kg.

  1. (a) Calculate the astronaut's weight on Mars and on Jupiter. [2]
  2. (b) State the astronaut's mass on Jupiter. [1]
  3. (c) State two factors that determine the gravitational field strength at the surface of a planet. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 266 N on Mars; 1790 N (1786 N) on Jupiter
    W = mg: 72 × 3.7 = 266 N (B1); 72 × 24.8 = 1786 N (B1)
  2. (b) 72 kg
    mass is unchanged (B1)
  3. (c) the mass of the planet; the radius (size) of the planet
    B1 each
Q10Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Why does the Moon appear to change shape (show phases) during a month? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C As the Moon orbits the Earth, we see different portions of its sunlit half.
    Half of the Moon is always lit by the Sun; its changing position in orbit changes how much of that half faces us. A describes a lunar eclipse, which is rare, not the monthly phases.
Q11Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

The mean distance from the Sun to the Earth is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m. The speed of light and radio waves is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

  1. (a) Calculate the time taken for light from the Sun to reach the Earth, in minutes. [2]
  2. (b) On one day the distance between Earth and Mars is 2.25 × 10¹¹ m. A controller sends a command to a rover on Mars. Calculate the shortest time before the controller can receive a reply confirming the command. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the time in (b) is different on other days. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest why the rover is programmed to avoid obstacles by itself. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 500 s = 8.3 minutes
    t = d/v = 1.5 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 500 s (C1); 8.3 min (A1)
  2. (b) 1500 s (25 minutes)
    one way 2.25 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 750 s (C1); there and back 1500 s = 25 min (A1)
  3. (c) Earth and Mars orbit the Sun at different speeds/radii, so the distance between them changes
    B1
  4. (d) the long signal delay means a human controller cannot react quickly enough to steer it in real time
    B1
Q12Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A comet moves around the Sun in a very elongated elliptical orbit.

  1. (a) State where in its orbit the comet travels fastest, and where slowest. [2]
  2. (b) Explain your answer to (a) in terms of energy. [3]
  3. (c) State how the strength of the Sun's gravitational field changes as the distance from the Sun increases. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) fastest when closest to the Sun; slowest when furthest from the Sun
    B1 each
  2. (b) as the comet moves towards the Sun, gravitational potential energy decreases and kinetic energy increases (total energy is conserved), so it speeds up; moving away, kinetic energy is converted back to gravitational potential energy, so it slows down
    GPE decreases moving towards Sun (B1); KE increases so speed increases (B1); total energy constant (B1)
  3. (c) it decreases
    B1
Q13Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

The International Space Station (ISS) orbits the Earth in an approximately circular orbit of radius 6.78 × 10⁶ m measured from the centre of the Earth. It completes one orbit every 92.7 minutes.

  1. (a) Calculate the orbital speed of the ISS in km/s. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate how many complete orbits the ISS makes in one day. [1]
  3. (c) The Moon orbits much further from the Earth than the ISS. State and explain how the Moon's orbital speed compares with that of the ISS. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.7 km/s
    T = 92.7 × 60 = 5562 s (C1); v = 2π × 6.78 × 10⁶ ÷ 5562 (C1) = 7660 m/s = 7.7 km/s (A1)
  2. (b) 15 (15.5 orbits)
    1440 ÷ 92.7 = 15.5, so 15 complete orbits (B1)
  3. (c) the Moon is slower (about 1 km/s), because the Earth's gravitational field is much weaker at its greater distance
    slower (B1); weaker gravitational field / force at greater distance (B1)
Q14Stars & the Universe · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The Sun is a star. State the two elements it mainly consists of. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the process that releases energy in the Sun. [2]
  3. (c) Name the three regions of the electromagnetic spectrum in which the Sun radiates most of its energy. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) hydrogen and helium
    B1
  2. (b) nuclear fusion: hydrogen nuclei join (fuse) to form helium nuclei, releasing energy
    fusion (B1); hydrogen into helium (B1)
  3. (c) infrared, visible light and ultraviolet
    2 marks all three; 1 mark for two
Q15Stars & the Universe · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The Milky Way is one of billions of galaxies in the Universe. What is its approximate diameter? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 100 000 light-years

Q29Stars & the Universe · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take 1 year = 3.15 × 10⁷ s.

  1. (a) Explain why the age of the Universe can be estimated as 1/H₀, where H₀ is the Hubble constant. [2]
  2. (b) An older measurement gave H₀ = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second. Calculate the age of the Universe this value would give, in years. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) A student says that because nearly every galaxy is moving away from the Earth, the Earth must be at the centre of the Universe. Explain why this conclusion is not correct. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) H₀ = v/d, so 1/H₀ = d/v, which is the time taken for a galaxy moving at constant speed v to travel the distance d from a common starting point; this is the time since all galaxies were together
    1/H₀ = d/v (B1); time for galaxy to reach present distance from the start (B1)
  2. (b) 2.0 × 10¹⁰ years
    1 ÷ 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁸ = 6.25 × 10¹⁷ s (C1); ÷ 3.15 × 10⁷ = 2.0 × 10¹⁰ years (A1)
  3. (c) space itself is expanding, so every galaxy moves away from every other galaxy; an observer in any galaxy would see the same pattern, so there is no centre
    expansion of space / all galaxies moving apart from each other (B1); same view from any galaxy (B1)
