Q1Trading partners and balance of trade · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Illustrative breakdown of a country's exports by value: textiles 60%; food products (rice, fruit, fish, etc.) 18%; other manufactures 14%; other exports 8%.

  1. (a) State two of Pakistan's main imports. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the angle needed for textiles on a pie chart of these exports. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the angle needed for food products. [1]
  4. (d) Explain two ways in which a fall in world prices for textiles would affect Pakistan's balance of trade. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Petroleum (crude oil and petroleum products) and machinery
    1 mark each for any two: petroleum / crude oil; LNG; machinery; palm oil / edible oil; chemicals and fertilisers; iron and steel; vehicles and parts; electrical goods; tea; pulses.
  2. (b) 216°
    60% × 360 = 216°.
  3. (c) 64.8°
    18% × 360 = 64.8°.
  4. (d) Textiles earn most of Pakistan's export income, so lower prices would reduce the value of exports even if the same quantity is sold, which would widen the trade deficit; some mills would cut production or close, so the country would sell even fewer textiles while still needing to import oil, machinery and food, making the deficit worse.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Effects: lower export earnings; wider trade deficit because imports stay high; mills close / output falls; less foreign exchange to pay for imports; pressure on the rupee and need for loans.

Q5Trading partners and balance of trade · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Illustrative figures: Pakistan's exports to one neighbouring country fell from US$1.6 billion in Year 1 to US$1.0 billion in Year 3.

  1. (a) Name two neighbouring countries with which Pakistan currently trades by land. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage fall in exports to the neighbouring country. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why Pakistan's trade with its neighbouring countries is lower than might be expected. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Afghanistan and China
    1 mark each for any two: Afghanistan; China; Iran. (Land trade with India through Wagah has been suspended, so India is not credited.)
  2. (b) 37.5%
    1.6 − 1.0 = 0.6 (M1); 0.6 ÷ 1.6 × 100 = 37.5% (A1).
  3. (c) Political tensions and security problems lead to border closures, so lorries are held up for days and traders lose confidence; road and rail links across the mountains are poor and border crossings have slow customs procedures, which raise transport costs and make goods less competitive.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Reasons: political disputes and border closures; security problems; poor transport links across mountains and deserts; slow customs and paperwork; similar goods produced on both sides; informal trade/smuggling not recorded; sanctions on some neighbours.
Q6Trading partners and balance of trade · Standard · Paper 2 style · 9 marks

Illustrative monthly trade figures for a country (US$ billion):Month 1: exports 2.6, imports 4.9Month 2: exports 2.4, imports 5.2Month 3: exports 2.9, imports 4.6

  1. (a) Calculate the trade deficit for the three months. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate exports as a percentage of imports for the three months. Give your answer to one decimal place. [1]
  3. (c) Remittances from Pakistanis working abroad in the same three months were US$2.5 billion, US$2.7 billion and US$2.9 billion. Calculate whether remittances were enough to cover the trade deficit, and by how much. [2]
  4. (d) Explain two reasons why Pakistan's imports are usually much higher than its exports. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) US$6.8 billion
    Exports 2.6 + 2.4 + 2.9 = 7.9; imports 4.9 + 5.2 + 4.6 = 14.7 (M1); 14.7 − 7.9 = US$6.8 billion (A1).
  2. (b) 53.7%
    7.9 ÷ 14.7 × 100 = 53.7%.
  3. (c) Yes; remittances (US$8.1 billion) exceeded the deficit by US$1.3 billion
    2.5 + 2.7 + 2.9 = 8.1 (M1); 8.1 − 6.8 = US$1.3 billion more than the deficit (A1).
  4. (d) Pakistan produces only a small part of the oil and gas it needs, so it must import large amounts of petroleum and LNG to run transport, power stations and industry; its exports are mainly low-value goods such as cotton textiles and rice, while it imports expensive machinery, vehicles and electronics, so export earnings cannot match the import bill.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Reasons: dependence on imported fuel; imports of machinery and technology for industry; edible oil and other food imports; exports concentrated in a few low-value products; growing population and demand for consumer goods; energy shortages limit export industries.

Q12Trading partners and balance of trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) Pakistan has had GSP+ status with the European Union since 2014. What is GSP+ status? [2]
  2. (b) Name one country with which Pakistan has a free trade agreement. [1]
  3. (c) 'Trade agreements have done more to help Pakistan's balance of trade than any other factor.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) A European Union scheme that allows many exports from a developing country to enter the EU with zero or reduced import duties, in return for the country putting into practice international agreements on human rights, labour rights, the environment and good governance.
    1 mark for duty-free / reduced-tariff access to EU markets; 1 mark for the condition of implementing international conventions.
  2. (b) China
    1 mark: China; Sri Lanka; Malaysia.
  3. (c) For: GSP+ made Pakistani textiles, clothing and leather cheaper in EU markets, so exports to the EU rose and the EU became one of Pakistan's largest export markets.     Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points, one side only. L2 (3–4): developed points, mainly on one side, or simple points on both. L3 (5–6): developed points on both sides, at least one named example from Pakistan, and a judgement. Points to credit: For: GSP+ boost to textile and clothing exports to the EU; FTAs open markets. Against: China FTA raised imports; oil prices and import bill; exchange rate; energy shortages; low value-added exports; competition from Bangladesh, Vietnam; remittances matter for the balance of payments, not the balance of trade.
Q13Trading partners and balance of trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Illustrative trade figures for a country (US$ billion):Year 1: exports 30, imports 55Year 2: exports 31, imports 50Year 3: exports 32, imports 46

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage change in exports from Year 1 to Year 3. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage change in imports from Year 1 to Year 3. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]

Q14Trading partners and balance of trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between the balance of trade and the balance of payments. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two problems for Pakistan of a large and persistent trade deficit. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The balance of trade compares only the value of exports and imports of goods. The balance of payments records all money flows between a country and the rest of the world, including services, remittances, interest, loans and investment, so a country with a trade deficit may have a smaller current account deficit if remittances are large.
    1 mark trade = goods only; 1 mark payments = all transactions (services, remittances, capital/investment, loans); 1 mark example/link to remittances.
  2. (b) Foreign exchange reserves fall, so the country must borrow from abroad (e.g. the IMF) and debt rises; the rupee loses value, raising the price of imports and causing inflation.
    1 mark each for any two: falling reserves; borrowing/debt; currency depreciation; inflation; IMF conditions/austerity; slower growth.

Q21Factors affecting trade · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Q31Imports and exports · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why rice is an important export for Pakistan. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two reasons why the value of rice exports may fall in some years. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pakistan grows large amounts of rice, especially high-quality basmati in the Punjab (e.g. around Gujranwala and Sialkot), which sells at high prices in the Gulf, Europe and elsewhere. Rice exports earn foreign exchange and support the incomes of farmers, mill workers and traders.

Q32Imports and exports · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain two reasons why petroleum products and LNG form the largest group of Pakistan's imports. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest two ways in which Pakistan could reduce its fuel imports. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pakistan's own oil and gas production is small and gas reserves are declining, so it must buy fuel abroad. Demand is very high because transport, industry and thermal power stations all depend on oil and gas, so the import bill is large, especially when world prices are high.
    Two developed points: limited domestic reserves/declining gas; demand from transport, industry, power; thermal-dominated electricity; rising vehicle numbers; high world prices.
  2. (b) Generate more electricity from local and renewable sources such as hydel, solar, wind and Thar coal; improve public transport and energy efficiency to reduce fuel use.
    1 mark each for any two: renewables/hydel; Thar coal; more exploration; public transport (metro buses, ML-1 railway); electric vehicles; energy efficiency; reduce losses.
Q33Imports and exports · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Illustrative export figures for a country:Year 1: total exports US$26 billion, of which 70% were manufactured goodsYear 2: total exports US$32 billion, of which 75% were manufactured goods

  1. (a) Calculate the value of manufactured goods exported in Year 1 and in Year 2. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage increase in the value of manufactured exports. Give your answer to one decimal place. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why an increase in the share of manufactured goods in exports is good for a country's economy. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Year 1: US$18.2 billion; Year 2: US$24.0 billion
    Year 1: 70% × 26 = 18.2 (1); Year 2: 75% × 32 = 24.0 (1).
  2. (b) 31.9%
    (24.0 − 18.2) ÷ 18.2 × 100 = 31.87 ≈ 31.9%.
