Q1Large-scale manufacturing and location · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Name one large-scale industry in Pakistan, other than textiles and sugar, that uses agricultural raw materials. [1]
  2. (b) State two reasons why several large car assembly plants have been built near Karachi. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why large-scale industry in Pakistan is concentrated in a few large cities such as Karachi and Lahore. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Leather (tanning) industry
    1 mark for any one: leather / tanning; flour milling; vegetable ghee and cooking oil; rice husking/processing; paper and board (from wheat straw or bagasse); dairy processing; tobacco/cigarettes.
  2. (b) Imported parts and machinery arrive at the nearby ports, so transport costs are low; Karachi has a large market and a big supply of skilled and unskilled labour.
    1 mark each for any two: near Karachi Port / Port Qasim for imported kits and parts; large local market; plenty of labour including skilled workers; banks and services; good road and rail links to the rest of the country; industrial land available.
  3. (c) These cities have large populations, so factories have both a big local market and a plentiful supply of labour; they already have power supplies, roads, railways and, in Karachi's case, ports, so firms save money by using existing infrastructure instead of building their own.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Reasons: large market; labour supply; existing infrastructure (power, gas, water, transport); port access (Karachi); banks, insurance and government offices; linked industries supplying parts and services (agglomeration).

Q3Large-scale manufacturing and location · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Put these stages of the cotton textile industry in the correct order: weaving, ginning, garment making, spinning, dyeing and finishing. [2]
  2. (b) What happens during ginning? [1]
  3. (c) Explain why Pakistan earns more money by exporting garments than by exporting raw cotton. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ginning → spinning → weaving → dyeing and finishing → garment making.
    2 marks all correct; 1 mark if one pair reversed.
  2. (b) The cotton fibre (lint) is separated from the seeds.
    1 mark.
  3. (c) Each stage of manufacturing adds value, so a kilogram of cotton made into garments sells for many times the price of raw cotton; garment making uses much labour, so it creates more jobs; exports of finished goods earn more foreign exchange; it reduces dependence on world raw cotton prices.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
Q4Large-scale manufacturing and location · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A large tannery in Punjab processes 2,000 animal hides a day. It uses about 250 litres of water to process each hide (illustrative figures).

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of water the tannery uses in one day. Give your answer in m³ (1 m³ = 1,000 litres). [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of water used in a year of 300 working days. [1]
  3. (c) Name two towns in Pakistan with many tanneries. [2]
  4. (d) Explain two ways in which waste water from tanneries can harm people and the environment. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 500 m³
    2,000 × 250 = 500,000 litres = 500 m³.
  2. (b) 150,000 m³
    500 × 300 = 150,000 m³.
  3. (c) Kasur and Sialkot
    1 mark each for any two: Kasur; Sialkot; Karachi (Korangi); Lahore; Multan.
  4. (d) Untreated waste water containing chromium and other chemicals is drained into canals, drains and rivers, where it poisons fish and makes the water unsafe for drinking or irrigation; it also seeps into the ground and contaminates groundwater used for hand pumps, so people living nearby may suffer from skin, stomach and other diseases.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Ways: chromium/chemical pollution of surface water; fish and aquatic life killed; polluted irrigation water damages crops and soil; groundwater contamination; health problems (skin diseases, stomach illness); bad smells.
Q5Large-scale manufacturing and location · Standard · Paper 2 style · 9 marks

Employment in large-scale manufacturing in a district of central Punjab (illustrative figures):Textiles 22,080; Food processing 10,080; Chemicals and fertilisers 5,760; Engineering 4,320; Other industries 5,760. Total 48,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the workers employed in textiles. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the angle needed for food processing on a pie chart. [2]
  3. (c) Using the data, describe the structure of large-scale manufacturing employment in this district. [2]
  4. (d) Explain two reasons why textile industries employ so many workers in districts like this. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 46%
    22,080 ÷ 48,000 × 100 = 46%.
  2. (b) 75.6°
    10,080 ÷ 48,000 = 0.21 (M1); 0.21 × 360 = 75.6° (A1).
  3. (c) Textiles is by far the largest employer, with almost half the workers (46%), more than twice food processing (21%); the other industries each employ 12% or less.
    1 mark for the dominance of textiles, 1 mark for a comparison using figures.
  4. (d) Cotton is grown nearby, so ginning, spinning and weaving mills are built close to the raw material, saving transport costs; many textile processes such as stitching and finishing need a lot of labour, and there is plenty of cheap labour in the surrounding farming villages.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Reasons: local raw cotton; labour-intensive processes; large supply of cheap labour; long history of the industry and skilled workers; export markets / government support for textiles.
Q6Large-scale manufacturing and location · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A sugar mill in central Sindh crushes 4,500 tonnes of sugar cane a day. On average 10.2% of the mass of the cane is recovered as sugar (hypothetical data).

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of sugar the mill produces in one day. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of sugar produced in a crushing season of 120 days. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage of the mass of cane that is lost (not turned into sugar). [2]
  4. (d) Use your answers to explain why sugar mills are located close to the sugar cane fields. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 459 tonnes
    4,500 × 0.102 = 459 t.
  2. (b) 55,080 tonnes
    459 × 120 = 55,080 t.
  3. (c) 89.8%
    4,500 − 459 = 4041 t lost (M1); 4041 ÷ 4,500 × 100 = 89.8% (A1).
  4. (d) Cane loses about nine-tenths of its weight in processing, so it is much cheaper to transport the sugar than the cane; cane is perishable and its sugar content falls quickly after cutting, so it must be crushed within a day or two; cane is bulky and heavy, and farmers deliver it by tractor-trolley over short distances.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
Q7Large-scale manufacturing and location · Standard · Paper 2 style · 9 marks

A cement plant near the Salt Range produces 7,500 tonnes of cement a day. It needs 1.6 tonnes of raw materials to make 1 tonne of cement (hypothetical data).

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of raw materials the plant uses each day. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the mass of raw materials lost during manufacture. [2]
  3. (c) Name the main raw material for cement and one area of Pakistan where cement plants are found. [2]
  4. (d) Explain two environmental problems caused by cement factories. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12,000 tonnes
    7,500 × 1.6 = 12,000 t.
  2. (b) 37.5%
    Loss per tonne = 0.6 t (M1); 0.6 ÷ 1.6 × 100 = 37.5% (A1).
  3. (c) Limestone (with clay / gypsum). Areas: Salt Range / Pothwar (e.g. Chakwal), Kohat, Dera Ghazi Khan, Daudkhel, Rohri, Thatta or Hyderabad.
    1 mark raw material; 1 mark area.
  4. (d) Dust from quarrying and kilns pollutes the air, causing breathing problems for nearby villagers and covering crops; quarrying limestone destroys hillsides and vegetation; kilns burn coal and give off carbon dioxide, adding to climate change; heavy trucks damage roads.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').

  1. (a) Calculate the total value of the textile exports. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the total earned by high value-added products (knitwear, bedwear and readymade garments). Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]

Q10Large-scale manufacturing and location · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 11 marks
  1. (a) Pakistan's cooking oil (vegetable ghee) industry depends heavily on imported palm oil. Name one country from which Pakistan imports palm oil. [1]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why many cooking oil and ghee factories are located in or near Karachi. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) 'A good supply of raw materials is the most important factor in the location of large-scale industry in Pakistan.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Indonesia
    1 mark: Indonesia or Malaysia.
  2. (b) The palm oil is imported by sea, so factories near the port avoid the cost of carrying the heavy raw material inland; Karachi itself is the largest market in Pakistan and has good road and rail links to the rest of the country, so finished products can be sent easily to other cities.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Reasons: imported raw material arrives at Karachi Port / Port Qasim; break-of-bulk location saves transport costs; large market; labour; transport links inland; existing industrial estates and power.
  3. (c) For: sugar mills are built in the cane-growing areas of Sindh and Punjab and cement plants near limestone in the Salt Range and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa because the raw materials are bulky or perishable. Against: many industries such as car assembly, pharmaceuticals and cooking oil choose Karachi and Lahore for their market, port access, labour and power supplies, and imported raw materials can be used anywhere near a port; government incentives in SEZs and industrial estates also attract firms. Judgement, e.g. raw materials matter most for weight-losing industries, but market and infrastructure matter more for most modern industries.

L3 (5–6): developed points on both sides, at least one named example from Pakistan, and a judgement. Points to credit: for: weight-losing / perishable raw materials (sugar, cement, cotton ginning, fertiliser near gas fields such as Daharki). Against: market, labour, power and gas, transport and ports, capital, government policy (SEZs, EPZs, estates), agglomeration.
Q11Large-scale manufacturing and location · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Pakistan Steel Mills was built at Port Qasim near Karachi. Explain two reasons for this location. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'Pakistan should depend on imported steel instead of producing its own.' Evaluate this statement. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Iron ore and coking coal had to be imported, so a site at a port avoided long and costly land transport; Karachi is the largest market for steel (engineering, shipbuilding, construction); a large labour force was available; flat coastal land and sea water for cooling; good rail and road links to the rest of the country.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
  2. Against: imports cost scarce foreign exchange and worsen the trade deficit; steel is needed for construction, engineering and defence, so self-reliance matters; a domestic steel industry creates jobs and linked industries; private steel re-rolling mills already exist. Judgement required.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points, one side or generic. L2 (3–4): developed points on one side, or simple points on both sides. L3 (5–6): developed points on both sides with a named Pakistani example and a reasoned judgement.

Q21Cottage and small-scale industry · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A women's group in a village in Sindh makes hand-stitched quilts (rilli). A middleman pays them Rs 4,000 per quilt and sells each quilt in a city shop for Rs 11,000 (illustrative figures).

  1. (a) Calculate the middleman's share of the final selling price as a percentage. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]

Q28Informal sector · Standard · Paper 2 style · 9 marks

A fruit vendor in Rawalpindi recorded his earnings (after paying for his stock) on six days of one week (illustrative figures):Monday Rs 1,450; Tuesday Rs 1,200; Wednesday Rs 1,800; Thursday Rs 950; Friday Rs 1,600; Saturday Rs 2,100.

  1. (a) Calculate the vendor's mean daily earnings for the six days. Give your answer to the nearest rupee. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the range of the daily earnings. [1]
  3. (c) Using the unrounded mean, estimate the vendor's earnings in a month of 26 working days, to the nearest rupee, and state whether this is above or below the monthly minimum wage of Rs 37,000 used in this question. [2]
  4. (d) Explain two reasons why the income of a street vendor is insecure. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 1,517
    Total = 1,450 + 1,200 + 1,800 + 950 + 1,600 + 2,100 = Rs 9,100 (M1); 9,100 ÷ 6 = 1,516.7 ≈ Rs 1,517 (A1).
  2. (b) Rs 1,150
    2,100 − 950 = Rs 1,150.
  3. (c) Rs 39,433; above the minimum wage
    9,100 ÷ 6 × 26 = 39,433.3 ≈ Rs 39,433 (M1); this is Rs 2,433 above Rs 37,000 (A1).
  4. (d) Earnings depend on the number of customers each day, which falls in bad weather, during Ramadan hours or when there are strikes, so the vendor cannot plan the family budget; vendors have no licence or legal protection, so their stalls can be removed by city officials in anti-encroachment drives and they lose both stock and income.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Reasons: daily earnings vary with customers and weather; no sick pay if ill; removal by authorities / no legal right to the pitch; rising prices of stock; competition from other vendors; no savings or insurance.
Q29Informal sector · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Explain two ways in which the informal sector is linked to the formal sector of Pakistan's economy. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) Read the two views.View A: The government should register all informal businesses and make them pay tax.View B: The government should leave the informal sector alone because it provides jobs for the poor.Which view do you agree with more? Give reasons for your answer, referring to both views. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Many export garment and football factories give work to home-based workers who stitch or finish goods at home for piece rates, so informal workers are part of the supply chain of formal firms; waste pickers collect paper, plastic and metal from city rubbish and sell it through dealers to recycling factories, which use it as cheap raw material.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to'). Links: subcontracting / home-based piece work for export firms; waste pickers supplying recycling industries; informal transport (rickshaws, loaders) carrying goods for formal businesses; street vendors selling factory-made goods; formal workers buying cheap goods and services from the informal sector.
  2. (b) View A: registration would widen the tax base, so the government could spend more on schools and hospitals; registered workers could receive social security and legal protection and could borrow from banks. View B: most informal businesses earn very little, so taxes and paperwork could force them to close and increase unemployment and poverty; the sector absorbs migrants that formal industry cannot employ.
