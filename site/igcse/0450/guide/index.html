IGCSE Business Studies (0450)

Exam guide

Paper structure, topic weights, banker topics, trends, common mistakes and grade thresholds, from our analysis of the May/June 2022 to May/June 2026 papers.

Figures are estimates from one variant of each paper per session. Always check the current syllabus on cambridgeinternational.org.

Paper structure

IGCSE Business Studies is assessed by two written papers. Both are compulsory, both carry the same weight, and there is no choice of questions on either (80 + 80 = 160 marks).

Component Duration Marks Format
Paper 1 Short Answer and Data Response 1 hour 30 minutes 80 4 questions of 20 marks, each based on a short business scenario, sometimes with a small table of figures
Paper 2 Case Study 1 hour 30 minutes 80 4 questions of 20 marks, all about one business described in a separate insert with appendices

Paper 1 layout (the same in every session). Every question is split into five parts:

  • (a) and (b), 2 marks each: a definition, two short points, or a quick calculation.
  • (c), 4 marks: outline two points, normally linked to the scenario. Now and then it is plain recall, such as "identify four".
  • (d), 6 marks: explain two points, or one advantage and one disadvantage, in the context of the business.
  • (e), 6 marks: a judgement. It is either "Do you think …? Justify your answer" or "Explain two … Which is likely to be the most important?".

Paper 2 layout (the same in every session). Each question has an (a) part worth 8 marks and a (b) part worth 12 marks. The (a) parts ask you to explain two or four points, and one (a) per paper is usually generic (no case context needed). The (b) parts set out two or three options, such as two sources of finance, two locations or three promotion methods, and ask which the business should choose. Two or three (b) parts per paper send you to an appendix, and about one per paper wants the choice backed "using suitable calculations".

Changes 2022–2026. The marks, timings and part structure did not change. The one shift is in Paper 1 part (e): every (e) in June 2022, November 2022 and November 2023 was a "Do you think …?" question, but three of the four in June 2026 were "Explain two … which is most important?". Each session has three variants of each paper (11/12/13 and 21/22/23). The grade threshold tables also list a separate 90-mark route (Component 50); none of its papers are in this set.

Bankers

Examined in every session, or in all but one:

  • Marketing mix. The biggest single area, on both papers every time. Know promotion and advertising methods, pricing methods, packaging, the product life cycle and extension strategies, new product development, distribution channels and ecommerce. On Paper 2 it is the most frequent 12-mark decision (which promotion, which channel, which product to drop).
  • Recruitment, selection and training. Induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training, internal versus external recruitment, part-time versus full-time staff, redundancy versus dismissal, employment law, and choosing between applicants shown in an appendix.
  • Costs and break-even. A break-even calculation or chart, margin of safety, fixed, variable and average cost, or economies and diseconomies of scale turn up nearly every session. Paper 2 often asks you to pick between two products using break-even or profit figures.
  • Environmental and ethical issues. External costs and benefits, pressure groups, sustainability, and whether acting ethically helps or harms profit. A 12-mark Paper 2 question on this came up in four of the nine sessions.
  • Production methods. Job, batch and flow production, lean production, efficiency and inventory control.
  • International economy. Globalisation, multinationals (benefits and drawbacks for a country), tariffs and quotas, and exchange rate changes.
  • Organisation and management. Leadership styles (especially democratic and laissez-faire), delegation, span of control, chain of command, the functions of management and trade unions.
  • Enterprise and growth. What makes a successful entrepreneur, business plans, why new firms fail, why some stay small, measuring size, and internal and external growth such as takeovers.
  • Economic issues. Unemployment, recession and the business cycle, interest rates, tax and government spending.

Rarely tested

  • Marketing strategy beyond the mix. Licensing, joint ventures, problems of selling in a new country and legal controls over marketing appeared in only two sessions (November 2024 and June 2025), but each time for more than one small part. The June 2025 examiner report named foreign-market entry as one of the weakest areas.
  • Statement of financial position. Classifying assets and liabilities came up in four sessions and not since November 2024. Easy marks if you have learned the definitions.
  • Classification of businesses. Economic sectors and public versus private sector, nearly always short definitions.
  • Achieving quality. Quality control versus quality assurance appeared once before 2025 but in each of the three latest sessions (see Trends).
  • Cash-flow forecasting and business objectives each appeared in only four sessions. Both are core syllabus ideas and can return at any time.

Trends

  • Paper 1 (e) wording is moving. "Explain two … which is most important?" went from zero of four (e) parts in 2022 to three of four in June 2026. Practise explaining two points fully and then ranking them with a reason.
  • Quality, location and motivation are rising. Quality was tested in all three of the most recent sessions, and location (choosing a country or site, reasons to relocate) in four of the last five.
  • Enterprise and growth stay strong, rising from about 5% of marks in 2022–2023 to over 8% in 2025–2026.
  • International trade has eased off. Tariffs, exchange rates and globalisation took about 8% of marks in 2022–2023 and about 4% since, though something international (usually multinationals) still appears each session.
  • Ratio analysis is a Paper 2 favourite. Current ratio, ROCE or profit margins, or comparing two firms' accounts, carried a 12-mark Paper 2 question in four sessions. Paper 1 always has at least one short calculation.

Question styles

  • Define (2 marks): a precise definition. Vague wording (for example, "short-term" instead of "within 12 months") usually costs the second mark.
  • Identify / State (2 or 4 marks): brief, separate points; no explanation needed.
  • Calculate … show your working (2 marks on Paper 1): break-even output, average cost, profit margin, ROCE, current ratio or total variable cost. Always write the formula, since it can earn a mark even if the answer is wrong.
  • Outline (4 marks): a point plus a link to the business. Analysis is not rewarded here.
  • Explain (6 marks on Paper 1, 8 on Paper 2): for each point, state it, apply it to the business, and develop why it matters.
  • Do you think …? / Which is most important? / Which option should the business choose? (6 marks on Paper 1, 12 on Paper 2): argue both sides, then give a supported decision. On Paper 2, also say why the option you rejected is weaker.
  • Using Appendix X and other information: read tables of accounts, research results, location data or applicant profiles, and comment on the figures rather than copying them out.

Common mistakes

Drawn from the examiner reports for June 2022, November 2022, June 2023 and June 2025 (the others were not in the set):

  • Imprecise definitions. Market share without "of total market sales", current liabilities without the 12-month limit, profit without "total costs", or batch production without saying the group moves through each stage together.
  • No application, or the same application twice. Every point needs its own link to the case (the product, the number of shops or factories, the type of business). Reusing "it makes shoes" for both points only scores once.
  • Repeating the knowledge point instead of developing it, or adding a new point instead of explaining the first one.
  • Weak evaluation. A bare decision, or a summary of what you already wrote, is not evaluation. Give the decision, the reason, and why it beats the alternative.
  • Mixing up terms. Examiners repeatedly flagged quality control versus quality assurance, market segmentation versus market research, globalisation versus multinationals, democratic leadership versus delegation, job rotation versus job enrichment, the business cycle versus the product life cycle, and tariffs versus quotas.
  • Wrong viewpoint or wrong business type. Writing about the franchisee when asked about the franchisor, about employees instead of the business, or about production when the business is a retailer.
  • Economics errors. In 2025 many candidates thought inflation rises in a recession.
  • Paper 2 calculations. Errors in break-even and margin workings, confusing gross profit with profit, missing units ($, million) and unlabelled figures. Weak areas named over the years include economies of scale, lean production, ecommerce, the statement of financial position, absenteeism and labour turnover, and entering foreign markets.
  • Listing instead of developing. On Paper 2 (b), a few well-explained points beat a long list.

Revision priorities for Oct/Nov 2026 and 2027

  1. The whole marketing mix. Promotion, pricing methods, packaging, product life cycle and extension strategies, distribution channels, and ecommerce. It is worth about a ninth of all marks.
  2. Break-even and costs. Formula, chart reading, margin of safety, average cost, and economies and diseconomies of scale, until the calculations are automatic.
  3. Ratios and accounts. Current ratio, ROCE, gross and profit margins, gross profit versus profit, and comparing two businesses or two years.
  4. People. Recruitment and training types, redundancy versus dismissal, leadership styles, financial and non-financial motivation (job rotation, enrichment, teamworking), and named communication methods.
  5. External influences. Ethics and the environment, pressure groups, multinationals and globalisation, exchange rates and tariffs, recession and interest rates.
  6. Production and quality. Job, batch and flow production, lean production, inventory, quality control versus assurance. Quality and location are trending up.
  7. Enterprise and growth. Entrepreneur characteristics, business plans, failure, staying small, measuring size, methods of growth.
  8. Quick review of quieter areas. Statement of financial position (absent since November 2024), entering foreign markets, stakeholder objectives and cash-flow forecasts.
  9. Exam technique. For every Paper 1 (e) and Paper 2 (b), practise a two-sided argument and a justified decision that says why the other option loses. Use a different piece of case detail for each point.

Comparison with O Level Business Studies (7115)

  • The papers are identical. Every paper in the sample (Paper 12 and Paper 22 for all 9 sessions, plus the Paper 22 inserts) was compared word for word with the matching 7115 paper. Apart from the qualification name on the cover, barcodes and copyright lines, the questions, contexts, data and marks are the same.
  • Topic weights are therefore the same as in the 7115 analysis, and the same tags are used.
  • Grade thresholds match too. The option Y totals and the Paper 12 and Paper 22 component boundaries are identical in every session where both files exist. The IGCSE also reports grades F and G, which O Level does not.
  • Other differences. 0450 sits three variants per paper (11–13 and 21–23), while 7115 usually has two. 0450 also lists the separate 90-mark Component 50 route in most of its threshold tables (all June sessions and November 2024); for 7115 it appears only in June 2026. Revision for one exam covers the other fully, and past papers from either code are equally useful practice.
