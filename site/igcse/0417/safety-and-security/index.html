Q1Security threats and prevention · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe what is meant by phishing. [2]
  2. (b) Describe two ways a user can protect themselves from phishing. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The criminal sends a legitimate-looking email (or message) that contains a link to a fake website, to trick the user into entering personal details such as passwords or bank details.
    1 mark each for any two of: email/message that appears to come from a genuine organisation | contains a link/attachment | link leads to a fake website | user tricked into giving personal/financial data.
  2. (b) Do not click links in unexpected emails – type the organisation's web address yourself; check the sender's address and look for spelling mistakes or urgent threats; use a spam filter.
    1 mark each for any two of: do not click links/open attachments from unknown senders | type the URL directly / use bookmarks | check the sender's email address carefully | look for poor spelling/grammar, generic greetings, urgency | use anti-spam/phishing filters | check for https/padlock before entering details | contact the organisation directly to confirm.
Q2Security threats and prevention · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Complete each sentence using the correct term from this list: smishing, vishing, pharming, shoulder surfing.(i) A fake text message that tries to get a person to call a number or visit a website is called ……(ii) Watching a person type their PIN at a cash machine is called ……(iii) A phone call in which a criminal pretends to be from the bank is called ……(iv) Malicious code that redirects a user from a genuine website to a fake one is called …… [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) smishing; (ii) shoulder surfing; (iii) vishing; (iv) pharming.
    1 mark for each correct term.
Q3Security threats and prevention · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Usman's computer has started running slowly, files are disappearing and strange messages appear on screen.

  1. (a) State what is meant by a computer virus. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how anti-virus software helps protect Usman's computer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A program that copies (replicates) itself and is designed to cause harm, such as deleting or corrupting files or making the computer malfunction.
    1 mark each for: program/software that replicates itself | causes damage – deletes/corrupts data or makes the computer crash/run slowly. Accept: attaches itself to other files.
  2. (b) It scans files and compares them with a database of known virus signatures; it also looks for suspicious behaviour (heuristics); infected files are quarantined or deleted, and the database must be updated regularly.
    1 mark each for any three of: scans files/memory/downloads | compares against a database of known virus signatures | heuristic checking for suspicious behaviour | quarantines or deletes infected files | warns the user | runs in the background / scans automatically | needs regular updates to recognise new viruses.
Q4Security threats and prevention · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State two signs that an email may be a phishing email. [2]
  2. (b) State one reason why a bank will never ask a customer to send their PIN by email. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It starts with a general greeting such as 'Dear customer', and it urges you to act quickly or your account will be closed.
    1 mark each for any two of: generic greeting | spelling/grammar mistakes | urgent or threatening tone | sender address does not match the organisation | asks for personal details/passwords | link address differs from the displayed text | unexpected attachment | offer too good to be true.
  2. (b) The bank already holds what it needs to verify the customer; a PIN must stay secret, and email is not secure, so any request for a PIN must be a scam.
    B1: PIN must be known only to the customer | email is not secure/can be intercepted | banks have other ways of verifying – any such request is fraud.
Q5Security threats and prevention · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe what is meant by a brute force attack. [2]
  2. (b) Give one way to make a system resistant to brute force attacks. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) An automated program tries many possible passwords or combinations one after another until the correct one is found.

Q6Security threats and prevention · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between phishing and pharming. [3]
  2. (b) Describe two methods that can be used to prevent pharming. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both try to get personal data using a fake website. In phishing the user must open an email and click a link to reach the fake site. In pharming, malicious code installed on the computer (or a corrupted DNS server) redirects the user to the fake site even when they type the correct web address, so the user does not need to click anything.
    1 mark for similarity: both aim to obtain personal data via fake websites. 1 mark: phishing relies on the user clicking a link in an email/message. 1 mark: pharming uses malicious code/altered DNS so a correctly typed URL is redirected without user action.
  2. (b) Use anti-malware software that can detect and remove pharming code; check that the website uses https and look at its certificate/address before entering details.
    1 mark each for any two of: up-to-date anti-malware/anti-spyware software | check URL is correct in the address bar | look for https/padlock and check digital certificate | use a trusted DNS service | be suspicious if a familiar site looks different.
Q7Security threats and prevention · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A shop owner in Faisalabad finds that customers' card details have been stolen after they used the card reader at the shop counter.

  1. (a) Describe card cloning and explain how it could have happened at the shop. [3]
  2. (b) Describe what is meant by key-logging software and how it could be used to commit card fraud. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Card cloning is copying the data from a card's magnetic stripe to make a fake card. A criminal could have attached a skimmer (a small reading device) to the card reader so that each card's stripe data was read when swiped, and a hidden camera or overlay keypad recorded the PIN.
    1 mark each for any three of: copying data from the card's magnetic stripe | to create a duplicate card | a skimmer fitted to/inside the reader | captures data when card is swiped | PIN recorded by hidden camera/fake keypad/shoulder surfing.
  2. (b) Spyware that records every key pressed on a keyboard and sends the log to a criminal; it could capture card numbers, PINs and passwords typed into online shopping or banking sites, which are then used to make purchases.
    1 mark each for any three of: a type of spyware | records keystrokes | sends the recorded data to a third party | captures card numbers/PINs/passwords typed by user | criminal uses the stolen details for purchases/withdrawals. Prevention not required.
Q8Security threats and prevention · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a firewall protects a computer network. [4]
  2. (b) State two things a firewall cannot prevent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A firewall sits between the network and the internet and examines all incoming and outgoing traffic. It checks each packet against rules set by the user or administrator and blocks traffic that does not meet them, such as from undesirable websites or unknown IP addresses. It can log attempted attacks and warn the user, and helps stop hackers gaining access.
    1 mark each for any four of: between the computer/network and the external network | monitors incoming and outgoing traffic | checks against a set of rules/criteria | blocks traffic that fails the rules | can block specified IP addresses/websites | keeps a log of traffic | warns the user of attempted access | helps prevent hacking/unauthorised access. Can be hardware or software.
  2. (b) It cannot stop a user deliberately sharing their password or data (or an employee misusing access); it cannot stop a virus brought in on a USB stick or one that is already on the computer.
    1 mark each for any two of: users bypassing it e.g. with their own modem | viruses on removable media | individuals within the network misusing access | social engineering / phishing where user gives away details | attacks via encrypted traffic it cannot inspect.
Q9Security threats and prevention · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) For each type of malware, describe what it does.(i) Trojan horse(ii) Ransomware(iii) Adware [3]
  2. (b) A worm does not need to attach itself to a file. Explain why worms can spread faster across a network than viruses. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Malicious code disguised as useful or legitimate software, which installs harmful programs (such as spyware) once run. (ii) Encrypts the user's files and demands payment before the decryption key is given. (iii) Displays unwanted advertisements on screen, often by redirecting the browser to advertising sites.
    1 mark for each correct description.
  2. (b) A worm is a stand-alone program that replicates itself and spreads through networks automatically by exploiting security holes, without needing a user to open or run an infected file.
    1 mark each for: stand-alone / does not need a host program | spreads by itself through the network / does not need user action.
Q10Security threats and prevention · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

A user posts to an online forum, but finds that the forum is full of offensive posts and links to dangerous downloads.

  1. (a) Describe the difference between a moderated forum and an unmoderated forum. [2]
  2. (b) Describe two problems caused by spam and one way to reduce spam. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In a moderated forum an administrator checks posts and removes those that break the rules before or after they appear; in an unmoderated forum posts are not checked, so anything can be posted.
    1 mark each: moderated – posts checked/filtered by an administrator, rule-breaking posts removed, users can be banned | unmoderated – no checks, posts appear without control.
  2. (b) Spam fills inboxes and wastes users' time and storage; it may contain phishing links or malware. To reduce it, use a spam filter (or never reply to spam / do not publish your email address).
    1 mark each for two problems: clogs inbox / wastes time deleting | uses storage/bandwidth | may contain phishing links / malware | replying confirms address is active. 1 mark for one reduction: spam filter | do not reply or click 'unsubscribe' on unknown mail | keep email address private | block senders | untick marketing opt-in boxes.
Q11Security threats and prevention · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a keylogger records what a user types. [2]
  2. (b) Describe one way to protect against keyloggers when a password is entered. [1]

Q12Security threats and prevention · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

A company's network administrator reports that someone outside the company gained unauthorised access to the server and changed customer records.

  1. (a) Describe what is meant by hacking and give one effect it could have on the company. [2]
  2. (b) Evaluate the use of user IDs with passwords, firewalls and two-factor authentication as methods for preventing hacking at this company. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hacking is gaining unauthorised access to a computer system. It can lead to data being deleted, changed or stolen, to identity theft of customers, and to loss of reputation and possible fines for the company.
    1 mark for definition (unauthorised access to a computer system). 1 mark for one effect: data deleted/corrupted/changed | data stolen / identity theft | malicious software planted | loss of customer trust/reputation | legal action/fines.
  2. (b) User IDs and passwords are cheap and give each user their own access level, but weak, reused or stolen passwords are easily exploited. Firewalls block traffic from unknown sources and log attempts but cannot stop an attacker using stolen valid credentials. Two-factor authentication means a stolen password alone is not enough, though it adds cost and inconvenience. Conclusion: a combination of all three, with strong password policies, gives much better protection than any single method.
    Max 4 marks for comments on methods, at least one benefit and one drawback required; 1–2 marks for reasoned conclusion. Passwords: cheap, levels of access | weak/reused/shared/phished. Firewall: blocks unauthorised incoming traffic, logs attempts | does not stop insiders or stolen credentials, needs correct configuration. 2FA: stolen password insufficient | extra device/cost, staff inconvenience, phone lost. Conclusion: layered security / combination best.
Q13Security threats and prevention · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A message arrives on Hamza's phone: 'Your parcel could not be delivered. Pay the Rs 150 redelivery fee within 2 hours at this link.' Hamza has not ordered anything. Explain why this message is likely to be smishing and what Hamza should do. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why criminals ask for a small payment such as Rs 150 rather than a large amount. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is unexpected (he ordered nothing), it creates urgency with a 2-hour deadline, and it asks for payment through a link, which would take him to a fake site to steal his card details. He should not click the link or reply, delete or report the message, and if unsure contact the courier using its official number or website.
    Up to 2 marks for reasons: unsolicited/he has no parcel | urgency/deadline pressure | link to unknown site | asks for card/payment details | small fee to make it seem harmless. Up to 2 marks for actions: do not click link | do not reply | delete/block/report as spam | check with company via official channels | install security software on phone.
  2. (b) A small amount seems believable and not worth worrying about, so more people pay without checking; the real aim is to capture the full card details, which are then used for much larger fraudulent payments.
    1 mark each for: small amount lowers suspicion / seems genuine | the goal is the card details / personal data, which are misused for larger amounts.
Q14Security threats and prevention · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

An online games company stores the account details of two million players, including email addresses, dates of birth and saved payment cards.

  1. (a) Describe three different security threats to this data. [3]
  2. (b) Describe three measures the company should take to protect the data, explaining how each one helps. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hackers breaking into the servers to steal the database; staff being tricked by phishing emails into revealing administrator passwords; ransomware encrypting the database and demanding payment.
    1 mark each for any three different threats applied to the company: hacking | phishing/social engineering of staff | malware/spyware | ransomware | pharming of players to fake login pages | key-loggers on players' computers | insider misuse | card fraud with stolen saved cards.
  2. (b) Encrypt the stored data so that if it is stolen it cannot be read without the key; use firewalls and keep server software patched to block unauthorised access; require two-factor authentication for staff logins so stolen passwords are not enough.
    1 mark per measure with how it helps, any three: encryption – data unreadable if stolen | firewall – blocks unauthorised traffic | 2FA/strong passwords for staff | access rights – staff see only what they need | anti-malware – detects malicious software | staff training on phishing | regular backups – recover after ransomware | do not store full card numbers / use payment provider.
Q15Security threats and prevention · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A company's online shop is taken offline by a DDoS attack that lasts for two hours.

  1. (a) Explain why a distributed denial of service (DDoS) attack stops a website from being available to its customers. [2]
  2. (b) Describe one method that a company can use to reduce the effect of a DDoS attack. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Many computers, often infected and controlled remotely, send so much traffic to the server that it cannot respond to genuine customers.
    1 mark: a very large amount of traffic from many computers (botnet). 1 mark: the server is overloaded and cannot serve real users.
  2. (b) Use a traffic filter or firewall that blocks traffic from suspicious sources; use a content delivery network or cloud service that spreads the load.
    1 mark: a valid method (filtering, rate limiting, load balancing, cloud DDoS protection). 1 mark: explained as a way the traffic is blocked or spread out.

Q21eSafety and data protection · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Q28eSafety and data protection · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A school wants to put photographs of students taking part in sports day on its public website, with each student's full name under the photo.

  1. (a) Discuss the eSafety and data protection issues the school should consider before doing this. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Photos with full names are personal data, so the school needs consent from parents (and students) and must use the images only for the stated purpose. Naming students alongside their school lets strangers identify and locate children, creating a risk of grooming. Images on a public site can be copied and misused. Some students may have safeguarding reasons not to be shown. Benefits: celebrating achievement and promoting the school. Conclusion: publish only with written consent, use first names or no names, avoid close-ups, or use a password-protected area.
    Up to 5 marks for issues/benefits, 1 mark for reasoned conclusion. Points: personal data requires consent | purpose limitation | identifying children + school location = safeguarding risk | images copied/edited/misused | permanent once online | some students must not be identifiable (custody/safeguarding) | right to withdraw consent | benefit – celebrates students, promotes school. Conclusion: consent, no surnames, private area, review regularly.

  1. (a) State what a cookie is. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why a user might refuse non-essential cookies when a website asks for permission. [2]

Q42SSL, encryption and digital certificates · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) Explain why the bookshop still needs other security measures even though it uses SSL. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The digital certificate, issued by a certificate authority, proves that the website really belongs to the bookshop, so customers know they are not on a fake site.     1 mark each for any two of: SSL protects data in transit only | stored data on server needs encryption/firewall/access control | does not prevent hacking of the server | does not stop phishing/malware on customer's computer.
Q43SSL, encryption and digital certificates · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A 40-bit encryption key is compared with a 128-bit key. Calculate how many times more possible keys the 128-bit key gives. Give your answer as a power of 2. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a longer key makes a brute force attack harder. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2⁸⁸ times more keys
    M1: 2¹²⁸ ÷ 2⁴⁰ = 2^(128−40). A1: 2⁸⁸ times more possible keys.
  2. (b) A brute force attack must try each possible key, and a longer key has far more possibilities, so it takes far longer.
    1 mark: more possible keys, so trying them all takes much longer.
