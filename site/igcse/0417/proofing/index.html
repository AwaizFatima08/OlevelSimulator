Q1Validation and verification together · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by validation. [1]
  2. (b) State what is meant by verification. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) An automatic check by the computer that data entered is sensible/reasonable/follows set rules.
    1 mark.
  2. (b) A check that data has been copied/entered accurately from the source document (or transferred without change).
    1 mark. Do not accept 'checks that data is correct'.

    All four correct: 2 marks; two or three correct: 1 mark.

Q10Validation and verification together · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A clerk is typing students' examination marks from paper mark sheets into a database. Explain why both validation and verification should be used. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Validation will reject marks that are impossible/unreasonable, e.g. 112 when the maximum is 100; but validation cannot detect a reasonable but wrong mark, e.g. 67 typed as 76; verification (double entry/visual check) compares the entry with the mark sheet so it finds these copying errors; but verification does not check the data is sensible – a mark copied correctly may still be impossible if the sheet is wrong; so together they reduce errors (although neither guarantees the data is correct).
    1 mark per point, max 4. Must refer to both methods.
Q11Validation and verification together · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how double data entry is used to verify data. [2]
  2. (b) Give one disadvantage of double data entry. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The data is entered twice (often by two different people); the computer compares the two versions and flags/rejects any differences so they can be checked against the source.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Takes twice as long/costs more in staff time; if both people make the same mistake it is not detected; it does not detect errors in the source document.
    1 mark.
Q12Validation and verification together · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a visual check is carried out. [2]
  2. (b) Give one disadvantage of a visual check. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) After data is entered, the person compares the data on the screen with the original source document, item by item, and corrects any differences.
    1 mark for comparing with the source, 1 mark for correcting differences.
  2. (b) The person may not notice errors (tiredness/boredom); slow for large amounts of data; the person who typed the data may make the same reading error again.
    1 mark.

Q14Validation and verification together · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

Q16Validation and verification together · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Contrast validation and verification. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Validation is carried out automatically by the computer, whereas verification is often done by a person (visual check) or by entering data twice; validation checks data is reasonable/follows rules, while verification checks data has been copied accurately from the source; validation can reject impossible values (e.g. 32/13/2026) but not correctly formatted wrong data, while verification can find copying mistakes but not errors in the source; validation uses checks such as range/presence/format, verification uses double entry/visual check.
    1 mark per contrasting point, max 4. Points must refer to both methods.

Q22Validation and verification together · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A bank in Karachi employs staff to type customer details from paper application forms into its computer system. Explain how validation and verification can be used to reduce errors in the data. Include examples relevant to the bank. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Validation: checks done by the computer as data is entered; presence check that the customer's name and CNIC number are entered; format check on the CNIC number (5 digits-7 digits-1 digit); range check on date of birth so the customer is at least 18; lookup check on branch code/account type; type check that the phone number contains only digits. Verification: double data entry – two staff type each form and the computer compares the two copies, flagging differences; visual check – the clerk compares the screen with the paper form.     1 mark per valid point with example, max 6. Max 4 if only one of validation/verification is covered.

Q26Validation and verification together · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe what a consistency check is. Give an example from a school database. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how a consistency check is different from a range check. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why data that passes all validation and verification checks may still be wrong. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It checks that data in two (or more) fields agrees/makes sense together; e.g. if Title is 'Mrs' then Gender must be F; or LeavingDate must be after JoiningDate; or a student in Year 7 cannot have an age of 17.
    1 mark for description, 1 mark for example.
  2. (b) A range check compares one field with fixed upper and lower limits; a consistency check compares the value in one field with the value in another field.
    1 mark.
  3. (c) Checks only find data that is unreasonable or wrongly copied; the source data itself could be wrong (e.g. the person gave the wrong information).
    1 mark.
Q27Validation and verification together · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A 13-digit book number uses a check digit. To find it, the first 12 digits are multiplied alternately by 1 and 3 (starting with 1), the results are added, and the check digit is the number that must be added to the total to reach the next multiple of 10 (0 if the total is already a multiple of 10). Calculate the check digit for 978969803421. [3]
  2. (b) A clerk types the number with the last two digits before the check digit swapped, 978969803412, followed by the check digit 4. Show that the computer will detect this error. [2]
  3. (c) State whether a check digit is a validation or a verification method. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4
    Total = 9+21+8+27+6+27+8+0+3+12+2+3 = 126 (M1 for weights, A1 for 126); next multiple of 10 is 130, so check digit = 4 (A1).
  2. (b) The weighted total for 978969803412 is 128, so the check digit should be 2, not 4; the numbers do not match and the entry is rejected.
    1 mark: total 128 / correct check digit 2; 1 mark: mismatch with 4 so error detected.
  3. (c) Validation.
    1 mark.

Q30Validation and verification together · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

  1. (a) mane → main; Mondey → Monday; there → their
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Only 'Mondey' would be detected because it is not in the dictionary; 'mane' and 'there' are real words, so the spell checker accepts them even though they are wrong in this context.
    1 mark for Mondey, 1 mark for explanation.
Q41Verification and proofreading · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) When proofreading a document you must make sure there are no widows or orphans. State what is meant by a widow and an orphan. [2]
  2. (b) Describe one way to prevent widows and orphans in a word-processed document. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Widow: the last line of a paragraph appears on its own at the top of a new page/column. Orphan: the first line of a paragraph appears on its own at the bottom of a page/column.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Turn on widow/orphan control in the paragraph settings (or insert a page/column break before the paragraph; keep lines together).
    1 mark.
Q42Verification and proofreading · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a visual check needs the original source document. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why it is better for a different person to carry out the visual check. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The data on screen must be compared with the original to see whether it has been copied accurately; without the source there is nothing to check it against, so only obvious errors could be spotted.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) The person who typed the data may repeat the same misreading; a second person is more likely to spot mistakes.
    1 mark.

Q45Verification and proofreading · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the autocorrect feature can introduce errors into a document. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how a writer can reduce this problem. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It automatically changes words it thinks are misspelt, without asking; it can replace correct names or technical words (for example a surname or a medicine name) with a common word, and the writer may not notice.

Identify three errors that should be found by proofreading and state the correction for each. [3]

  1. (a) Title names the wrong city – change to Lahore; vertical axis has no title/units – add 'Rainfall (mm)'; legend shows 'Series1' – rename the series or remove the legend since there is only one series.

Q50Verification and proofreading · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A charity in Peshawar must enter 3,600 paper application forms. One typist enters 45 forms per hour and is paid Rs 700 per hour. Calculate the cost of entering the forms once. [2]
  2. (b) The charity decides to use double data entry. Calculate the extra cost and explain what the extra cost buys. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 56,000
    3,600 ÷ 45 = 80 hours (M1); 80 × 700 = 56,000 (A1).
  2. (b) Extra Rs 56,000. Each form is typed twice and the computer compares the two copies, so typing errors that would not be detected by validation are found.
    1 mark: 56,000 (total 112,000); 1 mark: explanation of double entry benefit.
Q51Verification and proofreading · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A database report lists customers of a courier company in Lahore. When proofreading it, Zara finds that long customer names are cut off, for example 'Muhammad Abdul Rehm', and that one column heading reads 'CustTelNo'. Explain the cause of each problem and how to correct it. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Names cut off: the column in the report is too narrow for the longest data – widen it (or reduce the font size) so all of the data shows. 'CustTelNo': the field name has been used as the label – edit the label to a clear heading such as 'Telephone number'.
    1 mark for each cause and 1 mark for each correction.
Q52Verification and proofreading · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student types a travel guide about Pakistan. The spell checker underlines the correctly spelt place names Peshawar, Hunza and Skardu. Explain what the student should do with each place name and why she should not choose 'Change' or 'Change All'. [3]
  2. (b) The guide is written for a British publisher. State which dictionary language the student should set and why. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Choose 'Ignore All' (or 'Add to dictionary' if the names will be used again); the names are correct but are not in the software's dictionary; 'Change'/'Change All' would replace the correct names with the suggested (wrong) words throughout the document.
    1 mark for ignore/add, 1 mark for why flagged, 1 mark for danger of Change All.
  2. (b) English (UK), so that British spellings such as 'colour' and 'centre' are accepted and US spellings are flagged.
    1 mark.
Q53Verification and proofreading · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A research organisation in Islamabad must enter the answers from 5000 paper survey forms. Compare double data entry and visual checking as methods of verifying the data. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both compare the entered data with the source to find copying errors and neither finds errors in the source. Double entry is automatic in the comparison so it finds almost all differences, whereas a visual check relies on a person noticing errors and may miss some through tiredness; double entry needs each form typed twice so costs more staff time, while a visual check is a single re-read; for 5000 forms a visual check would be very tiring, so double entry is more reliable; double entry cannot detect the same mistake made twice.
    1 mark per comparison point, max 4; at least one similarity for full marks.
Q54Verification and proofreading · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A document is set out in two columns. After proofreading, a student notices that the subheading 'Visiting Lahore Fort' is the last line of the first column while its paragraph starts at the top of the second column. Explain why this is a problem and describe how to fix it. [3]
  2. (b) A table in the same document is split across two pages. Describe two ways to make the table easier to read. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A heading should stay with the text it introduces – the reader sees it separated from its content (unprofessional/harder to read); fix by setting 'Keep with next' on the heading/heading style or by inserting a column break before the subheading.
    1 mark for problem, 2 marks for a correct method described.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. move the table/insert a page break so it is on one page; set table rows not to break across pages/keep with next; repeat the header row on the second page.
    1 mark each.
