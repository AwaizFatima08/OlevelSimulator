Practice questions

Original questions written by HomiLabs in the style of the exam, with answers and mark schemes. They are not Cambridge past paper questions and have not been reviewed by Cambridge. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by software copyright. [1]
  2. (b) Give two actions that would break software copyright. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The legal right of the creator/owner of software to control how it is copied, used, changed and distributed.
    B1 legal protection/ownership – others need permission to copy or distribute.
  2. (b) Any two: making copies of the software and giving or selling them to others; installing it on more computers than the licence allows; using parts of the code in your own program without permission; renting or lending it out without permission; removing or bypassing the copy protection / using a cracked key; modifying it and distributing it without permission.
    1 mark each.
Q2Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by software piracy. [1]
  2. (b) Give two reasons why software piracy is harmful to software companies. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Illegally copying, distributing or using software without a valid licence / the copyright owner's permission.
    B1
  2. (b) Any two: loss of sales income; less money to invest in developing new software and updates; jobs may be lost; harm to reputation if pirated copies contain malware or faults.
    1 mark each.
Q3Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe three methods used by software producers to prevent their software being copied illegally. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three: a unique product/licence key must be entered during installation; online activation links the key to one device so it cannot be reused elsewhere; the user must accept a licence agreement before installing; holograms on packaging show it is genuine; a dongle (hardware key) must be plugged in for the software to run; the original disc/memory stick must be present to run it; the program code is encrypted and needs a key to run.
    1 mark each for any three described.
Q4Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks

Zain bought a licensed copy of a video-editing program. His friend asks Zain to install it on the friend's laptop as well.

  1. (a) Explain why Zain should not do this. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The licence usually allows use on one computer only, so installing it on another person's laptop is illegal copying/breaks copyright; the producer loses the income from a sale.
    B1 licence limits installations/is for Zain only. B1 breaks copyright law / illegal / unfair to producer.
Q5Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a hologram on software packaging helps to protect copyright. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Holograms are difficult to copy, so they show buyers that the product is genuine; customers and shops can recognise counterfeit (pirated) copies that lack the hologram.
    B1 hard to copy. B1 identifies genuine copy / detects fakes.
Q6Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why copyright laws are especially important for software. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Software can be copied quickly, easily and cheaply; every digital copy is identical to the original; copies can be distributed worldwide over the internet in seconds; developers invest a lot of time and money, so the law protects their income and encourages new software.
    1 mark each for any three.
Q7Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) When installing software, the user is asked to accept a licence agreement. Give three things the agreement is likely to state. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three: the number of devices/users the software may be installed on; that the software must not be copied or distributed; that it must not be modified or reverse engineered; whether it can be used for commercial purposes; that the user does not own the software, only a right to use it; the length of the licence; limits of the producer's responsibility.
    1 mark each.
Q8Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) A publisher sells ePublications that can only be opened on the buyer's registered devices. Describe how this protects the publisher's copyright. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The file is encrypted and linked to the buyer's account/devices, so if it is copied to another person's device it cannot be opened, stopping illegal sharing.
    B1 encrypted/locked to account or device. B1 copies cannot be read by others.
Q9Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between a single-user licence and a multi-user licence. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A single-user licence allows the software to be installed/used by one user on one computer; a multi-user licence allows a set number of users or computers (e.g. in a network) to use it.
    B1 each.
Q10Software copyright · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A student in Islamabad writes a mobile game and uploads it to an app store.

  1. (a) Give two ways the student could protect the game from being copied. [2]
  2. (b) State one benefit to the student of copyright law. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: include a licence agreement users must accept; require users to log in / activate with an account; encrypt or obfuscate the code; include a copyright notice; use the app store's licence-checking system.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) It gives the student the legal right to control and earn money from the game, and to take action against anyone who copies it.
    B1
Q11Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A college in Peshawar has 120 computers. A design program is installed on all of them, but timetabling means that at most 30 students ever use the program at the same time.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a concurrent-user licence. [1]
  2. (b) State the number of concurrent-user licences the college needs and explain why this may be cheaper than licensing every computer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A licence that allows a set maximum number of people to use the software at the same time, regardless of how many computers it is installed on.
    B1
  2. (b) 30 licences; the college pays for 30 simultaneous users instead of 120 individual installations.
    B1 30. B1 fewer licences than computers.
Q12Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A school in Lahore needs a mathematics program on 40 computers. Single-user licences cost Rs 4,500 each. A site licence for the whole school costs Rs 150,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of buying single-user licences for all 40 computers. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate how much the school saves by buying the site licence. [1]
  3. (c) Give one other advantage of a site licence for the school. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 180,000
    40 × 4,500 = 180,000. B1
  2. (b) Rs 30,000
    180,000 − 150,000 = 30,000. B1
  3. (c) More computers can be added later without buying extra licences (within the site); easier to manage one licence.
    B1
Q13Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A software audit at a logistics company in Karachi finds the accounts program installed on 75 computers, but the company owns only 60 licences. Each extra licence costs Rs 8,200.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of buying enough licences so that all installations are legal. [2]
  2. (b) State one other way the company could become legal. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 123,000
    M1 75 − 60 = 15 licences. A1 15 × 8,200 = 123,000.
  2. (b) Uninstall the program from the 15 unlicensed computers.
    B1
Q14Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a dongle is used to protect software. [2]
  2. (b) Give one disadvantage of this method for a legitimate user. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A dongle is a small hardware device plugged into a USB port; the software checks for the dongle and will only run while it is connected, so copies of the software are useless without it.
    B1 hardware device plugged into computer. B1 software only runs when it is present.
  2. (b) Any one: if the dongle is lost or damaged the software cannot be used; it takes up a USB port; it can only be used on one computer at a time.
    B1
Q15Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A security program is sold in packs, each licensed for up to 5 devices. A family in Rawalpindi has 4 laptops, 6 smartphones and 2 tablets that all need protecting.

  1. (a) Calculate the smallest number of packs the family must buy. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why installing the program from 2 packs on all 12 devices would be wrong. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3 packs
    M1 4 + 6 + 2 = 12 devices. A1 12 ÷ 5 = 2.4 → 3 packs.
  2. (b) Two packs cover only 10 devices; using it on 12 breaks the licence terms/copyright.
    B1
Q16Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain how 'trial versions' of software help software producers sell their product while still protecting it. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Users can try the program free for a limited time or with limited features, which encourages them to buy; after the time ends the software stops working (or features stay locked) until a licence key is purchased; so the full program is not given away.
    B1 try before buying / advertising. B1 time-limited or feature-limited. B1 needs purchase/key to unlock full version.
Q17Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A teacher in Gujranwala is making a presentation for 12-year-old students and wants to use photographs and music she found on the internet.

  1. (a) Explain why she should check the copyright of the photographs and music before using them. [2]
  2. (b) Describe two ways she can use suitable images legally. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The material belongs to its creator and is protected by copyright; using it without permission (or outside its licence terms) may be illegal, so she must check whether it is free to use, needs permission, or must be credited.
    B1 protected by copyright / owned by creator. B1 need permission or licence / acknowledgement.
  2. (b) Any two: use images that are copyright-free or licensed for reuse (and follow the licence terms, e.g. credit the creator); ask the owner for permission; buy a licence from a stock image library; take her own photos.
    1 mark each.
Q18Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

For a coursework project, a student copies large sections of program code from a website and submits it as his own work.

  1. (a) Explain two reasons why this is wrong. [2]
  2. (b) State what the student should have done if he wanted to use some of this code. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It may break the copyright of the code's author if permission/licence terms are not followed; it is plagiarism – presenting someone else's work as your own, which is dishonest and breaks exam/school rules.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Check the licence allows reuse and acknowledge/credit the source clearly (or ask permission).
    B1
Q19Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A school has a 50-computer licence for a science simulation program. The head teacher asks the IT technician to install it on the home laptops of the 12 science teachers as well.

  1. (a) Explain what the technician should check before doing this. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the total number of installations if all school computers and all teachers' laptops have the program. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Whether the licence terms allow home use / installation on staff devices, and whether the number of installations would exceed 50 (some licences count every installation).
    B1 licence terms on home/off-site use. B1 limit on number of installations.
  2. (b) 62
    50 + 12 = 62. B1
Q20Software copyright · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Some older programs would only run if the original disc was in the drive. Explain how this protected the software and give one drawback for the user. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The program checked for a special code/feature on the original disc that ordinary copying could not reproduce; a copied installation would not run without the original disc. Drawback: the user had to keep the disc with them and could not run the software if it was lost or scratched / the computer had no disc drive.
    B1 check for original disc. B1 copies lack the feature so will not run. B1 drawback.
Q21Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

An architect's office in Islamabad can buy design software for a one-off payment of Rs 60,000 per user, or pay a subscription of Rs 1,500 per user per month.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of months after which the total subscription cost equals the one-off payment. [2]
  2. (b) Give one advantage and one disadvantage of the subscription for the office, apart from cost. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 40 months
    M1 60,000 ÷ 1,500. A1 40.
  2. (b) Advantage: updates/new versions and support are included; can easily reduce the number of users. Disadvantage: the software stops working if payments stop; often needs internet to check the licence; price may rise.
    B1 advantage. B1 disadvantage.
Q22Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a product key on its own may not stop software piracy, and how online activation improves protection. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A product key can be copied and shared with others, so many people could install the software with the same key; it may also be generated by key-generating programs. Online activation sends the key (and details of the device) to the producer's server, which checks it has not already been used on more devices than allowed and ties it to that hardware, so a shared key will be rejected.
    B1 key can be shared. B1 or generated / no check of number of uses. B1 activation checks key with server. B1 linked to hardware/limits number of installs.
Q23Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe the differences between freeware, shareware and free (open source) software. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Freeware is free to use but is still protected by copyright, the source code is not available and users may not modify it. Shareware is free to try for a limited time or with limited features, after which the user must pay to continue. Free/open-source software is supplied with its source code, which users may study, modify and redistribute, subject to the conditions of its licence.
    1 mark each.
Q24Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A student in Multan downloads a 'cracked' copy of an expensive photo-editing program from a file-sharing site, saying that the company is rich and will not miss the money.

  1. (a) Discuss the legal, ethical and practical issues of the student's action. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Legal: it breaks copyright law and the licence terms; the student could be fined or face legal action. Ethical: it is unfair to the developers who are not paid for their work; it is effectively theft; argument that the company is rich does not make it right. Practical: cracked copies often contain viruses/spyware that can steal data; no updates or technical support; may stop working; a free alternative or student discount could be used instead. Conclusion.
    1 mark per valid point; at least two of legal/ethical/practical needed for full marks; 6th mark for reasoned conclusion.
Q25Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why software provided as an online (cloud-based) service is harder to pirate than software installed from a disc. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The program code runs on the company's servers rather than being copied onto the user's computer; users must log in to an account, so access is controlled and checked each time; a licence that is not paid for can be cancelled immediately.
    1 mark each.
Q26Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A software house in Lahore needs an office program for 25 staff. An annual licence costs Rs 9,600 per user. A volume discount of 15% is given for 20 or more users.

  1. (a) Calculate the total annual cost for 25 staff after the discount. [2]
  2. (b) Two staff leave, so only 23 licences are needed. Calculate the new annual cost. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 204,000
    M1 25 × 9,600 = 240,000. A1 × 0.85 = 204,000.
  2. (b) Rs 187,680
    23 × 9,600 × 0.85 = 187,680. B1 (still ≥ 20 users so discount applies).
Q27Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A programmer buys a licensed app and changes its code so that it works in Urdu, then sells the new version online.

  1. (a) Explain whether this is allowed under normal software copyright. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Not allowed: the programmer bought only a licence to use the app, not ownership of the code; licences normally forbid modifying/reverse engineering the code; selling the changed version distributes the copyright owner's work without permission, so it is illegal (unless the licence, e.g. some open-source licences, specifically permits it).
    B1 licence = right to use, not to change. B1 modification/reverse engineering forbidden. B1 distribution/selling without permission illegal.
Q28Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Discuss the risks to a company of using unlicensed software on its computers. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Legal: fines, legal action and having to pay for all licences; possible criminal charges for managers. Security: unlicensed copies may contain malware, risking data theft. Operational: no updates or security patches; no technical support; software may stop working when detected. Reputation: customers and partners lose trust if it becomes public. Also: some may argue it saves money in the short term, but costs of being caught are greater. Conclusion.
    1 mark per valid point; max 5 without a conclusion; full marks need a range of risk types.
Q29Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A start-up in Karachi plans to use open-source software for its office tasks instead of buying commercial software.

  1. (a) Evaluate this plan. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Benefits: usually no licence fee; source code available so it can be adapted; large user communities for help; no restriction on number of installations (depending on licence). Drawbacks: may have less official technical support; staff may need training if they are used to commercial programs; file compatibility with customers' software may be an issue; licence conditions still apply (e.g. changes may have to be shared if distributed); updates depend on the community. Conclusion.
    1 mark per valid point; max 4 if one-sided; 6th mark for conclusion.
Q30Software copyright · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Three friends share one paid account for an online music-making program, each logging in from their own computer.

  1. (a) Explain whether this is acceptable, and describe how the software company might detect and prevent it. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is usually not acceptable: the subscription licence is for one user, so sharing breaks the terms and the company loses income. Detection/prevention (any two/three): limiting the number of devices or simultaneous logins on one account; checking the location/IP address of logins; requiring two-factor authentication to a single phone; device registration.
    B1 breaks licence/one-user terms. B1 unfair/loss of income. Up to 2 for detection/prevention methods.
