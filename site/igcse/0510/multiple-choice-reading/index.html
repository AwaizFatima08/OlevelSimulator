Q1Multiple-choice questions on an article · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read the article about a young chess champion from Lahore. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.The girl who beat the grandmasterWhen twelve-year-old Mehak Rizvi sat down opposite a visiting grandmaster at a Lahore hotel last March, few people in the room expected the game to last long. Two hours later, the grandmaster stood up, shook her hand and resigned.Mehak learned the game at the age of six from her grandfather, who used to play with her every evening after dinner. 'He never let me win,' she says. 'That made me angry at first, but it taught me that I had to earn every victory.' By the time she was nine, she was beating everyone in her family, and her school chess teacher suggested she join a club in the city.Her parents were not sure at first. The club met three evenings a week, and her father worried that chess would take time away from her studies. However, her marks actually improved, and her mother noticed that she had become much more patient when doing her homework.Mehak now trains for about two hours a day. She spends one hour solving puzzles and the other playing online against opponents from other countries. She says that the hardest part is not learning new openings but staying calm when she makes a mistake. 'In the past, one bad move would ruin the whole game for me, because I couldn't stop thinking about it. Now I take a deep breath and focus on the next move.'Her dream is to become Pakistan's first female grandmaster. To reach that title, she will need to play in international tournaments, which are expensive to travel to. A local company has offered to pay for her trip to a youth championship in Malaysia next year, and she is already preparing.

  1. (a) Who first taught Mehak to play chess? [1]
  2. (b) What did Mehak learn from the way her grandfather played? [1]
  3. (c) Why were Mehak's parents unsure about the club? [1]
  4. (d) How does Mehak spend half of her daily training? [1]
  5. (e) What does Mehak find most difficult? [1]
  6. (f) What will happen next year? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C her grandfather
    She learned at six from her grandfather. The school teacher only suggested she join a club (A).
  2. (b) C that winning has to be deserved
    'He never let me win… I had to earn every victory'. She did get angry (A), but that was not the lesson.
  3. (c) B Her father thought it might affect her schoolwork.
    Her father worried chess would take time from her studies. Patience (A) was something her mother noticed later as an improvement.
  4. (d) B playing opponents abroad online
    One hour puzzles, one hour online against opponents from other countries. Openings (A) are mentioned as not the hardest part.
  5. (e) B keeping calm after a mistake
    'the hardest part is not learning new openings but staying calm when she makes a mistake'.
  6. (f) A Mehak will travel to Malaysia with a company's help.
    A local company will pay for her trip to a youth championship in Malaysia. Becoming a grandmaster (B) is only her dream.
