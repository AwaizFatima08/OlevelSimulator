Read the article about a teenager who keeps bees in northern Pakistan, and then answer the questions.The bee boy of the valleyWhen Bilal Khan was twelve, his grandfather gave him a wooden box with a small colony of bees inside. 'He told me that if I looked after them for one summer, I could keep any honey they made,' Bilal remembers. Five years later, the seventeen-year-old looks after forty hives on the hillside behind his family's house in Swat.At first, Bilal was stung almost every day. He soon learned that bees become nervous when people move quickly, so he now works slowly and calmly. He also checks the hives early in the morning, when most of the bees are still inside and the air is cool.The hardest time of year is winter. Snow covers the valley and there are no flowers, so Bilal feeds his colonies with sugar syrup until spring. In March, the apple and apricot trees begin to blossom and the bees become busy again.Bilal sells most of his honey at the Saturday market in Mingora, where customers pay more for honey from bees that have fed on wild herbs. He has used the money to buy a second-hand motorbike, which he uses to carry hives to new places when the flowers near his home are finished.His biggest ambition is to study agriculture at university. 'People think beekeeping is just about honey,' he says, 'but without bees, the farmers here would have far fewer apples.'

  1. (a) How old was Bilal when he was given his first bees? [1]
  2. (b) Who gave Bilal the bees? [1]
  3. (c) Why does Bilal now work slowly with the bees? [1]
  4. (d) What does Bilal feed the bees in winter? [1]
  5. (e) Where does Bilal sell most of his honey? [1]
  6. (f) What does Bilal use his motorbike for? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) twelve / 12
    Accept 12. Do not accept 17.
  2. (b) his grandfather
  3. (c) bees become nervous when people move quickly
    Accept 'so the bees don't get nervous'. Do not accept 'to avoid being stung' alone.
  4. (d) sugar syrup
    Accept 'syrup'. Do not accept 'sugar' alone.
  5. (e) (at the Saturday market) in Mingora
    Accept 'Saturday market'.
  6. (f) to carry / move hives to new places (when nearby flowers are finished)
    Do not accept 'to go to the market'.

The most popular books are adventure stories, followed closely by books about animals.In winter, when temperatures in Quetta can fall well below zero, the bus has a small heater so that children can sit and read inside. Next year, Mrs Baloch hopes to add a second bus that will visit villages outside the city.

  2. (b) What has replaced the seats inside the bus? [1]

  4. (d) How many books can a child borrow at one time? [1]
  5. (e) What kind of book is the most popular? [1]
  6. (f) Where will the second bus go? [1]
Show answer

  2. (b) shelves (holding books)
  3. (c) a local business owner
    Do not accept 'volunteers' or 'Mrs Baloch'.
  4. (d) two / 2
  5. (e) adventure stories
    Do not accept 'books about animals' (second most popular).
  6. (f) to villages outside the city

  4. (d) Why is summer not a good time for stargazing? [1]
  5. (e) How did Usman learn to use the telescopes? [1]
  6. (f) Why are guests asked not to use their phone screens? [1]
Show answer

  2. (b) light from streets and buildings

  3. (c) four in the afternoon / 4 p.m.
  4. (d) dust in the air makes the sky hazy

If a nest is too close to the water, they move the eggs carefully to a fenced area higher up the beach.About seven weeks later, the baby turtles hatch and race towards the waves. The greatest danger they face on the beach is from wild dogs and crows. Further out, plastic bags floating in the sea are a serious threat, because turtles mistake them for jellyfish.Hina, who studies marine biology, first joined the project after a school trip in Year 9. She says the most exciting moment of her life was watching 120 hatchlings reach the sea on a single night.

  4. (d) What do the volunteers use to mark a nest? [1]
  5. (e) What is the greatest danger to baby turtles on the beach? [1]
  6. (f) When did Hina first join the project? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) three
  2. (b) white light confuses the turtles (but they hardly notice red)
  3. (c) its back flippers
    Do not accept 'flippers' if 'front' is added.
  4. (d) a small wooden stick
  5. (e) wild dogs and crows
    Both needed. Do not accept 'plastic bags' (danger in the sea).

Grace says the most difficult part of the business is not making the paper but persuading people that it is just as good as ordinary paper.

  5. (e) Who are Grace's biggest customers today? [1]
  6. (f) According to Grace, what is the most difficult part of the business? [1]
Show answer

  2. (b) on her uncle's farm
    Accept 'looking at old stems on her uncle's farm'. Do not accept 'in her kitchen'.
  3. (c) they are boiled for several hours
  4. (d) pale yellow
  5. (e) hotels

Fewer young people want to spend years learning the skill, and cheaper printed stickers are replacing hand painting on some vehicles.

  6. (f) What now earns the workshop more money than lorries? [1]

  1. (a) jingle as the vehicle moves
  2. (b) his father
  3. (c) up to three weeks

Read the article about an indoor vertical farm in Dubai, and then answer the questions.Farming without fieldsIn a warehouse on the edge of Dubai, where summer temperatures outside reach 45 °C, rows of lettuce, rocket and basil grow in trays stacked twelve levels high. No sunlight reaches them, and there is no soil. This is a vertical farm, and it produces about three tonnes of leafy vegetables every day.Instead of sunlight, the plants receive light from red and blue LED lamps, which give the warehouse a pink glow. Red and blue are the colours plants use most for growth. Their roots hang in water containing exactly the nutrients they need. Sensors monitor temperature, humidity and carbon dioxide twenty-four hours a day, and a computer adjusts them automatically.Supporters point out several advantages. The farm uses about 95 % less water than growing the same crops in fields, which matters in one of the driest countries on earth. No pesticides are needed, because insects cannot get in. And because the farm is close to the city, vegetables reach supermarkets within hours, rather than being flown in from abroad.Critics, however, note that the lamps and air-conditioning use huge amounts of electricity, and unless this comes from renewable sources such as solar power, the environmental benefits are reduced. Vertical farms are also expensive to build, and so far they can grow only certain crops profitably. Wheat, rice and potatoes, for example, need too much space and light.The company plans to cover the warehouse roof with solar panels next year.

  1. (a) Why are red and blue lamps used? [1]
  2. (b) Give THREE things the sensors monitor. [3]
  3. (c) Give THREE advantages of the vertical farm, according to supporters. [3]
  4. (d) Give TWO criticisms of vertical farms. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) red and blue are the colours plants use most for growth
    Do not accept 'to give a pink glow'.
  2. (b) 1. temperature 2. humidity 3. carbon dioxide
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) 1. uses about 95 % less water 2. no pesticides needed (insects cannot get in) 3. vegetables reach supermarkets within hours / not flown from abroad
    1 mark each.
