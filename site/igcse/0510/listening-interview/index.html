Q1Multiple choice (A–C) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Mahnoor Ali, a young woman who runs a bakery in Karachi. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Mahnoor, you opened your bakery at just twenty-two. How did it start?Mahnoor: Honestly, by accident. I was studying accounting, and I baked cakes for friends' birthdays as a hobby. Then a friend posted a photo of one of my cakes online, and suddenly strangers were messaging me with orders. Within six months I was baking more than I was studying!Interviewer: What was the hardest part of starting a business?Mahnoor: People expect me to say money, but my family lent me enough to rent a small kitchen. Finding good staff wasn't easy either, but the real challenge was time. I was getting up at four every morning and finishing at ten at night. I didn't see my friends for months.Interviewer: What's your best-selling product?Mahnoor: Everyone assumes it's the chocolate cake, because that's what made me famous. But actually it's our cardamom biscuits. Office workers buy them by the box to have with their tea.Interviewer: And what's next for you?Mahnoor: I've been asked to open a second shop in Lahore, but I'm not ready for that. Instead, I want to start baking classes for teenagers on Saturday mornings. I think everyone should know how to bake bread.

  1. (a) How did Mahnoor's business begin?A She sold cakes to her classmates.B A friend shared a picture of her cake online.C She studied baking at college. [1]
  2. (b) What was the biggest difficulty when she started?A having enough timeB getting enough moneyC finding staff [1]
  3. (c) What does Mahnoor plan to do next?A open a shop in LahoreB teach baking to teenagersC sell more chocolate cakes [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B A friend shared a picture of her cake online.
    A friend posted a photo online and strangers started ordering. She studied accounting, not baking; she baked for friends' birthdays, not to sell to classmates.
  2. (b) A having enough time
    'The real challenge was time.' Money came from her family; staff was difficult but not the main problem.
  3. (c) B teach baking to teenagers
    She wants to start baking classes for teenagers. She says she is not ready for a second shop.
Q2Multiple choice (A–C) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Asad Mir, a wildlife photographer who photographs snow leopards in northern Pakistan. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Asad, snow leopards are famously hard to see. How long did it take you to photograph one?Asad: Three winters! I spent weeks in the mountains of Chitral and saw footprints and droppings, but never the animal. The first time I actually saw one, it was across a valley, too far away for a good photo.Interviewer: So how did you finally get your pictures?Asad: Local herders helped me most. They know exactly which paths the leopards use. I also set up camera traps – cameras that take a photo automatically when an animal walks past. Most of my best pictures come from those, not from me holding the camera.Interviewer: Aren't the herders angry with snow leopards for killing their goats?Asad: They used to be. But now there's an insurance scheme: if a leopard kills an animal, the family receives money. So herders are much less likely to harm the leopards.Interviewer: What do you most want people to feel when they see your photos?Asad: Not just that the animals are beautiful. I want them to realise that leopards and people can share the mountains.

  1. (a) What happened the first time Asad saw a snow leopard?A It was too far away to photograph well.B It ran away when it heard him.C He took his best photograph. [1]
  2. (b) Where do most of Asad's best photos come from?A photos he took himselfB photos taken by herdersC camera traps [1]
  3. (c) Why are herders less likely to harm snow leopards now?A They keep their goats indoors.B They earn money from tourists.C They are paid if a leopard kills an animal. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A It was too far away to photograph well.
    It was across a valley, too far for a good photo.
  2. (b) C camera traps
    'Most of my best pictures come from those [camera traps], not from me holding the camera.' Herders show paths but don't take the photos.
  3. (c) C They are paid if a leopard kills an animal.
    The insurance scheme gives families money.
Q3Multiple choice (A–C) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Danish, a sixteen-year-old who created a mobile phone app. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Danish, tell us about your app.Danish: It's called 'Bus Buddy'. It shows people in my city where the minibuses are and when the next one will arrive. My grandmother used to wait at the bus stop for ages in the heat, and I wanted to help her.Interviewer: How did you learn to make an app?Danish: My school doesn't teach programming, and I couldn't afford a course. So I watched free videos online every evening for about a year. My older sister helped me with the design, because she's much better at art than I am.Interviewer: How many people use it?Danish: About eight thousand now. I expected maybe a hundred – mostly my family!Interviewer: What have you learned from the experience?Danish: That things always go wrong! The first version crashed every time it rained, because the signal was weaker. But the users sent me really helpful messages, and that's how I fixed it.

  1. (a) Why did Danish create the app?A for a school competitionB to earn moneyC to help his grandmother [1]
  2. (b) How did Danish learn programming?A from free online videosB at schoolC on a paid course [1]
  3. (c) How did Danish solve the problem with the first version?A His sister redesigned it.B Users sent him helpful messages.C He bought a better phone. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C to help his grandmother
    His grandmother waited for ages in the heat and he wanted to help her.
  2. (b) A from free online videos
    His school doesn't teach it and he couldn't afford a course.
  3. (c) B Users sent him helpful messages.
    'The users sent me really helpful messages, and that's how I fixed it.' The sister helped with the design, not the fault.
Q4Multiple choice (A–C) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Shaista, one of the first women to drive a rickshaw in her city. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Shaista, why did you decide to drive a rickshaw?Shaista: After my husband became ill, I needed to support my three children. I tried sewing at home, but it didn't pay enough. My brother is a rickshaw driver, and he taught me to drive his on Sunday mornings when the roads were empty.Interviewer: How did people react?Shaista: Some men laughed at first and said I would crash. But most of my passengers are women and girls, and they're delighted. Many parents prefer me to take their daughters to school because they feel it's safer.Interviewer: What's the most difficult thing about the job?Shaista: The heat in summer is tiring, and the traffic is terrible, but the worst thing is finding a place to rest. There are very few places in the city where a woman driver can stop, have a cup of tea and use a clean bathroom.Interviewer: What would you say to other women?Shaista: If you need to work, don't let anyone tell you that a job is only for men.

  1. (a) Who taught Shaista to drive a rickshaw?A her husbandB a driving schoolC her brother [1]
  2. (b) Why do many parents choose Shaista to drive their daughters?A They feel it is safer.B She charges less.C She drives very fast. [1]
  3. (c) What does Shaista find most difficult?A the heavy trafficB the summer heatC finding somewhere to rest [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C her brother
    Her brother, a rickshaw driver, taught her on Sunday mornings.
  2. (b) A They feel it is safer.
    Parents feel it is safer.
  3. (c) C finding somewhere to rest
    'The worst thing is finding a place to rest.' Heat and traffic are mentioned as lesser problems.
Q5Multiple choice (A–C) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Zeeshan, who works at a bird sanctuary on a lake. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Zeeshan, what does your job involve?Zeeshan: I count the birds that visit the lake, especially the ones that fly here from Siberia every winter. I also repair the paths and hides where visitors watch the birds, and I give talks to school groups.Interviewer: Which part of the job do you enjoy most?Zeeshan: Counting birds at sunrise is magical, but I'd have to say the school visits. When a child sees a flamingo through binoculars for the first time, their face lights up.Interviewer: Have the numbers of birds changed?Zeeshan: Some species are coming in smaller numbers, mainly because hunters still shoot them on their journey, even though it's illegal. But ducks have increased since the lake was cleaned up two years ago.Interviewer: What's the best time to visit?Zeeshan: Between December and February, early in the morning. Bring warm clothes and, if you can, binoculars. Please don't bring bread to feed the birds – it isn't good for them.

  1. (a) Which part of his job does Zeeshan enjoy most?A counting birdsB talking to schoolchildrenC repairing paths [1]
  2. (b) Why are some species coming in smaller numbers?A The lake is polluted.B They are shot by hunters.C There is not enough food. [1]
  3. (c) What does Zeeshan ask visitors NOT to do?A feed the birds breadB come early in the morningC bring binoculars [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B talking to schoolchildren
    He says counting at sunrise is magical, 'but I'd have to say the school visits'.
  2. (b) B They are shot by hunters.
    Hunters still shoot them. The lake has been cleaned up.
  3. (c) A feed the birds bread
    Bread isn't good for the birds.

She sold vegetables at the market to buy my first pair of running shoes.Interviewer: What's your next goal?Grace: Everyone asks about the Olympics, and of course I'd love to go.   1. (a) What was Grace's aim in the marathon?A to win the raceB to finish within a certain timeC to lead at halfway [1]
  2. (b) Who does Grace say has helped her most?A her motherB her training groupC her coach [1]
  3. (c) What does Grace want to do first?A compete in the OlympicsB open a training camp for girlsC run another marathon [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B to finish within a certain time
    Her aim was to finish in under 2 hours 30 minutes.
  2. (b) A her mother
    'But really it's my mother.'
  3. (c) B open a training camp for girls
    'First I want to open a training camp for girls in my village.'
Q7Multiple choice (A–C) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Mrs Rubina Javed, a school librarian who started a reading project. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Mrs Javed, what is the 'Reading Buddies' project?Mrs Javed: Older students, from Class 9, spend twenty minutes twice a week reading with younger children from Class 3. They choose books together and talk about the stories.Interviewer: Why did you start it?Mrs Javed: I noticed that many younger children could read the words but didn't understand the stories. Teachers didn't have time to read with every child individually, so I thought the older students could help.Interviewer: Has it worked?Mrs Javed: Yes! Reading scores in Class 3 have gone up. But what surprised me most was the effect on the older students. Several of them told me they had stopped reading for fun, and this project made them start again.Interviewer: Were there any problems?Mrs Javed: At first we didn't have enough books at the right level, so I asked parents to donate books, and they gave us over five hundred.

  1. (a) Why did Mrs Javed start the project?A Older students wanted to become teachers.B The library had too many books.C Younger children did not understand what they read. [1]
  2. (b) What surprised Mrs Javed most?A Parents gave so many books.B Older students began reading for pleasure again.C Reading scores went up. [1]
  3. (c) How did she solve the problem with books?A Students wrote their own books.B She bought new books.C Parents donated books. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C Younger children did not understand what they read.
    Children could read the words but didn't understand the stories.
  2. (b) B Older students began reading for pleasure again.
    'What surprised me most was the effect on the older students.'
  3. (c) C Parents donated books.
    Parents donated over five hundred books.

They watch cartoons on phones all day, but they've never seen a puppet dance right in front of them. They shout and laugh. Adults enjoy it too, because it reminds them of their childhood.Interviewer: What are your plans?Kashif: I'm writing new stories about modern topics, like saving water, so that schools will invite us more often.

  1. (a) How did Kashif become a puppeteer?A His wife taught him.B He studied it at school.C He learned from his grandfather. [1]
  2. (b) What does Kashif find hardest when making a puppet?A attaching the stringsB sewing the clothesC carving the head [1]
  3. (c) Why is Kashif writing new stories?A Children are bored of old stories.B He wants schools to invite him more.C His grandfather asked him to. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C He learned from his grandfather.
    His grandfather performed and let him work his first puppet.
  2. (b) A attaching the strings
    'The hardest part is attaching the strings.'
  3. (c) B He wants schools to invite him more.
    New stories about modern topics 'so that schools will invite us more often'.

  2. (b) B road accidents
    'Most calls are actually road accidents.'
  3. (c) A teamwork
    'The most important thing is teamwork.'
Q10Multiple choice (A–C) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Nida Hassan, who presents the weather forecast on television. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Nida, did you always want to work on television?Nida: Not at all! I studied physics and then meteorology, the science of weather. I was working at the weather office, preparing forecasts, when the TV station asked for someone to present them. I was terrified at first.Interviewer: How is a forecast prepared?Nida: We use information from satellites, weather balloons and stations all over the country. Computers turn this into predictions, but forecasters still check them, because the computer can make mistakes, especially in the mountains.Interviewer: What's the hardest weather to predict?Nida: Thunderstorms. They can form very quickly over a small area, so one town gets heavy rain and the next stays dry.Interviewer: Do people complain when you're wrong?Nida: Of course! But the forecast for the next day is right about nine times out of ten now – much better than when I started.

  1. (a) What was Nida doing before she worked on television?A preparing forecasts at the weather officeB teaching physicsC studying journalism [1]
  2. (b) Why do forecasters check the computer's predictions?A Viewers ask them to.B Satellites sometimes stop working.C The computer can make mistakes. [1]
  3. (c) Why are thunderstorms hard to predict?A They happen mostly at night.B They form quickly over small areas.C They only happen in the mountains. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A preparing forecasts at the weather office
    She was working at the weather office, preparing forecasts.
  2. (b) C The computer can make mistakes.
    'The computer can make mistakes, especially in the mountains.'
  3. (c) B They form quickly over small areas.
    They form quickly over a small area.
Q11Multiple choice (A–C) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

You will hear an interview with Ustad Ghulam Hussain, a craftsman who makes glazed tiles in Sindh. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Ustad sahib, your family has made tiles for generations. When did you start?Ghulam Hussain: I was eight. In those days children learned by watching. My father never explained anything; he simply expected me to copy him. I made many mistakes, and he made me start again each time.Interviewer: What makes your tiles special?Ghulam Hussain: The blue colour. We make it from minerals, using a recipe that my family has kept secret for more than a century. Many people think it's the patterns that matter most, but anyone can copy a pattern. The colour is what customers recognise.Interviewer: Is the craft in danger?Ghulam Hussain: Factory-made tiles are cheaper, so fewer people buy handmade ones for their homes. Our main customers now are architects restoring old buildings, and some hotels. The real danger is that young people don't want to learn, because it takes years before you earn a good living.Interviewer: Are you teaching anyone?Ghulam Hussain: Two of my grandsons, and – for the first time in our family – my granddaughter. She is the most patient of all.

  1. (a) How did Ghulam Hussain learn his craft?A He attended a training school.B His father explained each step carefully.C He copied his father and started again whenever he made a mistake. [1]
  2. (b) According to Ghulam Hussain, what makes his tiles special?A the patternsB the colourC the low price [1]
  3. (c) What does he see as the greatest danger to the craft?A cheaper factory tilesB a lack of customers among architectsC young people not wanting to learn [1]
  4. (d) What does he say about his granddaughter?A She has started her own business.B She designs new patterns.C She is the most patient learner. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C He copied his father and started again whenever he made a mistake.
    His father never explained; he expected him to copy and made him start again after mistakes.
  2. (b) B the colour
    'The colour is what customers recognise.' He says anyone can copy a pattern.
  3. (c) C young people not wanting to learn
    'The real danger is that young people don't want to learn.' Factory tiles are a problem but not called the real danger.
  4. (d) C She is the most patient learner.
    'She is the most patient of all.'
Q12Multiple choice (A–C) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Dr Sameer Khan, the director of a planetarium. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Dr Khan, what exactly is a planetarium?Dr Khan: It's a theatre with a dome-shaped ceiling. We project images of the night sky onto the dome, so visitors feel as if they're lying under the stars. We can show the sky from any place on earth, and even from the past or future.Interviewer: Who are your visitors?Dr Khan: Mainly school groups during the week. At weekends it's families. What's interesting is that adults often ask more questions than the children!Interviewer: Has technology changed planetariums?Dr Khan: Enormously. When I started, we used a huge mechanical projector with hundreds of tiny lenses. Now it's all digital, so we can fly visitors to Mars or inside a storm on Jupiter. Some people say the old shows were more beautiful, and I partly agree, but the digital shows are far more flexible.Interviewer: What's your most popular show?Dr Khan: We expected the show about black holes to be the favourite. In fact, it's one about how ancient travellers used the stars to find their way across deserts and oceans.

  1. (a) What does Dr Khan find interesting about visitors?A Families prefer weekday visits.B Adults often ask more questions than children.C Children ask the most questions. [1]
  2. (b) What is Dr Khan's opinion of digital shows?A They are more flexible than the old shows.B They are less popular than the old shows.C They are more beautiful than the old shows. [1]
  3. (c) Which is the most popular show?A a trip to MarsB a show about navigating by the starsC a show about black holes [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Adults often ask more questions than children.
    'Adults often ask more questions than the children!'
  2. (b) A They are more flexible than the old shows.
    He partly agrees the old shows were more beautiful but says digital shows are 'far more flexible'.
  3. (c) B a show about navigating by the stars
    The show about how ancient travellers used the stars. Black holes were expected to be the favourite.
Q13Multiple choice (A–C) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Tomás Ruiz, a cook on a research ship in the Southern Ocean. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Tomás, what's it like cooking on a ship?Tomás: Challenging! When the waves are big, the whole kitchen moves. Every pot has to be fixed to the cooker with metal rails, and I never fill a pan more than half full.Interviewer: How do you plan the food for a long trip?Tomás: We're at sea for up to two months, with forty scientists and crew. I order everything before we leave. Fresh vegetables only last about three weeks, so after that it's frozen and tinned food. The thing people miss most isn't fruit, surprisingly – it's fresh milk.Interviewer: Is food important for the people on board?Tomás: Absolutely. The work is hard and the weather is grey for weeks. A good meal is the highlight of the day. On Sundays I cook something special, and on birthdays I always bake a cake, even in a storm.Interviewer: Do you ever get seasick?Tomás: I did on my first voyage. Now, after twelve years, never.

We had the right plan, but we explained it badly, and that created unnecessary opposition.

  1. (a) What surprised Maryam about the complaints?A Drivers complained more than expected.B Nobody complained at all.C Shopkeepers objected most strongly. [1]
  2. (b) What happened to shops on streets with cycle lanes?A Customers could not park.B Most had more customers.C Many closed down. [1]
  3. (c) What does Maryam regret?A choosing the wrong streetsB not explaining the plan well to residentsC building the cycle lanes too quickly [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C Shopkeepers objected most strongly.
    The strongest objections came from shopkeepers.
  2. (b) B Most had more customers.
    Most shops had more customers after a year.
  3. (c) B not explaining the plan well to residents
    'We had the right plan, but we explained it badly.'

Q30Multiple choice (A–C) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

You will hear an interview with Engineer Zara Ahmed, who works on the undersea cables that carry the internet. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Interviewer: Zara, most people think the internet travels through satellites. Is that right?Zara: It's a common belief, but almost all international internet traffic actually travels through cables on the sea floor. Satellites are useful for remote places, but cables carry far more data, far more cheaply.Interviewer: How big are these cables?Zara: In deep water, surprisingly small – about the width of a garden hose. Near the coast, where they can be damaged by ships' anchors or fishing nets, they're thicker and protected with steel wire, and often buried under the sea bed.Interviewer: What's the most common cause of damage?Zara: People sometimes joke about sharks biting cables, and it has happened, but it's very rare. Most damage is caused by fishing and anchors. Earthquakes under the sea can also break several cables at once.Interviewer: How are cables repaired?Zara: A special ship lifts the broken ends to the surface with a hook. Engineers join the fibres on board, which can take days, and then lower the cable back down.

  1. (a) According to Zara, how does most international internet traffic travel?A by radio towersB through cables on the sea floorC by satellite [1]
  2. (b) Why are cables thicker near the coast?A They are more likely to be damaged there.B The water is colder there.C They carry more data there. [1]
  3. (c) What is the most common cause of damage to cables?A sharksB fishing and anchorsC earthquakes [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B through cables on the sea floor
    Almost all international traffic travels through sea-floor cables.
  2. (b) A They are more likely to be damaged there.
    Near the coast they can be damaged by anchors or fishing nets.
  3. (c) B fishing and anchors
    'Most damage is caused by fishing and anchors.' Sharks are very rare; earthquakes are possible but less common.
