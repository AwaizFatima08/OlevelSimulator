Pakistan has large mangrove forests in the Indus delta, south of Karachi.Mangroves are remarkable because most plants die in salt water. Mangroves survive partly by getting rid of salt through their leaves, and their roots are specially adapted. Some species have roots that stick up out of the mud like pencils. Young fish hide among the roots until they are big enough to survive in the open sea, so local fishing families depend on healthy mangroves.Thirdly, and this is something many people don't realise, mangroves store a huge amount of carbon, much of it in the mud beneath them rather than in the trees themselves.In the past, mangroves were cut down for firewood and to make space for shrimp farms. But the good news is that replanting projects in Sindh have been very successful, and the area of mangrove forest there has grown in recent years.

If you notice these, see a doctor.My advice is simple: choose noise-cancelling headphones, which reduce background noise so you don't need to turn the music up, and carry foam earplugs if you go to concerts or weddings with loud music. They're cheap and they really work.

You will hear a talk by a psychologist about how memory works and how students can revise more effectively. Complete the notes. Write no more than three words or a number in each gap.Transcript (read it aloud, or read it silently)Most students revise in the same way: they read their notes again and again and highlight the important parts in bright colours. Unfortunately, research suggests that this is one of the least effective ways to learn. It makes material feel familiar, which tricks us into thinking we know it, but familiarity is not the same as being able to recall it in an exam.So what works better?
