Q1Partnerships · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State two advantages of forming a partnership rather than continuing as a sole trader. [2]
  2. (b) State two disadvantages of forming a partnership rather than continuing as a sole trader. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two, e.g. more capital is available; partners bring different skills and ideas; work and responsibility are shared; losses are shared; cover during illness or holidays.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. profits have to be shared; decisions take longer and partners may disagree; each partner is liable for the debts of the firm (unlimited liability); a partner is bound by the actions of the others.
    1 mark each.
Q2Partnerships · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State four items that are usually included in a partnership agreement. [4]
  2. (b) Explain the purpose of a partnership appropriation account. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any four: the ratio for sharing profits and losses; capital to be contributed by each partner; interest on capital (rate); interest on drawings (rate); partners' salaries; limits on drawings; procedure for admitting or retiring partners; how disputes are settled.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) It shows how the profit for the year is shared between the partners: interest on capital, salaries, interest on drawings and the share of the residual profit.
    1 mark: shows how profit is divided/appropriated. 1 mark: names at least two items.
Q3Partnerships · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Aziz and Bano share profits and losses in the ratio 3:2. Their profit for the year was $48,000. Interest on capital: Aziz $3,000, Bano $2,000. Bano receives a salary of $10,000. Interest on drawings: Aziz $500, Bano $300. What is Aziz's share of the residual profit?A $20,280B $19,800C $28,800D $13,520 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A $20,280
    Residual profit = 48,000 + interest on drawings 800 − interest on capital 5,000 − salary 10,000 = 33800. Aziz's share = 3/5 × 33800 = 20280. B forgets to add interest on drawings; D is Bano's share.
Q4Partnerships · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Hina and Omar are partners in a travel agency in Peshawar. Their partnership agreement provides for:• interest on capital at 5% per annum (capitals: Hina $80,000, Omar $50,000)• a salary for Omar of $12,000 per year• interest on drawings: Hina $640, Omar $410• residual profits and losses shared equallyThe profit for the year ended 31 December was $64,500.

  1. (a) Calculate the interest on capital for each partner. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the residual profit and each partner's share of it. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hina $4,000; Omar $2,500
    80,000 × 5% = 4,000 (1); 50,000 × 5% = 2,500 (1).
  2. (b) Residual profit $47,050; Hina $23,525; Omar $23,525
    64,500 + 640 + 410 (1) − 4,000 − 2,500 − 12,000 (1) = 47050 (1). Each ½ = 23525 (1).
Q5Partnerships · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Lerato and Sipho are partners. On 1 January Lerato's current account had a credit balance of $3,200 and Sipho's had a debit balance of $850. For the year ended 31 December:• interest on capital: Lerato $2,700, Sipho $1,800• salary: Sipho $9,000• share of residual profit: Lerato $15,600, Sipho $10,400• drawings: Lerato $24,000, Sipho $18,500• interest on drawings: Lerato $720, Sipho $480

  1. (a) Calculate the balance on each partner's current account on 31 December. State whether each is a debit or a credit balance. [6]
  2. (b) Explain what a debit balance on a partner's current account shows. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lerato $3,220 debit; Sipho $1,370 credit
    Lerato: 3,200 + 2,700 + 15,600 − 24,000 − 720 = -3220, i.e. debit 3220 (3). Sipho: −850 + 1,800 + 9,000 + 10,400 − 18,500 − 480 = 1370, credit (3). Method 2 each, answer with side 1 each.
  2. (b) The partner has withdrawn more (drawings and interest on drawings) than the total of his or her share of profit, interest and salary, so the partner owes this amount to the business.
    1 mark: drawings exceed amounts credited. 1 mark: partner owes the firm/reduces the partner's equity.
Q6Partnerships · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Amina and Bashir share residual profits and losses in the ratio 2:1. The partnership agreement provides for interest on drawings at 10% per annum and a salary for Amina of $7,500 per year. No interest is allowed on capital. The financial year ends on 31 December. Amina withdrew $6,000 on 1 April and Bashir withdrew $8,000 on 1 October; there were no other drawings. The profit for the year was $39,100.

  1. (a) Calculate the interest on drawings for each partner. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate each partner's share of the residual profit. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why a partnership agreement may charge interest on drawings. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Amina $450; Bashir $200
    Amina: 6,000 × 10% × 9/12 = 450 (1). Bashir: 8,000 × 10% × 3/12 = 200 (1). Charging a full year loses the mark.
  2. (b) Amina $21,500; Bashir $10,750
    Residual = 39,100 + 450 + 200 − 7,500 = 32250 (2). Amina 2/3 = 21500, Bashir 1/3 = 10750 (1).
  3. (c) To discourage partners from taking drawings early or in large amounts, which would reduce the cash available to the business.
    1 mark.
Q7Incomplete records · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A trader does not keep a full set of records. Her capital was $42,000 at the start of the year and $51,500 at the end. During the year her drawings were $12,000 and she introduced additional capital of $3,000. What was her profit for the year?A $18,500B $24,500C $9,500D $500 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A $18,500
    Profit = closing capital + drawings − capital introduced − opening capital = 51,500 + 12,000 − 3,000 − 42,000 = 18500. B adds the capital introduced instead of deducting it.
Q8Incomplete records · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by a statement of affairs. [1]
  2. (b) Guo Wei runs a phone repair stall. On 1 July his business had: equipment $9,400; inventory of spare parts $3,750; amounts owed by customers $2,180; cash $640; amounts owed to suppliers $1,960; loan from a relative $5,000; rent owing $300. Calculate his capital on 1 July. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A statement listing the assets and liabilities of a business on a particular date, used to calculate the owner's capital when full records are not kept.
    1 mark.
  2. (b) $8,710
    Assets = 15970 (1). Liabilities = 7260 (1). Capital = 15970 − 7260 = 8710 (1).

Q10Incomplete records · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Carlos sells sports goods and always earns a gross margin of 30% on revenue. For the year his revenue was $90,000, opening inventory was $7,200 and purchases were $61,500. He did not count his closing inventory.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of sales for the year. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the closing inventory. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the mark-up percentage that is equal to a gross margin of 30%. Give your answer correct to two decimal places. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $63,000
    Gross profit = 30% × 90,000 = 27,000; cost of sales = 90,000 − 27,000 = 63000.
  2. (b) $5,700
    7,200 + 61,500 − 63000 = 5700.
  3. (c) 42.86%
    Mark-up = gross profit ÷ cost of sales = 30 ÷ 70 × 100 = 42.86%.
Q11Incomplete records · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

On 20 April a fire destroyed most of the inventory of Mumtaz Crockery. The following information is available for the period from 1 January to 20 April:Inventory at cost on 1 January $14,800Purchases $39,600Revenue $52,000Mumtaz always uses a mark-up of 25% on cost. Undamaged inventory with a cost of $2,300 was saved.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of sales for the period. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the cost of the inventory destroyed by the fire. [3]
  3. (c) State the gross margin percentage that is equal to a mark-up of 25%. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $41,600
    52,000 × 100/125 = 41600.
  2. (b) $10,500
    Inventory that should be held = 14,800 + 39,600 − 41600 = 12800 (2). Destroyed = 12800 − 2,300 = 10500 (1).
  3. (c) 20%
    25 ÷ 125 × 100 = 20%.
Q12Incomplete records · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Daniyal owns a hardware shop but does not keep full records. On 1 January his business assets were $58,400 and liabilities $12,900. On 31 December assets were $71,250 and liabilities $15,600. During the year he took $1,200 cash each month for personal use and also took goods costing $950. In June he paid a legacy of $6,000 that he had received into the business bank account.

  1. (a) Calculate Daniyal's capital on 1 January and on 31 December. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for the year. [4]
  3. (c) State one disadvantage of calculating profit in this way rather than preparing an income statement. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 January $45,500; 31 December $55,650
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) $19,500
    Drawings = 1,200 × 12 + 950 = 15,350 (1). Profit = 55650 + 15,350 (1) − 6,000 (1) − 45500 = 19500 (1).
  3. (c) e.g. it shows only the total profit, not the revenue, gross profit or individual expenses, so the owner cannot see where costs can be controlled or calculate margins.
    1 mark.

Q14Manufacturing accounts · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by work in progress. [1]
  2. (b) State how opening and closing work in progress are used to calculate the cost of production. [2]
  3. (c) State two reasons why a manufacturing business prepares a manufacturing account. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Goods that have been partly manufactured at the end of the period (started but not yet finished).
    1 mark.
  2. (b) Opening work in progress is added to (and closing work in progress deducted from) the total of prime cost and factory overheads.
    1 mark opening added, 1 mark closing deducted.
  3. (c) Any two: to calculate the cost of production of goods completed; to find the cost per unit; to control and compare factory costs; to help set selling prices; to decide whether to make or buy.
    1 mark each.

Q17Manufacturing accounts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

For the year ended 31 December:Rent and rates $18,000 (three-quarters factory, one-quarter office)Insurance $4,800 (two-thirds factory, one-third office)Electricity $7,500 (60% factory, 40% office)Indirect factory wages $11,200Factory machinery cost $60,000; accumulated depreciation on 1 January $21,600. Q18Manufacturing accounts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Rahul Plastics produced 12,000 buckets in the year at a cost of production of $128,400. Inventory of finished goods was $9,600 at the start of the year and $11,200 at the end. Revenue for the year was $165,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of sales. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the gross profit. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the cost of production per bucket. [1]
  4. (d) Calculate the gross margin. Give your answer correct to two decimal places. [2]
  5. (e) Explain why the closing inventory of finished goods is valued at cost and not at selling price. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $126,800
    9,600 + 128,400 − 11,200 = 126800.
  2. (b) $38,200
    165,000 − 126800 = 38200.
  3. (c) $10.70
    128,400 ÷ 12,000 = 10.70.
  4. (d) 23.15%
    38200 ÷ 165,000 × 100 = 23.15%.
  5. (e) Valuing at selling price would include a profit that has not yet been earned, because the goods have not been sold (realisation/prudence). Inventory is valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value.
    1 mark: profit not yet realised/earned. 1 mark: prudence or lower of cost and NRV.
Q19Limited companies · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by limited liability for the shareholders of a company?A the company can only borrow a limited amount of moneyB shareholders can lose only the amount they have invested in their sharesC shareholders receive a fixed dividend every yearD the company has a limited number of shareholders [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B shareholders can lose only the amount they have invested in their shares
    With limited liability, shareholders' personal possessions cannot be used to pay the company's debts; the most they can lose is the amount paid for their shares. C describes a feature that ordinary shares do not have: ordinary dividends vary.
Q20Limited companies · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain two differences between ordinary shares and debentures. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ordinary shareholders are owners of the company, while debenture holders are lenders (creditors). Ordinary shareholders receive dividends that vary with profit and are paid only if declared; debenture holders receive a fixed rate of interest that must be paid whether or not there is a profit. (Also: shareholders usually have voting rights; debentures are repayable on a fixed date.)
    Two differences × 2 marks (1 for each side of the comparison).
Q21Limited companies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

On 1 January the equity of Indus Foods Ltd was: ordinary shares of $1 each $200,000; general reserve $30,000; retained earnings $47,500. During the year ended 31 December:• 50,000 further ordinary shares of $1 each were issued at par for cash• the profit for the year was $61,200• $15,000 was transferred to the general reserve• ordinary dividends of $24,000 were paid

  1. (a) Calculate the balance on each of the following on 31 December: (i) ordinary share capital, (ii) general reserve, (iii) retained earnings. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the total equity on 31 December. [1]
  3. (c) State the name of the financial statement in which these movements are shown. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) $250,000 (ii) $45,000 (iii) $69,700
    (i) 200,000 + 50,000 (1). (ii) 30,000 + 15,000 (1). (iii) 47,500 + 61,200 − 15,000 − 24,000 = 69700 (2).
  2. (b) $364,700
    250,000 + 45,000 + 69700 = 364700.
  3. (c) Statement of changes in equity
    1 mark.
Q22Limited companies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Kestrel Engineering Ltd has 6% debentures of $120,000, repayable in 2034. For the year ended 31 December the company's profit from operations was $84,600. Only $3,600 of debenture interest had been paid and recorded during the year. Retained earnings on 1 January were $52,300. During the year ordinary dividends of $30,000 were paid and $10,000 was transferred to the general reserve.

  1. (a) Calculate the debenture interest to be charged in the income statement. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for the year. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the retained earnings on 31 December. [3]
  4. (d) State where each of the following appears in the statement of financial position: (i) the debentures, (ii) the unpaid debenture interest. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $7,200
    120,000 × 6% = 7,200 for the full year.
  2. (b) $77,400
    84,600 − 7200 = 77400. (Deducting only the 3,600 paid loses the A mark.)
  3. (c) $89,700
    52,300 + 77400 (1) − 30,000 (1) − 10,000 (1) = 89700.
  4. (d) (i) non-current liabilities (ii) current liabilities (other payables/accruals) of $3,600
    1 mark each.
Q23Limited companies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Mariam owns a successful bakery as a sole trader. She plans to form a limited company and issue 60,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each at par. Calculate the amount the company would receive if all the shares were issued and fully paid. [1]
  2. (b) Advise Mariam whether she should convert her business into a limited company. Justify your answer by giving two advantages and two disadvantages. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) $30,000
    60,000 × 0.50 = 30,000.
  2. (b) Advantages: limited liability protects her personal assets; more capital can be raised by selling shares; the company continues even if a shareholder dies or leaves. Disadvantages: she must share profits (dividends) and control with other shareholders; more legal formalities and costs; financial statements may have to be published, so there is less privacy. Recommendation, e.g. convert if she needs large amounts of capital for expansion; otherwise remain a sole trader.
    Up to 2 marks advantages, up to 2 marks disadvantages, 1 mark for a justified recommendation (only if both sides are given).
Q24Clubs and societies · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) In the financial statements of a club, the accumulated fund is equivalent to which item in the financial statements of a sole trader?A capitalB profit for the yearC drawingsD bank balance [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A capital
    The accumulated fund is the members' interest in the club: assets less liabilities, the same as a sole trader's capital. The surplus for the year (not the fund itself) corresponds to profit.
Q25Clubs and societies · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A tennis club received subscriptions of $8,400 during the year. This included $200 owing from the previous year and $600 paid in advance for next year. At the start of the year $300 had been paid in advance for this year. At the end of the year $450 was owing for this year. What is the subscriptions income for the year?A $8,350B $8,450C $8,250D $9,150 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A $8,350
    8,400 − 200 (last year's arrears) − 600 (next year) + 300 (paid last year for this year) + 450 (owing) = 8350. C ignores the opening balances.
Q26Clubs and societies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State two differences between a receipts and payments account and an income and expenditure account. [2]
  2. (b) The Gulberg Chess Club had the following subscription balances: 1 January: in arrears $240, in advance $180. 31 December: in arrears $510. During the year $9,850 was received, which included the $240 arrears from last year and $360 for next year. Calculate the subscriptions income for the year. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: a receipts and payments account is a summary of the cash book, while an income and expenditure account is like an income statement; the receipts and payments account includes capital items, the income and expenditure account includes only revenue items; the income and expenditure account includes accruals, prepayments and depreciation, the receipts and payments account does not; the receipts and payments account ends with a cash/bank balance, the income and expenditure account with a surplus or deficit.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) $9,940
    9,850 − 240 − 360 + 180 + 510 = 9940. 1 mark for removing the two amounts not for this year, 1 for adding 180 and 510, 1 for the answer.
Q27Clubs and societies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

The Riverside Hiking Society runs a small café for members. For the year ended 31 December:Café takings $14,600; café purchases $8,900; café inventory 1 January $650, 31 December $820; café wages $3,100Subscriptions income $12,400Competition entry fees $1,350; competition prizes $980Rent of clubhouse $6,000Electricity paid $1,780; $160 was owing at 31 December (nothing owing at 1 January)Depreciation of equipment $1,500

  1. (a) Calculate the profit made by the café. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the surplus or deficit for the year. [4]
  3. (c) State the effect of the surplus on the accumulated fund. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,770
    Cost of sales = 650 + 8,900 − 820 = 8730 (1). Profit = 14,600 − 8730 − 3,100 (1) = 2770 (1).
  2. (b) Surplus of $6,100
    Income: 12,400 + café 2770 + net competition 370 = 15540 (2). Expenditure: 6,000 + electricity 1940 + 1,500 = 9440 (1). Surplus 6100 (1).
  3. (c) It increases the accumulated fund.
    1 mark.
Q28Sole traders · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A trader provides this information: opening inventory $7,300; purchases $52,600; carriage inwards $900; purchases returns $1,400; closing inventory $8,100. What is the cost of sales?A $51,300B $50,400C $54,100D $49,500 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A $51,300
    7,300 + 52,600 + 900 − 1,400 − 8,100 = 51300. B leaves out carriage inwards, which is part of the cost of purchases; C adds the returns instead of deducting them.
Q29Sole traders · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Jaya owns a gift shop. For the year ended 31 December: revenue $96,500; sales returns $1,500; inventory 1 January $8,000; purchases $61,000; inventory 31 December $9,500; wages paid $14,200; rent paid $7,200; discount received $350; equipment at cost $24,000.Additional information:• wages of $600 were owing at 31 December• rent of $600 had been paid in advance at 31 December• equipment is depreciated at 10% per annum on cost (straight-line method)

  1. (a) Calculate the gross profit. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for the year. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $35,500
    Net revenue 96,500 − 1,500 = 95,000 (1). Cost of sales 8,000 + 61,000 − 9,500 = 59500 (1). Gross profit 35500 (1).
  2. (b) $12,050
    35500 + 350 (1) − wages 14,800 (1) − rent 6,600 (1) − depreciation 2,400 = 12050 (1).
Q30Sole traders · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

The following information relates to Ewan's shop on 31 December, after the income statement had been prepared:Premises $90,000Fixtures at cost $18,000; accumulated depreciation $7,200Inventory $11,300Trade receivables $8,400; provision for doubtful debts $420Insurance prepaid $250Bank $3,170Trade payables $6,950Wages accrued $380Loan repayable in 2031 $25,000Capital 1 January $83,000; profit for the year $17,670; drawings $9,500

  1. (a) Calculate the total of the non-current assets. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the total of the current assets. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the working capital. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate Ewan's capital on 31 December and show that it equals the net assets of the business. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $100,800
    90,000 + (18,000 − 7,200).
  2. (b) $22,700
    11,300 + 7,980 + 250 + 3,170 = 22700 (1 for net receivables, 1 for total).
  3. (c) $15,370
    Current liabilities = 6,950 + 380 = 7330 (1). 22700 − 7330 = 15370 (1). The loan is non-current and is not included.
  4. (d) Capital $91,170; net assets $91,170
    83,000 + 17,670 − 9,500 = 91170 (1). Net assets = 100800 + 22700 − 7330 − 25,000 (1) = 91170 (1).
