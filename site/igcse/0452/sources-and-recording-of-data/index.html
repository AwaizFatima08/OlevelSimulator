Q1Cash book and petty cash book · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Zeeshan keeps a three-column cash book. A customer pays a debt by cheque and is allowed a cash discount. Where is the discount recorded in the cash book?A credit side, discount columnB debit side, discount columnC debit side, bank columnD credit side, bank column [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B debit side, discount column
    Discount allowed is recorded on the debit side (the same side as the receipt) in the discount column; the total is later debited to the discount allowed account. Credit side discount column is for discount received, so A is wrong.
Q2Cash book and petty cash book · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by an imprest system of petty cash. [2]
  2. (b) State two advantages to a business of using an imprest system. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A fixed float is given to the petty cashier; at the end of each period the amount spent is reimbursed so the float returns to the same fixed amount.
    B1 fixed amount (float) at the start of each period. B1 restored by the exact amount spent.
  2. (b) Any two: limits the cash held by the petty cashier; spending can be checked easily (cash + vouchers = imprest); small payments are kept out of the main cash book; frees the main cashier's time; trains junior staff.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q3Cash book and petty cash book · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Haroon sold goods on credit to Rehman Stores for $2,400. His terms allow a cash discount of 2.5% for payment within 10 days. Rehman Stores paid by cheque within 10 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount Rehman Stores will pay if it pays within the discount period. [1]
  2. (b) State the entries in Haroon's three-column cash book when the cheque is received. [2]
  3. (c) State the entry in Rehman Stores' account in Haroon's sales ledger. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,340
    2,400 × 97.5% = 2,340 (B1).
  2. (b) Debit side: bank column $2,340 and discount allowed column $60.
    B1 bank column debit 2,340. B1 discount column (debit side) 60.
  3. (c) Credit $2,340 bank and credit $60 discount allowed (total $2,400)
    B1 both credits; the account is cleared.

Q5Cash book and petty cash book · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A petty cashier works an imprest system with an imprest of $250. At the end of September the petty cash box holds $37. How much must the main cashier give the petty cashier to restore the imprest?A $37B $213C $250D $287 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B $213
    Amount spent = 250 − 37 = $213, and the imprest is restored by reimbursing exactly what was spent. $37 is the balance left, not the amount needed.

  4. (d) State where the total of the ledger column is posted. [1]

Q7Cash book and petty cash book · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

On 12 August Mehwish received a cheque for $1,560 from Tariq, a credit customer, in full settlement of a debt of $1,600. On 19 August the bank told her that the cheque had been dishonoured.

  1. (a) State the entry in Mehwish's cash book on 19 August. [1]
  2. (b) State the entry in Tariq's account in the sales ledger on 19 August. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the discount allowed on 12 August must also be cancelled. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Credit the bank column with $1,560
    B1 credit bank column (reverse of the original receipt).
  2. (b) Debit Tariq's account with $1,560
    B1: Tariq owes the money again.
  3. (c) The discount was given only because the debt was paid on time; as the payment failed, Tariq has not earned it, so $40 is debited to his account and credited to discount allowed.
    B1 discount conditional on payment. B1 entry: debit Tariq $40, credit discount allowed.
Q8Cash book and petty cash book · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) $1,200 credit balance

    B1.
  3. (c) Under current liabilities (bank overdraft)
    B1.
Q9Cash book and petty cash book · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

At the end of November the analysis columns of a petty cash book showed: postage $36, cleaning $58, travel $44, ledger (credit supplier Kashif) $48. The total of $186 was restored by cheque.

  1. (a) State the entries made in the general ledger for each analysis column total. [2]
  2. (b) State the double entry for the restoring cheque of $186. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the petty cash book is both a book of prime entry and part of the double-entry system. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Debit postage $36, cleaning $58 and travel $44 accounts in the general ledger. (The ledger column $48 is not a general ledger entry: it is debited to Kashif's account in the purchases ledger.)
    B1 debit, B1 the three expense accounts named with their totals.
  2. (b) Debit petty cash $186; credit bank (cash book bank column) $186
    B1 each side.
  3. (c) Payments are first recorded in it from vouchers (prime entry), and it also acts as the petty cash account, so its entries form one side of the double entry.
    B1 first record from source documents. B1 it is the petty cash ledger account itself.

Q11Cash book and petty cash book · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Ayesha runs a stationery shop in Multan. On 1 May 2026 her three-column cash book showed cash in hand $420 and a bank balance of $3,150 (debit).May 3 Cash sales $1,260May 6 Received a cheque from Kamran Ltd in full settlement of a debt of $2,000, less 2.5% cash discountMay 9 Paid Zaib Suppliers by cheque for a debt of $1,600, less 4% cash discountMay 12 Paid wages in cash $680May 15 Paid $800 cash into the bankMay 20 Bought office equipment, paying by cheque $2,450May 24 Received $735 cash from Hina in full settlement of her debt of $750May 28 Paid electricity by bank transfer $390

  1. (a) Calculate the balance on the cash column on 31 May 2026. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the balance on the bank column on 31 May 2026. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the totals of the discount allowed column and the discount received column. [2]
  4. (d) State the entry made on 15 May in the cash book, and explain why it is called a contra entry. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $935 (debit balance)
    Debits: 420 + 1,260 + 735 = 2,415 (M1). Credits: 680 + 800 = 1,480. Balance 935 debit (A1).
  2. (b) $1,524 (debit balance)
    Debits 3,150 + 1,950 + 800 = 5,900 and credits 1,536 + 2,450 + 390 = 4,376 (M1). Balance 1,524 debit (A1).

    Allowed: 50 (Kamran) + 15 (Hina) = 65 (B1). Received: 4% × 1,600 = 64 (B1).

    B1 cash column credit / bank column debit. B1 both accounts (cash and bank) are in the same book, so no posting to the ledger is needed.

Q26Books of prime entry · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

    B1 individual credits to customers. B1 total debited to sales returns.

Nasreen started keeping double-entry records on 1 January 2026. Her business had: premises $85,000; fixtures $12,500; inventory $9,300; trade receivables $4,700; bank $3,600; trade payables $5,900; a five-year loan $20,000.

  1. (a) Calculate Nasreen's capital on 1 January 2026. [2]
  2. (b) Prepare the opening journal entry (accounts, debit or credit, and amounts; a narrative is not required). [4]
  3. (c) State why a narrative is written under each journal entry. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $89,200
    Assets 115,100 (M1) − liabilities 25,900 = 89,200 (A1).
  2. (b) Debit: premises 85,000; fixtures 12,500; inventory 9,300; trade receivables 4,700; bank 3,600. Credit: trade payables 5,900; loan 20,000; capital 89,200. Totals 115,100 each.
    B1 assets debited. B1 liabilities credited. B1 capital credited 89,200. B1 totals 115,100.
  3. (c) To explain the reason for / give details of the entry
    B1.
Q28Business documents · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Rania returned damaged goods to her supplier. Which document will the supplier send to Rania?A invoiceB credit noteC debit noteD receipt [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B credit note
    A credit note is sent by the seller to reduce the amount the buyer owes. A debit note is sent by the buyer when requesting a credit, so C is the main distractor.
Q29Business documents · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Sarwar Supplies sold 40 desks at a list price of $75 each to Green Valley School. The invoice shows 20% trade discount and terms of 5% cash discount if paid within 14 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount shown as the total on the invoice. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the amount Green Valley School pays if it pays within 14 days. [1]
  3. (c) Explain the difference between trade discount and cash discount. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,400
    40 × 75 = 3,000 (M1); less 20% = 2,400 (A1).
  2. (b) $2,280
    2,400 × 95% = 2,280 (B1).
  3. (c) Trade discount: a reduction in list price for trade customers / bulk buying, not recorded in the ledger. Cash discount: an allowance for prompt payment, recorded in the books.
    B1 each.
Q30Business documents · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Hamza Foods sends monthly statements of account to Mubarak Restaurants. In August: balance owing on 1 August $1,260; invoice 3 August $3,480; credit note 9 August $290; cheque received 14 August $1,230 in settlement of the opening balance (cash discount $30); invoice 27 August $940.

  1. (a) Calculate the balance shown on the statement of account at 31 August. [2]
  2. (b) State two purposes of a statement of account. [2]
  3. (c) Before the credit note was received, Mubarak Restaurants had sent a document to Hamza Foods asking for the credit. Name this document. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $4,130
    Debits 1,260 + 3,480 + 940 = 5,680 (M1); less 290 + 1,230 + 30 = 1,550; 4,130 (A1).
  2. (b) Any two: tells the customer how much is owed; reminds the customer to pay; allows the customer to check the seller's records against its own; shows transactions for the month.
    1 mark each for any two.
  3. (c) Debit note
    B1.
