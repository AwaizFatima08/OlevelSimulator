Q2Depreciation methods and calculation · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Hamza Printers, Lahore, bought a printing machine on 1 January 2024 for Rs 1,250,000. Installation cost Rs 50,000. The machine is expected to last 5 years and then be sold for Rs 100,000. The straight-line method is used and the financial year ends on 31 December.

  1. (a) Calculate the annual depreciation charge. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the carrying amount of the machine at 31 December 2026. [2]
  3. (c) State the accounting concept applied when depreciation is charged, and explain how it is applied. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rs 240,000
    Cost = 1,250,000 + 50,000 installation = 1,300,000 (M1). (1,300,000 − 100,000) ÷ 5 = Rs 240,000 (A1).
  2. (b) Rs 580,000
    Three years' depreciation = 3 × 240,000 = 720,000 (M1). 1,300,000 − 720,000 = Rs 580,000 (A1).
  3. (c) Matching (accruals) concept: the cost of the machine is spread over the years in which it helps to earn revenue.
    1 mark for naming matching/accruals; 1 mark for explaining that the cost is charged against the revenue of the periods that benefit from the asset.

Show answer

Q8Depreciation methods and calculation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

SwiftDrop, a courier business in Nairobi, bought a delivery van for $40,000. It uses the reducing balance method at 40% per annum.

  1. (a) Calculate the depreciation charge for each of the four years using the reducing balance method. [3]
  2. (b) The van will be worth $5,184 after four years. Calculate the annual charge if the straight-line method were used instead. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the reducing balance method may be more suitable for the van. [2]
  4. (d) State the effect on profit for year 1 of using the reducing balance method instead of the straight-line method. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Year 1 $16,000; year 2 $9,600; year 3 $5,760; year 4 $3,456
    40% × 40,000 = 16,000; 40% × 24,000 = 9,600; 40% × 14,400 = 5,760; 40% × 8,640 = 3,456. M1 for applying 40% to the carrying amount, A2 all four correct (A1 for two or three correct).

    (40,000 − 5,184) ÷ 4 = $8,704.
  3. (c) A van loses more of its value in its early years, and repair costs rise as it gets older, so a high early charge gives a more even total cost each year.
    1 mark each for any two of: van loses most value early; carrying amount closer to resale value; maintenance rises with age so depreciation + repairs is more even; van used most heavily when new.
  4. (d) Profit is $7,296 lower.
    16,000 − 8,704 = 7,296 (M1); profit lower/decreases (A1).

Q11Depreciation methods and calculation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Lumière Bakery, in Montreal, bought an oven for $50,000 at the start of year 1. Its policy is to depreciate the oven at 20% per annum using the reducing balance method. Years 1 and 2 were correct, but in year 3 the bookkeeper charged 20% of the original cost.

  1. (a) Calculate the correct depreciation charge for year 3. [2]
  2. (b) State the effect of the error on the profit for year 3. [2]
  3. (c) State the effect of the error on the statement of financial position at the end of year 3. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $6,400
    CA at start of year 3 = 50,000 × 0.8² = 32,000 (M1); 20% × 32,000 = $6,400 (A1).
  2. (b) Profit understated by $3,600
    Charged 10,000, should be 6,400; difference 3,600 (M1); profit understated (A1).
  3. (c) Accumulated depreciation overstated by $3,600, so the carrying amount of non-current assets (and capital) is understated by the same amount.
    1 mark non-current assets/carrying amount understated by the amount; 1 mark capital (retained profit) understated.

Q16Depreciation methods and calculation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Farhan is opening a furniture workshop in Sialkot and will apply for a bank loan after his first year. He buys a CNC machine for $90,000, expected to last 5 years with a residual value of $10,000. He can use the straight-line method or the reducing balance method at 35% per annum. Profit before depreciation for year 1 is expected to be $60,000. He wants to choose the method that shows the higher profit.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit for year 1 under each method. [2]
  2. (b) Advise Farhan which method he should use. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Straight-line $44,000; reducing balance $28,500
    SL: (90,000 − 10,000) ÷ 5 = 16,000; 60,000 − 16,000 = 44,000 (B1). RB: 35% × 90,000 = 31,500; 60,000 − 31,500 = 28,500 (B1).
  2. (b) Advice should favour the method that best reflects how the machine is used, applied consistently, rather than the one that flatters the loan application (e.g. reducing balance if the machine loses value fast).
    Points for SL: higher year-1 profit by $15,500; simple; suits even use. Points for RB: matches heavy early use/fall in value; more prudent; profits more even when repairs rise. Consistency concept: once chosen, the method must be kept, so a switch later to boost profit is not allowed; a bank will look at cash flow and security, not only profit; choosing a method only to impress the bank misleads users. Up to 4 marks for points (max 3 one side), 2 marks for a justified decision.

Show answer
