Q1Statement of cash flows (IAS 7) · Core · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Extracts from the records of Noor Textiles Ltd for the year ended 31 December 2026:Profit from operations $84,600; depreciation $21,300; loss on disposal of equipment $1,400.Inventories increased by $5,800; trade receivables decreased by $3,200; trade payables increased by $2,700.

  1. (a) Prepare a reconciliation of profit from operations to cash generated from operations. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why depreciation is added back to profit from operations. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $Profit from operations 84,600Adjustments for: Depreciation 21,300 Loss on disposal of equipment 1,400 Increase in inventories (5,800) Decrease in trade receivables 3,200 Increase in trade payables 2,700Cash generated from operations 107,400
    B1 depreciation added; B1 loss on disposal added; B1 inventory increase deducted; B1 receivables decrease and payables increase added; B1 total 107,400.
  2. (b) Depreciation is an expense that reduced profit but involved no outflow of cash (the cash left when the asset was bought, shown under investing), so it is added back.
    1 mark: non-cash expense; 1 mark: cash outflow occurs on purchase / shown in investing.

Q11Statement of cash flows (IAS 7) · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Kingsway Holdings Ltd classifies interest received and dividends received as investing cash flows, and dividends paid as financing cash flows. For the year ended 31 December 2026 (all in $):Dividends received from investments: 4,200Interest received: 1,100Purchase of property, plant and equipment: 28,000Proceeds from sale of equipment: 9,300Purchase of shares in another company: 15,000Dividends paid to shareholders: 22,000

  1. (a) Calculate the net cash from (used in) investing activities for the year. [2]
  2. (b) State the section of the statement of cash flows in which dividends paid appear. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why dividends paid are not an investing activity. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Net cash used in investing activities of $28,400
    Dividends received 4,200 + interest received 1,100 − purchase of PPE 28,000 + sale proceeds 9,300 − purchase of shares 15,000 = −28,400. 1 mark for the correct signs; 1 for the total.
  2. (b) Financing activities
    Dividends paid to shareholders are a financing cash flow under the company's policy.
  3. (c) Investing activities are the purchase and sale of long-term assets and investments. Dividends paid are a return of money to the owners, which is a financing transaction, not a payment for an investment.
    1 mark for investing being about assets and investments; 1 mark for dividends being a payment to owners (a financing flow).

Borrowings at 31 December 2026 were $160,000. Net debt is borrowings less cash and cash equivalents.

  1. (a) Calculate the net change in cash and cash equivalents for 2026. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the net debt at 1 January 2026 and at 31 December 2026. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why net debt fell during 2026 even though borrowings rose. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) An increase of $54,000
    96,000 − 60,000 − 22,000 + 40,000 = 54,000. 1 mark for the correct signs; 1 for the total.
  2. (b) Net debt of $104,400 at 1 January 2026 and $90,400 at 31 December 2026
    1 January: 120,000 − 15,600 = 104,400. 31 December: closing cash 15,600 + 54,000 = 69,600; net debt = 160,000 − 69,600 = 90,400.
  3. (c) The new loan increased borrowings by $40,000, but cash rose by $54,000, so the cash increase was larger than the rise in debt. Net debt measures debt after deducting cash, so it fell by $14,000, meaning the company is less reliant on borrowing.
    1 mark for borrowings rising by 40,000; 1 mark for cash rising by more than that; 1 mark for net debt falling by 14,000 and the meaning of that for the company.
Q54Uses of cash flow statements · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Solaris Foods Ltd at 31 December 2026: total equity $480,000; non-current liabilities $220,000. Net cash from operating activities for 2026 was $118,000. Cash return on capital employed is defined here as net cash from operating activities as a percentage of capital employed, where capital employed is total equity plus non-current liabilities.

  1. (a) Calculate the capital employed at 31 December 2026, defined as total equity plus non-current liabilities. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the cash return on capital employed as a percentage, to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how this measure differs from return on capital employed based on profit. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $700,000
    480,000 + 220,000 = 700,000.
  2. (b) 16.9%
    118,000 ÷ 700,000 × 100 = 16.857%, so 16.9%. 1 mark for the formula; 1 for the answer.
  3. (c) Return on capital employed uses profit, which includes non-cash items and accrual adjustments, while this measure uses cash from operations, so it shows how much cash the capital actually produces and is less affected by depreciation policy. Both use the same denominator, so they can be compared.
    1 mark for profit including non-cash items; 1 mark for cash showing actual generation; 1 mark for the comparison or the effect of accounting policy.
Q55Uses of cash flow statements · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Tarn Ceramics Ltd, year ends 31 December. Profit from operations and net cash from operating activities were:2024: profit $150,000; net cash from operating activities $162,0002025: profit $175,000; net cash from operating activities $140,0002026: profit $230,000; net cash from operating activities $120,000Cash conversion = net cash from operating activities ÷ profit from operations × 100.

  1. (a) Calculate the cash conversion ratio for each year, to 1 decimal place. [3]
  2. (b) Comment on the trend and suggest one possible cause of it. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2024: 108.0%; 2025: 80.0%; 2026: 52.2%
    2024: 162,000 ÷ 150,000 = 108.0%. 2025: 140,000 ÷ 175,000 = 80.0%. 2026: 120,000 ÷ 230,000 = 52.2%. 1 mark for each correct year.
  2. (b) The ratio has fallen sharply, from above 100% to about 52%, so profits are increasingly not turning into cash. A likely cause is rising receivables or inventory, or lower customer payments, which ties up cash even as reported profit grows. Any reasonable cause supported by the data earns credit.
    1 mark for identifying the falling trend; 1 mark for explaining that profit is not turning into cash; 1 mark for a plausible cause linked to working capital.
Q56Uses of cash flow statements · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Northmere Print Ltd at 31 December 2026: current assets $240,000, of which inventory is $150,000; current liabilities $100,000. Its current ratio is 2.4.

  1. (a) Calculate the acid test ratio (current assets less inventory, divided by current liabilities), to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a company with a current ratio of 2.4 may still struggle to pay its suppliers on time. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.9
    (240,000 − 150,000) ÷ 100,000 = 0.9. 1 mark for removing inventory; 1 for the answer.
  2. (b) Most of the current assets are inventory, which cannot be turned into cash quickly, so the quick assets are only 0.9 times the current liabilities. Cash flows, not the ratio, decide whether bills can be paid on time. A company can have large stocks of slow-moving goods and still lack cash.
    1 mark for inventory being a large part of current assets; 1 mark for the acid test being below 1; 1 mark for the timing of cash flows; 1 mark for the point that the ratio does not show cash.
Q57Uses of cash flow statements · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Pinnacle Group bought 80% of the shares of Westbrook Ltd for $95,000 cash. Westbrook Ltd had cash of $12,000 at the date of acquisition.

  1. (a) Calculate the net cash outflow shown under investing activities for the acquisition. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the cash acquired is deducted from the consideration paid. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $83,000
    Cash paid 95,000 − cash acquired 12,000 = 83,000. 1 mark for the subtraction; 1 for the answer.
  2. (b) The group's cash has really increased by the cash it acquired, so the net cash cost of the purchase is the consideration less the cash obtained. Showing the gross figure would overstate the cash spent on the investment.
    1 mark for the net cost being the real cash spent; 1 mark for the gross figure overstating the outflow.
Q58Uses of cash flow statements · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Greenhill Ltd, year ended 31 December 2026: net cash used in operating activities $30,000 (after interest and tax paid); proceeds from sale of land $190,000; purchase of property, plant and equipment $25,000; repayment of a bank loan $60,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the net change in cash and cash equivalents for the year. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the net change excluding the sale of land. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the increase in cash is not a sign of financial health. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) An increase of $75,000
    −30,000 + 190,000 − 25,000 − 60,000 = 75,000. 1 mark for the signs; 1 for the total.
  2. (b) A decrease of $115,000
    75,000 − 190,000 = −115,000.
  3. (c) Operations used cash (an outflow of $30,000) and the cash increase came from a one-off sale of land. Without it cash would have fallen by $115,000, so the business is not generating cash from its trading. Once the land is sold it cannot be sold again, so the position is not sustainable.
    1 mark for operations being a cash outflow; 1 mark for the one-off nature of the land sale; 1 mark for the sustainability or trend point.
Q59Uses of cash flow statements · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate the view that the statement of cash flows gives a more reliable picture of a company's financial health than the statement of profit or loss. [10]
Show answer
  1. (a) Content points: for: cash is harder to manipulate and less affected by accruals, depreciation policy and judgements; it shows liquidity and the ability to pay debts and dividends; it reveals working capital problems that profit hides. Against: it is historical and does not show future cash flows; timing of year-end payments can distort it; it does not show profitability or asset values, which the statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position reveal; a single year can be misleading. A strong answer weighs both sides and reaches a judgement that the two statements are complementary.
    Band 1 (1–3): describes one or two features. Band 2 (4–6): explains reliability points on both sides with some balance. Band 3 (7–8): evaluates the view with reference to context. Band 4 (9–10): reaches a supported judgement that integrates both statements.
Q60Uses of cash flow statements · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Silverleaf Bakery reduced its inventory from $90,000 to $50,000 during the year by delaying purchases and selling stock.

  1. (a) Calculate the effect of the inventory reduction on net cash from operating activities. [1]
  2. (b) Explain two risks to the bakery of running down its inventory this far. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) An inflow of $40,000
    90,000 − 50,000 = 40,000 released as cash.
  2. (b) Any two, each explained: running out of stock on popular lines means lost sales; reduced choice can drive customers to rivals; ingredients bought in small batches can cost more; the rapid reduction may signal problems with supplier payments or demand.
    1 mark for each risk identified (2) and 1 mark for each explanation (2).
