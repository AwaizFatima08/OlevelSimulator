Q5Partnership or sole trader acquired by a company · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give two reasons why a limited company may buy an existing business rather than expand by opening new premises. [2]
  2. (b) Give two reasons why a sole trader may accept shares rather than cash for the business. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why purchased goodwill is shown in the buying company's statement of financial position, but the goodwill the sole trader had built up was not shown in the sole trader's books. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: it gains an established customer base and reputation at once; removes a competitor; gains skilled staff, suppliers and premises; quicker than organic growth; economies of scale.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any two: continues to share in future profits through dividends; shares may rise in value; limited liability; shares can be sold later; the company may not have enough cash.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) Purchased goodwill has a reliable cost – the amount actually paid above the fair value of net assets. Internally generated goodwill has no reliable cost or measurable value, so it is not recognised.
    1 mark each side.

Q10Partnership or sole trader acquired by a company · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Farid is a sole trader whose business has made an average profit of $46,000 a year. Zenith Ltd offers to buy his business for 200,000 ordinary shares of $1 each, valued at $1.50 per share. Farid would be employed by Zenith Ltd as branch manager at a salary of $30,000 a year. Zenith Ltd has paid a dividend of $0.09 per share for the last three years.

  1. (a) Calculate Farid's expected annual income if he accepts the offer. [2]
  2. (b) Advise Farid whether he should accept the offer. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) $48,000
    B1 dividend 200,000 × 0.09 = 18,000; B1 total 48,000.
  2. (b) For: expected income $48,000 is $2,000 more than now; a fixed salary is less risky than trading profit; shares are worth $300,000 and can be sold; limited liability; fewer working hours and responsibilities.Against: dividends are not guaranteed and may fall; the share price may fall; he loses control and becomes an employee who can be dismissed; he is a small minority shareholder with little say; the gain of $2,000 is small.Decision: e.g. accept, because income rises and risk falls, provided he is satisfied with the dividend record and a service contract.
    Up to 3 marks for points for, up to 3 marks for points against (max 5 for points); 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q11Partnership or sole trader acquired by a company · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Bright Ltd has 500,000 ordinary shares in issue. Its profit for the year after tax is expected to be $160,000. It plans to buy a partnership by issuing 150,000 new ordinary shares. Show answer
  1. (a) 32.00 cents
    B1.
  2. (b) $31,500
