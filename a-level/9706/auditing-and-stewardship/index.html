Q1Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Karakoram Foods Limited has 4,000 shareholders. The company is run by a board of five directors.

  1. (a) Explain the term stewardship in the context of a limited company. [2]
  2. (b) State two ways in which directors demonstrate good stewardship. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stewardship is the responsibility of the directors to look after the company's resources (assets) properly on behalf of the shareholders who own it, and to account to them for how those resources have been used.
    1 mark: directors manage/safeguard resources for shareholders; 1 mark: accountability/reporting to owners.
  2. (b) Any two: keeping proper accounting records; preparing annual financial statements that give a true and fair view; safeguarding assets and preventing fraud through internal controls; having the statements audited and presenting them at the annual general meeting; using resources efficiently in shareholders' interests.
    1 mark each.
Q2Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State two differences between an internal auditor and an external auditor. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: appointment — internal auditor is an employee appointed by management, external auditor is independent and appointed by the shareholders; reporting — internal auditor reports to the directors/audit committee, external auditor reports to the shareholders; purpose — internal audit reviews systems, controls and efficiency for management, external audit gives an opinion on whether the statements give a true and fair view; legal requirement — external audit is required by law for most companies, internal audit is optional; qualification/independence — external auditor must be a registered, independent accountant.
    2 marks per difference (both sides compared).

Show answer
  1. (a) Any three: right of access at all times to the company's books, records and vouchers; right to require information and explanations from directors and officers; right to receive notice of and attend general meetings; right to speak at general meetings on matters concerning them as auditor; right to make written representations if they are to be removed or not reappointed.
    1 mark each.
Q4Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain the meaning of the phrase 'a true and fair view'. [2]
  2. (b) State who is responsible for preparing a company's financial statements. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The financial statements are free from material misstatement and fairly present the company's financial performance and position, prepared in accordance with accounting standards and the law. It does not mean that every figure is exactly correct.
    1 mark: free from material misstatement / faithfully presented; 1 mark: in accordance with standards / not absolute accuracy.
  2. (b) The directors (not the auditors).
    1 mark.
Q5Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State who appoints the external auditors of a limited company, and to whom they report. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why it is important that the external auditor is independent of the directors. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) They are appointed by the shareholders (usually at the annual general meeting) and report to the shareholders.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) The auditor checks the statements prepared by the directors; if not independent they might be influenced to accept misstatements, so shareholders could not rely on the opinion.
    1 mark: checking directors' work / objective opinion; 1 mark: shareholders' reliance/confidence.

Show answer
  1. (a) Any four: title and addressee (the shareholders); the auditor's opinion; basis for opinion (statement that the audit was done under auditing standards and the auditor is independent); key audit matters (for listed companies); material uncertainty related to going concern, if any; responsibilities of the directors for the financial statements; responsibilities of the auditor; auditor's name, signature, address and date.
    1 mark each.
Q7Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain two threats to the independence of an external auditor. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Self-interest: e.g. the auditor owns shares in the client or depends heavily on its fees, so may avoid a qualified opinion. Self-review: the audit firm also prepares the accounts it audits. Familiarity: a long relationship or family ties with directors. Intimidation: directors threaten to remove the auditor. Advocacy: the auditor promotes the client's interests.
    2 marks each: threat named + example/explanation.

    1 mark each group; 1 mark each reason linked to reliability.
Q9Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain how publishing an audited annual report helps the directors carry out their stewardship responsibility. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The annual report shows shareholders how the directors have used the company's resources and what results they achieved; the audit gives independent assurance that this account is true and fair; shareholders can then question directors and vote on their reappointment at the AGM, so the directors are held accountable.
    1 mark: reports use of resources/results; 1 mark: independent assurance; 1 mark: accountability (AGM/votes).
Q10Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

During the audit of Chitral Tea Limited, the auditor identified the following errors in the draft statement of financial position, which shows net assets of $2,460,000:• Damaged inventory not yet written down to net realisable value: effect on net assets −$36,000• Accrued audit fee not recorded: effect on net assets −$18,000• Prepaid rent omitted: effect on net assets +$12,500

  1. (a) Calculate the corrected net assets of the company. [2]
  2. (b) The directors agree to make all the corrections. State the type of audit opinion that is likely to be given and explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,418,500
    2,460,000 − 36,000 − 18,000 + 12,500 = 2,418,500. M1 A1.
  2. (b) An unmodified opinion: once the misstatements have been corrected, the statements give a true and fair view in all material respects, so there is no reason to modify the opinion.
    1 mark unmodified; 1 mark explanation.
Q11Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by 'materiality' in an audit. [2]
  2. (b) State one benchmark an auditor might use to set a materiality threshold. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A misstatement is material if its omission or misstatement could influence the economic decisions of users of the financial statements.
    1 mark for the idea that users' decisions could be influenced; 1 mark for stating that the auditor judges size or nature against a threshold.
  2. (b) Profit before tax (or revenue, total assets, net assets).
    1 mark for any one benchmark.
Q12Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Mahnoor Traders Limited has trade receivables of $264,000 at its year end, owed by 180 customer accounts.

  1. (a) Name three audit procedures the external auditor could use to obtain evidence that trade receivables exist and can be recovered. [3]
  2. (b) State which of these procedures gives the most reliable evidence of existence, and give one reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three, e.g. direct confirmation of balances with customers; inspection of cash received after the year end; inspection of sales invoices and despatch records; review of the receivables ageing analysis; review of credit notes issued after the year end; discussion of recoverability with management.
    1 mark for each correctly named procedure, maximum 3.
  2. (b) Direct confirmation with customers (circularisation).
    1 mark for the procedure; 1 mark for the reason: the evidence comes directly from an independent third party.
Q13Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the audit expectation gap and give two causes of it. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The expectation gap is the difference between what users expect an audit to achieve (for example, a guarantee that the company is free of fraud and will not fail) and what an audit provides (reasonable assurance, based on sample testing, that the accounts give a true and fair view). Two causes: users misunderstand the auditor's role; the audit is sample-based and cannot detect everything, for example collusive fraud.
    1 mark for the definition; 1 mark each for any two causes: users misunderstand the auditor's role; the audit is sample-based; the standard wording of the report is not understood; company failures are blamed on auditors; collusive fraud is hard to detect. Maximum 4.
Q14Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Shahzad Plastics Limited owes its bank $900,000 under a loan due for repayment in eight months. The bank has said it will not renew the loan. The directors believe the company can continue trading and have not mentioned the loan problem in the notes to the financial statements.

  1. (a) State what the auditor must consider about whether the company can continue as a going concern. [2]
  2. (b) State what the directors should disclose, and what happens to the audit opinion if this disclosure is not made. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Whether the directors' assumption that the company is a going concern (able to continue operating for the foreseeable future) is appropriate, given the loan repayment and the bank's refusal to renew.
    1 mark for assessing the going concern assumption; 1 mark for relating it to the loan repayment and the bank's decision.
  2. (b) The directors should disclose the material uncertainty and their plans to deal with it (for example, negotiating a new loan). If they do not, the auditor is likely to give a qualified or adverse opinion.
    1 mark for disclosure of the material uncertainty; 1 mark for a qualified or adverse opinion.
Q15Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Hamza Logistics Limited employs three internal auditors. The internal audit department reports to the finance director, who is responsible for preparing the company's financial statements.

  1. (a) State one reason why this reporting line may reduce the objectivity of the internal auditors. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest one change that would improve their independence. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The finance director is responsible for the figures the internal auditors review, so they may be reluctant to criticise work that reports to their own line manager.
    1 mark for stating the threat (objectivity or self-review); 1 mark for explaining it.
  2. (b) Internal audit should report directly to the audit committee (or to the board) rather than to the finance director.
    1 mark for the change; 1 mark for explaining that it removes the finance director's influence over audit findings.
Q16Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Define sampling in audit testing and state two reasons why an auditor uses it rather than testing every transaction. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sampling is testing a selected subset of items from a population and using the results to form a conclusion about the whole population. Reasons: testing every item would cost too much and take too long; a well-chosen sample gives sufficient appropriate evidence at a reasonable cost.
    1 mark for the definition; 1 mark each for two reasons (cost; time or practicality).
Q17Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between the responsibilities of the directors and of the external auditor for the financial statements of a limited company. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The directors prepare the financial statements, keep proper accounting records and maintain internal control. The external auditor forms and expresses an independent opinion on whether the statements give a true and fair view.
    1 mark for the directors' preparation of the statements and records; 1 mark for the directors' internal control; 1 mark for the auditor's independent opinion.
Q18Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Bahawalpur Pharmacy Limited uses four controls: (i) a password is required before a user can post sales to the ledger; (ii) the bank statement is compared with the cash book every month; (iii) cash is kept in a safe that only the owner can open; (iv) a count of stock is compared with the stock records at the year end and differences are investigated.

  1. (a) For each control (i) to (iv), state whether it is a preventive or a detective control. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) preventive; (ii) detective; (iii) preventive; (iv) detective.
    1 mark for each correct classification. A preventive control stops an error or fraud happening; a detective control finds one after it has happened.
Q19Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

The sales manager and the warehouse supervisor of Dadu Sugar Mills Limited agree to record false despatches, so that $52,000 of stock appears to have been sold to a customer they control.

  1. (a) Explain why internal control is less effective against this type of fraud. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest one control that would reduce the risk of this fraud. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Collusion between two people in different roles defeats segregation of duties, because each can cover for the other. The despatch documents look genuine and the records agree with each other, so routine checks do not reveal the fraud.
    1 mark each for any three: collusion defeats segregation of duties; falsified documents look genuine; both people can override controls; routine checks compare records that have been falsified together; a sample may not select the items.
  2. (b) Independent confirmation of customer balances directly with customers.
    1 mark for any one sensible control, e.g. confirming balances with customers, rotating duties, or surprise checks by someone outside sales and warehousing.
Q20Auditing and stewardship · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why an unmodified audit opinion does not mean that a company will remain profitable in future years. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The audit opinion only states whether past financial statements give a true and fair view. It does not assess future profits, the company's strategy or its future cash flows.
    1 mark for stating the opinion relates to past statements; 1 mark for true and fair view being about historical reporting; 1 mark for stating that future profitability is not covered.
Q21Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Distinguish between an unmodified (unqualified) audit opinion and a qualified audit opinion. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Unmodified: the auditor concludes that the financial statements give a true and fair view in all material respects. Qualified: the auditor concludes that the statements give a true and fair view except for the effects of a specific matter, which is material but not pervasive (a material misstatement, or an inability to obtain enough evidence about one area).
    2 marks each: meaning + key words ('true and fair', 'except for', material but not pervasive).
Q22Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the three types of modified audit opinion and state when each is given. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Qualified opinion ('except for'): a misstatement or lack of evidence that is material but not pervasive. Adverse opinion: misstatements are material and pervasive, so the statements do not give a true and fair view. Disclaimer of opinion: the auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence and the possible effects are material and pervasive, so no opinion is expressed.
    1 mark each type named; 1 mark each correct circumstance.
Q23Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

The auditors of Ravi Garments Limited found that closing inventory was overstated by $48,000 because obsolete stock had been valued at cost. The directors refuse to adjust it. Profit before tax was $620,000. The audit firm treats any misstatement above 5% of profit before tax as material. No other problems were found.

  1. (a) Calculate the misstatement as a percentage of profit before tax, correct to two decimal places. [1]
  2. (b) Identify the type of audit opinion the auditor is likely to give. Justify your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.74%
    48,000 ÷ 620,000 × 100 = 7.74%.
  2. (b) A qualified ('except for') opinion. The overstatement of inventory is material (well above the 5% threshold the firm uses) but it affects only one area of the statements, so it is not pervasive; the rest of the statements give a true and fair view.
    1 mark qualified; 1 mark material (with reference to the figure); 1 mark not pervasive / limited to inventory.
Q24Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Mehran Retail Limited runs 45 supermarkets across Pakistan and has set up an internal audit department.

  1. (a) Explain three functions of an internal audit department. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Reviewing and testing internal controls (e.g. authorisation of purchases, segregation of duties) to see that they work; checking the accuracy and reliability of accounting records and reports to management; detecting and preventing fraud and error; reviewing the efficiency and value for money of operations; checking compliance with laws, regulations and company policies; helping the external auditor, who may rely on its work.
    2 marks each: function identified (1) + explanation (1), max 3 functions.
Q25Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain two limitations of an external audit. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Auditors test samples of transactions, not every one, so some errors may go undetected; many figures rely on judgement and estimates (e.g. depreciation, allowances, provisions), so the opinion is reasonable not absolute assurance; auditors rely on information and explanations from management, which may be incomplete or deliberately misleading (collusion/fraud); the audit is of past information and is out of date when published; time and cost limits.
    2 marks each: limitation + explanation.
Q26Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why shareholders of a large company need an external audit. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ownership is separated from control: shareholders do not run the company and cannot check the records themselves. The directors prepare the statements and could present results favourably to protect bonuses or reputation. An independent expert's opinion that the statements are true and fair gives shareholders confidence when assessing directors' stewardship and making decisions (buy/sell/vote). It also helps deter fraud and error.
    1 mark each for four points, or 2 marks each for two developed points.
Q27Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

After a cashier at Sadiq Motors Limited was found to have stolen $18,000 over two years, a shareholder complained at the annual general meeting that the auditors 'should have found the fraud'.

  1. (a) Explain the responsibility of (i) the directors and (ii) the external auditor in relation to fraud. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) The directors are primarily responsible for preventing and detecting fraud, by setting up and monitoring internal controls and a culture of honesty. (ii) The auditor must plan and perform the audit to obtain reasonable assurance that the statements are free from material misstatement whether caused by fraud or error; they are not required to find all fraud, but must report material fraud they discover to the appropriate level of management/shareholders.
    2 marks each part.
Q28Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State four responsibilities of the directors of a limited company relating to its financial records and statements. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any four: keep adequate accounting records; prepare annual financial statements that give a true and fair view and follow accounting standards; select suitable accounting policies and apply them consistently; make reasonable judgements and estimates; safeguard the company's assets; take reasonable steps to prevent and detect fraud; assess whether the company is a going concern; file the statements as required by law.
    1 mark each.
Q29Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The internal auditor of Indus Hardware Limited attended the year-end inventory count. The inventory records show a book value of $145,600. The physical count, valued at cost, is $138,950.

  1. (a) Calculate the inventory shortage and express it as a percentage of the book value, correct to two decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest three possible causes of the shortage. [3]
  3. (c) State one internal control that would reduce the risk of this happening again. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $6,650; 4.57%
    145,600 − 138,950 = 6,650; 6,650 ÷ 145,600 × 100 = 4.57%. M1 A1.
  2. (b) Any three: theft by staff or customers; goods sent out without being invoiced; recording errors (e.g. purchases recorded twice); damaged goods thrown away and not recorded; count errors; goods returned to suppliers not recorded.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) E.g. regular surprise inventory counts by staff independent of the storekeeper; restricted access to the store; goods-out notes authorised for every dispatch.
    1 mark.
Q30Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the term segregation of duties. [2]
  2. (b) Give two examples of segregation of duties in the purchases system of a company. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Dividing the stages of a transaction between different people so that no one person controls it from start to finish; this makes fraud harder because it would need collusion, and errors are more likely to be spotted.
    1 mark meaning; 1 mark benefit.
  2. (b) Any two: the person who orders goods is not the person who receives them; the person who records invoices is not the person who authorises payment; the person who signs payments is not the person who keeps the purchases ledger; a different person reconciles supplier statements.
    1 mark each.
Q31Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Swat Marble Limited has 2,500 shareholders, none of whom work in the business. This year its external auditor gave a qualified audit opinion.

  1. (a) Explain why the external auditor's report is addressed to the shareholders rather than to the directors. [2]
  2. (b) State three possible consequences for Swat Marble Limited of receiving a qualified audit opinion. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The auditor is appointed by, and acts for, the shareholders, who own the company but do not manage it. The report gives them an independent opinion on the statements the directors have prepared, so it is a check on the directors' stewardship.
    1 mark appointed by / acts for shareholders; 1 mark checks directors' stewardship for owners who do not run the company.
  2. (b) Any three: shareholders lose confidence in the directors and may vote against their reappointment; the share price may fall; banks may refuse loans or charge higher interest; suppliers may reduce credit; potential investors may be put off; the company's reputation may suffer.
    1 mark each.
Q32Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

The honorary auditor of the Peshawar Squash Club checks the treasurer's records for the year. Cash in hand at the start was $3,850. Receipts recorded were: subscriptions $46,200; event ticket sales $18,750; donations $9,500. All recorded payments, supported by receipts, totalled $61,400. The cash counted at the year end was $11,280.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of cash that should be held at the year end, and the amount missing. [3]
  2. (b) Explain two controls the club committee could introduce to improve the stewardship of its funds. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Should hold $16,900; missing $5,620
    3,850 + 46,200 + 18,750 + 9,500 − 61,400 = 16,900 (M1 A1); 16,900 − 11,280 = 5,620 (A1).
  2. (b) Any two developed: all receipts banked intact and promptly, with numbered receipts issued; two signatories required for payments; regular bank reconciliations reviewed by someone other than the treasurer; an independent honorary auditor checks the accounts annually; monthly reports to the committee comparing receipts with members' list.
    2 marks each: control + how it protects funds.
Q33Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Taj Hotels Limited reports profit before tax of $2,400,000. The auditor sets materiality at 5% of profit before tax. During the audit, the auditor finds an unrecorded accrual of $135,500 for utilities.

  1. (a) Calculate the materiality threshold. [1]
  2. (b) Explain whether this misstatement is material. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the misstatement as a percentage of profit before tax, correct to one decimal place. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $120,000
    1 mark: 5% × $2,400,000.
  2. (b) It is material, because $135,500 is greater than the threshold of $120,000, so the directors should correct it.
    1 mark for comparing the misstatement with the threshold; 1 mark for the conclusion and the need to correct it.
  3. (c) 5.6%
    1 mark: $135,500 ÷ $2,400,000 × 100 = 5.6%.
Q34Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between an adverse opinion and a disclaimer of opinion, and give one circumstance in which each would be given. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Adverse opinion: the auditor concludes the financial statements do not give a true and fair view because the misstatements are both material and pervasive. Disclaimer: the auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence and the possible effects are so material and pervasive that no opinion can be expressed. Example of an adverse opinion: directors refuse to correct errors that affect most of the statements. Example of a disclaimer: the records for the whole year were destroyed and cannot be reconstructed.
    1 mark for the adverse opinion explained; 1 mark for the disclaimer explained; 1 mark for a valid adverse example; 1 mark for a valid disclaimer example.
Q35Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe three audit procedures used to test whether trade payables are complete, and state the evidence that each procedure provides. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three, with description and evidence: review of invoices and payments made after the year end to find liabilities relating to the year (evidence of unrecorded liabilities); review of goods received notes and cut-off around the year end (evidence that goods received are recorded in the correct period); obtaining supplier statements and reconciling them to the ledger (evidence of balances owed); analytical review of purchases against the prior year (evidence of unusual gaps).
    2 marks per procedure: 1 for a clearly described procedure; 1 for the evidence it provides. Maximum 6.
Q36Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the role of an audit committee in supporting the external auditor and in strengthening the directors' stewardship. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any points explained, e.g.: recommends the appointment and fees of the external auditor to shareholders; agrees the scope of the audit; meets the external auditor without executive directors present to discuss findings; reviews significant accounting judgements; oversees the internal audit function; monitors internal controls and follows up audit recommendations; reviews the auditor's independence; reports to the board and shareholders.
    2 marks for each clearly described role that also explains how it supports the audit or stewardship; maximum 3 roles, maximum 6 marks.
Q37Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The internal auditor of Multan Packaging Limited tests a random sample of 40 sales invoices from a total of 1,200 invoices issued in the year. Three of the sampled invoices contain errors, and the total error in the sample is $4,350.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of invoices in the population that are expected to contain errors. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the projected total error in the population of invoices. [2]
  3. (c) State one reason why the internal auditor should still examine the three erroneous invoices in more detail. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 90 invoices
    1 mark for 3 ÷ 40 × 1,200; 1 mark for 90.
  2. (b) $130,500
    1 mark for the average error per sampled invoice: $4,350 ÷ 40 = $108.75; 1 mark for × 1,200 = $130,500.
  3. (c) The errors may indicate fraud or a systematic weakness in invoicing controls, and a projection from a small sample may be inaccurate.
    1 mark for a valid reason; 1 mark for an explanation.
Q38Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

At Quetta Fruit Exports Limited, the same cashier receives customer payments, records them in the receivables ledger and prepares the daily bank deposit slip. The owner rarely reviews the ledger or the deposit slips.

  1. (a) Identify the internal control weakness shown in this case. [1]
  2. (b) Recommend two controls that would reduce the risk of cash being stolen without detection. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lack of segregation of duties: one person handles the cash, records the receipt and prepares the deposit.
    1 mark for naming the weakness with the duties involved.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g.: separate the handling of cash from the recording of receipts; independent comparison of deposit slips with the bank statement by someone else; regular review of the receivables ledger by the owner; sequential numbered receipts; surprise cash counts.
    2 marks for each control: 1 for naming it; 1 for explaining how it reduces the risk.
Q39Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the external auditor may give a qualified opinion if the directors refuse to disclose a material uncertainty about the company's ability to continue as a going concern. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Inadequate disclosure of a material uncertainty means the financial statements are materially misstated. The auditor therefore concludes that they do not fully comply with the reporting framework and gives a qualified ('except for') opinion, or an adverse opinion if the effect is pervasive.
    1 mark for stating that the disclosure failure is a misstatement; 1 mark for a qualified 'except for' opinion; 1 mark for the materiality judgement; 1 mark for an adverse opinion if the effect is pervasive.
Q40Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

The directors of Bahawal Dairy Limited receive a bonus of 3% of profit before tax. Reported profit before tax is $1,500,000. The auditor finds an unrecorded expense of $80,000 for staff training that relates to this year.

  1. (a) Calculate the bonus based on the reported profit. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the corrected bonus and the amount overpaid to the directors. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why this bonus arrangement may encourage the directors to misstate profit. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $45,000
    1 mark: 3% × $1,500,000.
  2. (b) Corrected profit before tax is $1,420,000; corrected bonus $42,600; overpayment $2,400.

  3. (c) The bonus rises with reported profit, so the directors gain personally from overstating profit or understating expenses, which conflicts with their duty to shareholders.

Q41Auditing and stewardship · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the external auditor asks management to sign a written representation letter at the end of the audit. State one limitation of relying on this letter. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The letter confirms management's responsibility for the financial statements and that all information and records have been provided; it gives the auditor written evidence to use. Limitation: it is a statement by management, who may be biased or may be responsible for the misstatement, so it is not independent evidence and cannot replace other procedures.

Sukkur Hospital Trust buys a new X-ray machine for $210,000 at the start of the year.

(a) Any two: physical inspection of the machine (evidence that it exists and is in use); inspection of the supplier invoice and payment record (evidence of cost and purchase); inspection of ownership documents or the warranty registered in the trust's name (evidence of ownership); review of the asset register and its reconciliation to the ledger.

Q43Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Pak Agro Exports Limited has no internal audit department. Last year losses from undetected errors and staff fraud were estimated at $140,000. The finance director proposes an internal audit department costing $95,000 a year, which is expected to reduce such losses by 60%. The external auditor has said it may be able to rely on some of the internal auditors' work.

  1. (a) Calculate the expected annual saving in losses and the net annual cost or benefit of the internal audit department. [2]
  2. (b) Advise the directors whether they should set up the internal audit department. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Saving $84,000; net annual cost $11,000
    60% × 140,000 = 84,000 (M1); 95,000 − 84,000 = 11,000 net cost (A1).
  2. (b) Against: on these figures it costs $11,000 a year more than it saves; the saving is only an estimate; internal auditors are employees, so they are less independent than the external auditor and cannot replace the external audit. For: it reviews and tests internal controls continuously, deterring fraud as well as detecting it; it can improve the efficiency of operations and the reliability of records for management; the external auditor may rely on its work, which could reduce the external audit fee and close the $11,000 gap; it helps the directors show good stewardship of shareholders' assets. Decision: e.g. set it up, because the net cost is small and the non-financial benefits (stronger controls, possible lower audit fee) are likely to outweigh it.
    Up to 2 marks for points against (using the $11,000 figure), up to 3 marks for points in favour applied to the company, 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q44Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

During the year, the directors of Lahore Builders Limited: (1) bought land for $1.3 million from a company owned by the managing director's brother; an independent valuer had valued it at $1.0 million, and the transaction was not disclosed in the annual report; (2) increased their own bonuses by 40% while reducing the dividend per share from 12 cents to 8 cents; (3) published the annual report four months later than usual. The company's profit for the year rose by 12% and the directors set up an internal audit department.

  1. (a) Discuss whether the directors of Lahore Builders Limited have carried out their stewardship responsibilities properly. Conclude with a judgement. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) Against good stewardship: buying land from a company owned by the managing director's brother at 30% above valuation is a conflict of interest and may waste shareholders' funds; failure to disclose the related party transaction; directors' bonuses increased while dividends cut — possible self-interest; the delay in publishing accounts reduces accountability. For: profits rose by 12%; they set up an internal audit department and kept proper records; the land may have strategic value. Judgement: overall the directors have not fully discharged their stewardship duty because of the undisclosed related-party purchase and the bonus/dividend issue, although operational performance improved.
    Level 3 (7–8): balanced discussion applied to the case with a justified judgement. Level 2 (4–6): some applied points, may be one-sided, some judgement. Level 1 (1–3): general statements about stewardship.
Q45Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

A flood destroyed the head office of Sindh Rice Mills Limited, including most sales and inventory records for the year. Revenue, trade receivables and closing inventory together make up most of the figures in the financial statements. The directors have estimated these figures, but there is no supporting evidence for the auditor to test.

  1. (a) Identify the type of audit opinion the auditor should give. Justify your answer. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) A disclaimer of opinion. The auditor cannot obtain sufficient appropriate evidence for revenue, receivables and inventory, which affect most of the statements, so the possible effects are both material and pervasive. The auditor therefore does not express an opinion. A qualified opinion would only be suitable if the lack of evidence affected a limited area, and an adverse opinion needs evidence that the statements are actually misstated.
    1 mark disclaimer; 1 mark inability to obtain evidence; 1 mark material; 1 mark pervasive; 1 mark distinguishing from qualified/adverse.
Q46Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

The bank has withdrawn all loans from Margalla Shoes Limited and the directors have decided to put the company into liquidation shortly after the year end. However, they have prepared the financial statements on a going concern basis and refuse to change them or to disclose the planned liquidation.

  1. (a) Identify the type of audit opinion the auditor should give. Justify your answer. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) An adverse opinion. The company is not a going concern (bank has withdrawn finance, directors intend to liquidate), but the statements are prepared on a going concern basis, so assets are at cost less depreciation rather than realisable values and liabilities may be misclassified. This misstatement is material and affects the statements as a whole (pervasive), so they do not give a true and fair view. It is not a disclaimer because the auditor has sufficient evidence.
    1 mark adverse; 1 mark basis of preparation wrong; 1 mark material; 1 mark pervasive; 1 mark sufficient evidence/not a disclaimer.
Q47Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Thar Coal Services Limited reports profit before tax of $1,150,000. Its mining equipment cost $3,600,000 and has a useful life of 12 years with no residual value, but the directors have decided not to charge any depreciation on it this year. The external auditor disagrees. The directors tell shareholders that "the auditor is responsible for getting the figures right".

  1. (a) Calculate the straight-line depreciation for the year, the corrected profit before tax, and the misstatement as a percentage of the reported profit before tax, correct to two decimal places. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the directors' statement is wrong, and state with reasons the audit opinion likely to be given if the directors refuse to charge depreciation. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Depreciation $300,000; corrected profit before tax $850,000; misstatement 26.09%
    3,600,000 ÷ 12 = 300,000 (B1); 1,150,000 − 300,000 = 850,000 (B1); 300,000 ÷ 1,150,000 × 100 = 26.09% (B1).
  2. (b) The directors are responsible for preparing financial statements that give a true and fair view; the auditor only examines them and gives an independent opinion on them. The misstatement (26.09% of profit) is material, but it is confined to one item (depreciation, carrying amount of equipment and retained earnings) rather than the statements as a whole, so it is not pervasive. The auditor is therefore likely to give a qualified ('except for') opinion.
    1 mark directors prepare; 1 mark auditor gives opinion only; 1 mark material with reference to figure; 1 mark qualified because not pervasive.
Q48Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

At the annual general meeting of Kohat Cement Limited, shareholders must vote on reappointing Khan & Co as external auditors. Khan & Co received an audit fee of $85,000 and a further $240,000 for consultancy, including designing the company's new computerised accounting system. The same audit partner has led the audit for 11 years.

  1. (a) Calculate non-audit fees as a percentage of the total fees received from the company, correct to one decimal place. [1]
  2. (b) Advise the shareholders whether they should vote to reappoint Khan & Co as auditors. Justify your answer. [7]
Show answer
  1. (a) 73.8%
    240,000 ÷ (85,000 + 240,000) × 100 = 73.8%.
  2. (b) Against: non-audit fees are almost three times the audit fee (about 74% of total), creating a self-interest threat — the firm may be reluctant to qualify its opinion and risk losing lucrative consultancy work; the firm designed the new accounting system, so it would be auditing its own work (self-review threat); the audit partner has been in charge for 11 years (familiarity threat). For: the firm knows the business well, which may make the audit efficient and cheaper; no evidence of any wrongdoing; changing auditors has costs. Decision: shareholders should not reappoint unless safeguards are put in place (e.g. rotate the audit partner, stop providing system design/consultancy, or have a separate team); otherwise appoint a new independent firm.
    Up to 3 marks for independence threats applied, up to 2 marks for points in favour, 1 mark for safeguards, 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q49Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The directors of Faisal Textiles Limited each receive a bonus equal to 2% of the year's profit before tax. This year the directors postponed essential machine maintenance and changed the estimated useful life of machinery from 8 to 15 years, increasing profit by $1.2 million.

  1. (a) Discuss the conflict of interest between the directors and the shareholders of Faisal Textiles Limited, and suggest how the shareholders could reduce the risk that the directors act in their own interest. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Conflict: the directors' bonus depends on annual profit, so they have an incentive to boost short-term profit (e.g. by delaying maintenance, cutting training, or choosing accounting estimates that increase profit), while shareholders want long-term growth in value and reliable statements; directors also control the information shareholders receive. Ways to reduce it: link part of pay to long-term targets or share price over several years (or pay part in shares); appoint independent non-executive directors and an audit committee; keep a strong independent external audit; require clear disclosure of directors' remuneration and related-party transactions; vote on remuneration at the AGM.
    Up to 3 marks for explaining the conflict with reference to the case, up to 3 marks for suitable safeguards explained.
Q50Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The directors of Hala Ceramics Limited propose that the company's internal audit department, which reports to the finance director, should take over the work of the external auditor. This would save the external audit fee of $120,000 a year.

  1. (a) Evaluate the directors' proposal. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Against: internal auditors are employees who report to the finance director, so they are not independent of the people whose statements they would check; shareholders, lenders and other users need an independent opinion that the statements give a true and fair view; an external audit is normally a legal requirement for a limited company, so it cannot simply be replaced; the external auditor is appointed by and reports to the shareholders, whereas internal audit serves management. For: the saving of $120,000 a year; internal auditors know the business well. Conclusion: reject the proposal; keep both, and instead let the external auditor rely on the internal auditors' work where appropriate, which may reduce the fee.
    Up to 4 marks for points (at least one in favour for full marks) applied to the company, 1 mark for using the $120,000 or the reporting line, 1 mark for a justified conclusion.
Q51Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Lahore Paints Limited reports profit before tax of $3,000,000 and revenue of $45,000,000. The auditor sets materiality at 5% of profit before tax. The auditor finds three errors: (1) closing inventory is overstated by $210,000 because damaged paints were valued at cost; (2) a trade receivable of $95,000 from a customer in liquidation has not been written off; (3) an electricity bill of $80,000 for the year has not been accrued.

  1. (a) Calculate the corrected profit before tax. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the total misstatement as a percentage of reported profit before tax, correct to one decimal place. [1]
  3. (c) State, with reasons, whether the auditor should expect to modify the opinion. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,615,000
    1 mark for recognising that all three errors overstate profit; 1 mark for $3,000,000 − $210,000 − $95,000 − $80,000 = $2,615,000.
  2. (b) 12.8%
    1 mark: $385,000 ÷ $3,000,000 × 100 = 12.8%.
  3. (c) Yes. Materiality is $150,000 (5% of $3,000,000). The inventory error alone ($210,000) exceeds it, and the total misstatement of $385,000 is well above it. If the directors do not correct the errors, the auditor should give a qualified ('except for') opinion, or an adverse opinion if the errors are pervasive.
    1 mark for the threshold of $150,000; 1 mark for comparing the errors with it; 1 mark for the conclusion that the opinion may be modified; 1 mark for saying the directors should correct the errors first. Maximum 4.
Q52Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Iqbal & Partners is the external auditor of Rahim Steel Limited. The firm also prepares the company's tax returns and has offered to design its new payroll system. Fees from Rahim Steel are 38% of the firm's total fees. The audit engagement partner's brother is the company's finance director.

  1. (a) Identify two threats to the auditor's independence from these facts, naming each threat and explaining it. [4]
  2. (b) Evaluate two safeguards the firm could apply, and state whether they would be sufficient in this case. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Self-review threat: the firm would audit tax returns and a payroll system it prepared or designed. Self-interest threat: 38% of the firm's fees depend on one client, so the firm may be reluctant to report problems. Familiarity threat: the partner's family link to the finance director may make the partner too sympathetic. Any two.
    2 marks for each threat: 1 for naming it; 1 for explaining the link to these facts.
  2. (b) Safeguards: a separate audit partner and team with no role in the tax or payroll work; an independent second partner review; the audit committee approves all non-audit work. Evaluation: separation and independent review reduce the self-review and familiarity threats, but the fee-dependence threat remains, and a close family link is hard to safeguard. The firm should probably decline the payroll work and rotate the engagement partner, or the company should appoint a different auditor.
    2 marks for two suitable safeguards; 2 marks for evaluation of their effectiveness with a reasoned conclusion.
Q53Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Q54Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Sanjan Ceramic Works Limited reports profit before tax of $40,000 this year, compared with $1,200,000 last year. Revenue this year is $8,000,000. The auditor is deciding which materiality benchmark to use.

  1. (a) Calculate the materiality threshold using 5% of profit before tax. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the materiality threshold using 0.5% of revenue. [1]
  3. (c) State which benchmark the auditor should prefer, and explain why. [3]
  4. (d) A misstatement of $25,000 has been found. State whether it is material under each benchmark. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2,000
    1 mark: 5% × $40,000 = $2,000.
  2. (b) $40,000
    1 mark: 0.5% × $8,000,000 = $40,000.
  3. (c) Revenue (0.5%, giving $40,000) is more appropriate because profit has fallen sharply and is unusually small. A threshold based on profit would be too low, so the audit would be disproportionately detailed; revenue is a more stable measure of the company's size.
    1 mark for choosing revenue; 1 mark for profit being unusually low or volatile; 1 mark for the consequence (threshold too low, excessive testing).
  4. (d) Under the profit benchmark ($2,000) it is material; under the revenue benchmark ($40,000) it is not material.

Q55Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A fire destroyed 60% of the sales records of Gulberg Hotels Limited for the first six months of the year. The directors have prepared revenue of $3,200,000 for the year from bank statements and surviving records. The auditor cannot verify revenue for those six months but is satisfied with all other items.

  1. (a) Explain the difference between a qualified ('except for') opinion and a disclaimer of opinion. [3]
  2. (b) State which opinion is most appropriate here, and justify your answer with reference to how far the limitation affects the financial statements. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A qualified 'except for' opinion states that the statements are fair except for the effect of a material but not pervasive matter the auditor could not verify. A disclaimer means no opinion is expressed, because the possible effects of the limitation are both material and pervasive.
    1 mark for the qualified opinion (material but not pervasive, 'except for'); 1 mark for the disclaimer (no opinion expressed); 1 mark for the disclaimer requiring pervasive effects.
  2. (b) A qualified 'except for' opinion is most likely: the limitation is material because it covers revenue for six months, but it is confined to revenue and related items, and the other items were verified satisfactorily. A disclaimer would follow only if the limitation affected most of the statements.
    1 mark for the opinion; 1 mark for the limitation being material; 1 mark for it being limited (not pervasive); 1 mark for justifying with the items that were verified.
Q56Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Over three years, the accounts clerk at Pindi Pharma Stores Limited made 52 unauthorised payments to a fictitious supplier, totalling $186,400. The fraud was found by the internal auditor when a genuine supplier's statement did not agree with the ledger.

  1. (a) Calculate the average value of each unauthorised payment, to the nearest dollar. [1]
  2. (b) Identify two weaknesses in internal control that allowed the fraud. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the external auditor's sample may not have detected these payments, and what the internal auditor did that was more effective. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $3,585
    1 mark: $186,400 ÷ 52 = $3,584.62, so $3,585 to the nearest dollar.
  2. (b) Any two: the same clerk could create supplier records and authorise payments (no segregation of duties); no independent approval of payments to new or unfamiliar suppliers; the supplier master file was not reviewed; supplier statements were not reconciled to the ledger.
    1 mark for each valid weakness.
  3. (c) External audit uses a sample, and 52 small payments spread over three years could easily fall outside it. The fictitious supplier's documents looked genuine, so vouching a sample would not reveal the problem. The internal auditor reconciled a supplier statement, which is evidence from outside the company, to the ledger; this independent comparison exposed the fraud.
    1 mark for the sampling point; 1 mark for payments being small and documents looking genuine; 1 mark for the internal auditor using independent evidence; 1 mark for explaining the reconciliation as an independent check.
Q57Auditing and stewardship · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

On 30 December, the finance director of Hyderabad Textile Mills Limited instructs the accounts team to record $350,000 of sales for goods that have not yet been despatched. The finance director says the goods will be despatched in January and the sales are 'virtually certain'.

  1. (a) Explain why this instruction is an example of management override of controls, and why auditors treat such a risk as significant. [4]
  2. (b) State two audit procedures the external auditor should perform on this revenue. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how an audit committee could reduce the risk of this type of misstatement. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Management override is a senior manager using their authority to bypass normal controls to misstate results; here revenue is overstated by recording sales before they occur (a cut-off error). Auditors treat it as significant because management can override controls, the intention is to misstate results, and the entries can look ordinary, so they are hard to detect.
    1 mark for defining override; 1 mark for a senior manager bypassing normal controls; 1 mark for revenue being overstated (cut-off); 1 mark for explaining why the risk is significant and hard to detect.
  2. (b) Any two: cut-off testing, examining despatch documents and invoices dated before and after 31 December; review of journal entries made near the year end and the authority for them; direct confirmation of the sale with the customer; review of post year-end returns or credit notes.
    1 mark for each valid procedure.
  3. (c) The audit committee can require management to justify significant year-end adjustments and can meet the external auditor privately to discuss management's judgements and the cut-off findings.
    1 mark for a stated action; 1 mark for explaining how it reduces the risk.
