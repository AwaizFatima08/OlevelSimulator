Q1Income and expenditure account · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The new treasurer of Al-Huda Community Sports Club, Quetta, has prepared only a receipts and payments account. Members have asked for an income and expenditure account instead.

  1. (a) Explain three differences between a receipts and payments account and an income and expenditure account. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) R&P is a summary of the cash book; I&E follows the accruals basis. R&P includes capital items such as buying equipment; I&E has revenue items only. R&P ends with a cash/bank balance (an asset); I&E ends with a surplus or deficit. I&E includes non-cash items such as depreciation.
    For each of three differences: 1 mark identifying, 1 mark developing. Accept: cash v accruals basis; capital and revenue items v revenue only; closing balance v surplus/deficit; no depreciation/accruals in R&P; R&P covers all years' amounts received (e.g. arrears) v I&E for the current year only.

Q10Income and expenditure account · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Bluebell Gymnastics Club, Leeds, bought sprung floor equipment on 1 January 2023 for $18,000. The club depreciates its equipment at 25% a year on the reducing-balance method. Its year end is 31 December.

  1. (a) Calculate the carrying amount of the equipment at 31 December 2025, correct to the nearest dollar. [2]
  2. (b) The treasurer proposes instead to depreciate the same cost on the straight-line method over 6 years with no residual value. Calculate the depreciation charge for 2025 under this method. [1]
  3. (c) Give one reason why reducing-balance depreciation may suit gymnastics equipment better than straight-line. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $7,594
    Carrying amount = 18,000 × 0.75³ = 7,593.75, so 7,594. 1 mark for three years of 25% reduction on the reducing balance; 1 for the answer.
  2. (b) $3,000
    18,000 ÷ 6 = 3,000 per year.
  3. (c) Any one reason with a reason: the equipment loses more value in its early years (heavier use when new), so the charge falls as the carrying amount falls and matches the pattern of use.
    1 mark for stating the point; 1 mark for explaining why it fits the pattern of wear and use.
Q11Income and expenditure account · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Harmony Choir Society, Karachi, received a donation of Rs 250,000 on 1 March 2025 from a supporter. The donation can be used only to buy a music library. The society's year ends on 31 December. The library costs Rs 310,000 in total.

  1. (a) State whether the donation should be shown as income in the income and expenditure account for 2025. Explain how it should be shown instead. [3]
  2. (b) The society spent Rs 310,000 on the library in June 2025, using the restricted donation first. Calculate the amount paid from general funds. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the library should be depreciated once it is in use, and how this affects the income and expenditure account. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It should not be shown as general income in the income and expenditure account. It is a restricted donation for a specific purpose, so it is shown separately in the statement of financial position as a restricted or designated fund within the accumulated fund, and it is not part of the surplus for the year.
    1 mark for saying it is not general income; 1 mark for identifying a separate restricted fund within the accumulated fund; 1 mark for noting it does not change the surplus for the year.
  2. (b) Rs 60,000
    310,000 − 250,000 donation = 60,000 from general funds.
  3. (c) The library is a non-current asset used over several years, so its cost is spread over its useful life (matching). The depreciation charge is an expense in the income and expenditure account each year, which reduces the surplus.
    1 mark for matching cost to the years of use; 1 mark for the depreciation expense reducing the surplus in the I&E account.
