Q1Control accounts · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which item is recorded on the credit side of a sales ledger control account?A credit sales for the monthB a customer's cheque that has been dishonouredC an irrecoverable debt written offD interest charged to a customer on an overdue account [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C an irrecoverable debt written off
    Irrecoverable debts reduce the amount owed by customers, so they are credited. A dishonoured cheque (B) is debited because the customer owes the money again.

Q3Control accounts · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain two benefits to a business of keeping a sales ledger control account. [4]
  2. (b) State why agreement between the control account and the list of sales ledger balances does not prove that the sales ledger is free from errors. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two explained: it gives a quick total of trade receivables for the statement of financial position; it helps to locate errors to the sales ledger; it acts as a check on the work of the sales ledger clerk / helps detect fraud; it allows a trial balance to be drawn up without listing every customer.
    1 mark for each benefit identified, 1 mark for each development (2 × 2).
  2. (b) Some errors, such as posting an invoice to the wrong customer's account (error of commission) or omitting a transaction completely, affect neither total.
    1 mark for a valid reason with an example.

Q14Bank reconciliation · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Farah runs a dressmaking business in Multan. She records all receipts and payments in a cash book.

  1. (a) Explain two reasons why the balance on the bank statement may differ from the bank balance in the cash book. [4]
  2. (b) State two benefits of preparing a bank reconciliation statement regularly. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two explained: timing differences such as unpresented cheques (entered in the cash book but not yet paid by the bank) or lodgements not yet credited; items the bank has entered that the business does not yet know about, such as bank charges, interest, standing orders, direct debits, credit transfers or dishonoured cheques; errors in the cash book or by the bank.
    1 mark for each reason, 1 mark for each explanation (2 × 2).
  2. (b) Any two: errors in the cash book or by the bank are found quickly; the cash book is brought up to date; fraud is more likely to be detected; stale (old unpresented) cheques can be identified; it confirms the bank balance shown in the statement of financial position.
    1 mark each.

Q19Bank reconciliation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

On 31 January 2026 the bank statement of Tamarind Catering, Chennai, showed an overdraft of $2,380. The following items explain the difference from the cash book.1 Cheques of $4,120 paid to suppliers had not been presented.2 A lodgement of $3,560 had not been credited by the bank.3 Bank charges of $140 had not been entered in the cash book.4 A credit transfer of $920 from a customer had not been entered in the cash book.5 A cheque payment of $715 had been recorded in the cash book as $175.

  1. (a) Calculate the updated (correct) cash book balance at 31 January 2026. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the cash book balance before the corrections were made. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why an overdraft is shown as a debit balance on the bank statement but as a credit balance in the cash book. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Overdraft $2,940
    Work back from the bank statement: −2,380 − 4,120 + 3,560 = (2,940). M1 unpresented cheques deducted; M1 lodgement added; A1 overdraft 2,940.
  2. (b) Overdraft $3,180
    Updated = original − 140 + 920 − 540, so original = −2,940 + 140 − 920 + 540 = (3,180). M1 reversing the three items; M1 the error of $540 (715 − 175) treated correctly; A1 overdraft 3,180.
  3. (c) The bank statement is prepared from the bank's point of view: the customer owes the bank, so the customer is a debtor (debit). In the business's cash book the overdraft is money owed to the bank, a liability (credit).

Q30Purpose and limitations of verification · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Ayesha has just started work as an accounts assistant at a pharmacy chain in Islamabad. She believes that because her trial balance agrees, her ledger accounts must be correct.

  1. (a) State two purposes of preparing a trial balance. [2]
  2. (b) Explain three types of error that a trial balance will not reveal. Give an example of each. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: to check the arithmetical accuracy of double entry (debits = credits); to provide a summary of balances from which the financial statements are prepared; to help locate errors.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any three with an example: omission (a transaction not recorded at all); commission (posted to the wrong account of the same type); principle (posted to the wrong class of account, e.g. capital expenditure as revenue); original entry (wrong amount in the book of prime entry); complete reversal; compensating errors.
    1 mark per error type with a correct example.
