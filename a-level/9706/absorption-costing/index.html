  1. (a) A product uses direct materials of $14 and 2.5 direct labour hours at $12 per hour. Production overheads are absorbed at $8 per direct labour hour. The selling price is full production cost plus a mark-up of 25%. What is the selling price?A $85.33B $80.00C $64.00D $55.00 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B $80.00
    Full cost = 14 + 30 + 20 = 64; price = 64 × 1.25 = 80.00. A applies 25% as a margin on selling price (64 ÷ 0.75); C is full cost with no mark-up; D applies the mark-up to prime cost only (44 × 1.25).

Q10Overhead allocation and apportionment · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between allocation and apportionment of overheads. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest a suitable basis of apportionment for each of the following overheads.(i) Factory rent(ii) Depreciation of machinery(iii) Canteen costs(iv) Insurance of buildings [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Allocation charges a whole overhead to one cost centre because it was incurred wholly by that centre (e.g. a supervisor who works only in assembly). Apportionment shares an overhead between several cost centres on a fair basis because it is shared (e.g. factory rent).
    1 mark allocation – whole cost to one centre; 1 mark apportionment – shared cost split on a suitable basis.
  2. (b) (i) Floor area (ii) Cost or carrying amount of machinery (iii) Number of employees (iv) Floor area or value of buildings occupied
    1 mark each.

Q27Job, batch and unit costing · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A bakery in Karachi makes cupcakes for a café chain in batches of 1,200. On average 4% of the cupcakes in each batch are spoilt and have no value.

  1. (a) Calculate the total cost of one batch. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the cost per good cupcake. Give your answer to four decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) The bakery adds a mark-up of 30% to cost. Calculate the price per cupcake to the nearest cent. [1]
  4. (d) The café chain orders 3,000 good cupcakes. Calculate how many batches must be made. [2]

Q28Purpose and features of absorption costing · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by absorption costing. [2]
  2. (b) Give two reasons why a business uses absorption costing. [2]
  3. (c) State two differences between absorption costing and marginal costing. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A costing method in which all production costs – direct costs plus a share of fixed and variable production overheads – are charged to each unit, using overhead absorption rates.
    1 mark: all production costs including fixed overheads; 1 mark: charged to units / cost units via absorption rates.
  2. (b) Any two: IAS 2 requires inventory in the financial statements to include production overheads; it helps set prices that cover all costs; it recognises that fixed overheads are needed to produce output; it shows the full cost of jobs for quotations.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) Any two: absorption costing includes fixed production overheads in unit cost, marginal costing charges them as period costs; inventory is valued higher under absorption costing; profits differ when inventory levels change; marginal costing shows contribution, absorption costing does not.
    1 mark each.
Q29Purpose and features of absorption costing · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

In its first year Kestrel Ltd made 10,000 units and sold 8,500 units at $40 each. Variable production cost was $18 per unit. Fixed production overheads were $80,000, absorbed on the normal output of 10,000 units. Fixed selling and administration costs were $45,000. There was no opening inventory.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit using absorption costing. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit using marginal costing. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the two profits are different. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $74,000
    B1 full cost 26 per unit; B1 cost of sales 221,000; B1 profit 74,000.
  2. (b) $62,000
    B1 contribution 187,000; B1 profit 62,000.
  3. (c) Under absorption costing 1,500 units of closing inventory carry $8 each of fixed overhead ($12,000) forward to next year; under marginal costing all $80,000 is charged this year. So absorption profit is $12,000 higher.
    1 mark: fixed overhead in closing inventory; 1 mark: 1,500 × 8 = 12,000.
Q30Purpose and features of absorption costing · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Zephyr Ltd sells one product at $55. Variable production cost is $25 per unit. Fixed production overheads are $60,000 each period, absorbed on normal output of 6,000 units. Fixed non-production costs are $20,000 each period. Period 1 Period 2Production (units) 6,000 5,000Sales (units) 5,000 6,000There was no inventory at the start of Period 1.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit for each period using absorption costing. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for each period using marginal costing. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why total profit over the two periods is the same under both methods. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Period 1 $80,000; Period 2 $90,000
    B1 full cost 35 per unit; B1 Period 1 profit 80,000; B1 Period 2 under-absorption 10,000 (5,000 × 10 absorbed against 60,000); B1 Period 2 profit 90,000.
  2. (b) Period 1 $70,000; Period 2 $100,000
    B1 each.
  3. (c) Both total $170,000. Inventory is zero at the start and end of the two periods, so all fixed production overhead has been charged under both methods; absorption costing only moved $10,000 of overhead from Period 1 to Period 2 through the inventory built up and then sold.
    1 mark: same total 170,000; 1 mark: no opening/closing inventory over the two periods so timing difference reverses.
