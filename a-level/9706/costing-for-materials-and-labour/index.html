Q1Cost classification and behaviour · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which cost is a direct cost for a business that makes wooden tables?A timber used to make the tablesB rent of the factoryC salary of the factory supervisorD depreciation of the cutting machines [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A timber used to make the tables
    Timber can be traced directly to each table. Factory rent, supervision and depreciation (B, C, D) are production overheads: they are incurred for the factory as a whole and cannot be traced to a single table.
Q2Cost classification and behaviour · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Mehran Bakery, Lahore, had the following costs in June 2026: flour $18,400; packaging for each loaf $2,300; wages of bakers $26,500; depreciation of ovens $1,900; bakery rent $3,200; wages of the delivery van driver $2,750.

  1. (a) Classify each of the following costs as direct materials, direct labour, production overhead or non-production overhead.(i) flour(ii) wages of bakers(iii) depreciation of ovens(iv) wages of the delivery van driver [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the prime cost for the month. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) direct materials (ii) direct labour (iii) production overhead (iv) non-production (distribution) overhead
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) $47,200
    Prime cost = direct materials + direct labour = 18,400 + 2,300 + 26,500 = 47,200. M1 (packaging included as a direct material, rent excluded) A1.
Q3Cost classification and behaviour · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Shoaib Shoes, Sialkot, values its inventory of finished shoes at production cost.

  1. (a) Explain the difference between product costs and period costs. Give one example of each for a shoe manufacturer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Product costs are the costs of making the goods (direct materials, direct labour, production overheads); they are included in the value of inventory and charged as cost of sales when the goods are sold, e.g. leather. Period costs are not part of production (administration, selling and distribution); they are charged in full against profit in the period in which they are incurred, e.g. sales staff salaries.
    1 mark each definition, 1 mark each valid example.
Q4Cost classification and behaviour · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A semi-variable cost is $13,000 when 2,000 units are made and $22,000 when 5,000 units are made. What will the cost be when 6,500 units are made?A $19,500B $26,500C $28,600D $42,250 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B $26,500
    Variable cost per unit = 9,000 ÷ 3,000 = $3; fixed element = 13,000 − 2,000 × 3 = 7,000. At 6,500 units: 7,000 + 6,500 × 3 = 26,500. C treats the whole cost as variable (22,000 ÷ 5,000 × 6,500).
Q5Cost classification and behaviour · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) Use the high–low method to calculate the variable cost per unit. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the fixed cost per month. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the expected total cost for a month in which 7,000 units are made. [2]
  4. (d) State one limitation of the high–low method. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $4.40
    (40,220 − 28,780) ÷ (6,100 − 3,500) = 11,440 ÷ 2,600 = $4.40. M1 using the highest and lowest output (not the highest and lowest cost) A1.

  3. (c) $44,180

Q7Cost classification and behaviour · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A food processing company in Nairobi records three costs at two levels of output.Cost 1,000 units $ 3,000 units $X 6,000 6,000Y 4,500 13,500Z 5,000 9,000

  1. (a) Classify each cost X, Y and Z by its behaviour. Give a reason for each. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the total of the three costs at an output of 2,400 units. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) X is fixed (total unchanged when output changes); Y is variable (total rises in proportion: $4.50 per unit at both outputs); Z is semi-variable (rises with output but not in proportion: it has a fixed element).

Q8Cost classification and behaviour · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Taj Ceramics, Gujrat, makes tiles. Variable cost is $7.50 per unit. General fixed costs are $18,000 per month. One quality supervisor, paid $3,600 per month, is needed for every 2,500 units (or part of 2,500 units) produced each month.

  1. (a) Calculate the total monthly cost at outputs of 4,000, 5,000 and 6,000 units. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the cost per unit at each output. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the cost per unit falls by much less between 5,000 and 6,000 units than between 4,000 and 5,000 units. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4,000 units $55,200; 5,000 units $62,700; 6,000 units $73,800
    Supervisors needed: 2, 2 and 3. 1 mark each total.
  2. (b) $13.80; $12.54; $12.30
    2 marks all correct, 1 mark for two correct.

Q9Cost classification and behaviour · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Bengal Shirts, Dhaka, recorded total overheads of $186,000 in a month with 12,000 machine hours and $221,000 in a month with 15,500 machine hours. A new customer offers to buy 2,000 shirts at $11 each. Each shirt needs direct materials $3.20, direct labour $2.40 and 0.5 machine hours. The factory has spare capacity.

  1. (a) Calculate the variable overhead per machine hour and the fixed overhead per month. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the total variable cost of the special order. [2]
  3. (c) Advise the company whether it should accept the order. Justify your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Variable $10 per machine hour; fixed $66,000 per month
    (221,000 − 186,000) ÷ (15,500 − 12,000) = 10; 186,000 − 12,000 × 10 = 66,000. M1 A1 A1.
  2. (b) $21,200
    Materials and labour 2,000 × 5.60 = 11,200; variable overhead 1,000 hours × 10 = 10,000; total 21,200. M1 A1.
  3. (c) Accept, subject to the points below. Revenue 22,000 − variable cost 21,200 = contribution $800; fixed overhead does not change, so profit rises by $800. However the margin is very small; check there is spare machine capacity, that regular customers will not demand the same low price, and whether the order may lead to future work.
    1 mark contribution calculated; 1 mark relevant non-financial point(s); 1 mark clear decision consistent with the analysis.
Q10Inventory valuation (FIFO and AVCO) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Prices of raw materials have risen steadily during the year. Compared with AVCO, what is the effect of using FIFO?A higher closing inventory and higher gross profitB higher closing inventory and lower gross profitC lower closing inventory and higher gross profitD lower closing inventory and lower gross profit [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A higher closing inventory and higher gross profit
    Under FIFO the oldest (cheapest) units are issued first, so closing inventory is valued at the latest, higher prices. Cost of sales is lower, so gross profit is higher. B forgets that a higher closing inventory reduces cost of sales.

Q12Inventory valuation (FIFO and AVCO) · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Rania is choosing a method of valuing the inventory of spare parts for her motorcycle repair business in Amman.

  1. (a) State two advantages of the FIFO method of inventory valuation. [2]
  2. (b) State one advantage of the AVCO method. [1]
  3. (c) State why a company preparing financial statements under international standards may not use LIFO. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: follows the usual physical flow of goods; closing inventory is valued at recent prices; easy to understand; no averages need to be recalculated; acceptable under IAS 2.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any one: it smooths out price changes so that issue prices and profits fluctuate less; all units of the same item are given the same value.

    1 mark.

Q15Inventory valuation (FIFO and AVCO) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

SunPower Traders, Bahawalpur, sells solar panels.1 Apr opening inventory 20 panels at $1505 Apr bought 40 panels at $16014 Apr sold 45 panels at $240 each22 Apr bought 30 panels at $17029 Apr sold 35 panels at $250 each

  1. (a) Calculate the value of closing inventory on 30 April using FIFO. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the value of closing inventory on 30 April using AVCO (perpetual). Give your answer to the nearest cent. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the gross profit for April under each method. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $1,700
    10 panels remain, all from the 22 April purchase: 10 × 170 = 1,700. M1 A1.

Q16Inventory valuation (FIFO and AVCO) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Falcon Pharma Distributors uses AVCO. Its profits and closing inventory values were: Year 1 $ Year 2 $Profit for the year (AVCO) 71,200 84,000Closing inventory: AVCO 45,900 50,100Closing inventory: FIFO 48,600 52,300Opening inventory for year 1 was the same under both methods.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit for year 1 if FIFO had been used. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for year 2 if FIFO had been used. [3]
  3. (c) The directors receive a bonus based on profit and want to change from AVCO to FIFO. Advise them whether they should make this change. Justify your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $73,900
    Higher closing inventory raises profit: 71,200 + (48,600 − 45,900) = 73,900. M1 A1.
  2. (b) $83,500
    Opening inventory is also higher under FIFO, which reduces profit: 84,000 + (52,300 − 50,100) − (48,600 − 45,900) = 83,500. M1 closing adjustment; M1 opening adjustment; A1.
  3. (c) Points for: FIFO is allowed by IAS 2; inventory in the statement of financial position is closer to current prices; profit would have been higher in year 1.Points against: the consistency concept means a method should be changed only if the new one gives a fairer view, and the change must be disclosed; over time total profit is the same (year 2 would be lower); changing to raise bonuses is unethical and misleading to users.Decision: do not change merely to increase the bonus.
    1 mark points for/against using figures; 1 mark consistency/IAS 2; 1 mark justified decision.

  3. (c) State one reason for paying a guaranteed minimum wage. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Asif $442.00; Bilal $360.00; Chen $395.25
    Asif 520 × 0.85 = 442.00; Bilal 390 × 0.85 = 331.50, below the minimum so paid 360.00; Chen 465 × 0.85 = 395.25. 1 mark each.
  2. (b) $1,197.25; $0.87 per unit
    Total 1,197.25 ÷ 1,375 units = 0.87. 1 mark each.
  3. (c) Gives workers security of income when output is low for reasons outside their control (machine breakdown, shortage of materials); may be required by law.
    1 mark.
Q29Labour remuneration · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Twelve production workers at Margalla Furniture each worked 45 hours last week. The basic week is 40 hours at $10 per hour, and overtime is paid at time-and-a-half. The overtime was needed because of a general increase in orders, not for any particular job.

  1. (a) Calculate the total gross wages for the week. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate how much of the gross wages should be treated as direct labour and how much as production overhead. [2]
  3. (c) Explain when the overtime premium would be treated as a direct cost. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $5,700
    12 × (40 × 10 + 5 × 15) = 12 × 475. M1 A1.
  2. (b) Direct labour $5,400; production overhead $300
    All hours at basic rate are direct: 12 × 45 × $10 = 5,400. The overtime premium 12 × 5 × $5 = 300 is overhead because the overtime was due to the general level of work. 1 mark each.
  3. (c) When the overtime is worked at the specific request of a customer or for a particular job, the premium can be traced to that job and is charged to it as direct labour.
    1 mark specific job/customer; 1 mark charged directly to that job.
Q30Labour remuneration · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Workers at Rose Valley Packaging, Swat, are paid $14 per hour plus a bonus of 50% of the time saved, valued at the hourly rate. The standard time is 0.4 hours per unit. In one week both Farida and Gul worked 38 hours. Farida produced 110 units and Gul produced 92 units.

  1. (a) Calculate the gross pay of each worker for the week. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the labour cost per unit for each worker. Give your answers to the nearest cent. [2]
  3. (c) Discuss whether the bonus scheme benefits the company. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Farida $574; Gul $532
    Farida: standard time 110 × 0.4 = 44 hours, time saved 6 hours, bonus 50% × 6 × 14 = 42; pay 532 + 42 = 574. Gul: standard time 92 × 0.4 = 36.8 hours, no time saved, pay 38 × 14 = 532. 2 marks each.
  2. (b) Farida $5.22; Gul $5.78
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) Benefit: the faster worker's cost per unit is lower even after the bonus, because time saved is shared 50:50. Drawback: workers may rush and quality may fall; slow workers still receive full time-rate pay, so there is no penalty.
    1 mark each point.
