Q3Cost classification and behaviour · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Shoaib Shoes, Sialkot, values its inventory of finished shoes at production cost.

  1. (a) Explain the difference between product costs and period costs. Give one example of each for a shoe manufacturer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Product costs are the costs of making the goods (direct materials, direct labour, production overheads); they are included in the value of inventory and charged as cost of sales when the goods are sold, e.g. leather. Period costs are not part of production (administration, selling and distribution); they are charged in full against profit in the period in which they are incurred, e.g. sales staff salaries.
    1 mark each definition, 1 mark each valid example.

Q13Cost classification and behaviour · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Bengal Shirts, Dhaka, recorded total overheads of $186,000 in a month with 12,000 machine hours and $221,000 in a month with 15,500 machine hours. A new customer offers to buy 2,000 shirts at $11 each. Each shirt needs direct materials $3.20, direct labour $2.40 and 0.5 machine hours. The factory has spare capacity.

  3. (c) Advise the company whether it should accept the order. Justify your answer. [3]

Q16Inventory valuation (FIFO and AVCO) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Prices of raw materials have risen steadily during the year. Compared with AVCO, what is the effect of using FIFO? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A higher closing inventory and higher gross profit

Q24Inventory valuation (FIFO and AVCO) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Purchase prices have fallen steadily throughout the year. Which statement is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B AVCO gives a higher closing inventory value than FIFO

  2. (b) $19,960
    Output 13,800 B1; 13,800 × (9 − 3 − 1.80) = 57,960 M1; − 38,000 = $19,960 A1
  3. (c) Time rate $1.67; piece rate $1.80
    20,000 ÷ 12,000 B1; 1.80 B1
  4. (d) Profit rises by $3,960 a week if output really rises 15%. Against: labour cost per unit rises; quality risk; output forecast uncertain; extra inspection; workers may resist or the union object; slower workers earn less. For: motivation, higher output, more contribution. Decision.
    1 mark using the comparison; up to 2 marks for, up to 2 against; 1 mark justified decision (max 5)
Q59Labour remuneration · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A machine operator at Indus Steel Fabricators is paid $15 per hour for a 40-hour week and is paid for 52 weeks a year, including 6 weeks of paid holiday. The employer also pays social security contributions of 12% of gross pay. Of the hours the operator attends work, 8% are idle time (machine set-up and waiting for materials).

  1. (a) Calculate the total annual cost to the employer of the operator. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of productive hours a year. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the cost per productive hour, to the nearest cent. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why this rate, rather than $15 per hour, should be used when costing jobs. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $34,944
    31,200 × 1.12 M1 A1
  2. (b) 1,692.8 hours
    40 × 46 = 1,840 attended M1; × 92% A1
  3. (c) $20.64
    34,944 ÷ 1,692.8 M1 A1
  4. (d) Jobs must bear all labour costs, including holiday pay, employer's contributions and unavoidable idle time; using $15 would understate job costs and prices may not cover costs.
    1 mark all costs recovered; 1 mark consequence of under-costing
Q60Labour remuneration · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Workers at a garment factory in Korangi are paid $0.80 for each garment, plus $9 per hour for idle time caused by machine breakdowns. Each worker is guaranteed at least $420 per week in total. In one week a breakdown caused 6 hours of idle time for each of three workers. Output that week: Kiran 420 garments, Lubna 600 garments, Maryam 560 garments.

  1. (a) Calculate the gross pay of each worker. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate how much of the total wages is direct labour and how much is a production overhead. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the guarantee top-up is not charged as direct labour. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kiran $420; Lubna $534; Maryam $502
    Kiran 336 + 54 = 390, raised to guarantee 420 B1; Lubna 480 + 54 B1; Maryam 448 + 54 B1
  2. (b) Direct labour $1,264; overhead $192
    Piece earnings 336 + 480 + 448 = 1,264 direct B1; idle time 3 × 54 = 162 B1; guarantee top-up 30, both overhead, total 192 B1
  3. (c) It is not caused by producing particular garments; it relates to low output in general, so it is an indirect cost spread over all production through the overhead rate.
    1 mark not traceable to units; 1 mark overhead
