Q1Factory profit and unrealised profit · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Jade River Toys transfers completed goods from its factory to its warehouse at production cost plus 20%. The production cost of goods completed for the year was $300,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the transfer price of goods completed and the factory profit. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why a manufacturer may transfer goods to its trading account at a price above production cost. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Transfer price $360,000; factory profit $60,000
    20% × 300,000 = 60,000 factory profit (M1 A1); 300,000 + 60,000 = $360,000 (A1).
  2. (b) To compare the factory's cost with the price of buying the goods from outside, so the factory's efficiency (and the trading section's) can be judged separately; it can also be used to reward factory managers.

Q3Factory profit and unrealised profit · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Orchid Soaps, Ipoh, transfers its products from its factory to its shop at production cost plus a mark-up.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by 'unrealised profit' in a manufacturing business. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a provision for unrealised profit is needed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The factory profit added to goods that are still in finished goods inventory at the year end; it has not been earned because the goods have not been sold to customers.
    1 mark factory profit/mark-up in closing inventory; 1 mark not yet sold outside the business.
  2. (b) Without it, inventory would be shown above cost and profit would include profit the business has made from 'selling to itself', so profit and assets would be overstated.
    1 mark inventory must be at cost (not above); 1 mark profit/assets would be overstated (realisation/prudence).
Q4Factory profit and unrealised profit · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Show answer

    1 mark each.
