Q6Variance calculation and reconciliation · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A detergent maker in Dhaka uses a standard of 3 kg of powder base per carton at a standard price of $2.20 per kg. It calculates the material price variance when material is purchased. In September it bought 12,000 kg of powder base for $25,800 and used 11,200 kg to make 3,700 cartons.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual price paid per kg. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the material price variance, stating whether it is favourable or adverse. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the material usage variance, stating whether it is favourable or adverse. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the price variance is based on the 12,000 kg bought, not the 11,200 kg used. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $2.15
    25,800 ÷ 12,000 = 2.15 (B1).
  2. (b) $600 favourable
    12,000 × (2.20 − 2.15) (M1) = 600 F (A1).
  3. (c) $220 adverse
    Standard quantity 3,700 × 3 = 11,100 kg (M1); (11,100 − 11,200) × 2.20 = 220 A (A1).
  4. (d) The variance is identified when the purchase is made, so the buyer is held responsible at once and inventory can be held at standard price.
    B1.

Q56Reasons for variances and decisions · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 9 marks

A kitchenware factory in Gujrat received a rush order for 800 cooking pots at $70 each. Standard variable cost per pot: materials $20; labour 2 hours × $12 = $24; variable overhead $4. To finish on time all 1,600 labour hours had to be worked as overtime, paid at time-and-a-half. All other costs were at standard. The production manager is now being criticised for a large adverse labour rate variance.

  1. (a) Calculate the labour rate variance caused by the order. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the standard contribution from the order and the actual contribution after the overtime premium. [3]
  3. (c) Advise the directors whether the production manager should be blamed for the variance. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) $9,600 adverse
    Premium 1,600 × 6 (M1) = 9,600 A (A1).
  2. (b) Standard contribution $17,600; actual contribution $8,000
    Contribution per pot 70 − 48 = 22 (B1); × 800 = 17,600 (B1); 17,600 − 9,600 = 8,000 (B1).
  3. (c) The order still added $8,000 of contribution, so accepting it was profitable; the overtime was a planned consequence of the sales decision, not inefficiency. The variance should be reported separately or charged to the sales department that accepted the deadline. However, if overtime could have been avoided by better scheduling, part of the cost is controllable. Possible goodwill with the customer. Decision: do not blame the production manager.

  1. (a) Calculate the adverse variances caused by the breakdowns, naming each variance. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the net effect on profit of the decision to cut maintenance. [1]
  3. (c) Advise the managers whether they should keep the reduced maintenance budget. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Labour efficiency $6,300 A; variable overhead efficiency $2,100 A; material usage $2,600 A
    B1 each.
  2. (b) $5,000 worse (adverse)

  1. (a) Sales price $43,200 adverse; sales volume $36,000 favourable

Q60Reasons for variances and decisions · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A tile factory in Gujranwala is choosing between two labour standards for laying a batch of tiles: an ideal standard of 2.0 hours per batch (no breaks, no machine stoppages) and an attainable standard of 2.2 hours per batch. Last month 10,000 batches took 22,500 hours, paid at the standard rate of $15 per hour.

  1. (a) Calculate the labour efficiency variance using each standard. [2]
  2. (b) Advise the factory which standard it should use. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ideal $37,500 adverse; attainable $7,500 adverse
    (20,000 − 22,500) × 15 = 37,500 A (B1); (22,000 − 22,500) × 15 = 7,500 A (B1).
  2. (b) Attainable: a variance of $7,500 A (about 2.3%) shows workers were close to a realistic target, so it is useful for control and motivation. Ideal: permanent large adverse variances ($37,500) may demotivate staff and make it hard to see real problems; but it shows the maximum possible improvement. Decision: attainable standard, perhaps reviewing it regularly towards the ideal.
    1 mark each standard with figure; 1 mark motivation; 1 mark decision.
