Q3Corrections and draft profit adjustments · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

The draft statement of financial position of Bilal Biotech plc at 31 December 2025 shows intangible assets of $420,000, made up of:• an internally generated brand, valued at $150,000; the gain was credited to the statement of profit or loss• research costs of $70,000, debited to intangible assets• development costs of $200,000, correctly capitalised. Commercial production began on 1 October 2025, but no amortisation has been charged. The policy is straight-line amortisation over 4 years with time apportionment.The draft profit for the year is $560,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the corrected profit for the year ended 31 December 2025. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the corrected carrying amount of intangible assets at 31 December 2025. [2]
  3. (c) Explain the effect of the errors on users' view of the company. [2]

  1. (a) $327,500
    560,000 − 150,000 brand (B1) − 70,000 research (B1) − amortisation 200,000 ÷ 4 × 3/12 = 12,500 (B1) = 327,500 (A1).

Q90Research and development and intangibles · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Asman Medical Devices plc plans to issue new shares next year. It spent $1.2 million on research and development this year: $500,000 on research into new materials and $700,000 on developing a heart monitor, of which $450,000 meets all IAS 38 criteria. The directors propose to capitalise the whole $1.2 million 'to show investors how much we invest in the future'.

  1. (a) Discuss the directors' proposal. Advise them whether they should capitalise all the research and development spending. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) IAS 38 does not allow research to be capitalised under any circumstances, and development only when all criteria (technical feasibility, intention and ability to complete and use/sell, probable economic benefits, adequate resources, reliable measurement) are met. Capitalising everything would overstate assets and profit, break prudence and faithful representation, and could mislead investors in the share issue; auditors would qualify their report, damaging credibility and possibly causing legal problems. Capitalising qualifying development costs is required (not optional) and does raise profit legitimately. Advice: capitalise only the development spending that meets the criteria, expense the rest, and explain the R&D programme in the narrative report to attract investors.
    Level 3 (5–6): applies IAS 38 rules to both research and development, discusses effect on investors/auditors, justified advice. Level 2 (3–4): correct rules with limited discussion. Level 1 (1–2): simple statements.
