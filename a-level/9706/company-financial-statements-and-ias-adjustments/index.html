Q1Corrections and draft profit adjustments · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The draft profit for the year ended 31 December 2025 of Ravi Valley Cement plc was $286,400. The following matters have not been dealt with correctly.1 Inventory with a cost of $24,000 has a net realisable value of $17,500.2 Machinery bought on 1 January 2025 for $60,000 has not been depreciated. The policy is 10% per annum straight-line.3 The company has $200,000 6% debentures. Interest for the second half of the year has not been paid or accrued.4 An interim dividend of $15,000 was debited to administrative expenses.

  1. (a) Prepare a statement showing the corrected profit for the year ended 31 December 2025. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why an interim dividend paid must not be included in the statement of profit or loss. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Draft profit for the year 286,400Less inventory written down to NRV (24,000 − 17,500) (6,500)Less depreciation on new machinery (10% × 60,000) (6,000)Less debenture interest accrued (200,000 × 6% × 6/12) (6,000)Add interim dividend wrongly charged as an expense 15,000Corrected profit for the year 282,900
    1 mark each adjustment, total own figure.

    1 mark: appropriation/distribution to shareholders; 1 mark: shown in the SOCE / retained earnings.

Q3Corrections and draft profit adjustments · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Kohat Builders plc issued $250,000 9% debentures on 1 July 2025. It also had a 7% bank loan of $80,000 outstanding for the whole of the year ended 31 December 2025.

  1. (a) Explain why debenture interest is charged in the statement of profit or loss but ordinary dividends are not. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the finance costs for the year ended 31 December 2025. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Debenture holders are lenders: interest is a contractual cost of borrowing that must be paid whether or not profits are made, so it is an expense. Dividends are a distribution of profit to the owners and are shown in the statement of changes in equity.
    1 mark each point.
  2. (b) $16,850
    Debentures 250,000 × 9% × 6/12 = 11,250; loan 80,000 × 7% = 5,600; total 16,850. M1 time-apportioning; M1 loan interest; A1.

Hala Ajrak Printers plc bought a machine on 1 January 2022 for $240,000. It was depreciated straight-line over 10 years with a residual value of $20,000. On 1 January 2026 the directors decided that the machine has only 4 more years of useful life and a residual value of $12,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the carrying amount of the machine at 1 January 2026. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the depreciation charge for the year ended 31 December 2026 and the carrying amount at that date. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the change is not applied to the depreciation charged in earlier years. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $152,000
    Annual depreciation (240,000 − 20,000) ÷ 10 = 22,000; 4 years = 88,000; 240,000 − 88,000. M1 A1.
  2. (b) Depreciation $35,000; carrying amount $117,000
    (152,000 − 12,000) ÷ 4 = 35,000. M1 A1.
  3. (c) It is a change in an accounting estimate (IAS 8), not an error or a change of policy. It is applied prospectively, to the current and future years only; past figures were based on the best information available at the time.
    1 mark change in estimate; 1 mark prospective treatment.
Q21Provisions, contingencies and events after the reporting period · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

The directors of Kalash Mountain Resorts plc are preparing the financial statements and are unsure how to deal with a number of uncertain items.

  1. (a) Define the following terms as used in IAS 37: (i) a provision, (ii) a contingent liability. [2]
  2. (b) State the three conditions that must be met before a provision is recognised. [3]
  3. (c) State how a probable contingent asset is treated. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) A liability of uncertain timing or amount. (ii) A possible obligation whose existence depends on uncertain future events, or a present obligation that is not probable or cannot be measured reliably.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) There is a present obligation (legal or constructive) from a past event; an outflow of resources to settle it is probable; a reliable estimate of the amount can be made.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) It is disclosed in a note but not recognised in the statement of financial position.
    1 mark.
Q22Provisions, contingencies and events after the reporting period · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Indus Ferries plc has the following matters at 31 March 2026.1 A passenger is suing the company for injuries suffered on one of its ferries in January 2026. The lawyers say the company will probably lose, and estimate damages of $85,000.2 The company has guaranteed a $200,000 bank loan of a supplier. The supplier has had some cash-flow difficulties; the company's advisers consider a default possible but not probable.3 The company is claiming $40,000 compensation from a shipyard for late delivery of a ferry. The lawyers think the claim will probably succeed, but the shipyard has not agreed.

  1. (a) For each matter, state how it should be treated in the financial statements for the year ended 31 March 2026. Give reasons. [6]
  2. (b) The draft profit before dealing with these matters was $520,000. Calculate the revised profit. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 Recognise a provision of $85,000: present obligation from a past event, outflow probable, reliable estimate. Charge to profit or loss; show as a liability.2 Disclose as a contingent liability in a note only: an outflow is possible, not probable.3 Do not recognise; disclose as a contingent asset in a note because the inflow is probable but not virtually certain.
    1 mark treatment and 1 mark reason for each matter.
  2. (b) $435,000
    Only the provision affects profit.
Q23Provisions, contingencies and events after the reporting period · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

The draft profit of Saddar Electronics plc for the year ended 31 March 2026 is $310,000. The financial statements will be authorised for issue on 30 June 2026. These events occurred after the year end.1 20 April 2026: a customer who owed $18,000 at 31 March 2026 was declared bankrupt; nothing will be received.2 5 May 2026: a fire destroyed inventory worth $60,000 in the warehouse.3 12 May 2026: goods held in inventory at 31 March 2026 at a cost of $25,000 were sold for $19,000, with selling costs of $1,000.4 1 June 2026: the directors proposed a final dividend of $40,000.

  1. (a) Classify each event as adjusting or non-adjusting and state how each should be treated. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the revised profit for the year ended 31 March 2026. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 Adjusting: confirms the customer could not pay at the year end; write off $18,000.2 Non-adjusting: the condition (fire) arose after the year end; disclose in a note if material.3 Adjusting: gives evidence of NRV at the year end; write inventory down to 18,000.4 Non-adjusting: no obligation existed at the year end; disclose in a note, no liability.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) $285,000
    310,000 − 18,000 − (25,000 − 18,000) = 285,000. 1 mark each adjustment, 1 mark answer (non-adjusting events ignored).
Q24Provisions, contingencies and events after the reporting period · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Crescent Appliances plc sold 12,000 washing machines in 2025 with a one-year warranty. Past experience shows that 80% need no repairs, 15% need minor repairs costing $40 each and 5% need major repairs costing $250 each. The warranty provision brought forward from 2024 is $180,000. At 31 December 2025 the provision should equal the expected cost of repairs on the machines sold in 2025. The draft profit is $960,000. In addition:• a former employee is suing the company for $70,000; lawyers believe the claim will possibly, but not probably, succeed• the company's insurer has confirmed in writing that it will pay $15,000 for flood damage costs already charged as an expense in 2025; nothing has been recorded.

  1. (a) Calculate the warranty provision required at 31 December 2025. [3]
  2. (b) State the amount to be charged in the statement of profit or loss for the warranty provision. [1]
  3. (c) Explain how the lawsuit and the insurance claim should be treated. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate the revised profit for the year. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $222,000
    12,000 × (15% × 40 + 5% × 250) = 12,000 × 18.50. M1 expected value; M1 both outcomes; A1.
  2. (b) $42,000 (increase in the provision)
    1 mark.
  3. (c) Lawsuit: an outflow is only possible, so it is disclosed as a contingent liability, not provided for. Insurance: the inflow is virtually certain (written confirmation), so it is no longer contingent and is recognised as a receivable and income of $15,000.
    1 mark each.
  4. (d) $933,000
    960,000 − 42,000 + 15,000 = 933,000. M1 A1.
Q25Dividend decisions · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Neelum Mineral Water plc has 2,000,000 ordinary shares of $0.50 each. Profit for the year ended 31 December 2025 was $380,000 and retained earnings at that date are $1,150,000. An interim dividend of $0.04 per share has been paid. The directors propose a final dividend of $0.12 per share. The company has cash of $90,000 and plans to extend its factory next year.

  1. (a) Calculate the total cost of the proposed final dividend. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the dividend cover for the year if the final dividend is paid. Give your answer to two decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Advise the directors whether they should pay the proposed final dividend. Justify your answer. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) $240,000
    2,000,000 × $0.12.
  2. (b) 1.19 times
    Total dividends 80,000 + 240,000 = 320,000; 380,000 ÷ 320,000 = 1.19. M1 A1.
  3. (c) For: retained earnings of $1,150,000 mean the dividend is legally possible; shareholders expect a return and the share price may fall if it is cut; a stable dividend signals confidence.Against: cash is only $90,000, so the company would need to borrow about $150,000, adding interest; dividend cover of 1.19 is low, so most of this year's profit is paid out; the factory extension planned for next year needs funds.Decision: pay a reduced final dividend (for example $0.05 per share, $100,000) or delay payment until cash allows.
    Up to 2 marks for points for, up to 2 marks for points against (using figures), 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q26Dividend decisions · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Tarbela Engineering plc has 1,500,000 ordinary shares of $1 each, share premium of $400,000, retained earnings of $900,000 and cash of $260,000. The directors are considering two options.Option A: pay a cash dividend of $0.15 per share.Option B: make a bonus issue of 1 share for every 5 held, using the share premium account, and pay no cash dividend.

  1. (a) Show the effect of each option on share capital, share premium, retained earnings, total equity and cash. [4]
  2. (b) Advise the directors which option they should choose. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cash dividend Bonus issueOrdinary share capital no change +$300,000Share premium no change −$300,000Retained earnings −$225,000 no changeTotal equity −$225,000 no changeCash −$225,000 no change
    Option A: 1,500,000 × 0.15 = 225,000. Option B: 1,500,000 ÷ 5 = 300,000 shares × $1 = $300,000 from share premium. 1 mark each correct column pair (share capital/premium; retained earnings; total equity; cash).
  2. (b) Cash dividend: rewards shareholders with cash and meets their income expectations, but leaves only $35,000 cash, which may cause liquidity problems. Bonus issue: no cash leaves the company and reserves are kept for growth; shareholders receive more shares, but the share price will fall in proportion, so they gain no real wealth and may be disappointed. Decision: e.g. a bonus issue (or a smaller cash dividend) because cash is needed for operations.
    1 mark each for two relevant points, 1 mark use of figures, 1 mark justified decision.
Q27Prior period adjustments · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

While preparing the financial statements for the year ended 31 December 2025, the accountant of Kirthar Foods plc discovered that closing inventory at 31 December 2024 had been overstated by $36,000 because of a counting error. This is a material error. Retained earnings at 1 January 2025, as previously reported, were $512,000. The draft profit for 2025, calculated using the incorrect opening inventory, is $148,000. Dividends of $50,000 were paid in 2025.

  1. (a) Calculate the restated retained earnings at 1 January 2025. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the corrected profit for 2025. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the retained earnings at 31 December 2025. [2]
  4. (d) Explain how IAS 8 requires a material prior period error to be corrected. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $476,000
    512,000 − 36,000: the 2024 profit was overstated by the overstatement of closing inventory. M1 A1.
  2. (b) $184,000
    Opening inventory for 2025 was overstated, so cost of sales was too high: 148,000 + 36,000. M1 A1.
  3. (c) $610,000
    476,000 + 184,000 − 50,000 = 610,000. The error has reversed itself by the end of 2025. M1 A1.
  4. (d) It is corrected retrospectively: the comparative figures for the prior period are restated and the opening balance of retained earnings is adjusted in the statement of changes in equity, rather than putting the correction through the current year's profit.
    1 mark retrospective/restate comparatives; 1 mark adjust opening retained earnings.
Q28Prior period adjustments · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

In 2026 the auditors of Swat Marble plc found that a machine bought on 1 July 2024 for $80,000 had been debited to repairs in 2024. The company depreciates machinery at 20% per annum straight-line, with a time apportionment in the year of purchase. The error is material. The year end is 31 December. Retained earnings at 1 January 2026, as previously reported, were $645,000. The draft profit for 2026 is $210,000 (no depreciation has been charged on the machine).

  1. (a) Calculate the restated retained earnings at 1 January 2026. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the corrected profit for 2026. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the carrying amount of the machine at 31 December 2026. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the correction for 2024 and 2025 is not included in the profit for 2026. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $701,000
    2024: profit understated by 80,000 − depreciation 8,000 (6 months) = 72,000; 2025: depreciation 16,000 omitted. 645,000 + 72,000 − 16,000 = 701,000. 1 mark each step.
  2. (b) $194,000
    210,000 − 2026 depreciation 16,000.
  3. (c) $40,000
    80,000 − 8,000 − 16,000 − 16,000. M1 A1.
  4. (d) Including it would distort the 2026 profit and make comparison with earlier years misleading. IAS 8 requires material prior period errors to be corrected retrospectively through opening retained earnings, with comparatives restated.
    1 mark distortion/comparability; 1 mark IAS 8 retrospective treatment.
Q29Research and development and intangibles · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Solaris Devices plc, Islamabad, spent the following in the year ended 30 June 2026.1 Salaries of scientists investigating new battery materials, with no particular product in view: $42,0002 Testing a prototype of a new solar phone charger (all IAS 38 development criteria are met): $68,0003 Market research carried out before the charger project was approved: $9,0004 Designing tools for production of the new charger (criteria met): $25,000

  1. (a) Calculate the amount to be charged as an expense and the amount to be recognised as an intangible asset for the year. [4]
  2. (b) State two of the criteria that development expenditure must meet before it can be recognised as an asset under IAS 38. [2]

  1. (a) Expense $51,000; intangible asset $93,000

Q30Research and development and intangibles · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Medisonic plc capitalised development costs of $180,000 for a new ultrasound scanner in 2024. Commercial production began on 1 January 2025 and the costs are amortised straight-line over the expected sales life of 5 years. At 31 December 2026 a competitor launched a cheaper scanner, and the recoverable amount of the development costs was estimated at $90,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the amortisation charge for 2026. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the carrying amount of the development costs at 31 December 2026 before any impairment. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the impairment loss at 31 December 2026. [2]
  4. (d) Calculate the total charge in the statement of profit or loss for 2026 relating to the development costs. [1]
  5. (e) Explain why capitalised development costs are amortised. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $36,000
    180,000 ÷ 5.
  2. (b) $108,000
    180,000 − 2 × 36,000 (2025 and 2026). M1 A1.
  3. (c) $18,000
    108,000 − recoverable amount 90,000. M1 A1.
  4. (d) $54,000
    Amortisation plus impairment.
  5. (e) Matching: the costs are spread over the periods that benefit from sales of the product; the asset has a limited useful life, so its carrying amount should fall as the benefits are used up.
    1 mark matching; 1 mark finite life/benefits consumed.
