Q1Performance evaluation and advice · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Kiran owns a clothing boutique in Hyderabad (Sindh). Her ratios are: 2024 2025Gross margin 38% 41%Profit margin 14% 10%Return on capital employed 22% 17%Current ratio 1.9 : 1 1.8 : 1Trade receivables turnover 34 days 29 days

  1. (a) Identify two ratios that show an improvement in 2025. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the profit margin fell although the gross margin rose. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two actions Kiran could take to improve her profit margin. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gross margin (rose from 38% to 41%) and the trade receivables turnover (fell from 34 to 29 days).
    1 mark each with direction stated.
  2. (b) Operating expenses (such as rent, wages or advertising) rose faster than gross profit, so a smaller share of revenue was left as profit.
    1 mark expenses rose; 1 mark faster than gross profit / as % of revenue.
  3. (c) Cut operating expenses such as advertising or energy; move to cheaper premises or renegotiate rent.
    1 mark each for any two sensible expense-control or revenue actions.
Q2Performance evaluation and advice · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Esi runs a homeware shop in Kumasi. For 2025: cost of sales $384,000; inventory 1 January 2025 $40,000; inventory 31 December 2025 $56,000. In 2024 the rate of inventory turnover was 12 times.

  1. (a) Calculate the rate of inventory turnover for 2025. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest two possible reasons why the rate of inventory turnover has fallen from 12 times. [2]
  3. (c) Advise Esi whether she should sell her slow-moving inventory at reduced prices. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.00 times
    Average inventory (40,000 + 56,000) ÷ 2 = 48,000 (M1); 384,000 ÷ 48,000 = 8.00 times (A1).
  2. (b) Sales have slowed (fewer customers); Esi bought too much inventory or some lines are out of fashion.
    1 mark each for any two of: lower demand/sales; overbuying/bulk purchases; slow-moving or obsolete lines; higher selling prices reduced sales; new competitor.
  3. (c) Selling at a discount releases cash and storage space and avoids obsolescence, but lowers the gross margin and may train customers to wait for sales; a justified decision.
    For: cash released, less risk of obsolescence/damage, lower storage cost, turnover improves. Against: lower gross margin, damages brand image, customers wait for discounts. 1 mark per point (max 3), 1 mark decision.

Q4Performance evaluation and advice · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Ibrahim's grocery shop in Jhelum has capital employed of $250,000 and made a profit of $32,000 last year. He works full time in the shop and takes no salary. A supermarket has offered him a job as a manager at $24,000 a year. He could sell the shop for $250,000 and invest the money at 6% a year.

  1. (a) Calculate the return on capital employed for Ibrahim's shop, to two decimal places. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the total annual income Ibrahim would receive if he sold the shop for $250,000, invested the money and took the job. [2]
  3. (c) Advise Ibrahim whether to sell the shop. Justify your answer. [4]

  1. (a) 32,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 12.80%

    1 mark comparison using figures; 2 marks non-financial / other factors; 1 mark decision.

Q6Performance evaluation and advice · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Tendai sells farm supplies in Harare. In 2025 he increased the credit period for customers from 30 to 60 days to win more business; his trade receivables turnover in 2024 was 38 days and the business now has a bank overdraft. For 2025: credit sales $730,000; credit purchases $511,000; trade receivables $122,000; trade payables $42,000.

  1. (a) Calculate for 2025, to the nearest whole day: (i) the trade receivables turnover, (ii) the trade payables turnover. [4]
  2. (b) Advise Tendai whether to continue offering 60 days' credit. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 61 days (ii) 30 days
    122,000 ÷ 730,000 × 365 (M1) = 61 days (A1). 42,000 ÷ 511,000 × 365 (M1) = 30 days (A1).

    For keeping: sales grew; competitive; customer loyalty. Against: receivables days 38 → 61 while payables 30, overdraft interest, higher risk of irrecoverable debts. 1 mark per point (max 3, both sides), 1 mark decision.

Q10Performance evaluation and advice · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

(b) The trading performance is identical; the difference arises only because Leaseway rents premises (rent expense, no asset) while Ownhome owns them (depreciation, large asset in capital employed). Leaseway looks better on ROCE, Ownhome on margin. Different ways of financing and holding assets distort comparisons; Ownhome also gains from any rise in property value, which the ratios ignore.
    1 mark identical trading; 1 mark explanation of rent vs ownership; 1 mark each ratio affected differently; 1 mark further point.
Q104Limitations of ratios · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Bilal runs an electrical shop in Sukkur as a sole trader. Revenue $900,000; profit for the year $108,000. He takes no salary. A competitor, Zest Electricals Ltd, had revenue of $950,000 and profit from operations of $76,000 after paying its managing director a salary of $40,000. Bilal says his business is more profitable.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit margin of each business, to two decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Recalculate Bilal's profit margin after charging a notional salary equal to that paid to Zest's director. [2]
  3. (c) Evaluate Bilal's claim that his business is more profitable than Zest Ltd. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bilal: 108,000 ÷ 900,000 = 12.00%; Zest Ltd: 76,000 ÷ 950,000 = 8.00%
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) (108,000 − 40,000) ÷ 900,000 × 100 = 7.56%
    1 mark adjusted profit; 1 mark answer.
  3. (c) On reported figures Bilal's margin is 4 points higher, but a sole trader's profit includes the reward for the owner's work; after a $40,000 salary his margin falls to 7.56%, below Zest's 8.00%. His claim is not supported; other differences (rent, size, accounting policies) should also be checked.
    1 mark reported comparison; 1 mark adjusted comparison; 1 mark judgement.
Q105Limitations of ratios · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Pearl River Bakeries Ltd, Guangzhou, China, had revenue of $2,000,000 in 2025 and a profit from operations margin of 12%. Capital employed is $1,500,000. A large competitor has opened nearby; the directors forecast that in 2026 revenue will fall by 15% and the margin will fall to 10%. Capital employed will not change.

  1. (a) Calculate the profit from operations and the return on capital employed for 2025 and for the 2026 forecast, to two decimal places. [4]
  2. (b) Discuss the limitations of using last year's ratios to judge the company's future performance. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2025: 12% × 2,000,000 = 240,000; ROCE 240,000 ÷ 1,500,000 = 16.00%2026: 10% × 1,700,000 = 170,000; ROCE 170,000 ÷ 1,500,000 = 11.33%
    1 mark each figure.
  2. (b) Ratios are based on past results; the new competitor will change revenue and margins (forecast ROCE falls by 4.67 points). External factors (economy, exchange rates, costs) change; the forecast itself is an estimate. Ratios ignore management plans to respond. Use with forecasts and market information.
    1 mark historical; 1 mark use of figures; 1 mark external changes; 1 mark conclusion.
