Q1Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Two traders in Karachi, one selling dried fruit and one with a shop in Dubai, agree to work together for one shipment only and share the profit.

  1. (a) Explain the term 'joint venture'. [2]
  2. (b) State two differences between a joint venture and a partnership. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A short-term business arrangement in which two or more parties combine resources for a single, specific project and share the resulting profit or loss in an agreed ratio.
    1 mark: two or more parties for a specific/single project. 1 mark: temporary, sharing profit or loss in agreed ratio.
  2. (b) A joint venture ends when the project is completed, whereas a partnership continues indefinitely; joint venturers usually carry on their own separate businesses and there is usually no firm name or separate continuing set of books.
    1 mark each for any two: duration (temporary vs continuing); scope (one project vs ongoing business); venturers keep own businesses; no firm name / records often kept in venturers' own books.
Q2Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

A Pakistani rice mill is considering a joint venture with a packaging company in Malaysia to supply a large supermarket order.

  1. (a) State two reasons why the rice mill might enter into the joint venture. [2]
  2. (b) State two risks for the rice mill of entering into the joint venture. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) To share the cost and risk of the order; to use the partner's skills, contacts and packaging facilities.
    1 mark each: share risk/finance; access to expertise, contacts or market; complete an order too large alone.
  2. (b) Disagreements over how profit and costs are shared; the other venturer may fail to meet its obligations or may mishandle the money received.
    1 mark each: disputes; dependence on partner's performance; loss of control; unlimited liability for shared losses; delays in settlement.
Q3Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) After his share of profit is recorded, Ikram's 'Joint venture with Salma' account has a debit balance of $9,300. Explain what this balance shows and how it will be cleared. [2]
Show answer

Q4Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

Sialkot Leather sends handbags to an agent in Doha. The agent sells them for a commission and returns the money. A competitor instead works with a Doha trader who shares the profit on the goods equally.

  1. (a) Identify which arrangement is a consignment and which is a joint venture. [1]
  2. (b) Explain two differences between a consignment and a joint venture. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sialkot Leather and its agent: consignment. The competitor and the Doha trader: joint venture.
    B1 both correct.
  2. (b) In a consignment the consignor owns the goods until they are sold and bears all the profit or loss; the agent earns only commission. In a joint venture the venturers are co-owners for the project and share profit or loss in an agreed ratio. Also, a consignee acts for the consignor as agent, whereas joint venturers are like temporary partners with equal standing.
    Up to 2 marks per difference: ownership of goods; who bears profit/loss (commission vs share of profit); relationship (principal–agent vs co-venturers).

Q8Joint ventures · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

  1. (a) Calculate each venturer's share of the profit. [2]

Show answer
  1. (a) Usama $18,000; Wardah $30,000.
    48,000 × 3/8 = 18,000 (B1); × 5/8 = 30,000 (B1).

Q11Joint ventures · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Two venturers each record their own joint venture transactions in their own books. At the end of the venture a memorandum joint venture account is prepared.

  1. (a) Explain the purpose of the memorandum joint venture account. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the memorandum joint venture account is not part of the double-entry system. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It brings together the transactions of all venturers to calculate the profit or loss of the whole venture, so that it can be shared in the agreed ratio.
    1 mark: combines all venturers' transactions. 1 mark: to find total profit/loss to be shared.
  2. (b) Each transaction has already been recorded in a venturer's own 'Joint venture with …' account; the memorandum account is a working statement prepared from copies of those records, so no entries are posted from it.
    1 mark: transactions already recorded in venturers' own books. 1 mark: it is only a statement/memorandum, no double entry.
Q12Joint ventures · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

Joint venture transactions may be recorded either in a separate set of books with a joint bank account, or in each venturer's own books.

  1. (a) Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of keeping a separate set of books with a joint bank account. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Advantage: all receipts and payments pass through one account, so the venture's records are complete and transparent and the profit is easy to calculate. Disadvantage: it costs more time and money to open an account and keep extra books, which may not be worthwhile for a small, short venture.
    Up to 2 marks for an advantage (identify + explain) and up to 2 marks for a disadvantage (identify + explain).
Q13Joint ventures · Standard · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

At 31 December 2025, Amjad's joint venture with Bushra had been completed, but Bushra had not yet paid Amjad the $12,400 owed to him. Amjad's share of the venture profit was $5,100.

  1. (a) State how the $5,100 should be shown in Amjad's statement of profit or loss for the year ended 31 December 2025. [1]
  2. (b) State how the $12,400 should be shown in Amjad's statement of financial position at 31 December 2025, and explain why. [2]
  3. (c) Identify the accounting concept that requires the profit to be recognised in 2025 even though the cash has not been received. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) As income: 'share of profit on joint venture' $5,100.
    B1.
  2. (b) As a current asset (other receivable – amount due from Bushra), because it is money owed to Amjad that he expects to receive within a year.
    1 mark: current asset/receivable. 1 mark: reason.
  3. (c) Accruals (matching) concept.
    B1 accruals (also accept realisation).

Q21Joint ventures · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Haider Builders, Lahore, wants to bid for a bridge contract worth $2,400,000.Option 1: bid alone. Haider would have to hire specialist equipment and engineers; total costs are estimated at $2,280,000.Option 2: form a joint venture with a foreign engineering company. Total costs of the venture are estimated at $1,950,000 and Haider would receive 40% of the profit.

  1. (a) Calculate Haider's expected profit under each option. [2]
  2. (b) Advise Haider Builders which option to choose. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Option 1 $120,000; Option 2 $180,000.
    Option 1: 2,400,000 − 2,280,000 = 120,000 (B1). Option 2: (2,400,000 − 1,950,000) × 40% = 180,000 (B1).
  2. (b) For the joint venture: higher expected profit ($60,000 more); the foreign partner's expertise lowers the risk of cost overruns and delays; Haider gains experience for future bridge contracts; less finance needed. Bidding alone keeps all profit but the margin is only 5% of contract value, so a small overrun would cause a loss. Decision: choose the joint venture, provided a clear written agreement sets out duties, profit sharing and settlement.
    Up to 2 marks for points for the joint venture; up to 2 marks for points against / for bidding alone; 1 mark for use of the calculated figures (e.g. 60,000 difference, 5% margin); 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q22Joint ventures · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

Rashid and Tahir share joint venture profits equally. At the end of their venture in garden furniture, unsold goods that cost $6,000 are taken over by Rashid at an agreed value of $4,000, their net realisable value. Before this item, the memorandum joint venture account showed a profit of $15,000 (all goods counted as sold or taken over at cost).

  1. (a) Calculate the revised joint venture profit. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate each venturer's share of the revised profit. [1]
  3. (c) Discuss whether the goods should be taken over at net realisable value rather than cost. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $13,000
    Taking over at 4,000 rather than cost 6,000 reduces the credit by 2,000 (M1); 15,000 − 2,000 = 13,000 (A1).
  2. (b) $6,500 each
    13,000 ÷ 2 (B1).
  3. (c) Prudence: goods should not be valued above what they can be sold for, so $4,000 is appropriate if NRV is reliable. However, Rashid benefits if he later sells them for more, so the value must be agreed fairly and independently; Tahir bears half the $2,000 reduction. Conclusion: NRV is acceptable if it is agreed by both venturers or set by an independent valuation.
    1 mark: prudence / IAS 2 lower of cost and NRV. 1 mark: conflict of interest – taker benefits from low value. 1 mark: conclusion/agreement needed.

Q25Joint ventures · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

Qadir and Rizwan built a block of flats in Islamabad as a joint venture. They agreed that each venturer would receive interest at 10% per annum on money he contributed, from the date of payment until the venture ended on 31 December 2025, and that the remaining profit would be shared equally.Qadir paid $120,000 for the land on 1 January 2025. Rizwan paid $180,000 for construction on 1 April 2025. On 31 December 2025 all the flats were sold and Qadir received the proceeds of $360,000.

  1. (a) Calculate the interest due to each venturer. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit to be shared and each venturer's share. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the settlement payment and state who pays whom. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Qadir $12,000; Rizwan $13,500.
    Qadir 120,000 × 10% = 12,000 (B1); Rizwan 180,000 × 10% × 9/12 = 13,500 (B1).
  2. (b) Profit $34,500; $17,250 each.
    360,000 − 120,000 − 180,000 − 12,000 − 13,500 (M1) = 34,500 (A1); 17,250 each (A1).
  3. (c) Qadir pays Rizwan $210,750.
    Rizwan is due 180,000 + 13,500 + 17,250 = 210,750 (M1); Qadir holds the proceeds and pays him (A1).

Show answer
  1. (a) $8,200
