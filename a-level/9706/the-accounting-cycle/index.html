Q1Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature applies to a sole trader? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C The owner has unlimited liability for the business's debts.
    A sole trader is not a separate legal person, so the owner is personally liable for all business debts (unlimited liability). A and B describe limited companies.
Q2Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Areeba plans to open a small bakery in Multan as a sole trader.

  1. (a) State two advantages of operating as a sole trader. [2]
  2. (b) State two disadvantages of operating as a sole trader. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: owner keeps all the profit; full control and quick decisions; easy and cheap to set up; financial affairs are private (no need to publish accounts).
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Any two: unlimited liability; limited capital (own savings and loans only); heavy workload / no one to share responsibility; business may end if the owner dies or is ill; harder to borrow.
    1 mark each.
Q3Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the term limited liability. [2]
  2. (b) Explain what is meant by saying a limited company is a separate legal entity. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shareholders can lose no more than the amount they have paid (or agreed to pay) for their shares; their personal assets cannot be used to pay the company's debts.
    1 mark: loss limited to investment in shares; 1 mark: personal assets protected.
  2. (b) The company exists in law separately from its owners: it can own assets, enter contracts, sue and be sued in its own name, and it continues if shareholders change or die.
    1 mark: separate person in law; 1 mark: example (contracts, own assets, continuity).
Q4Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between ownership and control in a limited company. [2]
  2. (b) Give two reasons why limited companies must publish annual financial statements. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shareholders own the company, but the directors (who may not be shareholders) manage and control it on their behalf.
    1 mark owners = shareholders; 1 mark control = directors/managers.
  2. (b) Because shareholders do not run the company, they need information to judge the directors' performance (stewardship); creditors and lenders need information because their claims are limited to the company's assets (limited liability).
    1 mark each.
Q5Types of business organisation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which type of business organisation can raise finance by offering its shares for sale to the general public? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D public limited company
    Only a public limited company may offer shares to the general public (e.g. through a stock exchange). A private limited company can sell shares only privately, for example to family or invited investors.
Q6Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statements about a public limited company are correct?1 Its shares may be offered for sale to the general public.2 Its shareholders have unlimited liability.3 It is a separate legal entity from its shareholders.4 It must have only one owner. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 1 and 3

Q7Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State two differences between a private limited company and a public limited company. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest one reason why a family business might prefer to remain a private limited company. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: a private company cannot offer its shares to the general public, a public company can (and may be listed on a stock exchange); private company shares are usually transferred only with the directors' agreement, public company shares are freely traded; a public company usually needs a minimum share capital and stricter disclosure; a private company name ends in 'Limited/(Private) Limited', a public one in 'plc' or equivalent.
    2 marks per difference, comparing both types.
  2. (b) The family keeps control because shares cannot be sold to outsiders without approval, so there is no risk of takeover; also fewer disclosure requirements and lower costs.

Q8Types of business organisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Usman has run a computer accessories shop in Hyderabad as a sole trader for six years. He wants to open a second branch and start selling online. His brother, who has IT skills, offers to invest $40,000 and become a partner.

  1. (a) Advise Usman whether he should form a partnership with his brother. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: extra capital ($40,000) to open the second branch; brother's IT and online-sales skills add expertise; workload and decision-making shared; cover during illness or holidays. Q14Types of business organisation · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks

Zainab and Kiran are partners in an interior design business in Karachi. Turnover has tripled in three years and they have just won a contract to design the interior of a 200-room hotel, which will need expensive materials bought in advance. A cousin is willing to invest money but does not want to take part in running the business or have unlimited liability.

  1. (a) Advise Zainab and Kiran whether they should convert their partnership into a private limited company. Justify your answer. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) For conversion: limited liability protects their homes against claims (important given the large contract and its risks); the company is a separate legal entity, so it continues if a partner leaves; easier to raise finance — can issue shares to investors such as their cousin and use loan notes; may appear more credible to large clients. Against: set-up costs and legal formalities; must file accounts publicly (loss of privacy) and may require an audit; profits taxed as a company and owners paid through salaries and dividends; banks may still ask for personal guarantees, reducing the benefit of limited liability; shares in a private company cannot easily be sold. Decision: convert, because the growth in contract size and the need for outside capital make limited liability and access to share finance worth the extra cost.
    Level 3 (7–8): well-applied points on both sides and a justified decision. Level 2 (4–6): some applied points on both sides or one-sided with a decision. Level 1 (1–3): general features of partnerships/companies.
Q15Types of business organisation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Hassan bought 40,000 ordinary shares of $1 each in a private limited company at $1.25 per share, fully paid. Salman is a general partner in a firm of architects with one other partner.

  1. (a) State the maximum amount Hassan could lose as a shareholder, and the maximum Salman could lose as a partner. Explain the difference. [4]

Show answer

Q75Accounting equation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
