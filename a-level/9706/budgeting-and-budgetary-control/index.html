Q1Cash budgets · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Rehan sells sports equipment in Sialkot. Budgeted sales are: January $40,000; February $45,000; March $50,000. 20% of sales are for cash. Credit customers pay 60% in the month after the sale and 40% two months after the sale.

  1. (a) Calculate the total budgeted cash receipts from sales for March. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the receipts for March are different from the sales for March. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $44,400
    Cash sales 20% × 50,000 = 10,000 (B1); February credit 36,000 × 60% = 21,600 (B1); January credit 32,000 × 40% = 12,800; total 44,400 (B1).
  2. (b) Most sales are on credit, so cash from March credit sales arrives in April and May, while March receipts include cash from January and February credit sales.
    1 mark: timing difference due to credit terms. 1 mark: receipts include earlier months' sales / exclude part of current month's sales.
Q2Cash budgets · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A grocery wholesaler in Hyderabad buys all goods on credit. Budgeted purchases: April $30,000; May $36,000; June $42,000. Half of each month's purchases are paid in the following month, taking a 2% cash discount. The other half is paid two months after purchase.

  1. (a) Calculate the budgeted payments to suppliers in June. [3]
  2. (b) State one advantage and one disadvantage to the wholesaler of taking the cash discount. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $32,640
    May: 36,000 × 50% × 98% = 17,640 (M1 A1); April: 30,000 × 50% = 15,000; total 32,640 (A1).
  2. (b) Advantage: reduces the cost of purchases / increases profit. Disadvantage: cash leaves the business sooner, reducing liquidity.
    1 mark for each.
Q3Cash budgets · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Hina is opening a small café in Lahore and her bank has asked for a cash budget for the first six months.

  1. (a) Explain two reasons why a cash budget will be useful to Hina. [4]
  2. (b) State two items that appear in a budgeted statement of profit or loss but not in a cash budget. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It shows in advance any months with a cash shortage, so she can arrange an overdraft or delay spending. It shows months with surplus cash, which can be used to repay debt or invest. (Also: supports the loan application; lets actual cash flows be compared with plan.)
    Up to 2 marks per reason (identify 1, explain 1): plan for shortages/arrange finance; plan use of surpluses; support for bank/loan application; control by comparing actual with budget.
  2. (b) Depreciation; provision for doubtful debts (also: irrecoverable debts, profit or loss on disposal, accruals not yet paid).
    1 mark each for any two non-cash items.
Q4Cash budgets · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A furniture showroom in Karachi has budgeted sales of $80,000 in June and $90,000 in July. Wages earned are $24,000 in June and $28,000 in July. Three-quarters of each month's wages are paid in that month and the rest in the next month. Sales staff earn commission of 5% of sales, paid in the month after the sale.

  1. (a) Calculate the wages paid in July. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the commission paid in July. [1]
  3. (c) The showroom also charges depreciation of $1,800 a month on its vans. Explain how this affects the cash budget. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $27,000
    75% × 28,000 = 21,000 (M1) + 25% × 24,000 = 6,000; total 27,000 (A1).
  2. (b) $4,000
    5% × June sales 80,000 = 4,000 (B1).
  3. (c) It does not appear, because depreciation is not a payment of cash; the cash outflow happened when the vans were bought.
    1 mark: excluded / no effect. 1 mark: non-cash expense; cash paid when asset bought.

Q14Cash budgets · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

The cash budget of Saeed Packaging Ltd shows a cash deficit of $40,000 for the next three months, after which the balance becomes positive. The directors are considering two options:Option 1: an overdraft of $40,000 for three months at an interest rate of 12% per annum.Option 2: offer customers a 2.5% cash discount to pay one month earlier. The directors expect customers owing $64,000 to accept, which would remove the deficit.

  1. (a) Calculate the cost of Option 1. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the cost of Option 2. [1]
  3. (c) Advise the directors which option they should choose. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) $1,200
    40,000 × 12% × 3/12 = 1,200 (B1).
  2. (b) $1,600
    64,000 × 2.5% = 1,600 (B1).
  3. (c) Option 1 is cheaper by $400 and does not depend on customers' behaviour; the bank may withdraw an overdraft at short notice, and it adds to interest costs. Option 2 is more expensive, customers may not all accept, and once offered the discount may be expected permanently, reducing future profit; but it lowers the risk of irrecoverable debts and needs no bank approval. Decision: choose the overdraft because it is cheaper and more certain, provided the bank agrees.
    Up to 2 marks for points for Option 1 (cheaper by 400; certain amount; temporary). Up to 2 marks for points for/against Option 2 (uncertain take-up; permanent expectation; fewer bad debts; no security needed). 1 mark for use of figures. 1 mark for a clear decision consistent with the argument.

Q26Purpose of budgeting · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Al-Shifa Hospital, Karachi, currently sets departmental budgets centrally. The new finance director suggests participative budgeting.

  1. (a) Explain the term 'participative budgeting'. [2]
  2. (b) Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of participative budgeting for the hospital. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Budget holders (department managers) are involved in setting their own budgets with senior management, rather than having budgets imposed.
    1 mark: managers take part. 1 mark: contrast with imposed/top-down.
  2. (b) Advantages: department heads know their patient numbers and costs, so budgets are more realistic; managers feel ownership and are more motivated to meet them; better communication between departments. Disadvantages: takes longer; managers may build in budgetary slack; targets may not match the hospital's overall goals; senior managers may still override them, causing demotivation.
    Up to 3 marks for advantages and up to 3 for disadvantages, each developed in the hospital context.
Q27Purpose of budgeting · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

The directors of Shah Logistics Ltd plan to pay each depot manager a bonus if the depot's actual costs are below its budget.

  1. (a) Explain the term 'budgetary slack'. [2]
  2. (b) Advise the directors whether they should link the bonus to the budget. Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Deliberately overstating budgeted costs (or understating revenue) so the target is easier to achieve.
    1 mark: deliberate overstatement/understatement. 1 mark: to make the target easy to meet.
  2. (b) For: gives managers a clear target and incentive to control costs; aligns their goals with the company's. Against: encourages budgetary slack in the budget-setting stage; managers may cut essential spending (maintenance, training) to earn the bonus; ignores factors outside their control such as fuel prices; may harm service quality. Decision: link bonuses only if budgets are reviewed for slack and include non-financial targets, or use a flexed budget; otherwise do not.
    Up to 2 marks for points for; up to 3 marks for points against (slack, short-termism, uncontrollable costs, quality); 1 mark for a justified decision.
Q28Flexible budgets · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A bottling plant in Hub records electricity costs of $9,200 when output is 4,000 crates and $13,700 when output is 7,000 crates. The cost is semi-variable.

  1. (a) Use the high–low method to calculate the variable cost per crate and the fixed cost. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the flexed budget cost of electricity for an output of 5,800 crates. [1]
  3. (c) Actual electricity cost at 5,800 crates was $12,400. Calculate the variance and state whether it is favourable or adverse. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest one possible cause of this variance. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Variable $1.50 per crate; fixed $3,200.
    (13,700 − 9,200) ÷ (7,000 − 4,000) = 1.50 (M1 A1); fixed 9,200 − 4,000 × 1.50 = 3,200 (A1).
  2. (b) $11,900
    3,200 + 5,800 × 1.50 = 11,900 (B1).
  3. (c) $500 adverse
    12,400 − 11,900 = 500 A (B1).
  4. (d) An increase in the electricity tariff (or machines left running when idle).
    B1 any valid cause.

Q30Flexible budgets · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Thar Pumps budgeted to make and sell 8,000 pumps at $45 each. Variable cost per pump: materials $15, labour $9, variable overheads $3. Fixed costs were budgeted at $90,000.Actual results: 6,500 pumps made and sold at $46 each; materials $101,400; labour $60,450; variable overheads $20,800; fixed costs $88,000.The production manager says: 'Total costs were $35,350 below budget, so my department performed very well.'

  1. (a) Calculate the flexed budget profit for the actual output. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the total cost variance compared with the flexed budget, stating whether it is favourable or adverse. [2]
  3. (c) Evaluate the production manager's claim. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) $27,000
    Flexed revenue 6,500 × 45 = 292,500 (B1); flexed costs 6,500 × 27 + 90,000 = 265,500 (B1); profit 27,000 (B1).
