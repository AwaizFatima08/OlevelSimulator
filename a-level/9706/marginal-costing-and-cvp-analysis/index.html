Q2Marginal costing decisions · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A business in Sialkot makes school bags. Costs per bag: materials $8.50, labour $6.00, variable overhead $1.50. Fixed costs are $48,000 per month. Normal monthly output is 12,000 bags, sold at $25 each, and there is spare capacity of 3,000 bags. A charity offers to buy 2,000 bags at $19 each; it would need special delivery costing $1,400 in total.

  1. (a) Calculate the marginal cost of one bag. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the change in monthly profit if the order is accepted. [3]
  3. (c) State two non-financial factors the business should consider before accepting the order. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.50 + 6.00 + 1.50 = $16.00
    1 mark.
  2. (b) Contribution per bag 19 − 16 = $3; 2,000 × 3 = 6,000; less delivery 1,400 = increase of $4,600
    M1 contribution per unit; M1 deduct extra delivery cost; A1. Fixed costs ignored (no change).
  3. (c) Any two: existing customers may ask for the lower price; whether the order may lead to repeat business; effect on quality and staff workload; reliability of the new customer; damage to brand image if sold cheaply.
    1 mark each.

Q4Marginal costing decisions · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A factory in Karachi makes running shoes, sold at $50 a pair. For each pair: materials $12, labour $8, variable overhead $3, and a sales commission of 5% of the selling price. Fixed costs are $36,000 per month.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by contribution. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the contribution per pair. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the profit for a month in which 2,400 pairs are sold. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Selling price (or revenue) less variable costs; the amount left to pay the fixed costs and then provide profit.
    1 mark definition; 1 mark use towards fixed costs and profit.
  2. (b) Variable cost = 12 + 8 + 3 + 2.50 commission = 25.50; contribution = 50 − 25.50 = $24.50
    M1 commission included as a variable cost; A1.
  3. (c) 2,400 × 24.50 = 58,800; less 36,000 = $22,800
    M1; A1 OF.
Q5Marginal costing decisions · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Yasmin runs a kitchen-fitting business in Islamabad and has spare capacity. A one-off contract would need materials costing $2,400 (bought specially), 120 labour hours at $15 per hour and variable overhead of $4 per labour hour. Fixed overheads are normally absorbed at $10 per labour hour; they will not change if the contract is taken.

  1. (a) Calculate the minimum price Yasmin could charge for the contract. [3]
  2. (b) State two reasons why she might charge more than this minimum price. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Materials 2,400 + labour 120 × 15 = 1,800 + variable overhead 120 × 4 = 480 = $4,680
    M1 labour; M1 variable overhead; A1 (fixed overhead excluded).
  2. (b) Any two: to cover fixed costs; to make a profit; customer can afford more / market price is higher; avoid other customers expecting low prices; risk of cost over-runs.
    1 mark each.
Q6Marginal costing decisions · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Material is in short supply. Product X earns contribution of $24 per unit and uses 4 kg of material; product Y earns $30 and uses 6 kg; product Z earns $18 and uses 2 kg. In what order should production be ranked? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C Z, X, Y
    Contribution per kg: X 24 ÷ 4 = $6, Y 30 ÷ 6 = $5, Z 18 ÷ 2 = $9, so Z, X, Y. B ranks by contribution per unit, which ignores the scarce resource.

Q8Marginal costing decisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Rehman Motors makes 10,000 units a year of component K. Cost per unit: materials $6.40, labour $5.20, variable overhead $1.80, fixed overhead absorbed $4.60. A supplier offers component K at $15.50 per unit. If the company buys it in, the space freed can be rented out for $18,000 per year and a supervisor earning $9,000 per year would no longer be needed. Other fixed overheads would not change.

  1. (a) Calculate the relevant annual cost of making the component. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the net annual cost of buying the component and state whether the company should make or buy it. [3]
  3. (c) State two non-financial factors to consider before buying from the supplier. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (6.40 + 5.20 + 1.80) × 10,000 = $134,000
    M1 variable costs only; A1.
  2. (b) 155,000 − 18,000 rent − 9,000 salary saved = $128,000. Buying is cheaper by $6,000, so buy.
    M1 purchase cost less rent; M1 less salary saved; A1 decision.

Q9Marginal costing decisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A shop in Doha sells water filters at $50 each. Variable cost is $30 per filter and fixed costs are $90,000 per month. It sells 6,000 filters per month. The manager proposes cutting the price by 10%, which is expected to raise monthly sales by 25%.

  1. (a) Calculate the current monthly profit. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the monthly profit if the proposal is adopted and state the change in profit. [3]
  3. (c) State one factor, other than profit, the manager should consider. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (50 − 30) × 6,000 − 90,000 = $30,000
    M1; A1.

    M1 new price and volume; M1 contribution − fixed costs; A1.

    1 mark.
Q10Marginal costing decisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hashmi Electronics sells air purifiers at $80 each; variable cost is $44 each. Annual sales are 5,000 units and fixed costs are $130,000, including sales staff salaries of $24,000. It plans to stop paying the salaries and instead pay the sales staff a commission of 6% of the selling price. It expects sales to rise to 5,600 units.

  1. (a) Calculate the current annual profit. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the contribution per unit and the annual profit under the commission plan. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the break-even point in units under each plan. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (80 − 44) × 5,000 − 130,000 = $50,000
    M1; A1.
  2. (b) Commission 6% × 80 = 4.80; contribution = 36 − 4.80 = $31.20; profit = 31.20 × 5,600 − 106,000 = $68,720
    M1 commission as a variable cost; M1 fixed costs reduced by 24,000; A1.
  3. (c) Current: 130,000 ÷ 36 = 3,611.1 → 3,612 units. Commission plan: 106,000 ÷ 31.20 = 3,397.4 → 3,398 units.
    M1 each method; A1 both, rounded up to whole units.
Q11Marginal costing decisions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Ghazi Textiles can make 50,000 towels a year. It currently sells 38,000 towels a year in Pakistan at $22 each; variable cost $13 per towel; fixed costs $250,000 a year. A buyer in Kenya offers to buy 9,000 towels at $16 each. Shipping would cost an extra $0.80 per towel and a one-off export certificate would cost $6,000.

  1. (a) Advise the directors whether they should accept the export order. Justify your answer. [7]
Show answer
  1. (a) The order adds $13,800 to profit and there is enough spare capacity, so accept unless the risks below are judged too great. Either decision accepted if justified.
    Calculation (max 2): contribution 9,000 × (16 − 13 − 0.80) = 19,800 less certification 6,000 = 13,800 extra profit. For (max 3): spare capacity of 12,000 units is otherwise unused; covers some fixed costs; may lead to further export orders and a new market; keeps staff employed. Against (max 3): home customers may learn of the lower price and demand the same; uses 9,000 of 12,000 spare units, leaving little room for growth at home; exchange-rate and payment risks; the price is below full cost. Decision (1).

Q14Marginal costing decisions · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

A factory in Lahore has 20,000 machine hours a month. It makes one product, selling 8,500 units a month at $40 each; variable cost $22 per unit; 2 machine hours per unit. A customer asks for 2,000 units of a modified version at $30 each. Each needs 2 machine hours and has a variable cost of $21. A one-off design cost of $2,500 would be paid. Demand for the normal product is unchanged.

  1. (a) Calculate the spare machine hours and the number of units of the normal product that must be given up. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the effect on profit of accepting the order. [4]
  3. (c) Calculate the minimum price per unit at which the order would be worth accepting. [2]

  1. (a) Spare = 3,000 hours; order needs 4,000 hours; 1,000 hours short = 500 normal units.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Order contribution 2,000 × (30 − 21) = 18,000; lost contribution 500 × 18 = 9,000; design cost 2,500; increase in profit = $6,500

Q15Marginal costing decisions · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Mirza Ltd makes electric kettles. It sells 8,000 kettles a year at $35 each. Variable cost is $21 per kettle and fixed costs are $80,000 a year. The directors are considering two options for next year:Option 1: reduce the price to $32; sales are expected to rise to 10,500 kettles.Option 2: keep the price at $35 and spend $18,000 on advertising; sales are expected to rise to 9,600 kettles.

  1. (a) Advise the directors which option, if either, they should choose. Justify your answer with calculations. [7]
Show answer
  1. (a) Current profit $32,000; Option 1 profit $35,500; Option 2 profit $36,400. On profit alone Option 2 is better by $900, but the difference is small, so either option can be recommended with justification.
    Calculations (max 3): current profit 14 × 8,000 − 80,000 = 32,000; Option 1: 11 × 10,500 − 80,000 = 35,500; Option 2: 14 × 9,600 − 98,000 = 36,400. Points (max 3): Option 1 break-even 7,273 units, margin of safety 10,500 − 7,273 = 3,227; Option 2 break-even 7,000 units, margin of safety 2,600; the lower price may start a price war and lower brand image; Option 1 needs extra capacity and workload for 2,500 more units; advertising results are uncertain but could have longer-lasting effects; the sales forecasts are estimates. Decision with reason (1).

  3. (c) 24x − 192,000 = 28x − 240,000 → x = 12,000 units
    M1 equation; A1.

Q26CVP uses, assumptions and limitations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Zubair runs a hardware store in Mardan that sells several hundred different products.

  1. (a) State three assumptions made in cost–volume–profit analysis. [3]
  2. (b) Explain one limitation of using cost–volume–profit analysis for a business that sells several products. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three: selling price per unit is constant; variable cost per unit is constant; fixed costs are constant within the relevant range; all costs can be split into fixed and variable; all output is sold (no change in inventory); the sales mix is constant; only volume affects costs and revenue.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) A constant sales mix must be assumed. If customers buy more low-contribution items than planned, the average contribution falls and the true break-even point is higher than calculated.
    1 mark limitation; 1 mark explanation.
Q27CVP uses, assumptions and limitations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The directors of Jinnah Paints have drawn a break-even chart. The total revenue line starts at the origin and rises to $900,000 at 10,000 tins. The fixed cost line is horizontal at $240,000. The total cost line starts at $240,000 and rises to $690,000 at 10,000 tins. The lines cross at 5,333 tins.

  1. (a) Explain two ways the directors could use cost–volume–profit analysis when planning next year's production. [4]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why the actual profit at 10,000 units might differ from the profit shown by the break-even chart. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two, explained: find the break-even output, so they know the minimum sales needed to avoid a loss; set the sales level needed for a target profit; measure risk through the margin of safety; test the effect of changes in price, costs or volume (what-if analysis) before deciding.
    1 mark use; 1 mark explanation, × 2.
  2. (b) Any two: bulk discounts or overtime make variable cost per unit change; prices may have to be cut to sell more; fixed costs may step up when output rises; inventory may be built up instead of sold.
    1 mark each.

Q29Marginal versus absorption profit · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Khyber Foods began making jars of pickle on 1 June. June data: production 5,000 jars, sales 4,200 jars at $30 each. Variable production cost $12 per jar; fixed production overhead $35,000 (absorbed on the normal output of 5,000 jars, so $7 per jar); variable selling cost $1 per jar sold; fixed administration $18,000. There was no opening inventory.

  1. (a) Prepare a statement of profit for June using marginal costing. [3]
  2. (b) Prepare a statement of profit for June using absorption costing. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the two profit figures are different. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Marginal costing statement $ $Revenue (4,200 × 30) 126,000Variable cost of production (5,000 × 12) 60,000Less closing inventory (800 × 12) (9,600)Variable cost of sales (50,400)Variable selling costs (4,200)Contribution 71,400Fixed production overhead (35,000)Fixed administration (18,000)Profit 18,400

Q30Marginal versus absorption profit · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Ravi Plastics in Chennai absorbs fixed production overhead at $9 per unit, based on a normal output of 6,000 units a month. Actual fixed overhead is $54,000 each month. There was no inventory at the start of month 1. Month 1 Month 2Production (units) 6,000 5,500Sales (units) 5,000 6,300Profit under marginal costing $42,000 $55,000

  1. (a) Calculate the closing inventory in units at the end of each month. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for each month under absorption costing. [4]
  3. (c) Explain why the absorption profit is higher than the marginal profit in month 1 but lower in month 2. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Month 1: 6,000 − 5,000 = 1,000; month 2: 1,000 + 5,500 − 6,300 = 200
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Month 1: 42,000 + 1,000 × 9 = $51,000. Month 2: inventory falls by 800 units, so 55,000 − 800 × 9 = $47,800. Month 2 production is 500 units below normal, so 500 × $9 = $4,500 of overhead is under-absorbed and charged as an expense; the difference between the two profits is still fully explained by the change in inventory × $9.
    M1 inventory change × $9; A1 month 1; M1 decrease deducted; A1 month 2.
  3. (c) Month 1: inventory rises, so some fixed overhead is carried forward in closing inventory. Month 2: inventory falls, so fixed overhead carried forward from month 1 is released into cost of sales, reducing absorption profit.
    1 mark each.
