Q1Work-energy principle · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle of mass 0.4 kg is projected with speed 8 m s⁻¹ up a line of greatest slope of a smooth plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal. Use an energy method to find the distance the particle travels up the plane before coming to instantaneous rest. [3]
  2. (b) Find the speed of the particle when it has travelled 4 m up the plane. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.4 m
    M1 KE lost = PE gained: ½ × 0.4 × 8² = 0.4 × 10 × d sin 30°. A1 12.8 = 2d. A1 d = 6.4 m.
  2. (b) √24 = 4.90 m s⁻¹
    M1 ½ × 0.4v² = 12.8 − 0.4 × 10 × 4 × 0.5. A1 v = √24 = 4.90.
Q2Work-energy principle · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A child of mass 30 kg slides from rest down a playground slide in a park in Islamabad. The slide is 5 m long and its top is 2.5 m vertically above its bottom. The child reaches the bottom with speed 5 m s⁻¹. Find the work done against resistance. [3]
  2. (b) Assuming the resistance is constant, find its magnitude. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 375 J
    B1 PE lost = 30 × 10 × 2.5 = 750 J. B1 KE gained = ½ × 30 × 25 = 375 J. B1 WD against resistance = 375 J.
  2. (b) 75 N
    B1 375 ÷ 5 = 75 N.
Q3Work-energy principle · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A block of mass 5 kg is pulled from rest across a rough horizontal floor by a horizontal force of 40 N. The frictional force is 25 N. Find the work done by the 40 N force and the work done against friction when the block has moved 6 m. [2]
  2. (b) Use the work-energy principle to find the speed of the block at this point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 240 J and 150 J
    B1 40 × 6 = 240 J. B1 25 × 6 = 150 J.
  2. (b) 6 m s⁻¹
    M1 ½ × 5v² = 240 − 150. A1 v = 6.

Q6Work-energy principle · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A car of mass 1200 kg travels 400 m down a straight hill inclined at an angle α, where sin α = 0.05, with its engine switched off. The resistance to motion is a constant 300 N. The speed of the car at the top is 10 m s⁻¹. Find the loss in potential energy and the work done against resistance. [2]
  2. (b) Find the speed of the car at the bottom of the hill. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) PE loss 240 000 J; WD against resistance 120 000 J
    B1 1200 × 10 × 400 × 0.05 = 240 000. B1 300 × 400 = 120 000.
  2. (b) 17.3 m s⁻¹
    M1 work-energy equation: ½ × 1200(v² − 100) = 240 000 − 120 000. A1 v² = 300. A1 v = 17.3.

Q8Work-energy principle · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) The crate passes a point A with speed 1 m s⁻¹. Use the work-energy principle to find its speed after it has moved a further 15 m. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = 223 N; F = 55.7 N
    M1 R + 80 sin 20° = 250. A1 R = 223. A1 F = 0.25R = 55.7.
  2. (b) 4.94 m s⁻¹
    M1 WD by tension = 80 cos 20° × 15; WD against friction = F × 15. M1 ½ × 25(v² − 1) = difference. A1 v = 4.94.

Q11Work-energy principle · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A small block of mass 0.5 kg slides down a smooth curved ramp. It passes the point A with speed 2 m s⁻¹ and reaches the lowest point B, which is 3 m vertically below A. Find the speed of the block at B. [2]
  2. (b) From B the block moves along a rough horizontal surface, where the coefficient of friction is 0.25, and comes to rest at C. Find the distance BC and the work done against friction between B and C. [3]
  3. (c) The block is now replaced at A and projected with speed u m s⁻¹ so that it stops at a point 20 m beyond B. Find u. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8 m s⁻¹
    M1 ½v² = ½ × 2² + 10 × 3. A1 v = 8.
  2. (b) BC = 12.8 m; 16 J
    M1 friction = 0.25 × 5 = 1.25 N. M1 1.25 × BC = ½ × 0.5 × 64. A1 BC = 12.8 m and WD = 16 J.
  3. (c) u = √40 = 6.32
    M1 ½ × 0.5u² + 0.5 × 10 × 3 = 1.25 × 20. A1 u² = 40, u = 6.32.
Q12Work-energy principle · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block of mass 3 kg is projected with speed 12 m s⁻¹ up a line of greatest slope of a rough plane inclined at 30°. The coefficient of friction is 0.2. Using the work-energy principle, find the distance the block travels up the plane before coming to rest. [4]
  2. (b) Show that the block then slides back down, and find its speed when it returns to the point of projection. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10.7 m
    M1 F = 0.2 × 30 cos 30°. M1 ½ × 3 × 144 = 3 × 10 × d sin 30° + F d. A1 216 = d(15 + 6 cos 30°). A1 d = 10.7.
  2. (b) tan 30° = 0.577 > 0.2 so it slides back; speed 8.36 m s⁻¹
    B1 component of weight 15 N > maximum friction 6 cos 30° = 5.20 N. M1 ½ × 3v² = (15 − 5.196)d. A1 correct substitution. A1 v = 8.36.
Q13Work-energy principle · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A car of mass 1500 kg moves up a straight hill inclined at an angle α to the horizontal, where sin α = 0.1. The engine exerts a constant driving force of 2500 N and the resistance to motion is a constant 1500 N. The car passes point A at 25 m s⁻¹ and later passes point B at 15 m s⁻¹. Use the work-energy principle to find the distance AB. [4]
  2. (b) Find the work done by the driving force between A and B and the increase in potential energy. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 600 m
    M1 work-energy equation with four terms. A1 ½ × 1500(625 − 225) = 1500 × 10 × 0.1d + 1500d − 2500d. A1 300 000 = 500d. A1 d = 600 m.
  2. (b) 1 500 000 J; 900 000 J
    B1 2500 × 600. B1 1500 × 10 × 600 × 0.1.
Q14Work-energy principle · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². Particle A of mass 3 kg lies on a rough horizontal table, where the coefficient of friction is 0.2. It is attached to a light inextensible string which passes over a smooth pulley at the edge of the table to particle B of mass 2 kg, which hangs freely. The system is released from rest. Use an energy method to find the speed of the particles when B has fallen 1.5 m (A has not reached the pulley). [5]
  2. (b) At this instant the string breaks. Find how much further A travels along the table. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.90 m s⁻¹
    B1 PE lost by B = 2 × 10 × 1.5 = 30 J. B1 WD against friction = 0.2 × 30 × 1.5 = 9 J. M1 ½ × (3 + 2)v² = 30 − 9. A1 v² = 8.4. A1 v = 2.90.
  2. (b) 2.1 m
    M1 ½ × 3 × 8.4 = 0.2 × 30 × d. A1 d = 2.1 m.

Q26Power & driving force · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A car of mass 1000 kg has a constant power P W and experiences a constant resistance R N. Its greatest steady speed up a hill inclined at angle α, where sin α = 0.05, is 20 m s⁻¹, and its greatest steady speed down the same hill is 30 m s⁻¹. Find P and R. [5]

Show answer
  1. (a) P = 60 000, R = 2500

  2. (b) 24 m s⁻¹
    B1 60 000 ÷ 2500 = 24.
Q27Power & driving force · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) Show that the greatest steady speed V satisfies V³ + 200V − 15 000 = 0 and find V correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) −0.5 m s⁻² (slowing down)

  2. (b) V = 22.0 m s⁻¹
    M1 30 000/V = 400 + 2V². A1 V³ + 200V − 15 000 = 0. B1 V = 22.0 (by calculator / iteration).
Q28Power & driving force · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A car of mass 1000 kg accelerates along a straight level road. Its engine works at a constant 25 kW for 20 s, during which its speed increases from 10 m s⁻¹ to 25 m s⁻¹. Find the work done by the engine and the work done against resistance during these 20 s. [4]
  2. (b) The car travels 400 m in this time. Find the average resistance. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the driving force is not constant during the motion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 500 000 J by the engine; 237 500 J against resistance
    B1 WD by engine = 25 000 × 20 = 500 000 J. B1 KE gain = ½ × 1000 × (625 − 100) = 262 500 J. M1 WD against resistance = difference. A1 237 500 J.
  2. (b) 593.75 N (≈ 594 N)
    B1 237 500 ÷ 400.
  3. (c) Power is constant, so F = P/v decreases as v increases
    B1 F = P/v and v changes.
Q29Work done & power by a force · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A box is dragged at constant speed across a horizontal floor by a force of 50 N inclined at 30° above the horizontal. Find the work done by the force when the box moves 8 m. [2]
  2. (b) The box takes 4 s to move the 8 m. Find the average power of the force and the magnitude of the frictional force. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 346 J
    M1 50 cos 30° × 8. A1 346 J (200√3).
  2. (b) 86.6 W; 43.3 N
    B1 346 ÷ 4 = 86.6 W. B1 constant speed so friction = 50 cos 30° = 43.3 N.
Q30Work done & power by a force · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A pump on a farm near Multan raises water from a well. Each minute it lifts 300 kg of water through a vertical height of 15 m and delivers it at a speed of 4 m s⁻¹. Find the work done by the pump each minute. [3]
  2. (b) Find the power of the pump and the work it does in one hour. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 47 400 J
    B1 PE gain 300 × 10 × 15 = 45 000 J. B1 KE gain ½ × 300 × 16 = 2400 J. B1 47 400 J.
