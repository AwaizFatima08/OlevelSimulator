Q1Arrangements with conditions · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

The six letters of the word LAHORE are arranged in a line.

  1. (a) Find the number of different arrangements. [1]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements that begin with L and end with E. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 720
    6! = 720 (B1).
  2. (b) 24
    L and E fixed (M1); remaining 4 letters 4! = 24 (A1).
Q2Arrangements with conditions · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

Seven students, including Ali and Sara, stand in a line for a class photograph.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which Ali and Sara stand next to each other. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which Ali and Sara are not next to each other. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1440
    Treat as one unit: 6! × 2 (M1) = 1440 (A1).
  2. (b) 3600
    7! − 1440 (M1) = 5040 − 1440 = 3600 (A1).
Q3Arrangements with conditions · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

Four-digit numbers are formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. No digit may be used more than once.

  1. (a) How many different four-digit numbers can be formed? [1]
  2. (b) How many of these are even? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 840
    7 × 6 × 5 × 4 = 840 (B1).
  2. (b) 360
    Last digit 2, 4 or 6: 3 ways (M1); then 6 × 5 × 4: 3 × 120 = 360 (A1).
Q4Arrangements with conditions · Core · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Eight different cards, numbered 1 to 8, are arranged in a row.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which cards 1 and 2 are next to each other. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which cards 1 and 2 are next to each other and card 3 is at one end of the row. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10080
    M1 treat cards 1 and 2 as one block, giving 7 units: 7! arrangements; M1 × 2 for the order of 1 and 2 inside the block; A1 2 × 7! = 10080.
  2. (b) 2880
    M1 card 3 is at one of the 2 ends; M1 the block and the other 5 cards form 6 units in 6! ways, and the block has 2 internal orders; A1 2 × 6! × 2 = 2880.
Q5Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

The six letters of the word PLANET are arranged in a line.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which the two vowels are next to each other. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which the two vowels are not next to each other. [1]
  3. (c) Find the number of arrangements which begin and end with a consonant. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 240
    AE as a unit: 5! × 2 (M1) = 240 (A1).
  2. (b) 480
    720 − 240 = 480 (B1).
  3. (c) 288
    Ends: 4 × 3 = 12 (M1); middle 4! = 24; 12 × 24 = 288 (A1).
Q6Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

Four boys and three girls, all different, stand in a row.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which the three girls stand together. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which no two girls stand next to each other. [3]
  3. (c) Find the number of arrangements in which boys and girls alternate. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 720
    Girls as one unit: 5! × 3! (M1) = 720 (A1).
  2. (b) 1440
    Arrange boys 4! = 24 (B1). 5 gaps, choose and order 3 for the girls: 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 (M1). 24 × 60 = 1440 (A1).
  3. (c) 144
    Pattern BGBGBGB only (M1): 4! × 3! = 144 (A1).
Q7Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A shelf holds 10 different books: 5 mathematics books, 3 physics books and 2 chemistry books.

  1. (a) Find the number of ways of arranging the books so that the books of each subject are kept together. [3]
  2. (b) Find the number of ways of arranging the books so that no two physics books are next to each other. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8640
    Order of subjects 3! (M1); within subjects 5! × 3! × 2! (M1). 6 × 120 × 6 × 2 = 8640 (A1).
  2. (b) 1693440
    Arrange the other 7 books: 7! = 5040 (B1). 8 gaps, place the 3 physics books: 8 × 7 × 6 = 336 (M1). 5040 × 336 = 1 693 440 (A1).
Q8Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Five-digit numbers are formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, with no digit repeated.

  1. (a) How many of these numbers are greater than 50 000? [2]
  2. (b) How many of the numbers greater than 50 000 are odd? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1080
    First digit 5, 6 or 7 (M1): 3 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 1080 (A1).
  2. (b) 600
    Case first digit 5 or 7 (odd): last digit from the other 3 odd digits: 2 × 3 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 360 (M1 A1). Case first digit 6: last digit any of 4 odd digits: 1 × 4 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 240 (M1). Total 600 (A1).
Q9Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

A family of 3 adults and 4 children sit in a row of 7 chairs for a photograph.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which the 3 adults sit together. The children may be in any order. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which the 3 adults sit together and the 4 children sit together. [3]
  3. (c) Find the number of arrangements in which the first chair is occupied by a child. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 720
    M1 treat the 3 adults as one block: 5 units, 5! ways; M1 × 3! ways to order the adults; A1 120 × 6 = 720.
  2. (b) 288
    M1 two blocks can be ordered in 2! ways; M1 3! for the adults and 4! for the children; A1 2 × 6 × 24 = 288.
  3. (c) 2880
    M1 4 choices of child for the first chair, then 6! for the other six people; A1 4 × 720 = 2880.
Q10Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Four-digit numbers are formed from the digits 2, 3, 5, 6, 8 and 9. No digit may be repeated.

  1. (a) How many of these numbers are divisible by 5? [2]
  2. (b) How many of these numbers are divisible by 5 and greater than 5000? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 60
    M1 the last digit must be 5, the only available digit that is 0 or 5: 1 way; M1 the other three digits are chosen in order from the remaining 5 digits: 5 × 4 × 3 = 60; A1 60.
  2. (b) 36
    The last digit is 5 and the first digit must be 6, 8 or 9 (3 choices); M1 the middle two digits are chosen in order from the remaining 4 digits: 4 × 3 = 12; A1 3 × 12 = 36.
Q11Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Eight people, including Parveen, Qasim and Rehan, stand in a queue.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which Parveen and Qasim are at the two ends of the queue. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which exactly two of Parveen, Qasim and Rehan are next to each other (the third is not next to either of them). [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1440
    2 ways for the ends (M1), 6! for the rest: 1440 (A1).
  2. (b) 21600
    Arrange the other 5 people: 5! = 120 (B1). Choose the pair and its order: 3 × 2 = 6 (M1). Place the pair-unit and the third person in different gaps among the 6 gaps: 6 × 5 = 30 (M1). 120 × 6 × 30 = 21 600 (A1).
Q12Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Six men and four women, all different, sit in a row of ten seats at a wedding in Faisalabad.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which a man sits in each end seat and no two women sit next to each other. [4]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which all four women sit together. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 86400
    Arrange the men: 6! = 720 (B1). Women must go in the 5 gaps between men (not the ends) (M1): 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 = 120 (M1). 720 × 120 = 86 400 (A1).
  2. (b) 120960
    Treat the four women as one unit: 7 units in 7! ways (M1); women within the unit 4! ways: 5040 × 24 = 120 960 (A1).
Q13Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

Numbers between 3000 and 6000 are formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, with no digit repeated.

  1. (a) How many of these numbers are divisible by 5? [3]
  2. (b) How many of these numbers are odd? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 40
    Last digit must be 5 (B1). First digit 3 or 4 (5 is used) (M1): 2 × 5 × 4 = 40 (A1).
  2. (b) 200
    First digit 3: last from 1, 5, 7: 3 × 5 × 4 = 60 (M1). First 4: last from 1, 3, 5, 7: 4 × 20 = 80 (A1). First 5: last from 1, 3, 7: 60 (A1). Total 200 (A1).
Q14Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Seven students, P, Q, R, S, T, U and V, stand in a line.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which P does not stand at either end of the line. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which P does not stand at either end of the line and Q and R are not next to each other. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3600
    M1 P must be in one of the 5 middle positions; M1 × 6! for the other six people; A1 5 × 720 = 3600.
  2. (b) 2640
    M1 for each of the 5 middle positions of P, the 6 remaining positions contain exactly 4 adjacent pairs; M1 Q and R next to each other in 4 × 2 ways, and S, T, U, V in 4! ways: 192 per position, so 5 × 192 = 960 arrangements to exclude; A1 3600 − 960 = 2640.
Q15Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Ten people, including Aisha and Bilal, sit in a row of ten seats numbered 1 to 10 from left to right.

  1. (a) Find the number of seating arrangements in which Aisha sits in an odd-numbered seat and Bilal sits in an even-numbered seat. [3]
  2. (b) Find the probability that a random seating arrangement has Aisha in an odd-numbered seat and Bilal in an even-numbered seat. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1008000
    Aisha has 5 odd seats and Bilal has 5 even seats; the other 8 people sit in 8! = 40320 ways. 5 × 5 × 40320 = 1 008 000.
  2. (b) 5/18
    1 008 000/10! = 1 008 000/3 628 800 = 5/18.

Q18Selections (combinations) · Core · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A class has 9 students: 4 girls and 5 boys. A group of 3 students is chosen at random from the class.

  1. (a) Find the number of different groups that can be chosen. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of groups containing exactly one girl. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 84
    C(9,3) = 9!/(3!6!) = 84.
  2. (b) 40
    Choose 1 girl from 4 and 2 boys from 5: C(4,1) × C(5,2) = 4 × 10 = 40.

Q23Selections (combinations) · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

Twelve students are to be divided into three groups of four, to work on three different projects: Solar, Water and Recycling.

  1. (a) Find the number of ways of dividing the students into the three project groups. [2]
  2. (b) The three groups of four are formed as unnamed groups, with no project attached. Find the number of ways of doing this. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 34650
    The groups are distinct because each goes to a different project: 12!/(4! × 4! × 4!) = 34650.
  2. (b) 5775
    34650 ÷ 3! = 34650 ÷ 6 = 5775, since the three groups can be put in 3! orders.

Q25Selections (combinations) · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) Find the number of panels that contain at least one woman. [2]

Q26Selections (combinations) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Q30Selections (combinations) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) Find the number of ways of choosing three numbers whose sum is odd. [2]
Show answer

    Total C(15,3) = 455; 455 − 231 = 224.

Q32Probability via selections · Core · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A box holds 8 chocolates: 3 dark and 5 milk. Three chocolates are taken at random without replacement.

  1. (a) Find the probability that exactly two of the chocolates are dark. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that at least two of the chocolates are milk. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15/56
    C(3,2) × C(5,1)/C(8,3) = 3 × 5/56 = 15/56.
  2. (b) 5/7
    Exactly 2 milk and 1 dark: C(5,2) × C(3,1) = 30; all 3 milk: C(5,3) = 10. Total 40 out of C(8,3) = 56, so 40/56 = 5/7.
Q33Probability via selections · Core · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A club has 6 women and 4 men. A group of 4 people is chosen at random from the 10 members.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the group contains exactly 2 women. [3]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the group contains at least 3 women. [3]
Show answer

  2. (b) 1/5
    Fix E at each end (two of the three Es). The middle four letters E, L, V, N are all different: 4! = 24. 24/120 = 1/5.
Q74Probability via arrangements · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 9 marks

Three married couples sit in a random order in a row of six chairs.

  1. (a) Find the probability that each couple sits next to each other, with both people of each couple in adjacent chairs. [4]
  2. (b) Find the probability that no couple sits next to each other. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1/15
    Treat each couple as one block: 3! = 6 orders of the blocks, and each couple can be ordered in 2 ways: 2³ = 8. 6 × 8 = 48 out of 6! = 720, so 48/720 = 1/15.
  2. (b) 1/3
    Inclusion–exclusion: 6! − 3 × 5! × 2 + 3 × 4! × 4 − 3! × 8 = 720 − 720 + 288 − 48 = 240. Probability 240/720 = 1/3.
Q75Probability via arrangements · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A four-digit number is made by arranging four different digits chosen from 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5. Every such number is equally likely.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the number is even and greater than 3000. Give your answer as a fraction. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1/4
    Total 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 = 120. Even: last digit 2 or 4. Last 2: first digit 3, 4 or 5 (3 choices), middle two from the remaining 3 digits in 3 × 2 = 6 ways: 18. Last 4: first digit 3 or 5 (2 choices), middle 6 ways: 12. Favourable 30, so 30/120 = 1/4.
