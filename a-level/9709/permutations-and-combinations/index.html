Q1Arrangements with conditions · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

The six letters of the word LAHORE are arranged in a line.

  1. (a) Find the number of different arrangements. [1]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements that begin with L and end with E. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 720
    6! = 720 (B1).
  2. (b) 24
    L and E fixed (M1); remaining 4 letters 4! = 24 (A1).
Q2Arrangements with conditions · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

Seven students, including Ali and Sara, stand in a line for a class photograph.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which Ali and Sara stand next to each other. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which Ali and Sara are not next to each other. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1440
    Treat as one unit: 6! × 2 (M1) = 1440 (A1).
  2. (b) 3600
    7! − 1440 (M1) = 5040 − 1440 = 3600 (A1).
Q3Arrangements with conditions · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks

Four-digit numbers are formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7. No digit may be used more than once.

  1. (a) How many different four-digit numbers can be formed? [1]
  2. (b) How many of these are even? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 840
    7 × 6 × 5 × 4 = 840 (B1).
  2. (b) 360
    Last digit 2, 4 or 6: 3 ways (M1); then 6 × 5 × 4: 3 × 120 = 360 (A1).
Q4Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

The six letters of the word PLANET are arranged in a line.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which the two vowels are next to each other. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which the two vowels are not next to each other. [1]
  3. (c) Find the number of arrangements which begin and end with a consonant. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 240
    AE as a unit: 5! × 2 (M1) = 240 (A1).
  2. (b) 480
    720 − 240 = 480 (B1).
  3. (c) 288
    Ends: 4 × 3 = 12 (M1); middle 4! = 24; 12 × 24 = 288 (A1).
Q5Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

Four boys and three girls, all different, stand in a row.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which the three girls stand together. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which no two girls stand next to each other. [3]
  3. (c) Find the number of arrangements in which boys and girls alternate. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 720
    Girls as one unit: 5! × 3! (M1) = 720 (A1).
  2. (b) 1440
    Arrange boys 4! = 24 (B1). 5 gaps, choose and order 3 for the girls: 5 × 4 × 3 = 60 (M1). 24 × 60 = 1440 (A1).
  3. (c) 144
    Pattern BGBGBGB only (M1): 4! × 3! = 144 (A1).
Q6Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A shelf holds 10 different books: 5 mathematics books, 3 physics books and 2 chemistry books.

  1. (a) Find the number of ways of arranging the books so that the books of each subject are kept together. [3]
  2. (b) Find the number of ways of arranging the books so that no two physics books are next to each other. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8640
    Order of subjects 3! (M1); within subjects 5! × 3! × 2! (M1). 6 × 120 × 6 × 2 = 8640 (A1).
  2. (b) 1693440
    Arrange the other 7 books: 7! = 5040 (B1). 8 gaps, place the 3 physics books: 8 × 7 × 6 = 336 (M1). 5040 × 336 = 1 693 440 (A1).
Q7Arrangements with conditions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Five-digit numbers are formed using the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, with no digit repeated.

  1. (a) How many of these numbers are greater than 50 000? [2]
  2. (b) How many of the numbers greater than 50 000 are odd? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1080
    First digit 5, 6 or 7 (M1): 3 × 6 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 1080 (A1).
  2. (b) 600
    Case first digit 5 or 7 (odd): last digit from the other 3 odd digits: 2 × 3 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 360 (M1 A1). Case first digit 6: last digit any of 4 odd digits: 1 × 4 × 5 × 4 × 3 = 240 (M1). Total 600 (A1).
Q8Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Eight people, including Parveen, Qasim and Rehan, stand in a queue.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which Parveen and Qasim are at the two ends of the queue. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which exactly two of Parveen, Qasim and Rehan are next to each other (the third is not next to either of them). [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1440
    2 ways for the ends (M1), 6! for the rest: 1440 (A1).
  2. (b) 21600
    Arrange the other 5 people: 5! = 120 (B1). Choose the pair and its order: 3 × 2 = 6 (M1). Place the pair-unit and the third person in different gaps among the 6 gaps: 6 × 5 = 30 (M1). 120 × 6 × 30 = 21 600 (A1).
Q9Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Six men and four women, all different, sit in a row of ten seats at a wedding in Faisalabad.

  1. (a) Find the number of arrangements in which a man sits in each end seat and no two women sit next to each other. [4]
  2. (b) Find the number of arrangements in which all four women sit together. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 86400
    Arrange the men: 6! = 720 (B1). Women must go in the 5 gaps between men (not the ends) (M1): 5 × 4 × 3 × 2 = 120 (M1). 720 × 120 = 86 400 (A1).
  2. (b) 120960
    Treat the four women as one unit: 7 units in 7! ways (M1); women within the unit 4! ways: 5040 × 24 = 120 960 (A1).
Q10Arrangements with conditions · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

Numbers between 3000 and 6000 are formed from the digits 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, with no digit repeated.

  1. (a) How many of these numbers are divisible by 5? [3]
  2. (b) How many of these numbers are odd? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 40
    Last digit must be 5 (B1). First digit 3 or 4 (5 is used) (M1): 2 × 5 × 4 = 40 (A1).
  2. (b) 200
    First digit 3: last from 1, 5, 7: 3 × 5 × 4 = 60 (M1). First 4: last from 1, 3, 5, 7: 4 × 20 = 80 (A1). First 5: last from 1, 3, 7: 60 (A1). Total 200 (A1).

A committee of 5 people is to be chosen from 7 men and 6 women.

  1. (a) In how many ways can the committee be chosen? [1]
  2. (b) In how many ways can it be chosen if it contains exactly 2 women? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1287
    ¹³C₅ = 1287 (B1).
  2. (b) 525
    ⁶C₂ × ⁷C₃ = 15 × 35 (M1) = 525 (A1).
Q12Selections (combinations) · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A cricket squad has 15 players, including the captain and the vice-captain. A team of 11 is chosen from the squad.

  1. (a) How many different teams include the captain? [2]
  2. (b) How many different teams include the captain but not the vice-captain? [2]

  1. (a) 1001
    Captain in; choose 10 from 14 (M1): ¹⁴C₁₀ = 1001 (A1).
  2. (b) 286
    Choose 10 from 13 (M1): ¹³C₁₀ = 286 (A1).
Q13Selections (combinations) · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A team of 4 is to be selected from 5 men and 4 women.

  1. (a) Find the number of possible teams. [1]
  2. (b) Find the number of teams that contain at least 2 women. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 126
    ⁹C₄ = 126 (B1).
  2. (b) 81
    2 women: ⁴C₂ × ⁵C₂ = 60; 3 women: ⁴C₃ × 5 = 20; 4 women: 1 (M1 A1). Total 81 (A1).
Q14Selections (combinations) · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

Six students are to be chosen from a class of 12 to represent their school at a science fair in Peshawar. Two of the students, Hina and Bilal, refuse to be in the group together.

  1. (a) Find the number of possible groups. [3]
  2. (b) Find the number of groups that contain Hina but not Bilal. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 714
    Total ¹²C₆ = 924 (B1). Groups with both: ¹⁰C₄ = 210 (M1). 924 − 210 = 714 (A1).
  2. (b) 252
    ¹⁰C₅ = 252 (B1).
Q15Selections (combinations) · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A restaurant in Lahore offers 6 different starters, 8 different main courses and 4 different desserts.

  1. (a) A customer chooses one starter, one main course and one dessert. How many different meals are possible? [1]
  2. (b) A family chooses 4 different dishes from the whole menu. Find the number of selections that include at least one main course. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 192
    6 × 8 × 4 = 192 (B1).
  2. (b) 2850
    Total ¹⁸C₄ = 3060 (B1). No main course: ¹⁰C₄ = 210 (M1). 3060 − 210 = 2850 (A1).
Q16Selections (combinations) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A craft box contains 9 different beads: 4 red, 3 blue and 2 green. Five beads are chosen to make a bracelet.

  1. (a) Find the number of selections that contain at least one bead of each colour. [4]
  2. (b) Find the number of selections with exactly 3 red beads. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 98
    Total ⁹C₅ = 126 (B1). No red: ⁵C₅ = 1; no blue: ⁶C₅ = 6; no green: ⁷C₅ = 21 (M1 A1). Two colours missing is impossible with 5 beads. 126 − 28 = 98 (A1).
  2. (b) 40
    Choose 3 of the 4 red beads and 2 of the 5 non-red beads: ⁴C₃ × ⁵C₂ (M1) = 4 × 10 = 40 (A1).
Q17Selections (combinations) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Nine volunteers at a flood-relief camp are to be divided into groups.

  1. (a) Find the number of ways of dividing them into a group of 4, a group of 3 and a group of 2. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of ways of dividing them into three groups of 3, where the groups are not labelled. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1260
    ⁹C₄ × ⁵C₃ × ²C₂ = 126 × 10 × 1 (M1) = 1260 (A1).
  2. (b) 280
    ⁹C₃ × ⁶C₃ × ³C₃ = 1680 (M1). Groups unlabelled: divide by 3! (M1). 1680/6 = 280 (A1).
Q18Selections (combinations) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A project committee of 6 is chosen from 8 Pakistani engineers and 5 Chinese engineers.

  1. (a) Find the number of committees with more Pakistani than Chinese engineers. [3]
  2. (b) Two of the Pakistani engineers refuse to serve together. Find the number of committees with more Pakistani than Chinese engineers that do not include both of them. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1008
    4P 2C: 70 × 10 = 700; 5P 1C: 56 × 5 = 280; 6P: 28 (M1 A1). Total 1008 (A1).
  2. (b) 743
    Committees containing both: 4P 2C: ⁶C₂ × 10 = 150; 5P 1C: ⁶C₃ × 5 = 100; 6P: ⁶C₄ = 15, total 265 (M1 A1). 1008 − 265 = 743 (A1).
Q19Probability via selections · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A bag contains 5 red sweets and 4 blue sweets. Three sweets are taken at random without replacement.

  1. (a) Find the probability that all three sweets are red. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that exactly one sweet is red. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5/42
    ⁵C₃/⁹C₃ = 10/84 (M1) = 5/42 (A1).
  2. (b) 5/14
    ⁵C₁ × ⁴C₂/⁹C₃ = 30/84 (M1) = 5/14 (A1).
Q20Probability via selections · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A committee of 4 is chosen at random from 6 men and 5 women.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the committee contains more women than men. [3]
  2. (b) Find the probability that a particular man, Usman, is on the committee. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 13/66
    3W 1M: ⁵C₃ × 6 = 60; 4W: 5 (M1). Total ¹¹C₄ = 330 (B1). 65/330 = 13/66 (A1).
  2. (b) 4/11
    ¹⁰C₃/¹¹C₄ = 120/330 (M1) = 4/11 (A1).
Q21Probability via selections · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A box of 12 LED bulbs contains 3 faulty bulbs. An electrician takes 4 bulbs at random.

  1. (a) Find the probability that none of the 4 bulbs is faulty. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that at least one is faulty. [1]
  3. (c) Find the probability that exactly 2 are faulty. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 14/55
    ⁹C₄/¹²C₄ = 126/495 (M1) = 14/55 (A1).
  2. (b) 41/55
    1 − 14/55 = 41/55 (B1).
  3. (c) 12/55
    ³C₂ × ⁹C₂/495 = 108/495 (M1) = 12/55 (A1).
Q22Probability via selections · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Four married couples attend a dinner. Four of the eight people are chosen at random to give short speeches.

  1. (a) Find the probability that no married couple is chosen. [3]
  2. (b) Find the probability that exactly one married couple is chosen. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8/35
    Total ⁸C₄ = 70 (B1). No couple: one person from each couple, 2⁴ = 16 (M1). 16/70 = 8/35 (A1).
  2. (b) 24/35
    Choose the couple: 4; choose 2 of the other 3 couples, one person from each: ³C₂ × 2² = 12 (M1). 4 × 12 = 48 (A1). 48/70 = 24/35 (A1).
Q23Probability via selections · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A debate team of 5 is chosen at random from 7 girls and 6 boys.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the team contains at least 2 girls. [3]
  2. (b) Given that the team contains at least 2 girls, find the probability that it contains exactly 3 girls. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 392/429
    P(0 girls) = ⁶C₅/1287 = 6/1287; P(1 girl) = 7 × ⁶C₄/1287 = 105/1287 (M1 A1). 1 − 111/1287 = 1176/1287 = 392/429 (A1).
  2. (b) 25/56
    P(exactly 3 girls) = ⁷C₃ × ⁶C₂/1287 = 525/1287 (M1). Conditional: 525/1176 (M1) = 25/56 (A1).

Q25Arrangements with repeated letters · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

The letters of the word BANANAS are arranged in a line.

Q26Arrangements with repeated letters · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

The letters of the word TOMORROW are arranged in a line.

  1. (a) Find the number of different arrangements. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of different arrangements which begin and end with O. [2]
  3. (c) Find the number of different arrangements in which the two R's are not next to each other. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3360
    8 letters with O × 3 and R × 2: 8!/(3! 2!) (M1) = 3360 (A1).
  2. (b) 360
    Middle letters T, O, M, R, R, W: 6!/2! (M1) = 360 (A1).
  3. (c) 2520
    R's together: RR as a unit, 7!/3! = 840 (M1). 3360 − 840 = 2520 (A1).
Q27Arrangements with repeated letters · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 7 marks

The nine letters of the word ISLAMABAD are arranged in a line.

  1. (a) Find the number of different arrangements. [2]
  2. (b) Find the number of different arrangements in which no two A's are next to each other. [3]
  3. (c) Find the number of different arrangements that begin and end with A. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 60480
    A appears 3 times: 9!/3! (M1) = 60 480 (A1).
  2. (b) 25200
    Arrange I, S, L, M, B, D: 6! = 720 (B1). Choose 3 of the 7 gaps for the A's: ⁷C₃ = 35 (M1). 720 × 35 = 25 200 (A1).
  3. (c) 5040
    Middle 7 letters I, S, L, M, B, D, A all different: 7! (M1) = 5040 (A1).
Q28Probability via arrangements · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

The six letters of the word QUETTA are arranged in a random order.

  1. (a) Find the number of different arrangements. [1]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the two T's are next to each other. [2]
  3. (c) Find the probability that the arrangement begins and ends with a vowel. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 360
    6!/2! = 360 (B1).
  2. (b) 1/3
    TT as a unit: 5! = 120 (M1). 120/360 = 1/3 (A1).
  3. (c) 1/5
    Choose and order 2 of the 3 vowels U, E, A for the ends: 3 × 2 = 6 (M1). Middle 4 letters with T twice: 4!/2! = 12 (M1). 72/360 = 1/5 (A1).
Q29Probability via arrangements · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Five boys and three girls stand in a line in a random order.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the three girls are all next to each other. [3]
  2. (b) Find the probability that there is a boy at each end of the line. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3/28
    Girls as a unit: 6! × 3! = 4320 (M1). Total 8! = 40 320 (B1). 4320/40 320 = 3/28 (A1).
  2. (b) 5/14
    5 × 4 × 6! = 14 400 (M1). 14 400/40 320 = 5/14 (A1).
Q30Probability via arrangements · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

The eight letters of the word PESHAWAR are arranged in a random order.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the arrangement begins with a vowel. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that there are exactly two letters between the two A's. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3/8
    Begins with A: 7! = 5040; begins with E: 7!/2! = 2520 (M1). (5040 + 2520)/20 160 = 3/8 (A1).
  2. (b) 5/28
    Total 8!/2! = 20 160 (B1). Positions for the A's: (1,4), (2,5), (3,6), (4,7), (5,8): 5 ways (M1). Other 6 letters 6! = 720 (M1). 3600/20 160 = 5/28 (A1).
