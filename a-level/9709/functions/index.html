Q1Transformations of graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The point P(4, −3) lies on the curve y = f(x). State the coordinates of the image of P on the curve y = f(x + 2). [1]
  2. (b) State the coordinates of the image of P on the curve y = 3f(x). [1]
  3. (c) State the coordinates of the image of P on the curve y = f(−x) − 1. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2, −3)
    B1: translation by 2 units in the negative x-direction.
  2. (b) (4, −9)
    B1: stretch parallel to the y-axis, y-coordinate multiplied by 3.
  3. (c) (−4, −4)
    B1 x-coordinate −4 (reflection in the y-axis); B1 y-coordinate −4 (translation 1 unit down).
Q2Transformations of graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe fully the single transformation that maps the curve y = sin x onto the curve y = sin(x/3). [2]
  2. (b) Describe fully the single transformation that maps the curve y = sin x onto the curve y = −sin x. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A stretch parallel to the x-axis with scale factor 3 (y-axis invariant).
    B1 stretch parallel to the x-axis; B1 scale factor 3.
  2. (b) A reflection in the x-axis.
    B1 reflection in the x-axis (or y = 0).
Q3Transformations of graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = x² − 4x + 7 is translated by the vector (−1, 3), written as a column vector with x-component −1 and y-component 3. Find the equation of the new curve in the form y = x² + bx + c. [3]
  2. (b) State the coordinates of the minimum point of the new curve. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = x² − 2x + 7
    M1 replace x by (x + 1): y = (x + 1)² − 4(x + 1) + 7; M1 add 3; A1 y = x² − 2x + 7.
  2. (b) (1, 6)
    B1: original minimum (2, 3) moves to (1, 6); or complete the square (x − 1)² + 6.
Q4Transformations of graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = x² − 5x is reflected in the x-axis and then translated by the vector (0, 6). Find the equation of the new curve. [2]
  2. (b) Find the x-coordinates of the points where the new curve meets the x-axis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −x² + 5x + 6
    B1 reflection: y = −x² + 5x; B1 add 6.
  2. (b) x = −1 and x = 6
    M1 −(x² − 5x − 6) = −(x − 6)(x + 1) = 0; A1 x = 6, x = −1.
Q5Transformations of graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = f(x) is transformed to the curve y = 2f(x − 1) + 3. Describe fully a sequence of transformations that maps y = f(x) onto y = 2f(x − 1) + 3, making clear the order in which they are applied. [4]
  2. (b) The point (3, 5) lies on y = f(x). Find the coordinates of its image on y = 2f(x − 1) + 3. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Translation by (1, 0); then stretch parallel to the y-axis, scale factor 2; then translation by (0, 3). (Equivalent: stretch in y, factor 2, then translation (1, 3).)
    B1 translation 1 unit in positive x-direction; B1 stretch parallel to the y-axis, factor 2; B1 translation 3 units in positive y-direction; B1 correct order (the y-translation must come after the stretch).
  2. (b) (4, 13)
    B1 x = 3 + 1 = 4; B1 y = 2 × 5 + 3 = 13.
Q6Transformations of graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Express 2x² − 12x + 13 in the form a(x − b)² + c, where a, b and c are constants. [3]
  2. (b) Hence describe fully a sequence of two transformations that maps the curve y = x² onto the curve y = 2x² − 12x + 13. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2(x − 3)² − 5
    B1 a = 2; B1 b = 3; B1 c = −5.
  2. (b) Stretch parallel to the y-axis with scale factor 2, followed by a translation by the vector (3, −5).
    B1 stretch, parallel to y-axis, factor 2; B1 translation (3, −5); B1 order correct (stretch first). [Translation (3, 0), stretch factor 2, translation (0, −5) also acceptable.]
Q7Transformations of graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = x² + 2x for x ∈ ℝ. The curve y = f(x) is reflected in the y-axis and then translated by the vector (3, 0). The resulting curve is y = g(x). Find g(x) in the form x² + px + q. [3]
  2. (b) Find the x-coordinates of the points where y = g(x) crosses the x-axis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) g(x) = x² − 8x + 15
    M1 reflection gives y = f(−x) = x² − 2x; M1 replace x by (x − 3): (x − 3)² − 2(x − 3); A1 x² − 8x + 15.
  2. (b) x = 3 and x = 5
    M1 factorise (x − 3)(x − 5) = 0; A1 both values.
Q8Transformations of graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Show that √(4x − 8) can be written in the form k√(x − 2), stating the value of k. [1]
  2. (b) Hence describe fully a sequence of two transformations that maps the curve y = √x onto the curve y = √(4x − 8). [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 2
    B1 √(4(x − 2)) = 2√(x − 2).
  2. (b) Translation by the vector (2, 0) and a stretch parallel to the y-axis with scale factor 2 (in either order).
    B1 translation (2, 0); B1 stretch parallel to the y-axis; B1 scale factor 2. [Alternative: translation (8, 0) followed by stretch parallel to x-axis, factor ¼.]
Q9Transformations of graphs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = x³ is transformed by the following sequence: a translation by the vector (2, 0); then a stretch parallel to the y-axis with scale factor ½; then a reflection in the x-axis. Find the equation of the resulting curve. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the point where the resulting curve crosses the y-axis. [1]
  3. (c) Find the image of the point (1, 1) under the same sequence of transformations. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −½(x − 2)³
    B1 y = (x − 2)³; B1 y = ½(x − 2)³; B1 y = −½(x − 2)³.
  2. (b) (0, 4)
    B1 y = −½(−2)³ = 4.
  3. (c) (3, −½)
    B1 (3, 1) after translation, (3, ½) after stretch; B1 (3, −½).
Q10Transformations of graphs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = f(x) has a maximum point at (6, 10) and crosses the x-axis at (2, 0). The function g is defined by g(x) = 5 − 2f(½x). Find the coordinates of the stationary point on y = g(x) and state whether it is a maximum or a minimum. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the image of (2, 0) on y = g(x). [1]
  3. (c) Describe fully a sequence of transformations that maps y = f(x) onto y = g(x). [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (12, −15), a minimum point
    B1 x = 6 × 2 = 12; B1 y = 5 − 2 × 10 = −15; B1 minimum (the factor −2 reflects the curve in the x-axis).
  2. (b) (4, 5)
    B1 x = 4, y = 5 − 0 = 5.
  3. (c) Stretch parallel to the x-axis, factor 2; stretch parallel to the y-axis, factor 2; reflection in the x-axis; translation by (0, 5) — the translation applied last.
    B1 x-stretch factor 2; B1 y-stretch factor 2; B1 reflection in x-axis; B1 translation (0, 5) after the stretch and reflection (the stretch/reflection may be combined as a y-stretch with factor −2).
Q11Transformations of graphs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = x² − 6x + 5 for x ∈ ℝ. The curve y = f(x) is mapped onto the curve y = f(x + k) + m by a translation, and the vertex of the new curve is at (−1, 2). Find the values of k and m. [3]
  2. (b) Find the y-intercept of the new curve. [1]
  3. (c) Determine whether the new curve meets the line y = x + 4, giving a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 4, m = 6
    M1 complete the square: f(x) = (x − 3)² − 4, vertex (3, −4); A1 k = 4 (3 − k = −1); A1 m = 6 (−4 + m = 2).
  2. (b) 3
    B1 (0 + 1)² + 2 = 3.
  3. (c) Yes, at two points: x² + x − 1 = 0 has discriminant 5 > 0.
    M1 (x + 1)² + 2 = x + 4 gives x² + x − 1 = 0; A1 discriminant 1 + 4 = 5 > 0, so two points of intersection.
Q12Inverse function · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 5x − 3 for x ∈ ℝ. Find an expression for f⁻¹(x). [2]
  2. (b) Find the value of f⁻¹(12). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) f⁻¹(x) = (x + 3)/5
    M1 y = 5x − 3 ⇒ x = (y + 3)/5; A1.
  2. (b) 3
    B1 (12 + 3)/5 = 3.
Q13Inverse function · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = (2x + 1)/(x − 3) for x ≠ 3. Find an expression for f⁻¹(x). [3]
  2. (b) Verify your answer by finding f(5) and then f⁻¹ of your result. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) f⁻¹(x) = (3x + 1)/(x − 2), x ≠ 2
    M1 y(x − 3) = 2x + 1; M1 collect x terms: x(y − 2) = 3y + 1; A1 f⁻¹(x) = (3x + 1)/(x − 2).
  2. (b) f(5) = 11/2 and f⁻¹(11/2) = 5
    B1 f(5) = 11/2; B1 f⁻¹(11/2) = (33/2 + 1)/(11/2 − 2) = (35/2)/(7/2) = 5.
Q14Inverse function · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The function g is defined by g(x) = 7 − 3x for −2 ≤ x ≤ 4. Find the range of g. [2]
  2. (b) Find an expression for g⁻¹(x) and state the domain of g⁻¹. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) −5 ≤ g(x) ≤ 13
    M1 g(4) = −5, g(−2) = 13; A1 correct inequality.
  2. (b) g⁻¹(x) = (7 − x)/3, −5 ≤ x ≤ 13
    M1 x = (7 − y)/3; A1; B1 domain equals the range of g.
Q15Inverse function · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = x² − 8x + 21 for x ≥ 4. Express f(x) in the form (x − a)² + b. [2]
  2. (b) State the range of f. [1]
  3. (c) Find an expression for f⁻¹(x) and state the domain of f⁻¹. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x − 4)² + 5
    B1 a = 4; B1 b = 5.
  2. (b) f(x) ≥ 5
    B1 (minimum value 5 at x = 4).
  3. (c) f⁻¹(x) = 4 + √(x − 5), domain x ≥ 5
    M1 y − 5 = (x − 4)²; A1 x = 4 + √(y − 5) (positive root as x ≥ 4); B1 domain x ≥ 5.
Q16Inverse function · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 3 − 2x² for x ≤ 0. Explain why f has an inverse, but the function g(x) = 3 − 2x² for x ∈ ℝ does not. [2]
  2. (b) State the range of f. [1]
  3. (c) Find an expression for f⁻¹(x). [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) f is one-one on x ≤ 0 (strictly increasing there); g is not one-one, e.g. g(1) = g(−1) = 1.
    B1 f is one-one / increasing for x ≤ 0; B1 g is many-one, with a correct example or reference to symmetry about x = 0.
  2. (b) f(x) ≤ 3
    B1.
  3. (c) f⁻¹(x) = −√((3 − x)/2), for x ≤ 3
    M1 x² = (3 − y)/2; M1 square root; A1 negative root chosen because the domain of f is x ≤ 0.
Q17Inverse function · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 2x² + 12x + 11 for x ≥ k. Express 2x² + 12x + 11 in the form a(x + b)² + c. [3]
  2. (b) State the smallest value of k for which f has an inverse. [1]
  3. (c) For this value of k, find an expression for f⁻¹(x) and state its domain. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2(x + 3)² − 7
    B1 a = 2; B1 b = 3; B1 c = −7.
  2. (b) k = −3
    B1 (x-coordinate of the vertex).
  3. (c) f⁻¹(x) = −3 + √((x + 7)/2), x ≥ −7
    M1 (x + 3)² = (y + 7)/2; A1 x = −3 + √((y + 7)/2); B1 domain x ≥ −7.
Q18Inverse function · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 2 + 4/(x − 1) for x > 1. State the range of f. [1]
  2. (b) Find an expression for f⁻¹(x). [3]
  3. (c) State the domain and the range of f⁻¹. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) f(x) > 2
    B1.
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = 1 + 4/(x − 2)
    M1 y − 2 = 4/(x − 1); M1 x − 1 = 4/(y − 2); A1 f⁻¹(x) = 1 + 4/(x − 2).
  3. (c) Domain x > 2; range f⁻¹(x) > 1
    B1 domain; B1 range.
Q19Inverse function · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = x² − 4x + 6 for x ≥ 2. Find an expression for f⁻¹(x) and state its domain. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the solutions of f(x) = f⁻¹(x) are the same as the solutions of f(x) = x for this function. [1]
  3. (c) Hence solve the equation f(x) = f⁻¹(x). [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) f⁻¹(x) = 2 + √(x − 2), x ≥ 2
    M1 complete the square: (x − 2)² + 2; A1 f⁻¹(x) = 2 + √(x − 2); B1 domain x ≥ 2.
  2. (b) f is increasing, so the graphs of y = f(x) and y = f⁻¹(x) (reflections of each other in y = x) can only meet on the line y = x.
    B1 reference to reflection in y = x with f increasing.
  3. (c) x = 2 and x = 3
    M1 x² − 4x + 6 = x ⇒ x² − 5x + 6 = 0; A1 (x − 2)(x − 3) = 0; A1 x = 2, 3 (both in the domain).
Q20Inverse function · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 3 + 2 sin x for −½π ≤ x ≤ ½π. State the range of f. [1]
  2. (b) Find an expression for f⁻¹(x). [2]
  3. (c) Find the exact value of f⁻¹(4). [1]
  4. (d) Explain why f would not have an inverse if its domain were changed to 0 ≤ x ≤ π. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 ≤ f(x) ≤ 5
    B1.
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = sin⁻¹((x − 3)/2), 1 ≤ x ≤ 5
    M1 sin x = (y − 3)/2; A1.
  3. (c) π/6
    B1 sin⁻¹(½) = π/6.
  4. (d) On 0 ≤ x ≤ π the function is not one-one, e.g. f(π/6) = f(5π/6) = 4.
    B1 not one-one with a reason.
Q21Composite function · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The functions f and g are defined by f(x) = 2x − 5 for x ∈ ℝ and g(x) = 1/(x + 1) for x ≠ −1. Find the value of gf(3). [2]
  2. (b) Find an expression for fg(x) as a single fraction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ½
    M1 f(3) = 1; A1 g(1) = ½.
  2. (b) fg(x) = (−5x − 3)/(x + 1)
    M1 2/(x + 1) − 5; A1 (2 − 5x − 5)/(x + 1) = (−5x − 3)/(x + 1).
Q22Composite function · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The functions f and g are defined for x ∈ ℝ by f(x) = 3x + 2 and g(x) = x² − 1. Find expressions for fg(x) and gf(x). [3]
  2. (b) Solve the equation fg(x) = gf(x), giving your answers in exact form. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) fg(x) = 3x² − 1; gf(x) = 9x² + 12x + 3
    B1 fg(x) = 3(x² − 1) + 2 = 3x² − 1; M1 (3x + 2)² − 1; A1 9x² + 12x + 3.
  2. (b) x = −1 ± √3/3
    M1 6x² + 12x + 4 = 0 ⇒ 3x² + 6x + 2 = 0; M1 formula; A1 x = (−6 ± √12)/6 = −1 ± √3/3 (≈ −0.423, −1.58).
Q23Composite function · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The functions f and g are defined for x ∈ ℝ by f(x) = x² + 4x and g(x) = 2x − 3. Find and simplify an expression for gf(x). [2]
  2. (b) Solve the equation fg(x) = 12. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) gf(x) = 2x² + 8x − 3
    M1 2(x² + 4x) − 3; A1.
  2. (b) x = 5/2 or x = −3/2
    M1 (2x − 3)² + 4(2x − 3) = 12; M1 put u = 2x − 3: u² + 4u − 12 = 0, u = 2 or −6; A1 x = 5/2, −3/2.
Q24Composite function · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = (x + 3)/(x − 1) for x ≠ 1. Show that ff(x) = x. [3]
  2. (b) Hence state an expression for f⁻¹(x). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ff(x) = x (shown)
    M1 substitute: ((x + 3)/(x − 1) + 3) / ((x + 3)/(x − 1) − 1); M1 multiply through by (x − 1): (4x)/(4); A1 = x.
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = (x + 3)/(x − 1)
    B1 f is self-inverse.
Q25Composite function · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = x² − 6x + 10 for x ≥ 3, and g is defined by g(x) = 2x + k for x ≥ 0, where k is a constant. State the range of f and the range of g in terms of k. [2]
  2. (b) Find the least value of k for which the composite function fg exists. [2]
  3. (c) Using k = 3, solve the equation fg(x) = 17. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) f(x) ≥ 1; g(x) ≥ k
    B1 f(x) = (x − 3)² + 1 ≥ 1; B1 g(x) ≥ k.
  2. (b) k = 3
    M1 range of g must lie within the domain of f (x ≥ 3); A1 k ≥ 3, least value 3.
  3. (c) x = 2
    M1 fg(x) = (2x + 3 − 3)² + 1 = 4x² + 1; M1 4x² = 16; A1 x = 2 only (x = −2 is outside the domain of g).
Q26Composite function · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The functions f and g are defined for x ∈ ℝ by f(x) = 2x + 1 and g(x) = x² − 2. Find f⁻¹(x) and gf(x). [2]
  2. (b) Solve the equation gf(x) = f⁻¹(x). [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) f⁻¹(x) = (x − 1)/2; gf(x) = 4x² + 4x − 1
    B1 f⁻¹; B1 gf.
  2. (b) x = 1/8 or x = −1
    M1 4x² + 4x − 1 = (x − 1)/2 ⇒ 8x² + 7x − 1 = 0; M1 (8x − 1)(x + 1) = 0; A1 both values.
Q27Domain & range · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 5 − (x − 2)² for 0 ≤ x ≤ 5. Find the range of f. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why f does not have an inverse. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) −4 ≤ f(x) ≤ 5
    B1 maximum 5 at x = 2; M1 evaluate ends f(0) = 1, f(5) = −4; A1 −4 ≤ f(x) ≤ 5.
  2. (b) f is not one-one on 0 ≤ x ≤ 5, e.g. f(1) = f(3) = 4.
    B1.
Q28Domain & range · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 3/(x + 2) for x ≥ 1. Find the range of f. [2]
  2. (b) The function g is defined by g(x) = x² − 1 for x ∈ ℝ. Find an expression for gf(x) and the range of gf. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0 < f(x) ≤ 1
    B1 f(1) = 1 and f decreasing; B1 f(x) > 0 (tends to 0).
  2. (b) gf(x) = 9/(x + 2)² − 1; range −1 < gf(x) ≤ 0
    B1 gf(x); M1 use the range of f as the domain of g; A1 −1 < gf(x) ≤ 0.
Q29Domain & range · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 4 sin x − 1 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 7π/6. Find the range of f. [3]
  2. (b) Find the largest value of a such that f, with domain 0 ≤ x ≤ a, has an inverse. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) −3 ≤ f(x) ≤ 3
    B1 maximum of sin x is 1 at x = π/2, giving 3; M1 least value of sin x on the domain is sin(7π/6) = −½; A1 −3 ≤ f(x) ≤ 3.
  2. (b) a = π/2
    B1 f is increasing up to x = π/2 only.
Q30Domain & range · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = x² − 6x + c for 1 ≤ x ≤ 7, where c is a constant. The range of f is −4 ≤ f(x) ≤ 12. Find the value of c and verify that the greatest value is 12. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why f does not have an inverse, and state the largest domain of the form 3 ≤ x ≤ b, with b ≤ 7, on which it would have one. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) c = 5; f(7) = 49 − 42 + 5 = 12
    M1 minimum at x = 3: c − 9 = −4; A1 c = 5; B1 f(7) = 12 and f(1) = 0 < 12.
  2. (b) f(1) = f(5) = 0, so f is not one-one; on 3 ≤ x ≤ 7 it is one-one (b = 7).
    B1 not one-one with example; B1 b = 7.
