Q1Argand loci · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe fully the locus of points in an Argand diagram representing complex numbers z that satisfy |z − 3 + 2i| = 4. [2]
  2. (b) Find the greatest value of |z| for points on this locus. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A circle with centre (3, −2), i.e. the point 3 − 2i, and radius 4.
    B1 circle centre 3 − 2i; B1 radius 4.
  2. (b) √13 + 4 ≈ 7.61
    M1 distance from origin to centre = √(3² + 2²) = √13; A1 √13 + 4 = 7.61.

Q10Argand loci · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z = x + iy satisfies |z − 3| = 2|z + 3|. Show that the locus of z is a circle, and find its centre and radius. [4]
  2. (b) Find the greatest and least values of |z| for points on this locus. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) centre (−5, 0), radius 4
    Square both sides: (x − 3)² + y² = 4[(x + 3)² + y²] (M1). Expand: x² − 6x + 9 + y² = 4x² + 24x + 36 + 4y² (M1). Simplify: x² + 10x + y² + 9 = 0 (M1). Complete the square: (x + 5)² + y² = 16, a circle with centre (−5, 0) and radius 4 (A1).
  2. (b) greatest 9, least 1
    Distance from the origin to the centre is 5, so |z| ranges from 5 − 4 = 1 to 5 + 4 = 9 (M1, A1, A1).
Q11Argand loci · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The complex number z = x + iy satisfies arg(z + 2 − i) = 3π/4. Find the Cartesian equation of the locus of z, and state any restriction on x. [4]
  2. (b) Determine whether the point with z = −4 + 3i lies on this locus. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −x − 1, with x < −2
    z + 2 − i = (x + 2) + (y − 1)i. An argument of 3π/4 means the real part is negative and the imaginary part is positive with equal magnitude (M1, M1). So y − 1 = −(x + 2), giving y = −x − 1 (A1). The real part x + 2 must be negative, so x < −2 (A1).
  2. (b) Yes
    Check y = −x − 1 with x = −4, y = 3: −(−4) − 1 = 3, and x < −2, so it lies on the locus (M1, A1). Direct check: arg(−2 + 2i) = 3π/4.

Q29Complex arithmetic & equations · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) Show that 1 + i is a root and find all four roots. [4]

    The complex conjugate −2i is also a root (B1, B1).

Q75Polynomial with complex roots · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation z⁴ − 2z² + 4 = 0, giving each root in the form x + iy with exact values. [4]
  2. (b) Hence write z⁴ − 2z² + 4 as a product of two quadratic factors with real coefficients, each containing √6. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ±(√6 + √2 i)/2 and ±(√6 − √2 i)/2
