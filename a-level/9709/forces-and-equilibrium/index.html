Q1Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A box of mass 5 kg rests in equilibrium on a rough plane inclined at 25° to the horizontal.

  1. (a) Find the normal reaction and the frictional force acting on the box. [3]
  2. (b) Find the least possible value of the coefficient of friction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = 45.3 N, F = 21.1 N up the slope
    M1 resolve perpendicular: R = 50 cos 25°; A1 45.3 N; B1 F = 50 sin 25° = 21.1 N.
  2. (b) 0.466
    M1 F ≤ μR so μ ≥ 21.13/45.32; A1 μ ≥ tan 25° = 0.466.
Q2Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻².

  1. (a) A particle of mass 3 kg is on the point of slipping down a rough plane inclined at angle α to the horizontal, where sin α = 0.6. Find the normal reaction, the frictional force and the coefficient of friction. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = 24 N, F = 18 N, μ = 0.75
    B1 cos α = 0.8; B1 R = 30 × 0.8 = 24 N; B1 F = 30 × 0.6 = 18 N; B1 μ = 18/24 = 0.75.
Q3Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A car of mass 1200 kg is parked with its brakes on, at rest on a straight road on a hill near Murree. The road is inclined at 8° to the horizontal. Treat the car as a particle.

  1. (a) Find the frictional force and the normal reaction acting on the car. [3]
  2. (b) Find the least possible coefficient of friction between the tyres and the road. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) F = 1670 N, R = 11900 N
    M1 resolve along and perpendicular to the road; A1 F = 12000 sin 8° = 1670 N; A1 R = 12000 cos 8° = 11 900 N.
  2. (b) 0.141
    M1 μ ≥ F/R; A1 tan 8° = 0.141.
Q4Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A crate of mass 8 kg is on a rough plane inclined at 20° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.3. A force of magnitude P N acts on the crate up the line of greatest slope.

  1. (a) Find the least value of P for which the crate remains at rest. [4]
  2. (b) Find the greatest value of P for which the crate remains at rest. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.81 N
    M1 R = 80 cos 20° = 75.18 N; M1 friction acts up the slope, F = 0.3R = 22.55 N; M1 P + F = 80 sin 20°; A1 P = 4.81 N.
  2. (b) 49.9 N
    M1 friction now down the slope: P = 80 sin 20° + 22.55; A1 49.9 N.
Q5Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A block of mass 4 kg rests on a rough plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal, with coefficient of friction 0.2. A horizontal force of magnitude P N, in the vertical plane containing the line of greatest slope, acts on the block towards the plane, and the block is about to move up the plane.

  1. (a) Show that the normal reaction is (40 cos 30° + P sin 30°) N. [2]
  2. (b) Find the value of P. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 resolve perpendicular to the plane, horizontal force has component P sin 30° into the plane; A1.
  2. (b) P = 35.2
    M1 resolve along the plane: P cos 30° = 40 sin 30° + F; M1 F = 0.2R; A1 P(cos 30° − 0.2 sin 30°) = 20 + 8 cos 30°; A1 P = 35.2.
Q6Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A box of mass 6 kg is on a rough ramp inclined at 10° to the horizontal at a warehouse in Faisalabad. A rope pulls the box with a force of 30 N acting at 15° above the line of greatest slope, up the ramp. The box is about to move up the ramp.

  1. (a) Find the coefficient of friction between the box and the ramp. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) μ = 0.362
    M1 resolve perpendicular: R + 30 sin 15° = 60 cos 10°; A1 R = 51.32 N; M1 resolve parallel: 30 cos 15° = F + 60 sin 10°; A1 F = 18.56 N; M1 μ = F/R; A1 0.362.
Q7Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle of mass 2.5 kg is on a rough plane inclined at 35° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.4. A force of magnitude P N acts on the particle at an angle of 20° above the line of greatest slope, directed up the plane.

  1. (a) Find the set of values of P for which the particle remains in equilibrium. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.66 ≤ P ≤ 20.9
    M1 resolve perpendicular: R = 25 cos 35° − P sin 20°; M1 about to slide up: P cos 20° = 25 sin 35° + 0.4R; A1 P = 20.9; M1 about to slide down: P cos 20° + 0.4R = 25 sin 35°; A1 P = 7.66; M1 both cases used with correct friction directions; A1 7.66 ≤ P ≤ 20.9; B1 check R > 0 in both cases.
Q8Friction & equilibrium on a slope · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Find the greatest angle of a slope on which a block can rest without any applied force, if the coefficient of friction is 0.5. [2]
  2. (b) A block of mass 3 kg is placed on a slope inclined at 40° with the same coefficient of friction, and a force of 12 N acts on it up the line of greatest slope. Determine whether the block remains at rest, and find the magnitude and direction of the frictional force. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 26.6°
    M1 tan θ = μ; A1 26.6°.
  2. (b) Remains at rest; friction 7.28 N up the slope
    M1 R = 30 cos 40° = 22.98 N; A1 maximum friction 11.49 N; M1 force needed to hold: 30 sin 40° − 12 = 7.28 N; A1 7.28 < 11.49 so at rest; A1 friction 7.28 N acting up the slope.
Q9Resultant of coplanar forces · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Three horizontal forces act on a particle: 12 N due east, 9 N due north and 5 N due west. Find the magnitude of the resultant force and the angle it makes with due east. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 11.4 N at 52.1° north of east
    M1 resultant components 7 N east, 9 N north; M1 √(7² + 9²); A1 11.4 N; A1 tan⁻¹(9/7) = 52.1° north of east.
Q10Resultant of coplanar forces · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Two forces of magnitudes 20 N and 15 N act at a point O. The 20 N force acts at 30° above the positive x-axis and the 15 N force acts at 120° to the positive x-axis. Find the magnitude of the resultant and the angle it makes with the positive x-axis. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25 N at 66.9° to the positive x-axis
    M1 the forces are perpendicular (or resolve both); A1 magnitude 25 N; M1 angle with the 20 N force tan⁻¹(15/20) = 36.9°; A1 30° + 36.9° = 66.9°.
Q11Resultant of coplanar forces · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Forces of magnitudes 10 N, 14 N and P N act at a point. The 10 N force acts along the positive x-axis, the 14 N force acts at 60° anticlockwise from the positive x-axis and the P N force acts along the negative y-axis. The resultant of the three forces acts along the positive x-axis.

  1. (a) Find the value of P. [2]
  2. (b) Find the magnitude of the resultant. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = 12.1
    M1 resultant has no y-component: 14 sin 60° − P = 0; A1 P = 7√3 = 12.1.
  2. (b) 17 N
    M1 10 + 14 cos 60°; A1 17 N.
Q12Resultant of coplanar forces · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Three horizontal ropes pull on a ring fixed to a post on a boat at Keamari harbour. The forces are 25 N on a bearing of 040°, 18 N on a bearing of 160° and 30 N on a bearing of 270°.

  1. (a) Find the magnitude of the resultant force and its bearing. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.09 N on a bearing of 286°
    M1 resolve east: 25 sin 40° + 18 sin 160° − 30 = −7.774; M1 resolve north: 25 cos 40° + 18 cos 160° = 2.237; A1 both components; M1 magnitude √(7.774² + 2.237²); A1 8.09 N; A1 bearing 270° + tan⁻¹(2.237/7.774) = 286°.
Q13Resultant of coplanar forces · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Three forces act at a point O: 6 N along the positive x-axis, 8 N along the positive y-axis, and F N acting in the third quadrant at an angle θ below the negative x-axis. The resultant of the three forces is 5 N along the positive y-axis.

  1. (a) Find F and θ. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) F = 6.71, θ = 26.6°
    M1 resolve along x: 6 − F cos θ = 0; M1 resolve along y: 8 − F sin θ = 5; A1 F cos θ = 6, F sin θ = 3; A1 F = √45 = 6.71; A1 θ = tan⁻¹(0.5) = 26.6°.
Q14Resultant of coplanar forces · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Two forces of magnitudes P N and Q N act at a point, and the angle between their directions is 130°. Their resultant has magnitude 20 N and is perpendicular to the force of magnitude P N. Find Q and P. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Q = 26.1, P = 16.8
    M1 resolve along P: P + Q cos 130° = 0; M1 resolve perpendicular to P: Q sin 130° = 20; A1 Q = 20/sin 50° = 26.1; M1 P = Q cos 50°; A1 16.8.
Q15Equilibrium of a particle (strings) · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A lantern of mass 2 kg hangs in equilibrium from two light strings. One string makes an angle of 30° with the horizontal and the other makes an angle of 60° with the horizontal, on the other side.

  1. (a) Find the tension in each string. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10 N (string at 30°) and 17.3 N (string at 60°)
    M1 resolve horizontally: T₁ cos 30° = T₂ cos 60°; M1 resolve vertically: T₁ sin 30° + T₂ sin 60° = 20; A1 one equation solved for a ratio, T₂ = √3 T₁; A1 T₁ = 10 N; A1 T₂ = 17.3 N.
Q16Equilibrium of a particle (strings) · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle of mass 0.8 kg hangs in equilibrium from two light strings, each making an angle of 40° with the vertical.

  1. (a) Find the tension in each string. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.22 N
    M1 by symmetry the tensions are equal; M1 2T cos 40° = 8; A1 T = 5.22 N.
Q17Equilibrium of a particle (strings) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A ball of mass 3 kg is attached to one end of a light string whose other end is fixed. The ball is held in equilibrium, with the string taut, by a horizontal force of 12 N.

  1. (a) Find the angle the string makes with the vertical and the tension in the string. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 21.8°, 32.3 N
    M1 resolve: T sin α = 12, T cos α = 30; A1 tan α = 0.4, α = 21.8°; M1 T = √(12² + 30²); A1 32.3 N.
Q18Equilibrium of a particle (strings) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². Two points A and B are on a horizontal ceiling, 1.0 m apart. A particle C of mass 5 kg is attached to light inextensible strings AC and BC, of lengths 0.8 m and 0.6 m, and hangs in equilibrium below AB.

  1. (a) Show that angle ACB is a right angle, and find the angles that AC and BC make with the horizontal. [3]
  2. (b) Find the tensions in AC and BC. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) AC at 36.9°, BC at 53.1° to the horizontal
    B1 0.8² + 0.6² = 1.0²; M1 cos(angle CAB) = 0.8/1.0; A1 36.9° and 53.1°.
  2. (b) T_AC = 30 N, T_BC = 40 N
    M1 horizontal: 0.8 T_A = 0.6 T_B; M1 vertical: 0.6 T_A + 0.8 T_B = 50; A1 T_A = 30 N; A1 T_B = 40 N.
Q19Equilibrium of a particle (strings) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A particle of weight 20 N hangs in equilibrium from two light strings. One string has tension 15 N and makes an angle of 25° above the horizontal. The other string, on the opposite side, has tension T N and makes an angle θ above the horizontal.

  1. (a) Find T and θ. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) T = 19.3, θ = 45.1°
    M1 horizontal: T cos θ = 15 cos 25°; M1 vertical: T sin θ + 15 sin 25° = 20; A1 T cos θ = 13.59, T sin θ = 13.66; A1 T = 19.3; A1 θ = 45.1°.
Q20Equilibrium of a particle (strings) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

A light inextensible string of length 1.8 m has its ends fixed at points A and B on the same horizontal level, 1.2 m apart. A small smooth ring of weight 6 N is threaded on the string.

  1. (a) The ring hangs at rest at the midpoint of the string. Find the tension in the string, correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) A horizontal force of magnitude F N, acting in the direction from A to B, is now applied to the ring, so that it rests vertically below B with both parts of the string taut. Show that the ring is 0.5 m below B, and find the tension in the string and the value of F. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.02 N
    M1 each half 0.9 m with horizontal 0.6 m, so vertical drop √(0.9² − 0.6²) = √0.45 = 0.671 m and sine of angle to horizontal = 0.671/0.9 = 0.745; M1 2T × 0.745 = 6; A1 T = 4.02 N.
  2. (b) BR = 0.5 m; T = 13/3 ≈ 4.33 N; F = 4
    M1 AR = 1.8 − BR and AR² = 1.2² + BR², so (1.8 − d)² = 1.44 + d²; A1 d = 0.5, AR = 1.3; B1 tension the same in both parts (smooth ring); M1 vertical: T + T × (5/13) = 6, so T = 13/3 = 4.33 N; A1 horizontal: F = T × 12/13 = 4 N.

Q23Equilibrium of a particle (forces at a point) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Three forces of magnitudes 10 N, P N and Q N act on a particle, which is in equilibrium. The 10 N force acts at 30° above the positive x-axis, the P N force acts along the negative x-axis and the Q N force acts at 60° below the positive x-axis.

  1. (a) Find the values of P and Q. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Q = 5.77, P = 11.5
    M1 resolve vertically: 10 sin 30° = Q sin 60°; A1 Q = 5.77; M1 resolve horizontally: P = 10 cos 30° + Q cos 60°; A1 cos values correct; A1 P = 11.5.
Q24Equilibrium of a particle (forces at a point) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A particle is in equilibrium under three forces. Two of them have magnitudes 12 N and 15 N, and the angle between their directions is 70°.

  1. (a) Find the magnitude of the third force. [3]
  2. (b) Find the angle between the third force and the 12 N force. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 22.2 N
    M1 third force balances the resultant of the other two; M1 R² = 12² + 15² + 2(12)(15) cos 70°; A1 22.2 N.
  2. (b) 141°
    M1 angle between resultant and 12 N force: sin φ = 15 sin 70°/22.18; A1 φ = 39.4°; A1 third force at 180° − 39.4° = 140.6° to the 12 N force.
Q25Equilibrium of a particle (forces at a point) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A particle is in equilibrium under four forces: 8 N along the positive x-axis, 6 N along the positive y-axis, 10 N acting at an angle θ above the negative x-axis, and F N along the negative y-axis.

  1. (a) Find θ and F. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) θ = 36.9°, F = 12
    M1 resolve along x: 10 cos θ = 8; A1 θ = 36.9°; M1 resolve along y: 6 + 10 sin θ = F; A1 F = 12.
Q26Equilibrium of a particle (forces at a point) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Three forces of magnitudes 20 N, 25 N and 30 N act at a point and are in equilibrium. Find the angle between the directions of the 20 N and 25 N forces. [3]
  2. (b) Find the other two angles between the directions of the forces. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 97.2°
    M1 resultant of 20 N and 25 N has magnitude 30 N; M1 30² = 20² + 25² + 2(20)(25) cos θ; A1 cos θ = −0.125, θ = 97.2°.
  2. (b) 124.2° (20 N and 30 N) and 138.6° (25 N and 30 N)
    M1 25² = 20² + 30² + 2(20)(30) cos φ; A1 φ = 124.2°; A1 360° − 97.2° − 124.2° = 138.6°.
Q27Friction & limiting equilibrium · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A box of mass 6 kg rests on rough horizontal ground. The coefficient of friction is 0.35. A horizontal force of 15 N acts on the box.

  1. (a) Determine whether the box moves, and state the magnitude of the frictional force. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) It does not move; friction is 15 N
    M1 R = 60 N; A1 maximum friction 0.35 × 60 = 21 N; A1 15 < 21, so at rest with friction 15 N.
Q28Friction & limiting equilibrium · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A crate of mass 10 kg on rough horizontal ground is pulled by a rope inclined at 25° above the horizontal. The tension in the rope is 40 N and the crate is about to move.

  1. (a) Find the coefficient of friction. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.436
    M1 resolve vertically: R + 40 sin 25° = 100; A1 R = 83.1 N; M1 resolve horizontally: F = 40 cos 25° = 36.25 N; M1 μ = F/R; A1 0.436.
Q29Friction & limiting equilibrium · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A worker pushes a box of mass 12 kg across a rough horizontal floor with a force of magnitude P N directed at 30° below the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.25.

  1. (a) Find the value of P for which the box is about to move. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) 40.5
    M1 resolve vertically: R = 120 + P sin 30°; M1 resolve horizontally: P cos 30° = F; M1 F = 0.25R; A1 P(cos 30° − 0.25 sin 30°) = 30; A1 P = 40.5.
Q30Friction & limiting equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Take g = 10 m s⁻². A sack of rice of mass 20 kg lies on a rough horizontal floor in a godown. The coefficient of friction is 0.5. A force of magnitude P N is applied at an angle θ above the horizontal.

  1. (a) Show that the sack is about to move when P = 100/(cos θ + 0.5 sin θ). [3]
  2. (b) Find the value of P when θ = 30°, and the corresponding normal reaction. [3]
  3. (c) Compare your answer to part (b) with the force needed when the sack is pulled horizontally, and explain the difference. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    M1 R = 200 − P sin θ; M1 P cos θ = 0.5R; A1 rearrange to the given result.
  2. (b) P = 89.6 N, R = 155 N
    M1 P = 100/(cos 30° + 0.5 sin 30°) = 100/1.116; A1 P = 89.6 N; B1 R = 200 − 89.6 sin 30° = 155 N.
  3. (c) Horizontal pull needs P = 100 N, more than 89.6 N; pulling upwards reduces the normal reaction and hence the maximum friction
    B1 θ = 0 gives P = 100 N > 89.6 N; B1 the upward component reduces R, so limiting friction 0.5R is smaller.
