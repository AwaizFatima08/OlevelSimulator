Q1Parametric differentiation · Core · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A curve is defined by the parametric equations x = t² + 3t, y = t³ − 2t. Find dy/dx in terms of t. [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where t = 1, giving your answer in the form ax + by + c = 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = (3t² − 2)/(2t + 3)
    B1 dx/dt = 2t + 3, dy/dt = 3t² − 2M1 dy/dx = (dy/dt) ÷ (dx/dt)A1 (3t² − 2)/(2t + 3)
  2. (b) x − 5y − 9 = 0
    B1 point (4, −1)B1 gradient 1/5M1 y + 1 = (1/5)(x − 4)A1 x − 5y − 9 = 0 (any equivalent integer form)
Q2Parametric differentiation · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A curve is defined for t > 0 by x = 1 + ln(2t), y = t² + 2/t. Show that dy/dx = 2t² − 2/t. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact coordinates of the stationary point of the curve. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) dx/dt = 1/t, dy/dt = 2t − 2/t², dy/dx = t(2t − 2/t²) = 2t² − 2/t
    B1 dx/dt = 1/tB1 dy/dt = 2t − 2/t²A1 divide to obtain the given result
  2. (b) (1 + ln 2, 3)
    M1 2t² − 2/t = 0 so t³ = 1, t = 1A1 x = 1 + ln 2, y = 3

  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the point on the curve at which the gradient is 3. [2]
Show answer

  2. (b) (11, 5)

  1. (a) A curve is defined by x = t² − 1, y = t³ + t. Find dy/dx in terms of t. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where t = 2, giving your answer in the form ax + by + c = 0. [3]

Q5Parametric differentiation · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The parametric equations of a curve are x = eᵗ + 1, y = e²ᵗ − 3t. Show that dy/dx = (2e²ᵗ − 3)/eᵗ. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the normal to the curve at the point where t = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = (2e²ᵗ − 3)/eᵗ
    B1 dx/dt = eᵗ and dy/dt = 2e²ᵗ − 3A1 divides to given answer
  2. (b) y = x − 1

Q6Parametric differentiation · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A curve is defined for t > 0 by x = √t, y = t² − t. Show that dy/dx = 2√t(2t − 1). [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where t = 4. [3]
Show answer

    B1 dx/dt = 1/(2√t)B1 dy/dt = 2t − 1A1 (2t − 1) × 2√t
  2. (b) y = 28x − 44
    B1 point (2, 12)M1 gradient 2 × 2 × 7 = 28A1 y − 12 = 28(x − 2) → y = 28x − 44
Q7Parametric differentiation · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) The parametric equations of a curve are x = e²ᵗ − 1, y = t eᵗ. Show that dy/dx = (1 + t)/(2eᵗ). [4]
  2. (b) Find the exact coordinates of the point on the curve at which the tangent is parallel to the x-axis. [2]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the normal to the curve at the origin. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = (eᵗ + t eᵗ)/(2e²ᵗ) = (1 + t)/(2eᵗ)
    B1 dx/dt = 2e²ᵗB1 dy/dt = eᵗ + t eᵗ (product rule)M1 divide and cancel eᵗ, A1 given answer
  2. (b) (e⁻² − 1, −e⁻¹)
    M1 1 + t = 0 so t = −1A1 x = e⁻² − 1, y = −e⁻¹
  3. (c) y = −2x
    B1 origin when t = 0, gradient 1/2A1 normal gradient −2, y = −2x
Q8Parametric differentiation · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) A curve has parametric equations x = 4 sin θ, y = 3 cos 2θ, for −½π < θ < ½π. Show that dy/dx = −3 sin θ. [3]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where θ = π/6, giving your answer in the form ax + by = c where a, b and c are integers. [3]
  3. (c) Find the Cartesian equation of the curve in the form y = f(x). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = −6 sin 2θ ÷ 4 cos θ = −12 sin θ cos θ ÷ 4 cos θ = −3 sin θ
    B1 dx/dθ = 4 cos θ, B1 dy/dθ = −6 sin 2θM1 use sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ and simplify to the given answer
  2. (b) 3x + 2y = 9
    B1 point (2, 3/2) and gradient −3/2M1 y − 3/2 = −3/2(x − 2)A1 3x + 2y = 9
  3. (c) y = 3 − 3x²/8
    M1 cos 2θ = 1 − 2 sin²θ = 1 − 2(x/4)²A1 y = 3 − 3x²/8
Q9Parametric differentiation · Standard · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) A curve has parametric equations x = tan θ, y = sin 2θ, for 0 < θ < ½π. Show that dy/dx = 2 cos²θ cos 2θ. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact coordinates of the point on the curve at which the tangent is parallel to the x-axis. [2]
  3. (c) Find the equation of the tangent to the curve at the point where θ = π/6, giving your answer in the form y = mx + c with m and c exact. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = 2 cos 2θ ÷ sec²θ = 2 cos²θ cos 2θ
    B1 dx/dθ = sec²θB1 dy/dθ = 2 cos 2θA1 divides and uses 1/sec²θ = cos²θ
  2. (b) (1, 1)

  3. (c) y = ¾x + √3/4
    B1 point (1/√3, √3/2)M1 gradient 2 × ¾ × ½ = ¾A1 c = √3/2 − ¾ × 1/√3 = √3/4
Q10Parametric differentiation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A curve is defined for t > 0 by x = ln t, y = t³ − 12t. Show that dy/dx = 3t³ − 12t. [2]
  2. (b) Find the exact coordinates of the stationary point of the curve. [3]
  3. (c) By considering the sign of dy/dx for values of t either side of your value, determine whether the stationary point is a maximum or a minimum. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) dy/dx = (3t² − 12) ÷ (1/t) = 3t³ − 12t
    B1 dx/dt = 1/t, dy/dt = 3t² − 12A1 given answer
  2. (b) (ln 2, −16)
    M1 3t(t² − 4) = 0, t > 0 → t = 2A1 x = ln 2A1 y = 8 − 24 = −16
  3. (c) Minimum
    M1 e.g. t = 1: dy/dx = −9 < 0; t = 3: dy/dx = 45 > 0 (x increases with t)A1 negative then positive → minimum

  2. (b) Find the equation of the normal to the curve at the point where t = 2, giving your answer in the form ax + by = c. [3]
