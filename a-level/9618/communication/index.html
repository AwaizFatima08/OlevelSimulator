Q1Networks & topologies · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give three differences between a local area network (LAN) and a wide area network (WAN). [3]
  2. (b) A textile company has offices in Faisalabad and Dubai connected together. State the type of network that connects the two offices. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) LAN covers a small area (one building/site), WAN covers a large geographical area (cities/countries). LAN hardware and cables are usually owned by the organisation; WAN uses leased lines/infrastructure owned by telecom companies. LAN usually has higher data rates and fewer errors than a WAN.
    1 mark each for any three contrasted differences: area covered; ownership of infrastructure; transmission rate; error rate/reliability; media used (LAN – cable/Wi-Fi, WAN – telephone lines, fibre, satellite).
  2. (b) WAN
    B1: WAN.
Q2Networks & topologies · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe a star topology. [2]
  2. (b) Give two advantages of a star topology compared with a bus topology. [2]
  3. (c) Give one disadvantage of a star topology. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each device is connected by its own cable to a central device (a switch or hub); all data between devices passes through this central device.
    B1: each device has its own link. B1: to a central switch/hub/server.
  2. (b) A fault in one cable affects only one device; data goes only to the destination device through the switch, so fewer collisions/better security and performance; easy to add devices without disrupting the network.
    1 mark each for any two.
  3. (c) If the central switch fails the whole network stops; more cable is needed.
    B1 any one.

  2. (b) Explain why the cable in topology (i) needs a terminator at each end. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) bus (ii) (full) mesh (iii) hybrid
    B1 each.
  2. (b) To absorb the signals at the ends of the cable so they are not reflected back and interfere with other signals.
    B1.

  1. (a) Define the term topology in the context of computer networks. [2]
Show answer

Q5Networks & topologies · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe a bus topology, and state one reason why it is no longer common in modern LANs. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) All devices share one main cable (backbone) with a terminator at each end. Data travels along the cable and is read by the device with the matching address. Reason: a break in the cable stops the whole network, and performance falls as more devices are added because of collisions.
    1 mark: all devices share one cable/backbone; 1 mark: terminators at each end and data read by the matching address; 1 mark: any reason (cable fault stops the network / collisions slow it as devices are added).
Q6Networks & topologies · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a fault is easier to find in a star network than in a bus network. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each computer has its own cable to the central switch, so a faulty cable or device can be isolated quickly. In a bus network a fault can be anywhere along the single shared cable.
    1 mark: each device has its own cable to a central point so the fault is isolated; 1 mark: in a bus the fault can be anywhere on the shared cable.
Q7Networks & topologies · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A research lab connects its six servers in a full mesh topology.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of links needed. [1]
  2. (b) A seventh server is added to the mesh. Calculate how many extra links are needed. [1]
  3. (c) Give one advantage and one disadvantage of using a mesh topology for these servers. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15
    B1: 6 × 5 ÷ 2 = 15.
  2. (b) 6
    B1: 7 × 6 ÷ 2 = 21; 21 − 15 = 6 (one to each existing server).
  3. (c) Advantage: there are alternative routes, so if one link fails data can still get through (fault tolerant / no single point of failure). Disadvantage: many cables and network ports are needed, so it is expensive and hard to set up.
    B1 advantage (redundant routes / fast direct links). B1 disadvantage (cost / amount of cabling / complex).
Q8Networks & topologies · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

A hospital in Peshawar has three buildings. Each building has its own star network with a switch. The three switches are connected to each other.

  1. (a) State the name of this topology and give one reason it suits the hospital. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how a data packet is sent from a computer in the X-ray department to a printer in the same building. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hybrid topology. Each building can be managed and expanded separately, and a fault in one building's star does not stop the others.
    B1 hybrid. B1 suitable reason.
  2. (b) The computer sends the packet along its cable to the building's switch. The switch reads the destination MAC address in the packet. It looks it up in its table and forwards the packet only to the port connected to the printer.
    B1 packet sent to switch. B1 switch reads destination (MAC) address. B1 forwarded only to the printer's port/link.
Q9Networks & topologies · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A research institute in Islamabad has nine separate laboratories. Each laboratory must be linked directly to every other laboratory, so that a fault on one cable does not stop any other link.

  1. (a) State the name of this topology. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of cables needed to link the nine laboratories this way. Show your working. [2]
  3. (c) Give one disadvantage of this topology for the institute. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Full mesh topology.
    B1: full mesh (every device has a direct link to every other device).
  2. (b) 36 cables. Working: 9 × 8 ÷ 2 = 36.
    M1: n × (n − 1) ÷ 2 or 9 × 8 ÷ 2; A1: 36.
  3. (c) Many cables and ports are needed, so it is expensive to install and difficult to maintain.
    B1: any one of: expensive; many cables; each device needs many network ports; hard to install or maintain.

  2. (b) Each part can be designed for its own needs (a ring in the labs and a star in the office), and a fault in one part need not stop the other part.

Q11Networks & topologies · Standard · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a token-passing ring network controls access to the cable, and explain why two computers cannot transmit at the same time. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A token (a special frame) travels round the ring. Only the computer holding the token may transmit. A computer that wants to send waits for the token, sends its data, then passes the token on. Only one computer holds the token, so there are no collisions.
    1 mark each for any three: token circulates; only the holder may transmit; a waiting computer must wait for the token; the token is passed on after sending; only one token exists, so no two computers send at once (no collisions).
Q12Networks & topologies · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A small design studio in Karachi uses a bus network with eight computers. It plans to double the number of computers.

  1. (a) Explain why the performance of the bus network will become worse as more computers are added. [2]
  2. (b) Describe what happens to the network if the main cable is cut between the fourth and fifth computers. [2]
  3. (c) Recommend a new topology for the studio and justify your choice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) All devices share one cable, so only one device can transmit at a time. More devices means more traffic and more collisions, so data has to be re-sent more often.
    B1 single shared cable / one transmission at a time. B1 more collisions / more waiting.
  2. (b) The whole network fails / the two halves cannot communicate, and signals reflect from the unterminated broken ends, causing errors even within each half.
    B1 network (or communication across the break) stops. B1 no terminator at the break – reflections/errors.
  3. (c) Star topology with a switch: each computer has its own link, the switch sends data only to the destination so there are far fewer collisions, and it is easy to add computers without stopping the network.
    B1 star. B1 justification linked to the studio (expansion / performance / fault isolation).
Q13Networks & topologies · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A bank has its head office in Islamabad and 11 branches in other cities (12 sites in total). The sites must be connected so that every branch can exchange data with every other site.

  1. (a) Calculate how many links are needed if the 12 sites are connected in a full mesh. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate how many links are needed if every branch is connected only to the head office, in a star arrangement. [1]
  3. (c) Discuss which arrangement the bank should use. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 66
    B1: 12 × 11 ÷ 2 = 66.
  2. (b) 11
    B1: one link per branch = 11.
  3. (c) Star: only 11 links so much cheaper; but all traffic passes through head office, which is a single point of failure, and branch-to-branch data must travel two links. Mesh: no single point of failure and direct routes, but 66 links is very expensive. A sensible decision is a star (or partial mesh) with a backup link at head office, because cost matters and most traffic goes to head office anyway.
    1 mark each, max 4: star cheaper/fewer links; star single point of failure; mesh redundancy/fault tolerance; mesh cost/complexity; reasoned conclusion (e.g. partial mesh / star with backup).
Q14Networks & topologies · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A tyre-fitting company in Karachi has 20 computers in its head office. The manager compares two ways to connect them: a star network with one switch, or a full mesh in which every computer has a direct cable to every other computer.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of cables needed for the star network. Assume each computer has one cable to the switch. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of cables needed for the full mesh. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the full mesh is rarely used for a network of this size, even though it gives the most reliable links. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20 cables (one from each computer to the switch).
    B1: 20 cables, one per computer.
  2. (b) 190 cables. Working: 20 × 19 ÷ 2 = 190.
    M1: n(n − 1) ÷ 2 with n = 20; A1: 190.
  3. (c) Any three: 190 cables are very expensive to buy and install; each computer needs 19 network ports; the cabling is hard to install, label and maintain; a star with a switch gives enough reliability for the office; a cable fault in a star affects only one computer.
    1 mark each for any three of the points above.
Q15Networks & topologies · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

Eight computers, A to H, are connected in a ring. In the first design, data can only travel clockwise: A→B→C→…→H→A. A message is sent from computer C to computer H.

  1. (a) State the number of hops (links crossed) the message takes from C to H. [1]
  2. (b) The ring is changed so that each computer can send in both directions. State the number of hops on the shortest route from C to H. [2]
  3. (c) Give one reason why a ring that works in both directions is more reliable than a one-direction ring. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5 hops (C→D→E→F→G→H).
    B1: 5 hops clockwise.
  2. (b) 3 hops, going anti-clockwise (C→B→A→H).
    M1: anti-clockwise route identified; A1: 3 hops (shortest route).
  3. (c) If one link fails, data can still reach its destination by travelling the other way round the ring, so the network keeps working.
    2 marks: data can take the other direction if one link fails; network keeps working after a single link fault.
Q16Network devices & media · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

    B1 each.
  2. (b) It converts digital signals from the computer into analogue signals for the line, and analogue back to digital (modulation/demodulation).
    B1.

Q21Network devices & media · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A factory in Sialkot has a long production hall. Computers at both ends of the hall are on the same wired network.

  1. (a) Explain why a repeater may be needed in the cable along the hall. [2]
  2. (b) The factory office has its own separate network segment. Describe the role of a bridge in joining the office segment to the factory segment. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Signals become weaker (attenuated) and distorted as they travel along a long cable. A repeater receives the signal and regenerates/boosts it so it can travel further.

  2. (b) A bridge connects two LAN segments that use the same protocol so they act as one network; it only passes on frames that are addressed to a device on the other segment, reducing unnecessary traffic.
    B1 joins two LANs/segments (same protocol). B1 filters traffic / forwards only frames for the other side.

Q23Network devices & media · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A 1.5 GB video file (take 1 GB = 10⁹ bytes) is copied across a local network to a computer on a connection of 100 Mbit/s (1 Mbit = 10⁶ bits).

  2. (b) Give one reason why the actual transfer time would be longer than your answer. [1]

  2. (b) Protocol headers and other overheads add data; network traffic shares the bandwidth; packets may need to be resent.
    B1: any one: overheads/headers; other traffic on the network; retransmission of lost packets.

Q27Network devices & media · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

A village school in Gilgit-Baltistan can connect to the internet either by a geostationary satellite or by a new fibre-optic line. The satellite is 35 786 km above the Earth's surface and radio signals travel at 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

  1. (a) Calculate the minimum time for a signal to travel from the school up to the satellite and down to the ground station. Give your answer in milliseconds, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why this delay is a problem for video lessons with a live teacher, even when the bandwidth is high. [2]
  3. (c) Give one advantage of satellite over fibre for this school. [1]

    M1: 2 × 35 786 000 ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 0.2386 s. A1: 239 ms.
  2. (b) A request and reply each go up and down (about 0.48 s round trip), so there is a noticeable lag in a two-way conversation; speech overlaps and the lesson feels delayed. Bandwidth affects how much data arrives, not how long it takes to arrive.
    B1 round trip roughly double / noticeable lag in two-way communication. B1 latency is different from bandwidth.
  3. (c) No cable needs to be laid over mountainous ground; can be set up quickly anywhere with a view of the sky.
    B1.
Q28Network devices & media · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A college is setting up a new computer room with 30 desktop computers, a laser printer and a file server. The computers will be used for online practical exams.

  1. (a) Identify three hardware devices, other than the computers, printer and cables, needed to build this LAN and connect it to the internet. Give the purpose of each. [3]

Q29Network devices & media · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why single-mode fibre is used for long-distance links, while multi-mode fibre is cheaper for shorter distances. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Single-mode fibre carries light along one path with little dispersion, so it can cover long distances; multi-mode fibre has several light paths that spread out (disperse) over distance, so it suits shorter runs; multi-mode is cheaper to make and install.
    1 mark each for any three: single-mode one path, less dispersion/attenuation, long distances; multi-mode several paths, more dispersion, shorter distances; multi-mode cheaper.
Q30Network devices & media · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A café in Lahore has 16 computers connected to one network. The owner is told to replace the hub with a switch.

  1. (a) Explain why a switch gives better performance than a hub in a café with 16 computers. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A hub shares one collision domain between all ports, so frames collide and bandwidth is shared. A switch sends each frame only to its destination port, so there are fewer collisions and each computer gets more of the bandwidth.
    1 mark: hub broadcasts to all ports so traffic is shared; 1 mark: switch sends frames only to the destination port; 1 mark: fewer collisions / more bandwidth for each computer.

Q36Internet, IP addressing & DNS · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the format of an IPv6 address. [2]
  2. (b) Write this IPv6 address in its shortest form: 2001:0db8:0000:0000:0000:ff00:0042:8329 [2]
  3. (c) Explain why IPv6 was introduced. Include in your answer how many times more addresses IPv6 has than IPv4, as a power of 2. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 128 bits written as eight groups of four hexadecimal digits, separated by colons; leading zeros in a group can be left out and one run of all-zero groups can be replaced by ::.

    B1 IPv4 addresses running out / too many devices. B1 2⁹⁶.
Q37Internet, IP addressing & DNS · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

An online clothing shop in Karachi runs its own web server. Its staff use laptops connected to the office network.

  1. (a) Explain the difference between a public IP address and a private IP address. [2]

  1. (a) The phone measures signal strength and detects that the new base station is stronger. The network transfers the call to the new base station. The call continues without being interrupted.

Q86Wireless & cellular networks · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A commuter makes a video call on her smartphone while riding the Lahore Metro bus from one end of the route to the other.

  1. (a) Describe how a cellular network is organised. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how the call continues without interruption as she moves from one cell to the next. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the call quality may fall in a crowded station. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The area is divided into small regions called cells, each with a base station (mast) that transmits and receives radio signals. Neighbouring cells use different frequencies so they do not interfere, and frequencies are reused in cells that are far apart. Base stations are connected by cable/fibre to the operator's core network and the internet.
    1 mark each, max 3: cells; base station/mast per cell; different frequencies for adjacent cells / frequency reuse; base stations linked to the wider network.
  2. (b) The network monitors signal strength from nearby base stations. When the signal from the next cell's base station becomes stronger, the call is handed over to that base station (handover/handoff) without dropping the connection.
    B1 signal strength monitored. B1 handover/handoff to next base station.
  3. (c) Many phones share the same cell's limited bandwidth/channels, so each gets a smaller share.
    B1.
Q87Wireless & cellular networks · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 2 marks

A smartphone is 3.6 km from a cell tower. Radio signals travel at 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

  1. (a) Calculate the one-way propagation delay of the radio signal, in microseconds. Show your working. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12 µs. Working: 3.6 km = 3600 m; time = 3600 ÷ (3 × 10⁸) = 1.2 × 10⁻⁵ s = 12 µs.

Show answer
  1. (a) Microwaves travel in a straight line and are blocked or weakened by buildings, hills or trees. The two dishes must be able to see each other directly for a reliable signal.

Q89Wireless & cellular networks · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

Two Wi-Fi routers in neighbouring flats in Islamabad both use channel 6.

  1. (a) Explain what happens when two neighbouring Wi-Fi routers use the same channel, and state one way to reduce the problem. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Their signals interfere with each other, so data must be re-sent and the connection becomes slower or drops. The problem can be reduced by changing one router to a different, non-overlapping channel (for example 1, 6 or 11 on 2.4 GHz).
    1 mark each for any three: signals interfere; slower speeds / dropped connections; data retransmitted; change to a different (non-overlapping) channel.
Q90Wireless & cellular networks · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Discuss whether a rural clinic in Gilgit-Baltistan should use a cellular network or a satellite link to send patient records. Give a justified recommendation. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cellular is usually faster and cheaper per GB, with lower delay, but it may have no coverage in remote valleys. Satellite covers almost any location, but has higher delay and cost and is affected by weather. A sensible recommendation is cellular where coverage exists, with satellite as a backup.
    1 mark each for any three valid points: coverage; speed and delay; cost; reliability/weather; a justified recommendation (may include backup).
