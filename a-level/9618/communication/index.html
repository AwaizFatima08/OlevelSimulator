Q1Networks & topologies · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give three differences between a local area network (LAN) and a wide area network (WAN). [3]
  2. (b) A textile company has offices in Faisalabad and Dubai connected together. State the type of network that connects the two offices. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) LAN covers a small area (one building/site), WAN covers a large geographical area (cities/countries). LAN hardware and cables are usually owned by the organisation; WAN uses leased lines/infrastructure owned by telecom companies. LAN usually has higher data rates and fewer errors than a WAN.
    1 mark each for any three contrasted differences: area covered; ownership of infrastructure; transmission rate; error rate/reliability; media used (LAN – cable/Wi-Fi, WAN – telephone lines, fibre, satellite).
  2. (b) WAN
    B1: WAN.
Q2Networks & topologies · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe a star topology. [2]
  2. (b) Give two advantages of a star topology compared with a bus topology. [2]
  3. (c) Give one disadvantage of a star topology. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each device is connected by its own cable to a central device (a switch or hub); all data between devices passes through this central device.
    B1: each device has its own link. B1: to a central switch/hub/server.
  2. (b) A fault in one cable affects only one device; data goes only to the destination device through the switch, so fewer collisions/better security and performance; easy to add devices without disrupting the network.
    1 mark each for any two.
  3. (c) If the central switch fails the whole network stops; more cable is needed.
    B1 any one.

  1. (a) Name the topology described in each case.(i) All devices are attached to one shared cable that has a terminator at each end.(ii) Every device has a direct link to every other device.(iii) Two star networks in separate buildings are joined by a single cable between their switches. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the cable in topology (i) needs a terminator at each end. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) bus (ii) (full) mesh (iii) hybrid
    B1 each.
  2. (b) To absorb the signals at the ends of the cable so they are not reflected back and interfere with other signals.
    B1.
Q4Networks & topologies · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A research lab connects its six servers in a full mesh topology.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of links needed. [1]
  2. (b) A seventh server is added to the mesh. Calculate how many extra links are needed. [1]
  3. (c) Give one advantage and one disadvantage of using a mesh topology for these servers. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15
    B1: 6 × 5 ÷ 2 = 15.
  2. (b) 6
    B1: 7 × 6 ÷ 2 = 21; 21 − 15 = 6 (one to each existing server).
  3. (c) Advantage: there are alternative routes, so if one link fails data can still get through (fault tolerant / no single point of failure). Disadvantage: many cables and network ports are needed, so it is expensive and hard to set up.
    B1 advantage (redundant routes / fast direct links). B1 disadvantage (cost / amount of cabling / complex).
Q5Networks & topologies · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

A hospital in Peshawar has three buildings. Each building has its own star network with a switch. The three switches are connected to each other.

  1. (a) State the name of this topology and give one reason it suits the hospital. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how a data packet is sent from a computer in the X-ray department to a printer in the same building. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hybrid topology. Each building can be managed and expanded separately, and a fault in one building's star does not stop the others.
    B1 hybrid. B1 suitable reason.
  2. (b) The computer sends the packet along its cable to the building's switch. The switch reads the destination MAC address in the packet. It looks it up in its table and forwards the packet only to the port connected to the printer.
    B1 packet sent to switch. B1 switch reads destination (MAC) address. B1 forwarded only to the printer's port/link.
Q6Networks & topologies · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A small design studio in Karachi uses a bus network with eight computers. It plans to double the number of computers.

  1. (a) Explain why the performance of the bus network will become worse as more computers are added. [2]
  2. (b) Describe what happens to the network if the main cable is cut between the fourth and fifth computers. [2]
  3. (c) Recommend a new topology for the studio and justify your choice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) All devices share one cable, so only one device can transmit at a time. More devices means more traffic and more collisions, so data has to be re-sent more often.
    B1 single shared cable / one transmission at a time. B1 more collisions / more waiting.
  2. (b) The whole network fails / the two halves cannot communicate, and signals reflect from the unterminated broken ends, causing errors even within each half.
    B1 network (or communication across the break) stops. B1 no terminator at the break – reflections/errors.
  3. (c) Star topology with a switch: each computer has its own link, the switch sends data only to the destination so there are far fewer collisions, and it is easy to add computers without stopping the network.
    B1 star. B1 justification linked to the studio (expansion / performance / fault isolation).
Q7Networks & topologies · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A bank has its head office in Islamabad and 11 branches in other cities (12 sites in total). The sites must be connected so that every branch can exchange data with every other site.

  1. (a) Calculate how many links are needed if the 12 sites are connected in a full mesh. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate how many links are needed if every branch is connected only to the head office, in a star arrangement. [1]
  3. (c) Discuss which arrangement the bank should use. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 66
    B1: 12 × 11 ÷ 2 = 66.
  2. (b) 11
    B1: one link per branch = 11.
  3. (c) Star: only 11 links so much cheaper; but all traffic passes through head office, which is a single point of failure, and branch-to-branch data must travel two links. Mesh: no single point of failure and direct routes, but 66 links is very expensive. A sensible decision is a star (or partial mesh) with a backup link at head office, because cost matters and most traffic goes to head office anyway.
    1 mark each, max 4: star cheaper/fewer links; star single point of failure; mesh redundancy/fault tolerance; mesh cost/complexity; reasoned conclusion (e.g. partial mesh / star with backup).
Q8Network devices & media · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) State the purpose of a modem when a home connects to the internet over a telephone line. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Connects devices in a LAN and forwards each frame only to the device it is addressed to, using MAC addresses. (ii) Connects different networks (e.g. the LAN to the internet) and directs packets towards their destination using IP addresses. (iii) Hardware that lets a device connect to the network; it has a unique MAC address. (iv) Lets wireless devices connect to a wired network using radio waves (Wi-Fi).
    B1 each.
  2. (b) It converts digital signals from the computer into analogue signals for the line, and analogue back to digital (modulation/demodulation).
    B1.

Q10Network devices & media · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A factory in Sialkot has a long production hall. Computers at both ends of the hall are on the same wired network.

  1. (a) Explain why a repeater may be needed in the cable along the hall. [2]
  2. (b) The factory office has its own separate network segment. Describe the role of a bridge in joining the office segment to the factory segment. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Signals become weaker (attenuated) and distorted as they travel along a long cable. A repeater receives the signal and regenerates/boosts it so it can travel further.
    B1 signal weakens over distance. B1 repeater regenerates/amplifies it.
  2. (b) A bridge connects two LAN segments that use the same protocol so they act as one network; it only passes on frames that are addressed to a device on the other segment, reducing unnecessary traffic.
    B1 joins two LANs/segments (same protocol). B1 filters traffic / forwards only frames for the other side.
Q11Network devices & media · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give three differences between a switch and a router. [3]
  2. (b) State why a router stores a routing table. [1]

  1. (a) A switch connects devices within one network; a router connects separate networks. A switch uses MAC addresses; a router uses IP addresses. A router can choose a route for packets and can connect a LAN to the internet / give it a public IP address; a switch cannot.

  2. (b) So it can decide which network or next router to send each packet to, to reach its destination.
    B1.
Q12Network devices & media · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

A village school in Gilgit-Baltistan can connect to the internet either by a geostationary satellite or by a new fibre-optic line. The satellite is 35 786 km above the Earth's surface and radio signals travel at 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

  1. (a) Calculate the minimum time for a signal to travel from the school up to the satellite and down to the ground station. Give your answer in milliseconds, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why this delay is a problem for video lessons with a live teacher, even when the bandwidth is high. [2]
  3. (c) Give one advantage of satellite over fibre for this school. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 239 ms
    M1: 2 × 35 786 000 ÷ 3.0 × 10⁸ = 0.2386 s. A1: 239 ms.
  2. (b) A request and reply each go up and down (about 0.48 s round trip), so there is a noticeable lag in a two-way conversation; speech overlaps and the lesson feels delayed. Bandwidth affects how much data arrives, not how long it takes to arrive.
    B1 round trip roughly double / noticeable lag in two-way communication. B1 latency is different from bandwidth.
  3. (c) No cable needs to be laid over mountainous ground; can be set up quickly anywhere with a view of the sky.
    B1.
Q13Network devices & media · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A college is setting up a new computer room with 30 desktop computers, a laser printer and a file server. The computers will be used for online practical exams.

  1. (a) Identify three hardware devices, other than the computers, printer and cables, needed to build this LAN and connect it to the internet. Give the purpose of each. [3]
  2. (b) The principal suggests using Wi-Fi only. Justify why wired connections are better for the exam computers. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Switch – connects all computers, printer and server and forwards data to the correct device. Router – connects the LAN to the internet. NIC in each device – connects it to the network / gives it a MAC address. (Also accept a firewall.)
    1 mark each for device + purpose, max 3.
  2. (b) Wired connections give a more reliable, constant data rate, so 30 computers do not compete for the same radio bandwidth; they are not affected by interference or walls; they are more secure because a device must be physically connected, so it is harder for others to intercept or join.
    1 mark each for any three: reliability/stability; speed/bandwidth not shared; less interference; security (cannot eavesdrop/join easily); exams need no dropped connections.

Q16Internet, IP addressing & DNS · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the format of an IPv6 address. [2]
  2. (b) Write this IPv6 address in its shortest form: 2001:0db8:0000:0000:0000:ff00:0042:8329 [2]
  3. (c) Explain why IPv6 was introduced. Include in your answer how many times more addresses IPv6 has than IPv4, as a power of 2. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 128 bits written as eight groups of four hexadecimal digits, separated by colons; leading zeros in a group can be left out and one run of all-zero groups can be replaced by ::.
    B1 128 bits / 8 groups of 16 bits. B1 hexadecimal with colons (shortening rules).
  2. (b) 2001:db8::ff00:42:8329
    B1 leading zeros removed (db8, 42). B1 three zero groups replaced by ::.
  3. (c) The number of IPv4 addresses (2³²) is not enough for all the devices now connected to the internet. IPv6 has 2¹²⁸ addresses, which is 2⁹⁶ times as many.
    B1 IPv4 addresses running out / too many devices. B1 2⁹⁶.
Q17Internet, IP addressing & DNS · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

An online clothing shop in Karachi runs its own web server. Its staff use laptops connected to the office network.

  1. (a) Explain the difference between a public IP address and a private IP address. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the web server should have a static public IP address, while the staff laptops can have dynamic private IP addresses. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A public IP address is unique on the whole internet and can be reached from anywhere on it. A private IP address is used only inside a LAN, can be reused in other LANs, and cannot be reached directly from the internet.
    B1 public – globally unique / routable on the internet. B1 private – only within a LAN / not routable / reusable.
  2. (b) Customers must always be able to find the server, so its public address must not change (the DNS record points to it). The laptops only need to be reached inside the office; the router can give them any free address each time they connect (by DHCP), which saves addresses and needs no manual set-up.
    B1 server must be reachable from the internet (public). B1 address must not change so DNS/customers can find it (static). B1 laptops only used internally / addresses assigned automatically and reused (dynamic private).
Q18Internet, IP addressing & DNS · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A student types www.example.edu.pk/timetable into a browser.

  1. (a) Identify the domain name and the path in this URL. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the Domain Name Service (DNS) finds the IP address of the web server. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Domain name: www.example.edu.pk (example.edu.pk); path: /timetable
    B1 each.
  2. (b) The browser sends the domain name to a DNS server (set by the ISP). The DNS server looks for the name in its own records/cache. If it does not have it, it passes the request to other DNS servers in the hierarchy (root, .pk, edu.pk …) until one with the record is found. The IP address is returned to the browser (and cached), which then uses it to request the page from the web server.
    1 mark each, max 4: request sent to DNS server; lookup in its database/cache; passed to another (higher-level) DNS server if not found; IP address returned to the browser; browser connects to that IP / cached for later; error returned if no record exists.

Q20Client-server & cloud · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe the client-server model. [2]
  2. (b) Give two differences between a client-server network and a peer-to-peer network. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A server is a central computer that provides services or resources (files, web pages, email, printing). Clients are computers that send requests to the server, which processes them and sends back the responses.

Q21Client-server & cloud · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) A call centre in Lahore uses thin clients. Give two advantages and one disadvantage of this. [3]

  1. (a) A thin client depends heavily on the server: most processing and storage is done by the server. A thick client can do most of its processing and store data itself, using the server only for some resources.

Q24Client-server & cloud · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

  1. (a) Calculate the total storage needed in TiB, correct to 2 decimal places. [2]

Q26Ethernet & CSMA/CD · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State what CSMA/CD stands for. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how CSMA/CD is used when a device on a shared Ethernet cable wants to send data. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Carrier Sense Multiple Access with Collision Detection
    B1.
  2. (b) The device listens to the cable to check whether it is idle (carrier sense). If it is idle it starts to transmit; if not, it waits. While transmitting it keeps checking for a collision (a change in voltage). If a collision is detected it stops sending and transmits a jam signal so all devices know. Each device then waits a random time before trying again; after repeated collisions the waiting time increases / it gives up after a set number.
    1 mark each, max 5: listens for traffic; transmits only if idle; monitors for collision during transmission; jam signal sent; random wait before retransmitting; repeated failures – longer wait / give up after limit.
Q27Ethernet & CSMA/CD · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why collisions can happen on a bus network even when every device uses carrier sense. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why collisions are rare on a modern Ethernet LAN where every computer is connected to its own port on a switch. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Two devices can both find the cable idle at the same moment and start transmitting together; because signals take time to travel along the cable, each device does not detect the other's transmission straight away.
    B1 two devices start at (nearly) the same time. B1 propagation delay – signal not yet reached the other device.
  2. (b) Each link between a computer and the switch is used only by that computer and the switch, and the switch can send and receive at the same time (full duplex). The switch sends each frame only to the destination port, so frames from different computers do not share a cable.
    B1 each device has a dedicated link / own collision domain. B1 full duplex or switch forwards to the destination port only.
Q28Ethernet & CSMA/CD · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

An Ethernet LAN uses this rule after a collision: after the nth collision of the same frame, a device waits k slot times, where k is chosen at random from 0 to 2ⁿ − 1 (inclusive). One slot time is 51.2 µs.

  1. (a) Calculate the longest possible wait after the third collision of a frame. [2]
  2. (b) Two devices collide for the first time. Calculate the probability that they choose the same k and so collide again. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the range of random waiting times grows after each collision. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 358.4 µs
    M1: k max = 2³ − 1 = 7. A1: 7 × 51.2 = 358.4 µs.
  2. (b) 1/2 (0.5)
    M1: k is 0 or 1 for each device. A1: P(same) = 2 × (1/2 × 1/2) = 1/2.
  3. (c) Repeated collisions mean the network is busy. A wider range makes it less likely that the devices choose the same waiting time again, so the chance of another collision falls, at the cost of a longer average wait.
    B1 network is busy/many devices competing. B1 larger range reduces chance of choosing the same slot / repeated collision.
Q29Wireless & cellular networks · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Wireless networks may use radio waves, microwaves or satellites. Give one benefit and one drawback of each. [3]
  2. (b) Give two advantages and two disadvantages of a wireless LAN compared with a wired LAN in a busy café in Islamabad. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Radio waves: travel through walls and over long distances / cheap; but low bandwidth and suffer interference. Microwaves: higher bandwidth than radio; but need line of sight and are absorbed by walls/rain. Satellites: cover very large or remote areas; but long delay (latency) and expensive.
    B1 each correct benefit + drawback pair.
  2. (b) Advantages: customers can connect their own devices without cables; easy to add many users / move around; no cabling to install. Disadvantages: less secure – signals can be intercepted, so encryption and passwords are needed; bandwidth is shared, so it slows with many users; interference and walls reduce range and reliability.
    1 mark each for two advantages and two disadvantages.
Q30Wireless & cellular networks · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A commuter makes a video call on her smartphone while riding the Lahore Metro bus from one end of the route to the other.

  1. (a) Describe how a cellular network is organised. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how the call continues without interruption as she moves from one cell to the next. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the call quality may fall in a crowded station. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The area is divided into small regions called cells, each with a base station (mast) that transmits and receives radio signals. Neighbouring cells use different frequencies so they do not interfere, and frequencies are reused in cells that are far apart. Base stations are connected by cable/fibre to the operator's core network and the internet.
    1 mark each, max 3: cells; base station/mast per cell; different frequencies for adjacent cells / frequency reuse; base stations linked to the wider network.
  2. (b) The network monitors signal strength from nearby base stations. When the signal from the next cell's base station becomes stronger, the call is handed over to that base station (handover/handoff) without dropping the connection.
    B1 signal strength monitored. B1 handover/handoff to next base station.
  3. (c) Many phones share the same cell's limited bandwidth/channels, so each gets a smaller share.
    B1.
