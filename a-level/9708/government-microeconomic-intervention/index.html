Q1Maximum and minimum prices · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A city government sets a maximum monthly rent for flats that is below the market equilibrium rent. Explain why this causes a shortage of rented flats. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why an illegal (shadow) market may develop for rented flats. [2]
  3. (c) State one effect of the maximum rent on the quality of rented flats. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) At the lower rent, quantity demanded rises and quantity supplied falls, so demand exceeds supply.
    1 mark: maximum rent is below equilibrium so it is binding. 1 mark: more households want to rent at the lower price (extension of demand). 1 mark: landlords supply fewer flats as returns fall (contraction of supply), e.g. convert them to offices or sell them – excess demand.
  2. (b) Because some tenants are willing to pay more than the legal maximum, landlords can charge extra payments secretly.
    1 mark: at the quantity supplied, tenants' willingness to pay is above the legal maximum. 1 mark: so landlords ask for illegal side payments (e.g. large 'key money' deposits), and flats go to those who can pay rather than those in most need.
  3. (c) Quality falls because landlords have less incentive and income to maintain them.
    B1.

  1. (a) Explain one reason why a government might set a minimum (support) price for sugarcane paid to growers by sugar mills. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the minimum price might lead to an inefficient use of scarce resources. [2]
Show answer

Q8Maximum and minimum prices · Standard · Paper 2 style · 10 marks

Invented data: in a country, the retail price of sugar rose to Rs 165 per kg after a poor sugar-cane harvest. The government then imposed a maximum retail price of Rs 140 per kg. A month later, newspapers reported that many shops had no sugar on their shelves.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage by which the maximum price is below the previous market price. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two likely effects of the maximum price on the sugar market. [4]
  3. (c) Consider whether a maximum price is more effective than a cash transfer to poor households for making sugar affordable. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15.2 %
    (165 − 140) ÷ 165 × 100 (M1) = 15.2 % (A1).
  2. (b) Shortage as Qd rises and Qs falls; illegal market or hoarding; supply diverted to other uses or smuggled abroad.
    2 marks each for two explained effects: shortage (excess demand) with empty shelves; illegal resale at higher prices; hoarding by traders expecting the control to end; mills cut supply or export; queues and time costs.
  3. (c) A cash transfer targets the poor and avoids shortages; a maximum price is cheaper for the budget but benefits all buyers and causes shortages.
    Analysis (up to 3): maximum price helps everyone who buys, including rich households and confectionery firms; causes shortage; no budget cost. Cash transfer targets poor, keeps market price, no shortage, but costs the government and may be spent on other goods. Evaluation (1): judgement, e.g. a cash transfer is likely more effective if there is a reliable register of poor households.

Q10Maximum and minimum prices · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain the effect of a minimum price set above equilibrium in a market where demand is price inelastic, and consider whether producers always gain from such a minimum price. [8]
Show answer
  1. (a) Explain: price rises to the minimum; quantity demanded falls only a little because demand is inelastic; quantity supplied rises, creating a surplus; with inelastic demand, consumer spending (producer revenue from sales) rises. Consider: producers who sell gain, but those who cannot sell the surplus lose unless the government buys it; higher output raises total costs; in the long run, extra entry can drive surplus up; if the government stops buying or the minimum is not enforced, prices may collapse; consumers may switch to substitutes over time (demand becomes more elastic). Q11Maximum and minimum prices · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

In the market for a basic medicine, demand is P = 100 − Q and supply is P = 20 + Q, where P is the price in dollars and Q is the quantity in thousands of packs per month (invented data).

  1. (a) Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity, and the consumer surplus and producer surplus at equilibrium. [3]
  2. (b) The government imposes a maximum price of $40. Calculate the new consumer surplus and producer surplus, assuming the units supplied go to the consumers who value them most. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the deadweight (welfare) loss and comment on whether consumers as a group gain from the maximum price. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = $60, Q = 40 thousand; consumer surplus $800 thousand; producer surplus $800 thousand
    100 − Q = 20 + Q gives Q = 40, P = 60 (B1). CS = ½ × 40 × (100 − 60) = 800 (B1). PS = ½ × 40 × (60 − 20) = 800 (B1).
  2. (b) Consumer surplus $1000 thousand; producer surplus $200 thousand
    Quantity supplied at $40: 40 = 20 + Q so Q = 20 (M1). CS = trapezium between demand and $40 from 0 to 20 = ½ × (60 + 40) × 20 = 1000 (A1). PS = ½ × 20 × 20 = 200 (A1).
  3. (c) Welfare loss $400 thousand; consumers who obtain the good gain (CS rises by $200 thousand) but 20 thousand units of demand are no longer met.
    Total surplus 1600 − 1200 = 400 (B1). Comment (B1): consumers as a group gain only if goods reach those who value them most; queuing or illegal markets would reduce this gain.
