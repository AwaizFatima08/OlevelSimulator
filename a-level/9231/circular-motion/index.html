Q1Horizontal circles · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A particle of mass 0.3 kg is attached to one end of a light inextensible string of length 0.8 m. The other end is fixed to a point O on a smooth horizontal table. The particle moves on the table in a horizontal circle with centre O at a constant speed of 4 m s⁻¹. Find the tension in the string. [2]
  2. (b) Find the angular speed of the particle and the time it takes to complete one revolution, giving the time correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6 N
    M1: T = mv²/r = 0.3 × 16/0.8.A1: T = 6 N.
  2. (b) ω = 5 rad s⁻¹; period 1.26 s
    B1: ω = v/r = 4/0.8 = 5 rad s⁻¹.B1: period = 2π/5 = 1.2566… ≈ 1.26 s.
Q2Horizontal circles · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A conical pendulum consists of a small bob of mass 0.2 kg on a light inextensible string of length 1.25 m, whose upper end is fixed at a point A. The bob moves in a horizontal circle below A with the string inclined at angle θ to the vertical, where cos θ = 0.8. Find the tension in the string. [2]
  2. (b) Find the angular speed of the bob. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.5 N
    M1: resolve vertically: T cos θ = mg.A1: T = 2/0.8 = 2.5 N.
  2. (b) √10 ≈ 3.16 rad s⁻¹
    B1: radius r = 1.25 × 0.6 = 0.75 m.M1: T sin θ = mω²r: 2.5 × 0.6 = 0.2 × ω² × 0.75.A1: ω² = 10, ω = 3.16 rad s⁻¹.
Q3Horizontal circles · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A car is driven round a horizontal roundabout in Lahore on a circular path of radius 40 m. The coefficient of friction between the tyres and the road is 0.6. Find the greatest speed at which the car can travel without slipping, correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) State, with a reason, whether the answer to part (a) depends on the mass of the car. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15.5 m s⁻¹
    M1: friction provides the centripetal force: F = mv²/r.M1: F ≤ μR = 0.6mg.A1: v² ≤ 0.6 × 10 × 40 = 240, v ≤ 15.5 m s⁻¹.
  2. (b) No: m cancels from mv²/r ≤ μmg.
    B1: mass cancels.

Q5Horizontal circles · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A section of a motorway near Islamabad is a horizontal circular arc of radius 120 m, banked at 15° to the horizontal. Find the speed at which a car can travel round the bend with no tendency to slip sideways, correct to 3 significant figures. [4]
  2. (b) A car travels round the bend faster than this speed. State the direction of the frictional force on the car. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 17.9 m s⁻¹
    M1: vertically R cos 15° = mg.M1: horizontally R sin 15° = mv²/120.M1: v² = 120g tan 15°.A1: v = 17.9 m s⁻¹.
  2. (b) Down the slope of the banking
    B1: friction acts down the slope to supply extra centripetal force.
Q6Horizontal circles · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A particle P of mass m moves on the smooth inner surface of a fixed hemispherical bowl of radius a, whose rim is horizontal. P describes a horizontal circle at a depth (3/5)a below the level of the rim, with constant angular speed ω. Show that the normal reaction on P has magnitude (5/3)mg. [3]
  2. (b) Find ω in terms of g and a. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = 5mg/3 (shown)
    M1: the reaction acts along the radius to the centre O of the bowl; its vertical component is R × (3a/5)/a.M1: R × 3/5 = mg.A1: R = 5mg/3.
  2. (b) ω = √(5g/(3a))
    B1: radius of circle = √(a² − 9a²/25) = 4a/5.M1: horizontal component R × (4/5) = mω²(4a/5).A1: ω² = R/(ma) = 5g/(3a).

Q9Horizontal circles · Standard · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². In a fairground 'rotor' ride, a person stands against the inside wall of a vertical cylinder of radius 2.5 m, which rotates about its vertical axis. The floor is then lowered. The coefficient of friction between the person and the wall is 0.5. Treating the person as a particle, show that the least angular speed of the cylinder for which the person does not slide down is 2√2 rad s⁻¹. [4]
  2. (b) Find the speed of the person when the cylinder rotates at this least angular speed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ω = 2√2 rad s⁻¹ (shown)
    M1: normal reaction R = mω²r provides centripetal force.M1: friction balances weight: F = mg with F ≤ μR.M1: mg ≤ 0.5 × mω² × 2.5.A1: ω² ≥ 8, ω ≥ 2√2.
  2. (b) 7.07 m s⁻¹
    B1: v = 2.5 × 2√2 = 5√2 = 7.07 m s⁻¹.
Q10Horizontal circles · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A bend on a race track is a horizontal circle of radius 50 m, and the track is banked at 20° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction between a car's tyres and the track is 0.3. Find the greatest speed at which the car can go round the bend without slipping up the slope, correct to 3 significant figures. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 19.3 m s⁻¹
    M1: friction F = 0.3R acts down the slope at the limiting case.M1: vertically: R cos 20° − 0.3R sin 20° = mg.M1: horizontally: R sin 20° + 0.3R cos 20° = mv²/50.M1: divide: v² = 50g (sin 20° + 0.3 cos 20°)/(cos 20° − 0.3 sin 20°).A1: v² = 372.7.A1: v = 19.3 m s⁻¹.
Q11Horizontal circles · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle P of mass 0.4 kg is attached to two light inextensible strings, each of length 0.5 m. The other ends of the strings are attached to fixed points A and B, where A is 0.8 m vertically above B. P moves in a horizontal circle with both strings taut and with angular speed 6 rad s⁻¹. Find the tension in each string. [5]
  2. (b) Find the least angular speed for which the string BP is taut. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) T(AP) = 6.1 N, T(BP) = 1.1 N
    B1: P is 0.4 m below A; radius = 0.3 m; each string makes angle θ with the vertical where cos θ = 0.8, sin θ = 0.6.M1: vertically: 0.8T_A − 0.8T_B = 0.4g ⇒ T_A − T_B = 5.M1: horizontally: 0.6(T_A + T_B) = 0.4 × 36 × 0.3 = 4.32 ⇒ T_A + T_B = 7.2.A1: T_A = 6.1 N.A1: T_B = 1.1 N.
  2. (b) 5 rad s⁻¹
    M1: T_B = 0 ⇒ T_A = 5, 0.6 × 5 = 0.4ω² × 0.3.A1: ω² = 25, ω = 5 rad s⁻¹.

Q13Horizontal circles · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A circular test track has radius 80 m and is banked at 25° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction between a vehicle's tyres and the track is 0.2. Find, correct to 3 significant figures, the least speed at which the vehicle can move round the track in a horizontal circle without slipping down the slope. [4]
  2. (b) Find the greatest speed at which the vehicle can move round the track without slipping up the slope. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 14.0 m s⁻¹
    M1: least speed: friction 0.2R acts up the slope.M1: R cos 25° + 0.2R sin 25° = mg.M1: R sin 25° − 0.2R cos 25° = mv²/80.A1: v² = 80g (sin 25° − 0.2 cos 25°)/(cos 25° + 0.2 sin 25°) = 194.9, v = 14.0 m s⁻¹.
  2. (b) 24.2 m s⁻¹
    M1: friction acts down the slope: R cos 25° − 0.2R sin 25° = mg; R sin 25° + 0.2R cos 25° = mv²/80.A1: v² = 587.9.A1: v = 24.2 m s⁻¹.
Q14Leaving the surface / string slack · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A particle P of mass m is attached to one end of a light inextensible string of length a, whose other end is fixed at O. P hangs at rest and is then projected horizontally with speed u. Show that P completes vertical circles provided u² ≥ 5ga. [4]
  2. (b) Take g = 10 m s⁻². Find the least value of u when a = 0.9 m, correct to 3 significant figures. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) u² ≥ 5ga (shown)
    M1: energy: v² at the top = u² − 4ga.M1: at the top: T + mg = mv²/a.M1: T ≥ 0 ⇒ v² ≥ ga.A1: u² − 4ga ≥ ga ⇒ u² ≥ 5ga.
  2. (b) 6.71 m s⁻¹
    B1: u = √45 = 6.71 m s⁻¹.
Q15Leaving the surface / string slack · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A particle is fixed to one end of a light rigid rod of length 0.5 m, freely pivoted at its other end O so that the particle moves in a vertical circle. Find the least speed the particle must have at its lowest point to make complete circles. [3]
  2. (b) The rod is replaced by a light inextensible string of the same length. Find the new least speed, and explain why it is greater. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) √20 ≈ 4.47 m s⁻¹
    M1: a rod can push, so the particle only needs speed ≥ 0 at the top.M1: u² − 2g(2 × 0.5) ≥ 0.A1: u ≥ √20 = 4.47 m s⁻¹.(Strictly u > √20 so that the particle passes over the top; accept u ≥ √20 as the least value.)
  2. (b) 5 m s⁻¹; a string cannot push, so tension ≥ 0 at the top needs v² ≥ ga there.
    B1: u² = 5ga = 25, u = 5 m s⁻¹.B1: explanation: string can only pull.
Q16Leaving the surface / string slack · Core · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A small ball moves in a vertical circle on the smooth inner surface of a fixed hollow cylinder of radius 1.6 m, whose axis is horizontal. Its speed at the lowest point is 7 m s⁻¹. Show that the ball does not reach the highest point while in contact with the surface. [2]
  2. (b) Find the height above the lowest point at which the ball leaves the surface, and its speed at that instant. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown: 7² = 49 < 5ga = 80
    M1: needs u² ≥ 5ga = 80 to stay in contact at the top.A1: 49 < 80, so it leaves the surface first.
  2. (b) Height 2.17 m (exactly 13/6 m); speed 2.38 m s⁻¹
    M1: at angle θ from the downward vertical, v² = 49 − 2g(1.6)(1 − cos θ).M1: R = 0: −mg cos θ = mv²/1.6 (θ > 90°).A1: cos θ = (32 − 49)/48 = −17/48; height = 1.6(1 + 17/48) = 2.17 m.A1: v² = −16 cos θ = 17/3, v = 2.38 m s⁻¹.

Q20Leaving the surface / string slack · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Take g = 10 m s⁻². A smooth solid hemisphere of radius 0.9 m is fixed with its plane face on horizontal ground. A particle is released from rest at the highest point and slides down the curved surface. Show that it leaves the surface when the radius to it makes an angle θ with the upward vertical where cos θ = 2/3, and find its speed then. [4]

  1. (a) cos θ = 2/3; speed √6 ≈ 2.45 m s⁻¹

Q21Leaving the surface / string slack · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) A particle P of mass m hangs at rest on a light inextensible string of length a attached to a fixed point O. P is projected horizontally with speed u and the string becomes slack when OP makes an angle of 150° with the downward vertical (that is, OP is 60° above the horizontal). Show that u² = ga(2 + (3/2)√3). [4]
  2. (b) Find, in terms of a, the greatest height above the lowest point reached by P in its subsequent motion. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) u² = ga(2 + 3√3/2) (shown)
    M1: T − mg cos θ = mv²/a with T = 0, θ = 150°: v² = −ga cos 150° = (√3/2)ga.M1: energy: u² = v² + 2ga(1 − cos 150°).M1: = (√3/2)ga + 2ga + √3 ga.A1: = ga(2 + 3√3/2).
  2. (b) a(1 + 9√3/16)
    B1: slack point is a(1 − cos 150°) = a(1 + √3/2) above the lowest point.M1: velocity is perpendicular to OP, at 30° above the horizontal; vertical component v/2.M1: further rise = (v/2)²/(2g) = (√3/2)ga/(8g) = √3a/16.A1: greatest height = a(1 + √3/2 + √3/16) = a(1 + 9√3/16).

Q26Vertical circles (energy & reaction) · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A small bead of mass m is threaded on a smooth circular wire of radius a, fixed in a vertical plane. The bead is projected from the lowest point with speed √(3ga). Show that when the radius to the bead makes an angle θ with the downward vertical, the reaction of the wire on the bead, measured towards the centre, is mg(1 + 3 cos θ). [4]
  2. (b) Find the value of cos θ when the bead first comes to instantaneous rest, and explain why it does not leave the wire. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = mg(1 + 3cos θ) (shown)
    M1: v² = 3ga − 2ga(1 − cos θ) = ga(1 + 2cos θ).M1: R − mg cos θ = mv²/a.A1: R = mg cos θ + mg(1 + 2cos θ).A1: = mg(1 + 3cos θ).
  2. (b) cos θ = −1/2 (θ = 120°); a bead threaded on a wire cannot leave it — the reaction simply changes direction (at cos θ = −1/3).
    B1: v = 0 ⇒ cos θ = −1/2.B1: threaded bead: reaction can act either way.

  2. (b) Take g = 10 m s⁻². For a particle of mass 0.5 kg on a string of length 0.8 m, the tension at the lowest point is three times the tension at the highest point. Find the speed at the highest point. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) T_bottom − T_top = 6mg (shown)
    M1: energy: u² = v² + 4ga (u at bottom, v at top).M1: T_bottom = mg + mu²/a; T_top = mv²/a − mg.M1: difference = 2mg + m(u² − v²)/a.A1: = 2mg + 4mg = 6mg.

    M1: 3T − T = 30 ⇒ T_top = 15 N.M1: 15 + 5 = 0.5v²/0.8.A1: v² = 32, v = 5.66 m s⁻¹.

Q29Vertical circles (energy & reaction) · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) A particle P of mass m is attached to a light inextensible string of length a whose other end is fixed at O. P is held with the string taut and horizontal and is then projected vertically downwards with speed √(3ga). Show that, when OP makes an angle θ with the downward vertical, the tension in the string is 3mg(1 + cos θ). [5]
  2. (b) Deduce that P just completes the circle, and state the speed of P at the highest point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) T = 3mg(1 + cos θ) (shown)
    M1: energy from the start (level of O): v² = 3ga + 2ga cos θ.M1: T − mg cos θ = mv²/a.A1: T = mg cos θ + 3mg + 2mg cos θ.A1: = 3mg(1 + cos θ).B1: correct handling of signs for θ > 90° (cos θ negative).
