Q8Goodness of fit · Core · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A confectioner in Bahawalpur says that the four colours of sweets in a large bag are equally common. A random sample of 200 sweets gave these colours:Red 58, Green 47, Yellow 52, Orange 43.

  1. (a) State suitable null and alternative hypotheses for a goodness-of-fit test of this claim, and show that the expected frequency for each colour is 50. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the value of the test statistic. [2]
  3. (c) Carry out the test at the 5% significance level and state your conclusion in context. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) H0: each colour has probability 0.25 (the colours are equally common). H1: the colours are not all equally common. Expected frequency = 200 × 0.25 = 50 for each colour.
    B1 hypotheses stated in terms of probabilities. B1 200 × 1/4 = 50 for each colour.
  2. (b) χ² = 2.52
    M1 Σ(O − E)²/E over the four colours. A1 (1.28 + 0.18 + 0.08 + 0.98) = 2.52 (accept 2.52 from the exact sum).
  3. (c) df = 4 − 1 = 3; critical value 7.815. 2.52 < 7.815, so do not reject H0. There is no evidence that the sweets are not equally common in each colour.
    B1 df = 3. B1 critical value 7.815. B1 comparison with the critical value and a conclusion in context.
Q9Goodness of fit · Core · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

In a genetics practical, guppies from one cross are counted. The theory predicts that 3 in every 4 offspring show the dominant colour and 1 in 4 shows the recessive colour. Out of 200 offspring, 158 showed the dominant colour and 42 the recessive colour.

  1. (a) Calculate the expected frequency of each group, and state the number of degrees of freedom. [2]
  2. (b) Carry out a goodness-of-fit test of the 3 : 1 ratio at the 5% significance level. [4]
  3. (c) Explain why the result of this test does not prove that the true ratio is exactly 3 : 1. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Expected: dominant 150, recessive 50. Degrees of freedom = 2 − 1 = 1.
    M1 200 × 3/4 = 150 and 200 × 1/4 = 50. A1 df = 1 (two classes, no parameter estimated).
  2. (b) χ² = 1.71; critical value 3.841. 1.71 < 3.841, so do not reject H0. The data are consistent with the 3 : 1 ratio.
    M1 Σ(O − E)²/E = (158 − 150)²/150 + (42 − 50)²/50. A1 1.71. B1 critical value 3.841. A1 conclusion in context.
  3. (c) A test that does not reject H0 only shows that the data are consistent with 3 : 1; other ratios may also fit a sample of this size, and random variation can hide small differences.
    B1 failing to reject H0 is not proof that H0 is true. B1 another ratio or random variation could fit the data as well.

  3. (c) A significant association shows only that the two variables are related. Other factors (such as family income, distance to school or the area the family lives in) may explain both the education level and the attendance, so the test cannot establish cause and effect.
    B1 association is not causation. B1 a confounding or other factor is named. B1 the test does not measure the direction or cause of the link.
