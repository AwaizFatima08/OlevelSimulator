Q15Goodness of fit · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) A toy company asked 60 children in Islamabad to choose their favourite of four colours for a new toy: red, blue, green and yellow. The choices were 20, 14, 10 and 16 respectively. Test, at the 5% significance level, whether all four colours are equally popular. [5]
  2. (b) A larger survey of 180 children produced exactly the same proportions choosing each colour. Show that the test statistic is now three times as large and state the conclusion of the same test. [3]
  3. (c) Comment on what this shows about the effect of sample size on a goodness-of-fit test. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) X² = 3.47, ν = 3, critical value 7.815; 3.47 < 7.815, so do not reject H₀: there is insufficient evidence that the colours differ in popularity
    B1 H₀: the four colours are equally popular; H₁: they are not; M1 each expected frequency 15; M1 X² = Σ(O − E)²/E = 3.47; B1 ν = 3, critical value 7.815; A1 3.47 < 7.815, so do not reject H₀: insufficient evidence.
  2. (b) X² = 10.4 (= 3 × 3.47); 10.4 > 7.815, reject H₀: the colours are not equally popular
    M1 every O and E is multiplied by 3, so each (O − E)²/E is multiplied by 9/3 = 3; A1 X² = 10.4; A1 exceeds 7.815, conclusion reverses.
  3. (c) Larger samples make the test more sensitive (more powerful): the same proportional discrepancy becomes significant
    B1
