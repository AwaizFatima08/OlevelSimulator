Q29Finding & using a PGF · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) A random variable X takes values 0, 1, 2, … with P(X = r) = (r + 1)/2^(r+2). Show that the PGF of X is G(t) = 1/(2 − t)². [3]
  2. (b) Find E(X) and Var(X). [4]
  3. (c) Find P(X ≤ 2). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown (see working).
    M1: G(t) = (1/4) Σ (r + 1)(t/2)ʳ.M1: Σ (r + 1)xʳ = (1 − x)⁻² with x = t/2.A1: G(t) = (1/4)(1 − t/2)⁻² = 1/(2 − t)².

Q30Finding & using a PGF · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) In a board game, a player must throw a six with a fair dice to start. Y is the number of throws needed. Show that the PGF of Y is G(t) = t/(6 − 5t). [2]
  2. (b) Explain why P(Y is odd) = (G(1) − G(−1))/2, and hence find this probability. [3]
  3. (c) Two players, Ayesha and Daniyal, take turns to throw the dice, Ayesha first; the first to throw a six starts the game. Use part (b) to state the probability that Ayesha starts. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown (see working).
    M1: G(t) = Σ (1/6)(5/6)^(r−1) tʳ = (t/6)/(1 − 5t/6).A1: = t/(6 − 5t).
  2. (b) 6/11
    M1: G(1) − G(−1) = Σ P(Y = r)(1 − (−1)ʳ) = 2 Σ over odd r of P(Y = r).M1: G(−1) = −1/11.A1: P(odd) = (1 + 1/11)/2 = 6/11.
  3. (c) 6/11
    B1: Ayesha starts exactly when the total number of throws is odd.
