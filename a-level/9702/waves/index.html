Q1Progressive waves (phase, intensity, CRO) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Ripples on the surface of a fish pond in Faisalabad have a frequency of 2.5 Hz and a wavelength of 0.84 m.

  1. (a) Define the frequency of a wave. [1]
  2. (b) Use the definitions of speed, frequency and wavelength to show that v = fλ. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the waves and their period. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The number of oscillations (wavelengths passing a point) per unit time.
    B1
  2. (b) In one period T the wave travels one wavelength λ, so v = λ/T, and f = 1/T gives v = fλ.
    M1 distance λ in time T; A1 f = 1/T leading to v = fλ
  3. (c) v = 2.1 m s⁻¹; T = 0.40 s
    v = 2.5 × 0.84 = 2.1 m s⁻¹ (B1); T = 1/2.5 = 0.40 s (B1)

Q5Progressive waves (phase, intensity, CRO) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A buoy floats on the sea near Gwadar harbour. As waves pass, the buoy makes 12 complete up-and-down oscillations in 60 s. The horizontal distance between adjacent wave crests is 18 m.

  1. (a) State what the motion of the buoy shows about the energy and the water carried by a progressive wave. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the frequency and the period of the waves. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the waves. [1]
  4. (d) Calculate the time taken for a crest to travel 450 m towards the shore, assuming the speed stays constant. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The wave transfers energy but the water (and buoy) only oscillates about a fixed position; no matter is transferred.
    B1: energy transferred without net transfer of the medium.
  2. (b) f = 0.20 Hz, T = 5.0 s
    f = 12/60 = 0.20 Hz (B1); T = 1/f = 5.0 s (B1).
  3. (c) 3.6 m s⁻¹
    v = fλ = 0.20 × 18 = 3.6 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  4. (d) 125 s
    t = 450 / 3.6 = 125 s (A1).

A microphone connected to a cathode-ray oscilloscope (CRO) detects a sound wave. The time-base is set at 2.0 ms per division and the Y-gain at 0.50 V per division. One complete cycle of the trace occupies 4.0 horizontal divisions and the vertical distance from a crest to a trough is 5.2 divisions.

  1. (a) Determine the frequency of the sound. [2]
  2. (b) Determine the amplitude of the signal. [1]
  3. (c) The speed of sound in air is 340 m s⁻¹. Calculate the wavelength of the sound. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 125 Hz
    T = 4.0 × 2.0 ms = 8.0 ms (C1); f = 1/0.0080 = 125 Hz (A1)
  2. (b) 1.3 V
    peak-to-peak = 5.2 × 0.50 = 2.6 V; amplitude = 1.3 V (B1)
  3. (c) 2.72 m
    λ = 340/125 = 2.72 m (B1)

Q11Progressive waves (phase, intensity, CRO) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A signal generator drives a loudspeaker. Trace 1 of a two-beam CRO shows the signal-generator voltage and trace 2 shows the output of a microphone in front of the loudspeaker. The time-base is set at 0.10 ms per division and one cycle of each trace occupies 5.0 horizontal divisions. The microphone is moved slowly away from the loudspeaker along a straight line. Starting from a position where the two traces are in phase, it must be moved 0.170 m before the traces are next in phase.

  1. (a) Calculate the frequency of the sound. [2]
  2. (b) State the wavelength of the sound and calculate the speed of sound in the air. [2]
  3. (c) From an in-phase position the microphone is moved a further 0.0425 m away from the loudspeaker. Calculate the phase difference between the two traces. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why a student should measure the distance moved through several successive in-phase positions rather than just one. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2000 Hz
    T = 5.0 × 0.10 ms = 0.50 ms (C1); f = 1/T = 2000 Hz (A1).
  2. (b) λ = 0.170 m; v = 340 m s⁻¹
    λ = distance between successive in-phase positions = 0.170 m (B1); v = fλ = 2000 × 0.170 = 340 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 90°
    Δφ = (0.0425/0.170) × 360° (C1) = 90° (A1).
  4. (d) A larger distance gives a smaller percentage uncertainty in λ (divide by the number of wavelengths).
    B1: smaller percentage (fractional) uncertainty.

A small loudspeaker at an open-air event emits sound of power 0.60 W uniformly in all directions. Assume no energy is absorbed by the air.

  1. (a) Define intensity. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the intensity of the sound at a distance of 5.0 m from the loudspeaker. [2]
  3. (c) Determine the distance from the loudspeaker at which the amplitude of the sound wave is half its value at 5.0 m. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Power transmitted per unit area (perpendicular to the direction of travel).
    B1
  2. (b) 1.9 × 10⁻³ W m⁻²

  3. (c) 10 m
    amplitude halved → intensity ÷ 4 (C1); I ∝ 1/r² so r doubles to 10 m (A1)
Q13Progressive waves (phase, intensity, CRO) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

One cycle of each trace occupies 6.0 divisions and the trace from B is shifted 1.5 divisions to the right (lagging) relative to that from A.

  1. (a) Calculate the frequency of the sound. [2]
  2. (b) Determine the phase difference between the two signals. [1]
  3. (c) Microphone B is moved slowly further from the source along the line from the source. Calculate the extra distance it must be moved, from its original position, before the two traces are next in phase. The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 333 Hz
    T = 6.0 × 0.50 ms = 3.0 ms (C1); f = 1/0.0030 = 333 Hz (A1)
  2. (b) 90° (π/2 rad)
    1.5/6.0 × 360° = 90° (B1)
  3. (c) 0.77 m
    λ = 340/333 = 1.02 m (C1); B lags by ¼ cycle, so it must move a further ¾λ to lag by a whole cycle (C1); 0.75 × 1.02 = 0.765 ≈ 0.77 m (A1)

Q15Progressive waves (phase, intensity, CRO) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

The intensity of sunlight just above the Earth's atmosphere is 1.36 kW m⁻². The mean distance from the Sun to the Earth is 1.50 × 10¹¹ m and from the Sun to Mars is 2.28 × 10¹¹ m. Assume that the Sun radiates uniformly in all directions and that no energy is absorbed between the planets.

  1. (a) Calculate the total power emitted by the Sun. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the intensity of sunlight at Mars. [2]
  3. (c) A rover on Mars carries solar panels of total area 2.0 m² facing the Sun, with an efficiency of 22%. Calculate the electrical power produced. [1]
  4. (d) Calculate the ratio (amplitude of the light wave at Mars)/(amplitude of the light wave at the Earth). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.85 × 10²⁶ W
    P = I × 4πr² (M1) = 1360 × 4π × (1.50 × 10¹¹)² = 3.85 × 10²⁶ W (A1).
  2. (b) 589 W m⁻²
    I ∝ 1/r² (M1): I = 1360 × (1.50/2.28)² = 589 W m⁻² (A1).
  3. (c) 259 W
