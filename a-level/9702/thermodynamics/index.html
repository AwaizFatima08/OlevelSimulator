Q1Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by the internal energy of a system. [2]
  2. (b) State the first law of thermodynamics, explaining the meaning of each symbol in ΔU = q + W. [2]
  3. (c) State how the first law is related to the principle of conservation of energy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The sum of a random distribution of kinetic and potential energies of the molecules in the system.
    B1 sum of kinetic and potential energies of molecules; B1 random distribution.
  2. (b) The increase in internal energy ΔU of a system equals the thermal energy q supplied to the system plus the work W done on the system.
    B1 ΔU = increase in internal energy; B1 q = heating of system, W = work done on system.
  3. (c) It is a statement of conservation of energy: the energy of the system changes only by the energy transferred to it by heating and by work.
    B1.
Q2Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A gas in a cylinder absorbs 500 J of thermal energy and, at the same time, does 200 J of work on its surroundings as it expands. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas. [2]
  2. (b) The gas is ideal. State and explain what happens to its temperature. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) +300 J (an increase)
    q = +500 J, W = −200 J (work done by gas) (M1); ΔU = 500 − 200 = +300 J (A1).
  2. (b) It rises: for an ideal gas the internal energy is the total kinetic energy of the molecules, which is proportional to the thermodynamic temperature.
    B1 temperature increases; B1 internal energy of ideal gas is only random KE ∝ T.
Q3Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A gas is compressed at a constant pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa from a volume of 2.5 × 10⁻³ m³ to 1.5 × 10⁻³ m³. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done on the gas. [2]
  2. (b) During the compression 40 J of thermal energy is lost from the gas. Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 100 J
    W = pΔV (M1) = 1.0 × 10⁵ × (2.5 − 1.5) × 10⁻³ = 100 J, done on the gas (A1).
  2. (b) +60 J
    q = −40 J, W = +100 J (M1); ΔU = −40 + 100 = +60 J (A1).
Q4Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the internal energy of an ideal gas consists only of the kinetic energy of its molecules. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a change in internal energy of an ideal gas is proportional to the change in its thermodynamic temperature. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) There are no intermolecular forces in an ideal gas (except during collisions), so the molecules have no potential energy; the internal energy is the random kinetic energy only.
    B1 no intermolecular forces; B1 so potential energy is zero.
  2. (b) Mean kinetic energy of a molecule = 3/2 kT, so the total kinetic energy of N molecules is 3/2 NkT; ΔU = 3/2 NkΔT ∝ ΔT.
    B1 mean KE ∝ T (= 3/2 kT); B1 U = N × mean KE so ΔU ∝ ΔT.
Q5Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A block of ice is melting in a glass of water at 0 °C on a summer afternoon in Lahore.

  1. (a) State what happens to the kinetic energy and to the potential energy of the molecules while the ice melts. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the increase in internal energy when 0.25 kg of ice melts at 0 °C. Assume the work done is negligible. (specific latent heat of fusion of ice = 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹) [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the internal energy increases even though the temperature does not. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kinetic energy stays the same (constant temperature); potential energy increases (bonds broken / molecules move further apart in the disordered liquid).
    B1 KE constant; B1 PE increases.
  2. (b) 8.35 × 10⁴ J
    q = mL = 0.25 × 3.34 × 10⁵ (M1); W ≈ 0, so ΔU = q = 8.35 × 10⁴ J (A1).
  3. (c) Internal energy includes potential energy, and this increases during melting.
    B1.
Q6Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) The air in a bicycle pump is compressed very quickly by pushing the handle in. Explain why q is approximately zero. [1]
  2. (b) 18 J of work is done on the air during this compression. State the change in internal energy of the air. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the pump feels warm afterwards. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The compression happens so quickly that there is no time for significant thermal energy transfer to or from the surroundings.
    B1.
  2. (b) +18 J
    ΔU = q + W = 0 + 18 = +18 J (B1).
  3. (c) The internal energy of the air increased, so its temperature rose; thermal energy then flows from the hot air to the pump barrel.
    B1 temperature of air rises; B1 heat transfer to the barrel.
Q7Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) For each process, state whether q, W and ΔU for the gas are positive, negative or zero:(i) an ideal gas is heated in a sealed rigid container;(ii) an ideal gas expands slowly at constant temperature;(iii) an ideal gas is compressed so quickly that no thermal energy is transferred. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) q +, W 0, ΔU +; (ii) q +, W −, ΔU 0; (iii) q 0, W +, ΔU +.
    2 marks per process (all three signs correct 2 marks; two correct 1 mark): (i) volume constant so W = 0, ΔU = q > 0; (ii) T constant so ΔU = 0, gas does work so W < 0, hence q = −W > 0; (iii) q = 0, work done on gas W > 0, so ΔU > 0.
Q8Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A gas in a cylinder is heated with 150 J of thermal energy. It expands at a constant pressure of 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa and its volume increases by 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done on the gas. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) −60 J
    Work done by gas = pΔV = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ = 60 J (M1); work done on gas W = −60 J (A1).
  2. (b) +90 J
    ΔU = q + W = 150 + (−60) (M1) = +90 J (A1).

Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by an isothermal change. [1]
  2. (b) An ideal gas is compressed isothermally and 250 J of work is done on it. Determine the thermal energy transferred, and state its direction. [3]

  1. (a) A change that takes place at constant temperature.

Q11Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

1.0 kg of water at 100 °C is turned into steam at 100 °C at atmospheric pressure (1.01 × 10⁵ Pa). The volume changes from 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 1.67 m³. The specific latent heat of vaporisation of water is 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done by the steam in pushing back the atmosphere. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the increase in internal energy of the water as it becomes steam. [2]
  3. (c) Explain, in terms of molecules, why most of the latent heat increases the internal energy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.69 × 10⁵ J
    W = pΔV = 1.01 × 10⁵ × (1.67 − 0.001) (M1) = 1.69 × 10⁵ J (A1).
  2. (b) 2.09 × 10⁶ J
    q = mL = 2.26 × 10⁶ J; W = −1.69 × 10⁵ J (M1); ΔU = 2.09 × 10⁶ J (A1).
  3. (c) Most of the energy is used to separate the molecules (break intermolecular bonds), increasing their potential energy; only a small part does work against the atmosphere.
    B1.
Q12Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

0.50 mol of an ideal monatomic gas is heated at a constant pressure of 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa from 300 K to 400 K. (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; U = 3/2 nRT) Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done by the gas. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the increase in internal energy of the gas. [1]
  3. (c) Use the first law to calculate the thermal energy supplied. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 416 J
    pΔV = nRΔT (M1) = 0.50 × 8.31 × 100 = 416 J (A1).
  2. (b) 623 J
    ΔU = 3/2 nRΔT = 623 J (B1).
  3. (c) 1040 J
    q = ΔU − W = 623 − (−416) (M1) = 1040 J (A1).
Q13Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A fixed mass of gas is taken round a cycle A→B→C→D→A. A (1.0 × 10⁻³ m³, 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa); B (3.0 × 10⁻³ m³, 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa); C (3.0 × 10⁻³ m³, 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa); D (1.0 × 10⁻³ m³, 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa). Each stage is a straight line on a p–V graph. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done on the gas in each of the four stages A→B, B→C, C→D and D→A. [3]
  2. (b) State the total change in internal energy of the gas for one complete cycle. [1]
  3. (c) Hence determine the net thermal energy supplied to the gas in one cycle. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A→B: −400 J; B→C: 0; C→D: +200 J; D→A: 0
    A→B expansion at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa: W = −2.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = −400 J (B1); B→C and D→A constant volume, W = 0 (B1); C→D: +1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = +200 J (B1).
  2. (b) 0
    Returns to the same state, so ΔU = 0 (B1).
  3. (c) 200 J
    0 = q + (−400 + 200) (M1); q = +200 J (net heat in, equal to the area enclosed) (A1).
Q14Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A gas expands from 1.5 × 10⁻³ m³ to 4.2 × 10⁻³ m³ against a constant atmospheric pressure of 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa while 520 J of thermal energy is supplied. (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹) Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done on the gas. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the increase in internal energy. [1]
  3. (c) The gas is 0.15 mol of an ideal monatomic gas. Calculate its rise in temperature. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) −273 J
    W = −pΔV = −1.01 × 10⁵ × 2.7 × 10⁻³ (M1) = −273 J (A1).
  2. (b) 247 J
    ΔU = q + W = 520 + (−272.7) = 247 J (B1).
  3. (c) 132 K
    ΔT = ΔU ÷ (3/2 nR) (M1) = 247.3 ÷ (1.5 × 0.15 × 8.31) = 132 K (A1).
Q15Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

2.0 mol of an ideal monatomic gas is in a sealed rigid steel container. 1.5 kJ of thermal energy is supplied to it. (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; U = 3/2 nRT) Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) State the work done on the gas and explain your answer. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the rise in temperature of the gas. [2]
  3. (c) The initial temperature is 290 K. Calculate the ratio (final pressure ÷ initial pressure). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Zero — the container is rigid, so the volume does not change.
    B1.
  2. (b) 60.2 K
    ΔU = q = 1500 J = 3/2 nRΔT (M1); ΔT = 1500 ÷ (1.5 × 2.0 × 8.31) = 60.2 K (A1).
  3. (c) 1.21
    p ∝ T at constant volume (M1); ratio = 350 ÷ 290 = 1.21 (A1).
Q16Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Use the kinetic model to explain why the temperature of a gas rises when a piston is pushed in quickly to compress it. [3]
  2. (b) Explain, using the first law, why the temperature rise is smaller if the piston is pushed in very slowly. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Molecules hitting the inward-moving piston rebound with a greater speed; so the mean kinetic energy of the molecules increases; mean KE ∝ T, so temperature rises.
    B1 molecules rebound faster from moving piston; B1 mean KE increases; B1 KE ∝ thermodynamic temperature.
  2. (b) With a slow compression there is time for thermal energy to leave the gas (q negative), so ΔU = q + W is smaller than W.
    B1 heat lost to surroundings; B1 ΔU = W + q with q < 0, so smaller ΔU and ΔT.
Q17Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Gas is trapped in an insulated cylinder by a freely moving piston of area 4.0 × 10⁻³ m², so the gas pressure stays at 1.1 × 10⁵ Pa. A heater inside the cylinder carries a current of 2.0 A at 12 V for 30 s, and the piston moves out by 6.0 cm. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the thermal energy supplied by the heater. [1]

  3. (c) Calculate the increase in internal energy of the gas. [1]
  4. (d) State one assumption made in (c). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 720 J
    q = VIt = 12 × 2.0 × 30 = 720 J (B1).
  2. (b) −26.4 J
    ΔV = AΔx = 4.0 × 10⁻³ × 0.060 = 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ m³ (C1); W = −pΔV = −26.4 J (A1).
  3. (c) 694 J
    ΔU = 720 + (−26.4) = 694 J (B1).

    B1.

Q20Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

During an expansion, a gas does 350 J of work on its surroundings and its internal energy falls by 120 J. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Write down the values of ΔU and W for the gas, with correct signs. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate q and state whether thermal energy enters or leaves the gas. [2]

Q21Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A gas is taken round a cycle. A→B: heated at constant volume, receiving 600 J of thermal energy. B→C: expands with no thermal energy transfer, doing 450 J of work on the surroundings. C→A: compressed at constant pressure back to A; 180 J of work is done on the gas. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Copy and complete a table giving q, W and ΔU (in J) for each stage A→B, B→C and C→A. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the net work done by the gas in one cycle. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the efficiency of the cycle, defined as net work done by the gas ÷ thermal energy supplied. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A→B: q +600, W 0, ΔU +600; B→C: q 0, W −450, ΔU −450; C→A: q −330, W +180, ΔU −150
    B1 A→B row; B1 B→C row; B1 ΔU(C→A) = −150 J since total ΔU over cycle = 0; B1 q(C→A) = ΔU − W = −150 − 180 = −330 J.
  2. (b) 270 J
    450 − 180 = 270 J (B1).
  3. (c) 45 %
    270 ÷ 600 = 0.45 = 45% (B1).
Q22Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

An ideal monatomic gas (U = 3/2 nRT = 3/2 pV) is at state A: pressure 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa, volume 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³, temperature 300 K. It is heated at constant volume to state B at 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa, then heated at constant pressure until its volume is 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³ (state C). Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the temperatures at B and at C. [2]
  2. (b) Show that the increase in internal energy from A to B is 600 J. [2]
  3. (c) For the change B→C, calculate the work done on the gas, the increase in internal energy and the thermal energy supplied. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) T_B = 900 K; T_C = 1800 K
    A→B: constant V, T ∝ p, T_B = 3 × 300 = 900 K (B1); B→C: constant p, T ∝ V, T_C = 2 × 900 = 1800 K (B1).
  2. (b) 600 J
    ΔU = 3/2 nRΔT = 3/2 Δ(pV) (M1) = 1.5 × (3.0 × 10⁵ − 1.0 × 10⁵) × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 600 J (A1).
  3. (c) W = −600 J; ΔU = 900 J; q = 1500 J
    W = −pΔV = −3.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = −600 J (B1); ΔU = 3/2 pΔV = 900 J (B1); q = ΔU − W = 1500 J (B1).
Q23Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A 2.0 kW electric kettle is boiling water at 100 °C at atmospheric pressure (1.01 × 10⁵ Pa). Assume all the electrical energy is used to turn water into steam. Specific latent heat of vaporisation of water = 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹; density of steam at 100 °C = 0.598 kg m⁻³. Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of water turned into steam each second. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of steam produced each second. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the rate at which work is done by the steam against the atmosphere, ignoring the volume of the liquid water. [2]
  4. (d) Hence calculate the rate of increase of internal energy of the water–steam system. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.85 × 10⁻⁴ kg s⁻¹
    2000 ÷ 2.26 × 10⁶ = 8.85 × 10⁻⁴ kg s⁻¹ (B1).
  2. (b) 0.00148 m³ s⁻¹
    V = m/ρ = 8.85 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.598 = 0.00148 m³ s⁻¹ (B1).
  3. (c) 149 W
    P = pΔV/Δt (M1) = 1.01 × 10⁵ × 0.00148 = 149 W (A1).
  4. (d) 1850 W
    2000 − 149 = 1850 W (B1).

Q27Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

0.60 mol of an ideal monatomic gas is cooled at a constant pressure of 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa from 450 K to 300 K. (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; U = 3/2 nRT) Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done on the gas. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the change in internal energy. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the thermal energy transferred and state its direction. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the thermal energy removed is greater in magnitude than the decrease in internal energy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 748 J
    W = −pΔV = −nRΔT = −0.60 × 8.31 × (300 − 450) (M1) = +748 J (A1).
  2. (b) −1120 J
    ΔU = 3/2 nRΔT = −1120 J (B1).
  3. (c) −1870 J (thermal energy leaves the gas)
    q = ΔU − W = −1120 − 748 (M1) = −1870 J (A1).
  4. (d) Work is being done on the gas as it contracts, which adds energy; so more thermal energy must be removed than the fall in U.
    B1.

Q30Internal energy & first law of thermodynamics · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Use the first law of thermodynamics in the form ΔU = q + W, where q is the thermal energy transferred to the system and W is the work done on the system.

  1. (a) Two identical samples of an ideal gas start in the same state. Sample X expands isothermally and sample Y expands with no thermal energy transfer (adiabatically), both to the same final volume. Use the first law to explain which sample ends at the lower temperature and which ends at the lower pressure. [4]
  2. (b) State which sample does more work on its surroundings, and explain using a p–V graph described in words. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Y ends at the lower temperature and the lower pressure. X: ΔU = 0, the work done by the gas is replaced by heat input, so T constant. Y: q = 0 and W is negative, so ΔU < 0 and T falls. At the same volume, p ∝ T, so Y has the lower pressure.
    B1 X: ΔU = 0 so q = −W (heat in balances work out); B1 Y: q = 0, W < 0 so ΔU < 0; B1 Y cooler; B1 same V so lower T means lower p (pV = nRT).
  2. (b) X: its pressure is higher at every volume during the expansion, so the area under its p–V curve is larger.
    B1 X; B1 its curve lies above Y's curve, so greater area = greater work.
