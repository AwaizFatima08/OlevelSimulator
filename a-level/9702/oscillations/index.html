Q1Simple harmonic motion · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define simple harmonic motion. [2]
  2. (b) Write down the defining equation of simple harmonic motion and state the meaning of each symbol. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Motion in which the acceleration is proportional to the displacement from a fixed point and is always directed towards that point (opposite to the displacement).
    B1 a ∝ x; B1 directed towards the fixed point / opposite to x
  2. (b) a = −ω²x; a acceleration, ω angular frequency, x displacement from equilibrium.
    B1 equation; B1 symbols
Q2Simple harmonic motion · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A mass hanging from a spring oscillates vertically. A student measures the time for 20 complete oscillations as 13.6 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the period of the oscillations. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the frequency. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the angular frequency. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the student times 20 oscillations rather than one. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.68 s
    13.6/20 = 0.68 s (B1)
  2. (b) 1.47 Hz
    1/0.68 = 1.47 Hz (B1)
  3. (c) 9.24 rad s⁻¹
    ω = 2π/T = 9.24 rad s⁻¹ (B1)
  4. (d) To reduce the percentage uncertainty in the period (reaction time is a smaller fraction of the total time).
    B1
Q3Simple harmonic motion · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define (i) the period and (ii) the angular frequency of an oscillation. [2]
  2. (b) The mains supply in Pakistan has a frequency of 50 Hz. Calculate its period and its angular frequency. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) the time for one complete oscillation; (ii) 2π × frequency (rate of change of phase angle), ω = 2π/T.
    B1 each
  2. (b) T = 0.020 s; ω = 314 rad s⁻¹
    T = 1/50 = 0.020 s (B1); ω = 2π × 50 = 314 rad s⁻¹ (B1)

Q7Simple harmonic motion · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A trolley held between two springs oscillates with simple harmonic motion of amplitude 5.0 cm and angular frequency 8.0 rad s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum speed of the trolley. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the trolley when its displacement is 3.0 cm. [2]

    a = ω²x = 64 × 0.030 = 1.9 m s⁻² (B1)
Q8Simple harmonic motion · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A block of mass 0.40 kg oscillates with simple harmonic motion of angular frequency 6.0 rad s⁻¹ and amplitude 5.0 cm.

  1. (a) Calculate the total energy of the oscillations. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the kinetic energy and the potential energy when the displacement is 3.0 cm. [2]
  3. (c) Describe how the kinetic energy varies with displacement x, and state the shape of the graph of kinetic energy against x. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.018 J
    E = ½mω²x₀² = ½ × 0.40 × 6.0² × 0.050² (C1) = 0.018 J (A1)
  2. (b) KE = 0.0115 J; PE = 0.0065 J

  3. (c) KE is maximum at x = 0 and zero at x = ±x₀; the graph is an inverted parabola.
    B1 max at centre, zero at ends; B1 inverted parabola

Q20Simple harmonic motion · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A trolley of mass 0.25 kg on a horizontal frictionless track is attached to a spring system that provides a restoring force F = −kx, where k = 40 N m⁻¹ and x is the displacement. The trolley is pulled 4.0 cm from equilibrium and released.

Q22Simple harmonic motion · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

  1. (a) At the top of the motion the platform's downward acceleration is greatest; if this exceeds g, the coin (whose maximum downward acceleration is g) cannot follow and leaves the surface.

  2. (b) 1.6 cm

Q25Damping & resonance · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by damping. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the motion of a system that is (i) lightly damped, (ii) critically damped, (iii) heavily damped, after being displaced and released. [3]
  3. (c) State which type of damping is used in the suspension of a car and explain why. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The loss of energy (and so a decrease in amplitude) of an oscillating system caused by resistive forces.
    B1
  2. (b) (i) oscillates with amplitude decreasing gradually (period nearly unchanged); (ii) returns to equilibrium in the shortest time without oscillating; (iii) returns to equilibrium slowly without oscillating.
    B1 each
  3. (c) Critical damping, so the car returns to its rest position quickly without bouncing.
    B1
Q26Damping & resonance · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by resonance. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the effect of increasing the damping on the graph of amplitude against driving frequency. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) When the driving frequency of a forced oscillation equals the natural frequency of the system, the amplitude is a maximum (maximum energy transfer).
    B1 driving frequency = natural frequency; B1 maximum amplitude
  2. (b) The peak amplitude is lower at all frequencies, the peak becomes broader (flatter), and the resonant frequency shifts slightly lower.
    B1 lower amplitude; B1 broader peak; B1 peak moves to lower frequency

  1. (a) State the natural frequency of the system and explain how you identified it. [2]
  2. (b) The oscillating mass is 0.30 kg. Using ω² = k/m, estimate the spring constant of the spring. [2]
  3. (c) The mass is replaced by one of 1.20 kg. State the new natural frequency. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) About 2.0 Hz, where the amplitude is a maximum (resonance).
    B1 2.0 Hz; B1 maximum amplitude at resonance
  2. (b) 47 N m⁻¹
    ω = 2π × 2.0 = 12.6 rad s⁻¹ (C1); k = 0.30 × 12.6² = 47 N m⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) 1.0 Hz
    f ∝ 1/√m: mass ×4 → f halved (B1)
Q29Damping & resonance · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A car of mass 1200 kg has a suspension of effective spring constant 6.0 × 10⁴ N m⁻¹. It drives over a series of speed bumps spaced 9.0 m apart along a road in Islamabad.

  1. (a) Calculate the natural frequency of vertical oscillation of the car on its suspension, using f = (1/2π)√(k/m). [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed at which the car would bounce with the largest amplitude, and explain your answer. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the shock absorbers are designed to give a large amount of damping (close to critical damping). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.1 Hz
    f = (1/2π)√(6.0 × 10⁴/1200) (C1) = 1.13 Hz (A1)
  2. (b) 10 m s⁻¹ (about 36 km h⁻¹)
    bumps act as a periodic driving force (B1); resonance when bump frequency v/9.0 = 1.13 Hz (C1); v = 10 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) The damping reduces the amplitude at resonance and makes the car return to its rest position quickly, without continuing to bounce.
    B1 smaller amplitude at resonance / oscillations die away quickly
Q30Damping & resonance · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A footbridge across a canal has a natural frequency of vertical oscillation of 2.0 Hz.

  1. (a) A group of joggers, each with a stride of 1.5 m, crosses the bridge in step. Calculate the jogging speed at which the bridge would oscillate with the largest amplitude. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why soldiers are often told to break step when marching across a bridge. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one way engineers can reduce the risk without changing the bridge's natural frequency. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.0 m s⁻¹
    resonance when step frequency = 2.0 Hz (C1); v = 2.0 × 1.5 = 3.0 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  2. (b) Marching in step gives a periodic driving force; if its frequency matches the natural frequency of the bridge, resonance causes large, possibly damaging, oscillations.
    B1 periodic force at one frequency; B1 resonance → large amplitude
  3. (c) Fit dampers (e.g. tuned mass dampers or viscous dampers) to increase damping.
    B1
