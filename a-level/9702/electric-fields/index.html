Q4Electric potential & potential energy · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Define the electric potential at a point. [2]
  2. (b) A point charge of +6.0 nC is in a vacuum. Calculate the electric potential at a point 0.15 m from the charge. Take ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the potential at 0.30 m from the charge and explain why it is not double the value in (b). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The electric potential at a point is the work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity to that point.
    1 mark for work done; 1 mark for per unit positive charge from infinity.
  2. (b) 360 V
    V = Q/(4πε₀r) = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 6.0 × 10⁻⁹/0.15 = 360 V.
  3. (c) 180 V; the potential is inversely proportional to r, so doubling r halves V (it does not double).
    V = 360/2 = 180 V. 1 mark for the value; 1 mark for V ∝ 1/r, so doubling the distance halves the potential.
Q5Electric potential & potential energy · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) An electron is at rest at a point where the electric potential is −50 V. It is moved to a point where the potential is +120 V. Explain, in terms of the electric potential energy, why the electron gains kinetic energy. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the change in electric potential energy of the electron. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the speed of the electron at the point where the potential is +120 V, using the energy change in (b). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The potential energy of a negative charge is U = qV, so it is higher at lower potential. Moving to higher potential lowers its electric potential energy, and that energy is converted into kinetic energy (energy is conserved).
    1 mark for U = qV with q negative; 1 mark for energy conservation with KE increasing.
  2. (b) -2.72 × 10⁻¹⁷ J
    ΔU = qΔV = (−1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)(120 − (−50)) = (−1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)(170) = −2.72 × 10⁻¹⁷ J.
  3. (c) 7.73 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹
    KE gained = 2.72 × 10⁻¹⁷ J. ½mv² = 2.72 × 10⁻¹⁷, so v = √(2 × 2.72 × 10⁻¹⁷/9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 7.73 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.

Q12Electric potential & potential energy · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A point charge of +2.0 nC is fixed at the origin and a point charge of −3.0 nC is fixed at x = 0.40 m on the same line. Calculate the electric potential at the midpoint between the charges. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the electric potential at the point P, which is on the same line at x = 0.30 m. [2]

Q13Electric potential & potential energy · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A particle of mass 3.0 mg and charge +2.0 μC is released from rest at a point where the electric potential is +400 V. Calculate the kinetic energy it gains when it reaches a point where the potential is +100 V. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the particle at this point. [2]
  3. (c) State whether the result depends on the path taken between the two points. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ J
    ΔKE = qΔV = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ × (400 − 100) = 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ J.
  2. (b) 20 m s⁻¹

    1 mark.
Q14Electric potential & potential energy · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A singly-charged positive ion of mass 2.99 × 10⁻²⁶ kg is accelerated from rest through a potential difference of 1.5 kV in a vacuum. Explain why its kinetic energy after acceleration equals qV. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the ion after acceleration. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the acceleration must take place in a vacuum. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The work done by the electric field on the ion equals the change in electric potential energy, qV. With no other forces doing work, all of this becomes kinetic energy: ΔKE = qV.
    1 mark for work done by the field equals qΔV; 1 mark for energy conservation.

  3. (c) Gas molecules would collide with the ion and reduce its speed, so the ion would not gain the full kinetic energy qV.
    1 mark.

Q20Electric potential & potential energy · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the proton after it has moved this distance. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the acceleration of the proton is constant while it is in the field. [1]
Show answer

  3. (c) The plates are 2.0 mm apart. Calculate the potential difference between the plates. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The field between large parallel plates is E = σ/ε₀, where σ = Q/A is the charge per unit area; the field does not depend on the separation of the plates.

  2. (b) 5.65 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹
    E = Q/(ε₀A) = 2.0 × 10⁻⁸/(8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.040) = 5.65 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹.
