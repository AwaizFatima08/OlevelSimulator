Q1Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A student drops a bar magnet vertically through a coil of wire connected to a data-logger. It is suggested that the maximum e.m.f. E induced in the coil is related to the height h from which the magnet is released above the coil by E = k hⁿ, where k and n are constants.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between E and h. Explain how your results could be used to determine the values of k and n. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Vary h; measure peak E with a data-logger or oscilloscope; keep the same magnet, coil (turns, area) and orientation; release from rest through a tube to keep it vertical; plot lg E against lg h: gradient = n, y-intercept = lg k.
    Mark points: independent variable h, dependent variable E (B1); control: same magnet/strength, same coil (number of turns, area), magnet passes through same point/same orientation (B1, B1). Measure h with a metre rule from bottom of magnet to top of coil (B1); connect coil to data-logger/CRO, read the peak e.m.f. from the trace (B1); release magnet from rest, use a vertical plastic tube as a guide (B1). Plot lg E against lg h (B1); n = gradient, k = 10^(y-intercept) (B1). Safety: use a soft landing (sand tray/foam) so the falling magnet does not break or hit feet (B1). Detail: use a set-square to check the tube is vertical; repeat each h and average peak E; use a high data-logging rate; check the straight line confirms the relationship; keep magnet away from steel; avoid magnet touching the tube sides (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q2Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A flat circular coil carries a constant current. It is suggested that the magnetic flux density B at the centre of the coil is related to the radius r of the coil by B = k/r, where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between B and r. Explain how your results could be used to determine a value for k. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Make coils of the same number of turns but different radii (wind round cylinders of known diameter); keep current constant with a variable resistor and ammeter; measure B at the centre with a calibrated Hall probe; plot B against 1/r: straight line through origin, gradient = k.
    Mark points: independent r, dependent B (B1); control: same current, same number of turns (B1). Circuit: d.c. supply, ammeter, rheostat in series with coil (B1); Hall probe at the centre, plane of probe perpendicular to field/along axis (B1); measure diameter with a metre rule or calipers and halve it (B1); use the Hall probe calibrated (or zero it away from the coil) (B1). Plot B against 1/r (B1); k = gradient, relationship valid if straight line through origin (B1). Safety: coil may get hot – switch off between readings / do not touch the coil (B1). Detail: adjust rheostat to keep current constant; take the probe reading with current reversed and average to remove Earth's field; measure diameter in several directions; use a large current for a large B, but avoid overheating; mount the probe in a clamp so its position is fixed (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q3Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A coil of resistance wire is placed in a beaker of water and a current I passes through it for a fixed time. It is suggested that the rise in temperature Δθ of the water is related to I by Δθ = kI², where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between Δθ and I. Explain how your results could be used to determine a value for k. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Vary I with a rheostat; heat the same mass of water for the same time; measure starting and final temperatures with a thermometer; plot Δθ against I²: straight line through origin, gradient = k.
    Mark points: independent I, dependent Δθ (B1); control: same mass/volume of water, same heating time, same coil/wire (B1). Circuit: low-voltage d.c. supply, ammeter in series, rheostat (B1); thermometer (or temperature sensor) in water, initial and final readings, stir (B1); measure time with a stopwatch, measure mass of water with a balance (B1); fresh water at same starting temperature each time (B1). Plot Δθ against I² (B1); k = gradient, valid if straight line through origin (B1). Safety: coil may be hot – allow to cool/use tongs; keep electrical connections dry (B1). Detail: insulate/lag the beaker and use a lid; read highest temperature after switching off; keep current constant during heating by adjusting rheostat; insulated wire so current does not pass through the water; repeat and average; choose times giving Δθ of several degrees (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q4Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

An alternating current of constant amplitude in a long solenoid induces an e.m.f. in a small search coil placed inside it. It is suggested that the peak e.m.f. E₀ induced in the search coil is related to the frequency f of the current by E₀ = kf, where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between E₀ and f. Explain how your results could be used to determine a value for k. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Use a signal generator to drive the solenoid, with an a.c. ammeter to keep the current amplitude constant; place the search coil at the centre, coaxial; measure peak e.m.f. on an oscilloscope (Y-gain × peak height); find f from the generator or CRO time-base; plot E₀ against f: gradient = k.
    Mark points: independent f, dependent E₀ (B1); control: same current amplitude in solenoid, same position and orientation of search coil (B1). Circuit: signal generator (a.c. source) connected to solenoid with a.c. ammeter in series (B1); search coil connected to CRO (B1); E₀ = vertical height of peak × Y-gain (B1); f from signal generator dial or period from time-base, f = 1/T (B1). Plot E₀ against f (B1); k = gradient, valid if straight line through origin (B1). Safety: solenoid may become hot at large currents – switch off between readings (B1). Detail: coil axis parallel to solenoid axis; centre of solenoid where field uniform; adjust generator output to keep current constant at each f; use wide frequency range (e.g. 100 Hz – 5 kHz); measure peak-to-peak and halve; repeat each reading (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q5Planning an investigation · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A capacitor of capacitance C is charged and then discharged through a fixed resistor of resistance R. It is suggested that the time t½ for the potential difference across the capacitor to fall to half of its initial value is related to C by t½ = RC ln 2.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between t½ and C. Explain how your results could be used to determine a value for R and to test the relationship. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Charge capacitors of different C (use a range, e.g. in parallel combinations) from a d.c. supply with a two-way switch; discharge through the same R; measure V against time with a voltmeter/data-logger and find t½; plot t½ against C: straight line through origin, gradient = R ln 2 so R = gradient/ln 2.
    Mark points: independent C, dependent t½ (B1); control: same resistor R, same initial charging p.d. (B1); circuit: d.c. supply, two-way switch, capacitor, resistor, voltmeter across capacitor (B1, B1); time with stopwatch started as switch moved or use data-logger to record V–t and read time for V₀/2 (B1); determine C from labelled values or measure with capacitance meter (B1). Plot t½ against C (B1); relationship valid if straight line through origin, R = gradient/ln 2 (B1). Safety: electrolytic capacitors connected the right way round/not exceeding rated voltage, discharge before handling (B1). Detail: choose large R (e.g. ≥ 10 kΩ) and C (100–1000 µF) so t½ is several seconds; high-resistance voltmeter (digital) so it does not discharge the capacitor; repeat and average t½; measure R with an ohmmeter for comparison; combine capacitors in parallel to vary C; time several halvings or use data-logger at high rate (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q6Planning an investigation · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

It is suggested that the resistance R of a thermistor is related to its thermodynamic temperature T by R = A e^(B/T), where A and B are constants.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between R and T. Explain how your results could be used to determine values for A and B. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Heat the thermistor in a water bath; measure the temperature with a thermometer and R with an ohmmeter (or V and I); convert to kelvin; plot ln R against 1/T: gradient = B, y-intercept = ln A.
    Mark points: independent T, dependent R (B1); control: same thermistor, same current/avoid self-heating (B1). Thermistor in a test tube in a water bath heated by a Bunsen/electric heater, stirred (B1); thermometer next to thermistor (B1); R measured with ohmmeter, or ammeter in series and voltmeter in parallel with R = V/I (B1); convert θ to T = θ + 273 (B1). Plot ln R against 1/T (B1); B = gradient, A = e^(y-intercept) (B1). Safety: hot water – use tongs/gloves, place apparatus on a stable surface (B1). Detail: waterproof the thermistor leads (plastic tube or sealed bag); stir and wait for thermal equilibrium before each reading; heat slowly/switch off heater while reading; use ice-water and boiling water to give a wide range; small current to avoid self-heating; repeat with temperature falling and average (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q7Planning an investigation · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A mass hanging on a spring oscillates vertically. A thin card of area S is attached horizontally to the mass so that air resistance damps the oscillations. The amplitude A decreases with time t according to A = A₀ e^(−λt). It is suggested that λ = c Sᵖ, where c and p are constants.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between λ and S. Explain how your results could be used to determine the values of c and p. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) For each card area, record the amplitude against time (e.g. motion sensor or video with a scale); find λ from the gradient of ln A against t; vary S using square cards of different side; plot lg λ against lg S: gradient = p, intercept = lg c.
    Mark points: independent S, dependent λ (B1); control: same total oscillating mass (card + mass), same spring, same initial amplitude (B1). Measure S by measuring card sides with a ruler (B1); determine A at intervals using a motion sensor below the mass/data-logger, or video with a vertical metre rule and frame timer (B1); find λ: plot ln A against t, λ = −gradient (B1); repeat for several areas (B1). Plot lg λ against lg S (B1); p = gradient, c = 10^(y-intercept) (B1). Safety: keep feet clear in case the mass falls / clamp stand secured with a G-clamp (B1). Detail: add balancing masses so total mass is constant; keep card horizontal (spirit level); small initial amplitude so card stays horizontal; no draughts; use a fiducial marker; repeat each λ; use lightweight stiff card (D1 each, max 6).
