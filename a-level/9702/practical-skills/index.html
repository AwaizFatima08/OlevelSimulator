Q1Planning an investigation · Core · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A light-dependent resistor (LDR) is placed at a distance d from a small filament lamp in a darkened room. It is suggested that the resistance R of the LDR is related to d by R = k dⁿ, where k and n are constants.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between R and d. Explain how your results could be used to determine the values of k and n. Describe in words a labelled diagram showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Vary d; measure R with an ohmmeter (or V and I with voltmeter and ammeter, R = V/I); keep lamp power (supply p.d.) constant and the LDR facing the lamp in a darkened room; plot lg R against lg d: gradient = n, y-intercept = lg k.
    Independent variable d, dependent variable R (B1); keep the lamp's brightness constant – same lamp, constant p.d. across it (monitor with a voltmeter) (B1); measure d with a metre rule from the filament to the face of the LDR (B1); measure R with an ohmmeter, or with a voltmeter across and ammeter in series with the LDR, R = V/I (B1); carry out in a darkened room / shield from background light (B1); plot lg R against lg d (B1); n = gradient, k = 10^(y-intercept) (B1); safety: lamp gets hot – allow to cool before touching / do not touch the bulb (B1). Detail: LDR and filament at the same height and LDR facing the lamp; take readings after R has settled; measure background resistance with the lamp off; use a range of d with the smallest d not so close that heating affects the LDR; repeat readings and average; relationship valid if the graph is a straight line (D1 each). Marks: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable) – 3; methods of data collection (workable arrangement, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph and how the constants are found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q2Planning an investigation · Core · Paper 5 style · 9 marks

A student wants to investigate how the secondary output p.d. V_s of a simple transformer depends on the number of turns N_s on the secondary coil. The primary coil (fixed number of turns) is connected to a low-voltage a.c. supply. Insulated wire can be wound around the other side of a laminated iron C-core to make the secondary coil. It is suggested that V_s = k N_s, where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Identify the independent variable, the dependent variable and two variables that must be kept constant. [3]
  2. (b) Describe how V_s should be measured. [2]
  3. (c) State the graph that should be plotted and explain how it would show whether the suggested relationship is valid and how k is found. [3]
  4. (d) State one safety precaution specific to this experiment. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Independent: N_s; dependent: V_s; constant: primary p.d. (r.m.s. supply voltage) and number of primary turns (also same core, same frequency).
    B1 independent and dependent correct; B1 one control; B1 a second control (primary p.d./primary turns/frequency/same core)
  2. (b) Connect an a.c. voltmeter (or oscilloscope) across the secondary coil; read the r.m.s. value (or peak from CRO).
    B1 a.c. voltmeter/CRO across secondary; B1 read r.m.s./peak value consistently, nothing else connected (open secondary)
  3. (c) Plot V_s against N_s; valid if a straight line through the origin; k = gradient.
    B1 V_s against N_s; B1 straight line through origin; B1 k = gradient
  4. (d) Use a low-voltage supply (isolating transformer) and switch off before rewinding the secondary; never connect the coils to the mains directly.
    B1 any one sensible specific precaution, e.g. low-voltage supply / switch off before changing turns / coil may get hot
Q3Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A student drops a bar magnet vertically through a coil of wire connected to a data-logger. It is suggested that the maximum e.m.f. E induced in the coil is related to the height h from which the magnet is released above the coil by E = k hⁿ, where k and n are constants.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between E and h. Explain how your results could be used to determine the values of k and n. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Vary h; measure peak E with a data-logger or oscilloscope; keep the same magnet, coil (turns, area) and orientation; release from rest through a tube to keep it vertical; plot lg E against lg h: gradient = n, y-intercept = lg k.
    Mark points: independent variable h, dependent variable E (B1); control: same magnet/strength, same coil (number of turns, area), magnet passes through same point/same orientation (B1, B1). Measure h with a metre rule from bottom of magnet to top of coil (B1); connect coil to data-logger/CRO, read the peak e.m.f. from the trace (B1); release magnet from rest, use a vertical plastic tube as a guide (B1). Plot lg E against lg h (B1); n = gradient, k = 10^(y-intercept) (B1). Safety: use a soft landing (sand tray/foam) so the falling magnet does not break or hit feet (B1). Detail: use a set-square to check the tube is vertical; repeat each h and average peak E; use a high data-logging rate; check the straight line confirms the relationship; keep magnet away from steel; avoid magnet touching the tube sides (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q4Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A flat circular coil carries a constant current. It is suggested that the magnetic flux density B at the centre of the coil is related to the radius r of the coil by B = k/r, where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between B and r. Explain how your results could be used to determine a value for k. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Make coils of the same number of turns but different radii (wind round cylinders of known diameter); keep current constant with a variable resistor and ammeter; measure B at the centre with a calibrated Hall probe; plot B against 1/r: straight line through origin, gradient = k.
    Mark points: independent r, dependent B (B1); control: same current, same number of turns (B1). Circuit: d.c. supply, ammeter, rheostat in series with coil (B1); Hall probe at the centre, plane of probe perpendicular to field/along axis (B1); measure diameter with a metre rule or calipers and halve it (B1); use the Hall probe calibrated (or zero it away from the coil) (B1). Plot B against 1/r (B1); k = gradient, relationship valid if straight line through origin (B1). Safety: coil may get hot – switch off between readings / do not touch the coil (B1). Detail: adjust rheostat to keep current constant; take the probe reading with current reversed and average to remove Earth's field; measure diameter in several directions; use a large current for a large B, but avoid overheating; mount the probe in a clamp so its position is fixed (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q5Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A coil of resistance wire is placed in a beaker of water and a current I passes through it for a fixed time. It is suggested that the rise in temperature Δθ of the water is related to I by Δθ = kI², where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between Δθ and I. Explain how your results could be used to determine a value for k. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Vary I with a rheostat; heat the same mass of water for the same time; measure starting and final temperatures with a thermometer; plot Δθ against I²: straight line through origin, gradient = k.
    Mark points: independent I, dependent Δθ (B1); control: same mass/volume of water, same heating time, same coil/wire (B1). Circuit: low-voltage d.c. supply, ammeter in series, rheostat (B1); thermometer (or temperature sensor) in water, initial and final readings, stir (B1); measure time with a stopwatch, measure mass of water with a balance (B1); fresh water at same starting temperature each time (B1). Plot Δθ against I² (B1); k = gradient, valid if straight line through origin (B1). Safety: coil may be hot – allow to cool/use tongs; keep electrical connections dry (B1). Detail: insulate/lag the beaker and use a lid; read highest temperature after switching off; keep current constant during heating by adjusting rheostat; insulated wire so current does not pass through the water; repeat and average; choose times giving Δθ of several degrees (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Q6Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

An alternating current of constant amplitude in a long solenoid induces an e.m.f. in a small search coil placed inside it. It is suggested that the peak e.m.f. E₀ induced in the search coil is related to the frequency f of the current by E₀ = kf, where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between E₀ and f. Explain how your results could be used to determine a value for k. You should draw a labelled diagram (describe it in words here) showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Use a signal generator to drive the solenoid, with an a.c. ammeter to keep the current amplitude constant; place the search coil at the centre, coaxial; measure peak e.m.f. on an oscilloscope (Y-gain × peak height); find f from the generator or CRO time-base; plot E₀ against f: gradient = k.
    Mark points: independent f, dependent E₀ (B1); control: same current amplitude in solenoid, same position and orientation of search coil (B1). Circuit: signal generator (a.c. source) connected to solenoid with a.c. ammeter in series (B1); search coil connected to CRO (B1); E₀ = vertical height of peak × Y-gain (B1); f from signal generator dial or period from time-base, f = 1/T (B1). Plot E₀ against f (B1); k = gradient, valid if straight line through origin (B1). Safety: solenoid may become hot at large currents – switch off between readings (B1). Detail: coil axis parallel to solenoid axis; centre of solenoid where field uniform; adjust generator output to keep current constant at each f; use wide frequency range (e.g. 100 Hz – 5 kHz); measure peak-to-peak and halve; repeat each reading (D1 each, max 6). Marks are awarded for: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable(s)) – 3; methods of data collection (workable apparatus/circuit, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph to plot, how the constant is found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
Q7Planning an investigation · Standard · Paper 5 style · 15 marks

A tall vertical tube of liquid (for example glycerol) is used to study falling steel spheres. It is suggested that the terminal velocity v of a sphere is related to its radius r by v = k r², where k is a constant that depends on the liquid.

  1. (a) Design a laboratory experiment to test the relationship between v and r. Explain how your results could be used to determine the value of k. Describe in words a labelled diagram showing the arrangement of your apparatus. In your account you should pay particular attention to:• the procedure to be followed• the measurements to be taken• the control of variables• the analysis of the data• any safety precautions to be taken. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Vary r using spheres of different diameter (micrometer, r = d/2); time each sphere between two marks far down the tube where it has reached terminal velocity; v = distance ÷ time; keep the same liquid at constant temperature; plot v against r²: straight line through the origin, gradient = k.
    Independent variable r, dependent variable v (B1); keep the temperature of the liquid constant (viscosity changes with temperature) and use the same liquid/same density of sphere material (B1); measure diameter with a micrometer screw gauge, r = d/2 (B1); two rubber bands (markers) on the tube a measured distance x apart, low enough for the sphere to have reached terminal velocity; time between marks with a stopwatch; v = x/t (B1); check terminal velocity reached by showing the time between two pairs of equally spaced marks is the same (B1); plot v against r² (B1); relationship valid if straight line through origin; k = gradient (B1); safety: wipe up spilt glycerol (slippery), or use a tray under the tube (B1). Detail: drop sphere along the axis of a wide tube to reduce wall effects; release just below the surface to avoid air bubbles; use a magnet to retrieve spheres; read marks at eye level to avoid parallax; use a video camera with a timer for fast spheres; repeat each r and average; thermometer in the liquid (D1 each). Marks: defining the problem (independent and dependent variables, key control variable) – 3; methods of data collection (workable arrangement, how each quantity is measured) – 3; method of analysis (graph and how the constants are found) – 2; safety (specific to this set-up) – 1; additional detail – up to 6. Total 15.
