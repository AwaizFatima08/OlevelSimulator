Q1Centripetal acceleration & force · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A car of mass 1200 kg travels around a flat roundabout in Islamabad at a constant speed of 12 m s⁻¹ along a circular path of radius 25 m.

  1. (a) Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the car. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the resultant force on the car and state its direction. [2]
  3. (c) State what provides this force. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.76 m s⁻²
    a = v²/r (M1) = 144/25 = 5.76 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 6910 N towards the centre of the roundabout
    F = ma = 1200 × 5.76 = 6910 N (A1); towards the centre (B1).
  3. (c) Friction between the tyres and the road
    B1: (sideways) friction of road on tyres.
Q2Centripetal acceleration & force · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A ball on a string moves at constant speed in a horizontal circle on a smooth horizontal table, the other end of the string being fixed to a peg at the centre.

  1. (a) Explain why the ball is accelerating even though its speed is constant. [2]
  2. (b) State the direction of the resultant force on the ball and explain why this force does no work on the ball. [2]
  3. (c) The string breaks. Describe the subsequent path of the ball, as seen from above. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Its direction of motion (velocity) changes continuously; acceleration is rate of change of velocity.
    B1: velocity is a vector / direction changes; B1: change in velocity means acceleration.
  2. (b) Towards the centre of the circle; it is always perpendicular to the velocity, so there is no displacement in the direction of the force.
    B1: towards centre; B1: force perpendicular to motion → no work done, so kinetic energy (speed) constant.
  3. (c) A straight line along the tangent to the circle at the point of release.
    B1: tangential straight line (no resultant horizontal force).
Q3Centripetal acceleration & force · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A ball of mass 0.25 kg is whirled in a vertical circle on a light string of length 0.80 m. At the top of the circle its speed is 4.0 m s⁻¹. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the tension in the string when the ball is at the top of the circle. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the minimum speed at the top for the string to remain taut. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.55 N
    At top, T + mg = mv²/r (M1); T = 0.25 × 16/0.80 − 0.25 × 9.81 (M1) = 2.55 N (A1).
  2. (b) 2.80 m s⁻¹
    T = 0 → mg = mv²/r (M1); v = √(gr) = 2.80 m s⁻¹ (A1).
Q4Centripetal acceleration & force · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The drum of a washing machine has radius 0.25 m and spins at 1200 revolutions per minute during the spin cycle.

  1. (a) Calculate the angular speed of the drum in rad s⁻¹. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the centripetal force needed to keep a 0.10 kg piece of wet cloth moving with the drum wall. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why water leaves the clothes through the holes in the drum wall. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 126 rad s⁻¹
    ω = 1200 × 2π/60 = 126 rad s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 395 N
    F = mω²r (M1) = 0.10 × 126² × 0.25 = 395 N (A1).
  3. (c) The drum wall pushes the clothes towards the centre, but where there is a hole there is no force on the water, so it continues in a straight line (tangentially) out through the hole.
    B1: water needs a centripetal force to stay in the circle; B1: at the holes no force acts on it, so it moves off along a tangent.
Q5Centripetal acceleration & force · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

An athlete swings a hammer of mass 7.26 kg on a wire so that it moves in a circle of radius 1.9 m. Just before release the speed of the hammer is 25 m s⁻¹. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the tension in the wire just before release. Assume the hammer moves in a horizontal circle and ignore its weight. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the hammer and compare it with g. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2390 N
    T = mv²/r (M1) = 7.26 × 625/1.9 = 2390 N (A1).
  2. (b) 329 m s⁻², about 34 times g
    a = v²/r = 329 m s⁻² (A1); ratio a/g ≈ 34 (A1).
Q6Centripetal acceleration & force · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A section of a racing track is a horizontal circular arc of radius 80 m, banked at 15° to the horizontal. A car of mass m travels round it. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) By considering the vertical and horizontal components of the normal contact force N, show that the speed at which no friction is needed is given by v² = rg tan θ. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate this speed. [1]
  3. (c) Explain what additional force is needed if the car travels faster than this speed, and state its direction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) N cos θ = mg and N sin θ = mv²/r; dividing gives tan θ = v²/(rg).
    B1: N cos θ = mg (no vertical acceleration); B1: N sin θ = mv²/r (provides centripetal force); B1: divide to obtain v² = rg tan θ.
  2. (b) 14.5 m s⁻¹
    v = √(80 × 9.81 × tan 15°) = 14.5 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) Friction acting down the slope (towards the centre side), to provide the extra centripetal force.
    B1: N sin θ alone is too small to give mv²/r; B1: friction needed, acting down the banking (parallel to surface).
Q7Centripetal acceleration & force · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A large Ferris wheel at a funfair has radius 20 m and turns at a constant rate, making one revolution every 60 s. A passenger of mass 60 kg sits in a seat that stays upright. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the passenger. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the contact force of the seat on the passenger at the top and at the bottom of the wheel. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the passenger feels lighter at the top. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.219 m s⁻²
    ω = 2π/60 = 0.105 rad s⁻¹ (M1); a = ω²r = 0.219 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) top 575 N; bottom 602 N
    Top: mg − N = ma → N = 60(9.81 − 0.219) = 575 N (M1 A1); bottom: N − mg = ma → N = 602 N (A1).
  3. (c) The seat pushes up with a force less than her weight because part of her weight provides the centripetal force.
    B1: contact force (felt as weight) is less than mg at the top.
Q8Centripetal acceleration & force · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

In a 'well of death' show at a fair in Pakistan, a motorcyclist rides around the inside of a vertical cylindrical wooden wall of radius 5.0 m, moving in a horizontal circle. The total mass of rider and motorcycle is 250 kg and the speed is 15 m s⁻¹. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the normal contact force of the wall on the motorcycle and rider. [2]
  2. (b) State the size of the frictional force on the tyres and explain why it has this value. [2]
  3. (c) The maximum frictional force available is 0.60 times the normal contact force. Calculate the minimum speed at which the rider can go round without sliding down. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.13 × 10⁴ N
    N = mv²/r (M1) = 250 × 225/5.0 = 11 250 ≈ 1.13 × 10⁴ N (A1; accept 1.1 × 10⁴ N).
  2. (b) 2450 N
    B1: there is no vertical acceleration, so friction (upwards) balances weight; B1: 250 × 9.81 = 2450 N.
  3. (c) 9.04 m s⁻¹
    0.60 mv²/r = mg (M1); v = √(gr/0.60) = 9.04 m s⁻¹ (A1).
Q9Centripetal acceleration & force · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A passenger aircraft of mass 5.0 × 10⁴ kg flies at a constant speed of 120 m s⁻¹ and turns in a horizontal circle of radius 2.0 km by banking its wings. The lift force acts perpendicular to the wings. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Explain how the lift force allows the aircraft to turn in a horizontal circle. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the angle of bank. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the lift force. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) When banked, the lift (perpendicular to the wings) is tilted; its vertical component balances the weight and its horizontal component provides the centripetal force.
    B1: vertical component of lift = weight; B1: horizontal component of lift towards centre = mv²/r.
  2. (b) 36.3°
    tan θ = v²/(rg) = 14400/(2000 × 9.81) (M1); θ = 36.3° (A1).
  3. (c) 6.08 × 10⁵ N
    L cos θ = mg (M1); L = 5.0 × 10⁴ × 9.81/cos 36.3° = 6.08 × 10⁵ N (A1).
Q10Centripetal acceleration & force · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A small coin is placed on a horizontal turntable 8.0 cm from its centre. The speed of rotation is increased slowly; the coin starts to slide when the turntable reaches 45 revolutions per minute.

  1. (a) Calculate the centripetal acceleration of the coin just before it slides. [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the maximum frictional force on the coin as a fraction of its weight. [1]
  3. (c) The turntable is now set to 33 revolutions per minute. Calculate the greatest distance from the centre at which the coin can be placed without sliding. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.78 m s⁻²
    ω = 45 × 2π/60 = 4.71 rad s⁻¹ (M1); a = ω²r = 1.78 m s⁻² (A1).
  2. (b) 0.181
    F/mg = a/g = 0.181 (A1).
  3. (c) 14.9 cm
    Same maximum acceleration: r = a/ω² (M1) with ω = 3.46 rad s⁻¹ → r = 0.149 m (A1).

Q12Centripetal acceleration & force · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A small metal bob of mass 0.15 kg hangs from a light string 1.2 m long. It moves in a horizontal circle at constant speed so that the string makes an angle of 30° with the vertical (a conical pendulum). g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the tension in the string. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the bob. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the time for one revolution. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.70 N
    Vertical: T cos 30° = mg (M1); T = 0.15 × 9.81/cos 30° = 1.70 N (A1).
  2. (b) 1.84 m s⁻¹
    r = 1.2 sin 30° = 0.60 m (B1); T sin 30° = mv²/r (M1); v = √(gr tan 30°) = 1.84 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 2.05 s
    T = 2πr/v = 2.05 s (A1).
Q13Centripetal acceleration & force · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A car of mass 900 kg drives over a humpbacked bridge whose top is a vertical circular arc of radius 30 m. g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Calculate the normal contact force on the car at the top of the bridge when its speed is 12 m s⁻¹. [3]
  2. (b) Determine the maximum speed at which the car can cross the top of the bridge without losing contact with the road. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the driver finds it harder to steer and brake near the top of the bridge at high speed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4510 N
    mg − N = mv²/r (M1); N = 900 × 9.81 − 900 × 144/30 (M1) = 4510 N (A1).
  2. (b) 17.2 m s⁻¹
    N = 0 → v² = gr (M1); v = √(9.81 × 30) = 17.2 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) The normal contact force is reduced, so the maximum friction between tyres and road is reduced.
    B1: normal force smaller at higher speed; B1: friction (grip) depends on the normal force, so less force available for steering/braking.
Q14Centripetal acceleration & force · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A ball of mass 0.20 kg on a light inextensible string of length 0.70 m is swung in a vertical circle. At the lowest point its speed is 6.0 m s⁻¹. Air resistance is negligible; g = 9.81 m s⁻².

  1. (a) Use conservation of energy to calculate the speed of the ball at the top of the circle. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the tension in the string at the top and at the bottom of the circle. [4]
  3. (c) State where in the circle the string is most likely to break, with a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.92 m s⁻¹
    ½mv_b² = ½mv_t² + mg(2r) (M1); v_t² = 36 − 4 × 9.81 × 0.70 = 8.53 → v_t = 2.92 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) top 0.476 N; bottom 12.2 N
    Top: T + mg = mv²/r (M1) → T = 0.20 × 8.53/0.70 − 1.962 = 0.476 N (A1); bottom: T − mg = mv²/r (M1) → T = 0.20 × 36/0.70 + 1.962 = 12.2 N (A1).
  3. (c) At the bottom, where the tension is greatest.
    B1: at the bottom — weight acts away from the centre so tension must supply mg + mv²/r and speed is greatest.
Q15Centripetal acceleration & force · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 8 marks

A student investigates circular motion with a rubber bung of mass m tied to a string. The string passes through a vertical glass tube held in the hand and a mass of 0.100 kg hangs from its lower end, so the tension in the string is F = 0.981 N. The bung is whirled in a horizontal circle of radius r and the period T is measured:r = 0.300 m, T = 0.49 sr = 0.400 m, T = 0.58 sr = 0.500 m, T = 0.62 sr = 0.600 m, T = 0.69 sr = 0.700 m, T = 0.76 sr = 0.800 m, T = 0.79 s

  1. (a) Show that T² = (4π²m/F) r, where F is the tension in the string. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate T² for each reading. [1]
  3. (c) The best-fit straight line through the origin of a graph of T² against r has gradient 0.798 s² m⁻¹. Determine the mass of the bung. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest two reasons why the tension in the string may not equal the weight of the hanging mass, and explain one way to reduce the uncertainty in T. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) F = mω²r and ω = 2π/T, so F = 4π²mr/T², giving T² = (4π²m/F)r.
    M1: F = mrω² with ω = 2π/T; A1: rearrangement shown.
  2. (b) T² / s²: 0.240, 0.336, 0.384, 0.476, 0.578, 0.624
    B1: all correct to 3 s.f.
  3. (c) 0.020 kg
    m = gradient × F/(4π²) (M1) = 0.798 × 0.981/39.5 = 0.020 kg (A1).
  4. (d) Friction between string and tube; string not exactly horizontal; time 10 revolutions and repeat.
    B1 each for any two: friction at the tube; string not horizontal (only a component of tension is horizontal / r measured to the top of the tube is not the true radius); mass of string. B1: time 10 or 20 revolutions and divide; repeat and average; video with frame-by-frame timing.

Q23Kinematics of circular motion (radian, angular speed) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define angular speed. [1]

A cyclist turns the front chain-wheel (radius 0.10 m) at 1.5 revolutions per second. The chain connects it to a rear sprocket of radius 0.040 m fixed to the rear wheel. The bicycle does not skid.

  1. (a) Calculate the speed of the chain. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the angular speed of the rear sprocket. [2]
  3. (c) The rear wheel, radius 0.34 m, turns with the rear sprocket. Calculate the speed of the bicycle. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.942 m s⁻¹
    v = 2πfr = 2π × 1.5 × 0.10 = 0.942 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 23.6 rad s⁻¹
    Chain speed same at both sprockets (M1); ω = 0.942/0.040 = 23.6 rad s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 8.01 m s⁻¹
    Same ω as sprocket (M1); v = 23.6 × 0.34 = 8.01 m s⁻¹ (A1).

A child sits 2.5 m from the centre of a playground roundabout that makes 6 complete revolutions in 50 s at a steady rate.

  1. (a) Calculate the angular speed of the roundabout. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the child. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the angle the child turns through in 3.0 s, in radians and in degrees. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.754 rad s⁻¹
    Period = 50/6 = 8.33 s (M1); ω = 2π/T = 0.754 rad s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 1.88 m s⁻¹
    v = ωr = 1.88 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 2.26 rad = 130°
    θ = ωt = 2.26 rad (A1); × 180/π = 130° (A1).

A potter in Multan shapes clay on a wheel. Clay at the edge of the wheel, 0.18 m from the axis, moves at 2.4 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the angular speed of the wheel. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of revolutions per minute. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the angle turned in 0.50 s and the number of revolutions this represents. [2]

  1. (a) 13.3 rad s⁻¹

Q27Kinematics of circular motion (radian, angular speed) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

On an athletics track the inside lane (lane 1) running line is a semicircular bend of radius 36.5 m. Each lane outward adds 1.22 m to the radius, so lane 8 is 7 lanes out. A runner in lane 1 runs round the bend at a constant speed of 9.0 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the angular speed of the runner in lane 1 about the centre of the bend. [1]
  2. (b) A runner in lane 8 stays level with the lane-1 runner (on the same radius line) throughout the bend. Calculate the speed needed in lane 8. [2]
  3. (c) Each bend is a semicircle. Calculate how much further a runner in lane 8 travels round one bend than a runner in lane 1, and hence explain why starting positions are staggered. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.247 rad s⁻¹
    ω = v/r = 9.0/36.5 = 0.247 rad s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 11.1 m s⁻¹
    Same ω; r₈ = 36.5 + 7 × 1.22 = 45.0 m (M1); v = ωr = 11.1 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 26.8 m
    Δs = πΔr = π × 8.54 = 26.8 m (M1 A1); stagger makes all lanes the same total distance.
Q28Kinematics of circular motion (radian, angular speed) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A computer hard disc spins at 7200 revolutions per minute. The read head is over a track 3.0 cm from the axis.

  1. (a) Calculate the speed of the disc surface under the head. [2]
  2. (b) Each bit of data occupies 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ m along the track. Calculate the time for one bit to pass under the head. [1]
  3. (c) The head moves to a track at radius 0.015 m. State and explain the effect on the rate at which bits pass the head if bits are the same length. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 22.6 m s⁻¹
    ω = 7200 × 2π/60 = 754 rad s⁻¹ (M1); v = ωr = 22.6 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 4.42 × 10⁻⁹ s
    t = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷/22.6 = 4.42 × 10⁻⁹ s (A1).
  3. (c) The rate halves, because the linear speed v = ωr halves at the same angular speed.
    B1: ω unchanged; B1: v ∝ r, so half the speed → half the bit rate (time per bit doubles).
Q29Kinematics of circular motion (radian, angular speed) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

An electric motor in a flour mill turns a small pulley of radius 2.5 cm at 1450 revolutions per minute. A belt that does not slip connects it to a larger pulley of radius 10.0 cm on the machine.

  1. (a) Calculate the speed of the belt. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the rotation rate of the machine pulley in revolutions per minute. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the centripetal acceleration of a small piece of belt as it passes round the motor pulley. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.80 m s⁻¹
    ω = 1450 × 2π/60 = 152 rad s⁻¹ (M1); v = ωr = 3.80 m s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 363 rev min⁻¹ (362.5)
    Belt speed same, so ω ∝ 1/r (M1): 1450 × 2.5/10.0 = 362.5 ≈ 363 rev min⁻¹ (A1; accept 362).
  3. (c) 576 m s⁻²
    a = v²/r = 3.80²/0.025 = 576 m s⁻² (A1).
Q30Kinematics of circular motion (radian, angular speed) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A fan with three identical, equally spaced blades is lit by a stroboscope flashing 25 times per second.

  1. (a) Explain why the fan appears stationary when it turns at certain speeds. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the lowest angular speed of the fan for which it appears stationary. [2]
  3. (c) State the next higher rotation frequency, in revolutions per second, at which the fan appears stationary. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Between flashes each blade moves exactly to the position of a blade (the same or another) at the previous flash, so every flash shows the same pattern.
    B1: between flashes the fan turns through a multiple of 120° (the angle between blades); B1: so the image looks identical at each flash.
  2. (b) 52.4 rad s⁻¹
    Turns 1/3 rev in 1/25 s (M1) → f = 25/3 = 8.33 rev s⁻¹ → ω = 52.4 rad s⁻¹ (A1).
  3. (c) 16.7 rev s⁻¹
    2/3 rev per flash → 16.7 rev s⁻¹ (A1).
