Q1Errors & uncertainties · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A student uses a micrometer screw gauge to measure the diameter of a wire. She does not notice that the gauge reads +0.03 mm when its jaws are closed. Which statement about her readings is correct?A They have a random error, so they are precise but not accurate.B They have a systematic error, so they may be precise but are not accurate.C They have a random error that can be removed by averaging many readings.D They have a systematic error that can be removed by averaging many readings. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B They have a systematic error, so they may be precise but are not accurate.
    A zero error shifts every reading by the same amount: a systematic error. Readings can still be close together (precise) but are all wrong by 0.03 mm (not accurate). Averaging (C, D) reduces random error only, not a systematic one.
Q2Errors & uncertainties · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A student in Multan measures a cylindrical pencil. Its length L is 14.6 ± 0.1 cm (metre rule) and its diameter d is 7.12 ± 0.01 mm (micrometer).

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the length. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the diameter. [1]
  3. (c) The volume of the pencil is V = πd²L/4. Calculate V in cm³ and its absolute uncertainty. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.68 %
    0.1/14.6 × 100 = 0.68 % (B1)
  2. (b) 0.14 %
    0.01/7.12 × 100 = 0.14 % (B1)
  3. (c) V = 5.81 ± 0.06 cm³
    V = π × 0.712² × 14.6/4 = 5.81 cm³ (C1); % uncertainty = 0.68 + 2 × 0.14 = 0.97 % (C1); ΔV = 0.0097 × 5.81 = 0.06 cm³, so 5.81 ± 0.06 cm³ (A1)
Q3Errors & uncertainties · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between a systematic error and a random error. [2]
  2. (b) For each of the following, state whether it causes a systematic or a random error and suggest how it can be reduced.(i) An electronic balance reads 0.4 g with nothing on its pan.(ii) A student's reaction time varies when starting and stopping a stopwatch to time a falling ball. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Systematic: readings all differ from the true value by the same amount or in the same direction. Random: readings scatter unpredictably either side of the true value.
    B1: systematic error – same size/same sign for every reading (e.g. zero error); B1: random error – scatter of readings about the mean, different each time
  2. (b) (i) systematic – take the zero reading and subtract it from every reading (or tare the balance). (ii) random – repeat and average, or use light gates connected to a timer.
    B1 systematic; B1 correct for zero error/tare; B1 random; B1 repeat and average or use light gates/video with timer
Q4Errors & uncertainties · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Five readings of the diameter of a wire are 2.43 mm, 2.45 mm, 2.41 mm, 2.46 mm and 2.44 mm. Which value and uncertainty best represent these readings?A 2.44 ± 0.01 mmB 2.44 ± 0.03 mmC 2.44 ± 0.05 mmD 2.438 ± 0.005 mm [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 2.44 ± 0.03 mm
    Mean = 12.19/5 = 2.438 ≈ 2.44 mm; uncertainty = half the range = (2.46 − 2.41)/2 = 0.025 ≈ 0.03 mm. C uses the full range; D quotes too many s.f.
Q5Errors & uncertainties · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In a laboratory in Lahore, the length L of a simple pendulum is 0.865 ± 0.002 m. The time for 20 oscillations is 37.4 ± 0.2 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the period T of the pendulum. [1]
  2. (b) The acceleration of free fall is given by g = 4π²L/T². Calculate g. [2]
  3. (c) Determine the absolute uncertainty in g and write g with its uncertainty to an appropriate number of significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) T = 1.87 s
    T = 37.4/20 = 1.87 s (B1)
  2. (b) g = 9.77 m s⁻²
    g = 4π² × 0.865/1.87² (C1) = 9.77 m s⁻² (A1)
  3. (c) g = 9.8 ± 0.1 m s⁻²
    %L = 0.002/0.865 = 0.23 %; %T = 0.2/37.4 = 0.53 % (C1); %g = 0.23 + 2 × 0.53 = 1.3 % (C1); Δg = 0.013 × 9.77 = 0.13 → g = 9.8 ± 0.1 m s⁻² (A1)
Q6Errors & uncertainties · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

The potential difference across a lamp is 6.24 ± 0.05 V and the current in it is 0.320 ± 0.004 A.

  1. (a) Calculate the resistance R of the lamp filament. [1]
  2. (b) Determine the absolute uncertainty in R. [2]
  3. (c) State which of the two meter readings contributes more to the uncertainty in R. Explain your answer. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) R = 19.5 Ω
    R = V/I = 6.24/0.320 = 19.5 Ω (B1)
  2. (b) ± 0.4 Ω
    %R = 0.05/6.24 × 100 + 0.004/0.320 × 100 = 0.80 + 1.25 = 2.05 % (C1); ΔR = 0.0205 × 19.5 = 0.40 Ω (A1)
  3. (c) The current reading
    Its percentage uncertainty (1.25 %) is larger than that of the voltage (0.80 %) (B1)
Q7Errors & uncertainties · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A quantity X is calculated from X = a²b/√c. The percentage uncertainties are 2 % in a, 3 % in b and 4 % in c. What is the percentage uncertainty in X?A 5 %B 7 %C 9 %D 11 % [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 9 %
    %X = 2 × 2 + 3 + ½ × 4 = 4 + 3 + 2 = 9 %. 11 % comes from using 4 % for c without halving it.
Q8Errors & uncertainties · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

The accepted value of g in a city is 9.81 m s⁻². Two students measure g.Student A: 9.62, 9.63, 9.61, 9.62 m s⁻²Student B: 9.70, 9.95, 9.85, 9.74 m s⁻²

  1. (a) Calculate the mean value of g for each student. [2]
  2. (b) State, with reasons, which set of results is more precise and which is more accurate. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest what type of error is present in student A's results. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: 9.62 m s⁻²; B: 9.81 m s⁻²
    A: 38.48/4 = 9.62 (B1); B: 39.24/4 = 9.81 (B1)
  2. (b) A is more precise (small spread); B is more accurate (mean equals the accepted value).
    B1 A precise – readings close together; B1 B accurate – mean close to 9.81 m s⁻²
  3. (c) A systematic error (e.g. length measured to the wrong point of the bob).
    B1 systematic
Q9Errors & uncertainties · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A steel ball bearing from a bicycle workshop has mass m = 28.4 ± 0.1 g. Its diameter d is measured with vernier calipers as 1.90 ± 0.02 cm.

  1. (a) Show that the density of the ball is about 7.9 × 10³ kg m⁻³. [2]
  2. (b) Determine the absolute uncertainty in the density. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest, with a reason, which measurement should be made more precisely to reduce the uncertainty in the density most effectively. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ρ = 7.91 × 10³ kg m⁻³
    ρ = m/(πd³/6) = 6 × 28.4/(π × 1.90³) = 7.91 g cm⁻³ (C1) = 7.91 × 10³ kg m⁻³ (A1)
  2. (b) ± 0.3 × 10³ kg m⁻³
    %m = 0.35 % (C1); %d = 1.05 %, ×3 = 3.16 % (C1); total 3.5 % → Δρ = 0.28 × 10³ ≈ 0.3 × 10³ kg m⁻³ (A1)
  3. (c) The diameter, e.g. using a micrometer or digital calipers.
    B1 diameter; B1 its percentage uncertainty is multiplied by 3 / it dominates the total
Q10Errors & uncertainties · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A spring has length 24.3 ± 0.1 cm when unloaded. When a weight of 4.00 ± 0.02 N hangs from it, its length is 31.8 ± 0.1 cm.

  1. (a) Calculate the extension of the spring and its absolute uncertainty. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the spring constant k and its absolute uncertainty. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the percentage uncertainty in the extension is much larger than that in either length reading. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.5 ± 0.2 cm
    x = 31.8 − 24.3 = 7.5 cm (B1); uncertainties add when subtracting: 0.1 + 0.1 = 0.2 cm (B1)
  2. (b) k = 53 ± 2 N m⁻¹
    k = 4.00/0.075 = 53.3 N m⁻¹ (C1); %k = 0.5 % + 2.7 % = 3.2 % (C1); Δk = 1.7 → 53 ± 2 N m⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) The extension is a small difference of two similar lengths, but the absolute uncertainties add.
    B1
Q11Errors & uncertainties · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A trolley of mass 0.250 ± 0.002 kg travels at constant speed. It covers 1.500 ± 0.005 m in a time measured with a stopwatch as 0.62 ± 0.02 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the speed v of the trolley. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the kinetic energy of the trolley. [1]
  3. (c) Determine the absolute uncertainty in the kinetic energy. [3]
  4. (d) Suggest one change to the experiment that would reduce the uncertainty in E most effectively. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) v = 2.42 m s⁻¹
    v = 1.500/0.62 = 2.42 m s⁻¹ (B1)
  2. (b) 0.73 J
    E = ½ × 0.250 × 2.42² = 0.73 J (B1)
  3. (c) ± 0.06 J
    %v = 0.33 + 3.2 = 3.6 % (C1); %E = 0.8 + 2 × 3.6 = 7.9 % (C1); ΔE = 0.079 × 0.73 = 0.06 J (A1)
  4. (d) Measure the time with light gates and an electronic timer (or use a longer distance).
    B1 – timing uncertainty dominates
Q12SI units & homogeneity · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which of the following is an SI base unit?A newtonB coulombC kelvinD volt [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C kelvin
    The kelvin is one of the base units (with kg, m, s, A, mol). The coulomb is a derived unit (A s), a common distractor.
Q13SI units & homogeneity · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Distinguish between a base quantity and a derived quantity. [1]
  2. (b) Show that the unit of pressure, the pascal, is kg m⁻¹ s⁻² in SI base units. [2]
  3. (c) Express the joule in SI base units. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A base quantity is one of the chosen fundamental quantities (e.g. mass, length, time); a derived quantity is defined in terms of base quantities.
    B1
  2. (b) kg m⁻¹ s⁻²
    p = F/A; N = kg m s⁻² (C1); kg m s⁻² ÷ m² = kg m⁻¹ s⁻² (A1)
  3. (c) kg m² s⁻²
    J = N m = kg m s⁻² × m (B1)
Q14SI units & homogeneity · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which pair of quantities have the same SI base units?A work and powerB momentum and impulseC force and pressureD charge and current [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B momentum and impulse
    Momentum kg m s⁻¹; impulse N s = kg m s⁻² × s = kg m s⁻¹. Charge (A s) and current (A) differ by a factor of s.
Q15SI units & homogeneity · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by an equation being homogeneous. [1]
  2. (b) Show that the equation v² = u² + 2as is homogeneous. [2]
  3. (c) The pressure p at depth h in a liquid of density ρ is p = ρgh. Show that this equation is homogeneous. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each term in the equation has the same base units.
    B1
  2. (b) All terms have units m² s⁻².
    v², u²: (m s⁻¹)² = m² s⁻² (M1); 2as: m s⁻² × m = m² s⁻² (A1)
  3. (c) Both sides kg m⁻¹ s⁻².
    ρgh: kg m⁻³ × m s⁻² × m (M1) = kg m⁻¹ s⁻² = unit of p (A1)
Q16SI units & homogeneity · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the volt. [1]
  2. (b) Show that the volt is kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ in SI base units. [2]
  3. (c) Hence determine the SI base units of resistance. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) One joule of energy transferred per coulomb of charge (1 V = 1 J C⁻¹).
    B1
  2. (b) kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹
    V = J/C = kg m² s⁻² ÷ (A s) (C1) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) kg m² s⁻³ A⁻²
    Ω = V/A (B1)
Q17SI units & homogeneity · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A small sphere of radius r moving at speed v through a thick oil experiences a drag force F given by F = Crv, where C is a constant. What are the SI base units of C?A kg s⁻¹B kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹C kg m s⁻¹D kg m⁻² s⁻¹ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹
    C = F/(rv) = kg m s⁻² ÷ (m × m s⁻¹) = kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹. A forgets to divide by r.
Q18SI units & homogeneity · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Water flows steadily through a narrow pipe in a hospital drip. The volume flow rate Q is given by Q = πr⁴Δp/(8ηL), where r is the radius of the pipe, L its length, Δp the pressure difference between its ends and η a property of the liquid called viscosity.

  1. (a) State the SI base units of volume flow rate Q and of pressure difference Δp. [2]
  2. (b) Determine the SI base units of η. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Q: m³ s⁻¹; Δp: kg m⁻¹ s⁻²
    B1 each
  2. (b) kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹
    η = πr⁴Δp/(8QL) (C1); m⁴ × kg m⁻¹ s⁻² ÷ (m³ s⁻¹ × m) = kg m⁻¹ s⁻¹ (A1)
Q19SI units & homogeneity · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The speed v of a transverse wave on a stretched sitar string depends on the tension T in the string and its mass per unit length μ. A student suggests v = k T^x μ^y, where k is a dimensionless constant.

  1. (a) Write down the SI base units of T and of μ. [2]
  2. (b) Use base units to find the values of x and y. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why this method cannot give the value of k. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) T: kg m s⁻²; μ: kg m⁻¹
    B1 each
  2. (b) x = ½, y = −½
    kg: x + y = 0; m: x − y = 1; s: −2x = −1 (M1 for setting up, M1 solving); x = ½, y = −½ (A1)
  3. (c) k is a pure number with no units, so it does not appear in the unit equation.
    B1
Q20SI units & homogeneity · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A student writes s = ut + at² for uniformly accelerated motion. Show that this equation is homogeneous. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the equation can be homogeneous but still incorrect. [1]
  3. (c) The gravitational force between two masses is F = Gm₁m₂/r². Determine the SI base units of G. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each term has unit m.
    ut: m s⁻¹ × s = m (M1); at²: m s⁻² × s² = m (A1)
  2. (b) Homogeneity cannot check dimensionless constants; the correct equation has ½at².
    B1
  3. (c) m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻²
    G = Fr²/(m₁m₂) (C1) = kg m s⁻² × m² ÷ kg² = m³ kg⁻¹ s⁻² (A1)
Q21Scalars & vectors · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Distinguish between a scalar quantity and a vector quantity. [1]
  2. (b) From the list below, state all the vector quantities.kinetic energy, momentum, temperature, weight, speed, acceleration, power [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A scalar has magnitude only; a vector has magnitude and direction.
    B1
  2. (b) momentum, weight, acceleration
    B2 all three and no scalars; B1 two correct
Q22Scalars & vectors · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

A traveller at Karachi airport pulls a wheeled suitcase with a force of 50.0 N along the handle. The handle makes an angle of 35° with the horizontal floor.

  1. (a) Calculate the horizontal component of the force. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the vertical component of the force. [1]
  3. (c) The suitcase moves at constant velocity. State the size of the frictional force on it. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 41.0 N
    50.0 cos 35° = 41.0 N (B1)
  2. (b) 28.7 N
    50.0 sin 35° = 28.7 N (B1)
  3. (c) 41.0 N
    Equal and opposite to the horizontal component (B1)
Q23Scalars & vectors · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Two horizontal forces act on a crate on a smooth floor: 12.0 N towards the east and 5.0 N towards the north.

  1. (a) Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force on the crate. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the angle between the resultant force and the east direction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 13.0 N
    R = √(12.0² + 5.0²) (C1) = 13.0 N (A1)
  2. (b) 22.6° north of east
    tan θ = 5.0/12.0 (C1); θ = 22.6° (A1)
Q24Scalars & vectors · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A light aircraft leaves Islamabad heading due north with a speed of 240 km h⁻¹ relative to the air. A steady wind blows from west to east at 60 km h⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the magnitude of the velocity of the aircraft relative to the ground. [2]
  2. (b) Determine the direction of this velocity as an angle from north. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 247 km h⁻¹
    v = √(240² + 60²) (C1) = 247 km h⁻¹ (A1)
  2. (b) 14.0° east of north
    tan θ = 60/240 (C1); θ = 14.0° east of north (A1)
Q25Scalars & vectors · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A ferry crosses a river 180 m wide. The water flows parallel to the banks at 1.5 m s⁻¹. The speed of the ferry relative to the water is 3.0 m s⁻¹. The ferry must travel straight across, perpendicular to the banks.

  1. (a) Calculate the angle between the direction in which the ferry must be steered and the straight-across direction (perpendicular to the banks) so that the ferry moves straight across. State whether the ferry is steered upstream or downstream. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the time taken to cross the river. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 30° upstream of the straight-across direction (60° to the bank)
    The upstream component of the ferry's velocity must cancel the current: sin θ = 1.5/3.0 (C1); θ = 30°, steered upstream (A1)
  2. (b) 69 s
    speed across = 3.0 cos 30° = 2.60 m s⁻¹ (C1); t = 180/2.60 = 69 s (A1)
Q26Scalars & vectors · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A street lamp of weight 30.0 N hangs at rest from two cables tied at the same point. One cable makes an angle of 40° with the horizontal and the other makes an angle of 60° with the horizontal, on the opposite side.

  1. (a) State the condition for the point where the cables meet to be in equilibrium. [1]
  2. (b) By resolving forces horizontally and vertically, determine the tension in each cable. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The resultant force on it is zero (the horizontal components balance and the vertical components balance).
    B1 resultant (vector sum of) force zero
  2. (b) Cable at 40°: 15.2 N; cable at 60°: 23.3 N
    Horizontal: T₁ cos 40° = T₂ cos 60° (C1); vertical: T₁ sin 40° + T₂ sin 60° = 30.0 (C1); solving T₂ = 23.3 N (A1); T₁ = 15.2 N (A1)
Q27Estimates & prefixes · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the best estimate of the kinetic energy of a family car travelling on a motorway?A 10³ JB 10⁴ JC 10⁶ JD 10⁸ J [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 10⁶ J
    m ≈ 1500 kg, v ≈ 30 m s⁻¹: E = ½ × 1500 × 30² ≈ 7 × 10⁵ J ≈ 10⁶ J. 10⁴ J would correspond to a speed of only about 4 m s⁻¹.

  2. (b) Write 0.000 038 A and 72 000 000 W using a suitable prefix. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 4.50 × 10⁻⁷ m (ii) 2.5 × 10⁹ Hz (iii) 3.3 × 10³ Ω (iv) 4.70 × 10⁻⁴ F
    B2 all four; B1 two or three
  2. (b) 38 µA; 72 MW
    B1 each
Q29Estimates & prefixes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Estimate the gain in gravitational potential energy of a student who runs up one flight of stairs between two floors of a school building. State the values you assume. [2]
  2. (b) The student takes about 4 s. Estimate the useful power output. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) About 2 × 10³ J (e.g. 60 kg, 3 m: 1.8 × 10³ J)
    B1 sensible values (mass 50–80 kg, height 3–4 m); B1 mgh in range 1.5–3 × 10³ J
  2. (b) About 400–500 W (e.g. 1770/4 = 440 W)
    B1 P = E/t, consistent with (a), in range 300–800 W
Q30Estimates & prefixes · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Estimate the volume of air in a typical classroom of floor area 8 m × 6 m and height 3 m, and hence its mass. The density of air is 1.2 kg m⁻³. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the weight of this air. [1]
  3. (c) Most students are surprised by this answer. Suggest why we do not feel the weight of the air in a room. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 144 m³; about 170 kg
    V = 8 × 6 × 3 = 144 m³ (C1); m = 1.2 × 144 = 170 kg (A1)
  2. (b) 1.7 × 10³ N
    W = 173 × 9.81 = 1.7 × 10³ N (B1)
  3. (c) Air pressure acts in all directions, so the forces on our body from the air balance.
    B1
