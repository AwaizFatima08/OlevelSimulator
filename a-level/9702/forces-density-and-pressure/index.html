  2. (b) On a see-saw in a park in Karachi, Ali (mass 32 kg) sits 1.5 m from the pivot. His sister (mass 24 kg) sits on the other side. The see-saw is uniform and pivoted at its centre. Calculate how far from the pivot she must sit for the see-saw to balance. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) For a body in equilibrium, the sum of the clockwise moments about any point equals the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point.

  2. (b) 2.0 m

Q7Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A worker uses a straight crowbar of length 1.20 m to lift the edge of a heavy stone slab. The crowbar rests on a small brick that acts as a pivot 0.15 m from the end of the bar that is under the slab. The worker pushes down at the other end with a force of 250 N at right angles to the bar. Ignore the weight of the crowbar.

  1. (a) State the two conditions for a rigid object to be in equilibrium. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the force exerted by the crowbar on the slab, assuming this force is at right angles to the bar. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the worker can lift the slab more easily if the brick is moved closer to the slab. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The resultant force on it is zero; the resultant torque (moment) about any point is zero.
    B1 resultant force zero; B1 resultant torque/moment zero (about any point)
  2. (b) 1.75 × 10³ N
    M1 moments about pivot: F × 0.15 = 250 × 1.05; A1 F = 1750 N
  3. (c) The distance from the pivot to the slab decreases and the distance to the worker's hand increases, so the same applied moment gives a larger force on the slab.
    B1 perpendicular distance of effort from pivot increases / of load decreases; B1 so greater force for same applied force (principle of moments)
Q8Turning effects, couples & equilibrium · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A wheel brace used to undo a car's wheel nut has two handles. A mechanic pushes one handle up and the other down, with forces of 60 N each, perpendicular to the brace. The hands are 0.40 m apart, one on each side of the nut.

  1. (a) Calculate the torque applied to the nut. [2]
  2. (b) State the resultant force exerted on the brace by the two hands and explain why the nut is not pushed sideways. [2]
  3. (c) The mechanic now uses only one hand, 0.20 m from the centre of the nut. Calculate the force needed to give the same torque. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 24 N m
    C1 torque = F × perpendicular separation; A1 24 N m
  2. (b) Zero; the two forces are equal in size and opposite in direction (they form a couple), so they only produce turning.
    B1 zero; B1 equal and opposite / couple gives torque only
  3. (c) 120 N
    C1 F × 0.20 = 24; A1 F = 120 N
