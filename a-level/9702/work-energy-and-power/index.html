Q1Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement defines the work done by a force?A the product of the force and the time for which it actsB the product of the force and the displacement in the direction of the forceC the product of the force and the distance from a pivotD the energy transferred per unit time by the force [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B the product of the force and the displacement in the direction of the force
    Work = force × displacement in the direction of the force. A is impulse, C is a moment, D is power.
Q2Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A worker drags a crate 15 m along a level floor using a rope. The rope is at 30° to the horizontal and the tension in it is 120 N. How much work does the tension do on the crate?A 900 JB 1.04 kJC 1.56 kJD 1.80 kJ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 1.56 kJ
    W = Fs cos θ = 120 × 15 × cos 30° = 1560 J ≈ 1.56 kJ. 900 J uses sin 30° (the vertical component, which does no work along the floor); 1.80 kJ ignores the angle.
Q3Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define work done. [1]
  2. (b) A shopkeeper in Faisalabad lifts a 25 kg sack of rice vertically through 1.4 m onto a cart at constant speed. Calculate the work done on the sack. [2]
  3. (c) State the form of energy that increases as the sack is lifted, and explain why the kinetic energy of the sack does not change. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Work done = force × displacement in the direction of the force.
    B1: force × distance moved in the direction of the force (not just 'force × distance').
  2. (b) 343 J
    Force needed = weight = 25 × 9.81 = 245 N (C1); W = 245 × 1.4 = 343 J (A1)
  3. (c) Gravitational potential energy increases; KE is constant because the speed is constant.
    B1 gravitational potential energy; B1 speed constant so ½mv² unchanged (resultant force zero).
Q4Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A constant resultant force F acts on a body of mass m, initially at rest, and moves it a distance s in the direction of the force. Use the concept of work done to show that the kinetic energy of the body is ½mv², where v is its final speed. [3]
  2. (b) A car of mass 1200 kg travels along the motorway at 72 km h⁻¹. Calculate its kinetic energy. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) W = Fs; F = ma; v² = 2as so s = v²/2a; W = ma × v²/2a = ½mv², and the work done becomes kinetic energy.
    M1 W = Fs and F = ma; M1 v² = u² + 2as with u = 0 gives s = v²/(2a); A1 substitution giving W = ½mv² = KE
  2. (b) 2.40 × 10⁵ J
    72 km h⁻¹ = 20 m s⁻¹ (C1); KE = ½ × 1200 × 20² = 2.40 × 10⁵ J (A1)
Q5Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A child of mass 32 kg starts from rest at the top of a water slide at a water park in Karachi. The bottom of the slide is 6.5 m vertically below the top.

  1. (a) Assuming there is no friction, calculate the speed of the child at the bottom of the slide. [2]
  2. (b) The actual speed at the bottom is 9.6 m s⁻¹. Calculate the energy transferred to thermal energy by friction during the descent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 11.3 m s⁻¹
    mgh = ½mv² so v = √(2gh) (C1) = √(2 × 9.81 × 6.5) = 11.3 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  2. (b) 566 J
    loss of GPE = 32 × 9.81 × 6.5 = 2040 J; gain in KE = ½ × 32 × 9.6² = 1475 J (C1); energy dissipated = 566 J (A1)
Q6Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A person of mass 70 kg travels in a lift from the ground floor to the third floor of an office building in Islamabad. Each floor is 3.2 m high.

  1. (a) Calculate the increase in her gravitational potential energy. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the expression ΔEp = mgΔh can be used here but not for a rocket travelling 3000 km above the Earth. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6590 J
    Δh = 3 × 3.2 = 9.6 m (C1); ΔEp = mgΔh = 70 × 9.81 × 9.6 = 6590 J (A1)
  2. (b) Near the surface g is (almost) constant over a 9.6 m change; far above the surface g decreases with height, so mgΔh is not valid.
    B1 equation assumes a uniform gravitational field (constant g); B1 g varies significantly over large heights
Q7Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A stone is released from rest at a height H above the ground. Air resistance is negligible. When the stone is at a height H/4 above the ground, what is the ratio(kinetic energy of the stone) : (gravitational potential energy of the stone measured from the ground)?A 1 : 3B 1 : 4C 3 : 1D 4 : 1 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 3 : 1
    GPE lost = mg(3H/4) = KE gained. GPE remaining = mg(H/4). KE : GPE = 3H/4 : H/4 = 3 : 1. 1 : 3 is the inverse; 4 : 1 compares total energy with GPE.
Q8Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) The mass of a toy car is halved and its speed is doubled. By what factor does its kinetic energy change?A it stays the sameB ×2C ×4D ×8 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B ×2
    KE = ½mv². New KE = ½(m/2)(2v)² = 2 × ½mv², so it doubles. ×4 forgets that the mass was halved; ×8 squares the factor wrongly.
Q9Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A pendulum consists of a bob of mass 0.25 kg on a light string of length 1.20 m. The bob is pulled aside until the string makes an angle of 35° with the vertical, and is released from rest.

  1. (a) Show that the bob falls through a vertical height of about 0.22 m as it moves to the lowest point. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the bob at the lowest point. Assume air resistance is negligible. [2]
  3. (c) State and explain whether a bob of mass 0.50 kg released in the same way would have a different speed at the lowest point. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) h = 1.2(1 − cos 35°) = 0.217 m
    M1 height of bob below pivot at release = 1.2 cos 35° = 0.983 m; A1 h = 1.20 − 0.983 = 0.217 m ≈ 0.22 m
  2. (b) 2.06 m s⁻¹
    ½mv² = mgh (C1); v = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.217) = 2.06 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) No: the speed is the same.
    B1 same speed; B1 m cancels in mgh = ½mv² (both GPE lost and KE gained are proportional to m).
Q10Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A gas in a cylinder is heated. It expands at a constant pressure of 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa, pushing a piston outwards. Its volume increases from 2.4 × 10⁻³ m³ to 3.1 × 10⁻³ m³.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done by the gas. [2]
  2. (b) The piston has a cross-sectional area of 0.012 m². Calculate the force exerted on the piston by the gas and the distance moved by the piston. [2]
  3. (c) Use your answers to (b) to confirm your answer to (a), and hence explain why W = pΔV is a form of 'work = force × displacement'. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 105 J
    W = pΔV (C1) = 1.5 × 10⁵ × (3.1 − 2.4) × 10⁻³ = 105 J (A1)
  2. (b) force = 1800 N; distance = 0.0583 m
    F = pA = 1.5 × 10⁵ × 0.012 = 1800 N (B1); distance = ΔV/A = 0.0007/0.012 = 0.0583 m (B1)
  3. (c) F × d = 1800 × 0.0583 = 105 J, the same; since F = pA and d = ΔV/A, Fd = pA × ΔV/A = pΔV.
    B1 numerical confirmation; B1 algebra F d = (pA)(ΔV/A) = pΔV
Q11Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A cricket ball of mass 0.16 kg is hit vertically upwards with an initial speed of 18 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum height the ball would reach if there were no air resistance. [2]
  2. (b) The ball actually rises to a height of 14.2 m. Calculate the average force of air resistance on the ball during its upward motion. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the ball takes longer to fall back from its highest point than it took to rise. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16.5 m
    ½mv² = mgh (C1); h = v²/2g = 18²/(2 × 9.81) = 16.5 m (A1)
  2. (b) 0.256 N
    initial KE = ½ × 0.16 × 18² = 25.9 J; GPE at top = 0.16 × 9.81 × 14.2 = 22.3 J (C1); work against air resistance = 3.63 J (C1); F = 3.63/14.2 = 0.256 N (A1)
  3. (c) Air resistance acts upwards on the way down, so the downward acceleration is less than g (and the speed at each height is lower).
    B1 air resistance opposes motion: on the way up it adds to the weight (deceleration > g), on the way down it reduces the acceleration below g.
Q12Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A student releases a trolley from rest on a ramp. The trolley's starting height above the bottom of the ramp is h. A card on the trolley passes through a light gate at the bottom, and a timer gives the speed v.h/m: 0.10, 0.20, 0.30, 0.40, 0.50v/m s⁻¹: 1.30, 1.84, 2.25, 2.60, 2.91

  1. (a) Calculate v² for each result. [1]
  2. (b) It is suggested that v² is directly proportional to h. Use the data to test this suggestion. [2]
  3. (c) If all the gravitational potential energy were converted to kinetic energy, v² = 2gh. Determine the fraction of the GPE that is converted into kinetic energy of the trolley. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest one reason why not all the GPE becomes kinetic energy, and one improvement to the way v is measured. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) v²/m² s⁻²: 1.69, 3.39, 5.06, 6.76, 8.47
    B1 all five values correct to 3 s.f. (1.69, 3.39, 5.06, 6.76, 8.47)
  2. (b) v²/h = 16.9, 16.9, 16.9, 16.9, 16.9 m s⁻², constant within experimental error, so the suggestion is supported.
    M1 calculates v²/h (or plots v² against h: straight line through the origin) for all values; A1 valid conclusion: ratio constant (16.9) so proportional
  3. (c) about 0.86
    gradient of v² against h = 16.9 m s⁻² (C1); fraction = 16.9/(2 × 9.81) = 0.86 (A1)
  4. (d) Friction/air resistance (or energy to rotate the wheels); use a longer card or measure the card width with vernier calipers / use a data logger with two light gates.
    B1 friction at the axles or air resistance or rotational KE of wheels; B1 sensible improvement to the timing/measurement of v
Q13Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A car of mass 1500 kg travelling at 25 m s⁻¹ brakes and comes to rest in a distance of 48 m.

  1. (a) Calculate the average braking force. [2]
  2. (b) The car now travels at 50 m s⁻¹ and brakes with the same average force. Determine the braking distance and explain your reasoning. [2]
  3. (c) State the energy change that takes place in the brakes. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9770 N
    KE = ½ × 1500 × 25² = 4.69 × 10⁵ J (C1); F = KE/d = 4.69 × 10⁵/48 = 9770 N (A1)
  2. (b) 192 m
    M1 KE ∝ v², so KE is 4 times greater; A1 work Fd = KE, so d = 4 × 48 = 192 m
  3. (c) Kinetic energy → thermal (internal) energy of the brakes and surroundings.
    B1 kinetic to thermal energy
Q14Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A block of mass 2.0 kg is released from rest and slides 3.0 m down a rough slope inclined at 25° to the horizontal. It reaches a speed of 4.0 m s⁻¹. What is the average frictional force on the block?A 2.96 NB 5.33 NC 8.29 ND 19.6 N [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 2.96 N
    Loss of GPE = mg × 3.0 sin 25° = 24.9 J. Gain in KE = ½ × 2.0 × 4.0² = 16 J. Work against friction = 24.9 − 16 = 8.87 J, so F = 8.87/3.0 = 2.96 N. 5.33 N = KE/3.0 and 8.29 N = GPE/3.0 are the main distractors.
Q15Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A horizontal force F pulls a trolley along a straight track. F increases uniformly from 0 to 12 N as the displacement x increases from 0 to 0.50 m, then stays constant at 12 N from x = 0.50 m to x = 2.00 m.

  1. (a) Explain how the work done by a variable force can be found from a graph of force F against displacement x. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the total work done by the force over the 2.00 m. [2]
  3. (c) The trolley has mass 1.5 kg and starts from rest on a horizontal frictionless track. Calculate its speed at x = 2.00 m. [2]
  4. (d) In a repeat experiment there is a constant frictional force of 2.0 N. Determine the new speed at x = 2.00 m. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is the area under the force–displacement graph.
    B1 area between the line and the x-axis
  2. (b) 21.0 J
    triangle: ½ × 0.50 × 12 = 3.0 J; rectangle: 12 × 1.50 = 18 J (C1); total = 21.0 J (A1)
  3. (c) 5.29 m s⁻¹
    ½ × 1.5 × v² = 21 (C1); v = 5.29 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  4. (d) 4.76 m s⁻¹
    work against friction = 2.0 × 2.00 = 4.0 J; KE = 21 − 4.0 = 17 J (C1); v = √(2 × 17/1.5) = 4.76 m s⁻¹ (A1)
Q16Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A skier of mass 68 kg starts from rest at the top of a straight slope at Naltar. The slope is 120 m long and inclined at 12° to the horizontal. A constant total resistive force of 45 N acts on the skier.

  1. (a) Calculate the loss of gravitational potential energy of the skier on the slope. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the speed of the skier at the bottom of the slope. [3]
  3. (c) At the bottom the ground becomes horizontal. The resistive force stays at 45 N. Calculate the distance the skier travels on the horizontal ground before stopping. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16600 J
    vertical drop = 120 sin 12° = 24.9 m (C1); ΔGPE = 68 × 9.81 × 24.9 = 16600 J (A1)
  2. (b) 18.2 m s⁻¹
    work against resistance = 45 × 120 = 5400 J (C1); KE = 16600 − 5400 = 11200 J (C1); v = √(2 × 11200/68) = 18.2 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) 250 m
    KE = work against resistance: 11200 = 45 × d (C1); d = 250 m (A1)

A box of mass 15 kg is pulled from rest up a ramp 4.0 m long, inclined at 20° to the horizontal, by a constant force of 110 N parallel to the ramp. A constant frictional force of 18 N acts on the box.

  1. (a) Calculate the work done by the 110 N force. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the increase in gravitational potential energy of the box. [2]
  3. (c) Determine the speed of the box at the top of the ramp. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 440 J
    W = 110 × 4.0 = 440 J (B1)
  2. (b) 201 J
    height gained = 4.0 sin 20° = 1.37 m (C1); ΔGPE = 15 × 9.81 × 1.37 = 201 J (A1)
  3. (c) 4.71 m s⁻¹
    work against friction = 18 × 4.0 = 72 J (C1); KE = 440 − 201 − 72 = 167 J (C1); v = √(2 × 167/15) = 4.71 m s⁻¹ (A1)
Q18Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A loaded truck of mass 9000 kg and a motorcycle of mass 180 kg each have a momentum of 2.7 × 10⁴ kg m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Show that the kinetic energy Eₖ of a body of mass m and momentum p is given by Eₖ = p²/2m. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the kinetic energy of the truck and of the motorcycle. [2]
  3. (c) Both vehicles are brought to rest by the same constant braking force. State and explain which vehicle travels the greater distance while braking. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) p = mv so v = p/m; Eₖ = ½m(p/m)² = p²/2m.
    M1 v = p/m; A1 substitution into ½mv²
  2. (b) truck: 40500 J; motorcycle: 2.02 × 10⁶ J
    Eₖ = (2.7 × 10⁴)²/(2 × 9000) = 40500 J (B1); (2.7 × 10⁴)²/(2 × 180) = 2.02 × 10⁶ J (B1)
  3. (c) The motorcycle (50 times the distance), because it has 50 times more kinetic energy and work done = force × distance.
    B1 motorcycle; B1 braking work Fd = Eₖ, so d ∝ Eₖ, which is 50 times greater for the motorcycle
Q19Work & energy conservation (KE, GPE) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A ball is dropped from rest from a height of 2.00 m onto a hard floor. It rebounds to a height of 1.50 m. Air resistance is negligible.

  1. (a) Calculate the speed of the ball just before it first hits the floor and just after it leaves the floor. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the ball's kinetic energy lost in the first bounce. [1]
  3. (c) Each bounce loses the same fraction of kinetic energy. Determine the number of the bounce after which the ball first fails to rise to 0.50 m. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) before: 6.26 m s⁻¹; after: 5.42 m s⁻¹
    v = √(2gh): √(2 × 9.81 × 2.0) = 6.26 m s⁻¹ (B1); √(2 × 9.81 × 1.5) = 5.42 m s⁻¹ (B1)
  2. (b) 25 %
    KE ∝ height: 1.5/2.0 = 0.75, so 25 % lost (B1)
  3. (c) after the 5th bounce (heights 1.50, 1.13, 0.84, 0.63, 0.47 m)
    M1 heights form 2.0 × 0.75ⁿ: 1.50, 1.13, 0.84, 0.63, 0.47 m; A1 5th bounce

Q21Power & efficiency · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

An electric motor raises a lift and its passengers, of total mass 450 kg, at a constant speed of 1.8 m s⁻¹.

  1. (a) Define power. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the useful output power of the motor. [2]
  3. (c) The electrical input power to the motor is 11 kW. Calculate the efficiency of the lift system. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Power is the work done (energy transferred) per unit time.
    B1 work done/energy transferred per unit time
  2. (b) 7950 W
    P = Fv = mgv (C1) = 450 × 9.81 × 1.8 = 7950 W (A1)
  3. (c) 72.2 %
    efficiency = useful output/total input × 100 % (C1) = 7950/11 000 × 100 = 72.2 % (A1)
Q22Power & efficiency · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A student of mass 55 kg runs up a staircase at her school in Peshawar. There are 24 steps, each 0.17 m high, and she takes 6.2 s.

  1. (a) Calculate the gain in gravitational potential energy of the student. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the average useful power developed by the student. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the actual power output of the student's muscles is greater than your answer to (b). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2200 J
    height = 24 × 0.17 = 4.08 m (C1); ΔEp = 55 × 9.81 × 4.08 = 2200 J (A1)
  2. (b) 355 W
    P = 2200/6.2 = 355 W (B1)
  3. (c) Some energy is also transferred as thermal energy in the body (muscles are inefficient) and to KE of moving limbs.
    B1 muscles not 100 % efficient / energy wasted as heat / KE of the body and limbs
Q23Power & efficiency · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A 60 W filament lamp emits 5.4 W of visible light. An 8.0 W LED lamp emits the same amount of visible light.

  1. (a) Define efficiency. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the efficiency of each lamp. [2]
  3. (c) A household replaces one filament lamp with an LED lamp. The lamp is used for 5.0 hours per day. Calculate the electrical energy saved in one year (365 days), in kW h. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Efficiency = useful energy (or power) output ÷ total energy (or power) input.
    B1
  2. (b) filament lamp: 9.0 %; LED lamp: 68 %
    B1 5.4/60 × 100 = 9.0 %; B1 5.4/8.0 × 100 = 68 %
  3. (c) 94.9 kW h
    power saved = 60 − 8.0 = 52 W (C1); 52 × 5.0 × 365 = 94 900 W h = 94.9 kW h (A1)
Q24Power & efficiency · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) C 20 kW
    At constant speed the driving force equals the resistive force, 800 N. P = Fv = 800 × 25 = 20000 W = 20 kW. 32 W divides F by v; 10 kW halves the answer as if using average speed.
Q25Power & efficiency · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A car of mass 1100 kg has an engine with a maximum useful output power of 60 kW.

  1. (a) Show that the power P needed to move an object at a constant speed v against a resistive force F is given by P = Fv. [2]
  2. (b) On a level road at its maximum speed, the total resistive force on the car is 2400 N. Calculate the maximum speed. [2]
  3. (c) At a speed of 15 m s⁻¹ the resistive force is 1200 N. Calculate the maximum acceleration of the car at this speed. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = W/t = F × s/t = Fv
    M1 P = work/time = Fs/t; A1 s/t = v so P = Fv
  2. (b) 25.0 m s⁻¹
    driving force = resistive force = 2400 N (C1); v = P/F = 60 000/2400 = 25.0 m s⁻¹ (A1)
  3. (c) 2.55 m s⁻²
    driving force = 60 000/15 = 4000 N (C1); resultant force = 4000 − 1200 = 2800 N (C1); a = 2800/1100 = 2.55 m s⁻² (A1)
Q26Power & efficiency · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

At a hydroelectric dam in northern Pakistan, water falls through a vertical height of 105 m to the turbines. The flow rate is 380 m³ s⁻¹. The density of water is 1000 kg m⁻³. Assume that the water starts from rest and leaves the turbines with negligible speed.

  1. (a) Calculate the loss of gravitational potential energy of the water per second. [2]
  2. (b) The overall efficiency of the turbines and generators is 85 %. Calculate the electrical output power. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the electrical energy produced in one day. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.91 × 10⁸ W
    mass per second = 380 × 1000 = 3.80 × 10⁵ kg (C1); mgh per second = 3.80 × 10⁵ × 9.81 × 105 = 3.91 × 10⁸ J s⁻¹ (A1)
  2. (b) 3.33 × 10⁸ W
    0.85 × 3.91 × 10⁸ = 3.33 × 10⁸ W (B1)
  3. (c) 2.87 × 10¹³ J
    E = Pt = 3.33 × 10⁸ × 24 × 3600 = 2.87 × 10¹³ J (B1)
Q27Power & efficiency · Standard · Paper 3 style · 7 marks

A student uses a small electric motor to lift masses m through a height of 0.80 m. The supply p.d. is constant at 6.0 V. She measures the current I and the time t for each lift.m/kg: 0.100, 0.200, 0.300, 0.400I/A: 0.18, 0.24, 0.29, 0.34t/s: 2.4, 2.9, 3.5, 4.4

  1. (a) For m = 0.300 kg, calculate (i) the useful energy output and (ii) the electrical energy input. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the efficiency of the motor for each mass. [2]
  3. (c) Describe how the efficiency varies with the mass lifted. [1]
  4. (d) The time t was measured with a hand-held stopwatch. Identify one significant source of uncertainty in t and suggest an improvement. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 2.35 J (ii) 6.09 J
    B1 mgh = 0.300 × 9.81 × 0.80 = 2.35 J; B1 VIt = 6.0 × 0.29 × 3.5 = 6.09 J
  2. (b) efficiency/%: 30.3, 37.6, 38.7, 35.0
    C1 efficiency = mgh/(VIt) × 100 %; A1 all four correct (30.3, 37.6, 38.7, 35.0 %)
  3. (c) It increases to a maximum at about 0.3 kg and then decreases.
    B1 rises then falls (maximum near 0.3 kg)
  4. (d) Reaction time is large compared with 2–4 s; use light gates/data logger or video with a timer, or lift through a greater height.
    B1 human reaction time significant / difficult to judge start and end; B1 light gates at start and finish, or video, or larger h
Q28Power & efficiency · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The blades of a wind turbine near Jhimpir in Sindh sweep a circle of radius 40 m. Wind of speed 9.0 m s⁻¹ blows directly towards the blades. The density of air is 1.2 kg m⁻³.

  1. (a) Show that the mass of air passing through the circle swept by the blades per second is ρAv, where A is the area of the circle, and calculate its value. [2]
  2. (b) Hence show that the kinetic energy of the air arriving per second is ½ρAv³, and calculate it. [2]
  3. (c) The electrical output of the turbine is 0.80 MW. Calculate its efficiency, and explain why the efficiency can never be 100 %. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 54300 kg s⁻¹
    M1 volume per second = A × v (air moves v metres per second through area A), mass per second = ρAv; A1 1.2 × π × 40² × 9.0 = 54300 kg s⁻¹
  2. (b) 2.20 × 10⁶ W
    M1 KE per second = ½(mass per second)v² = ½(ρAv)v² = ½ρAv³; A1 ½ × 1.2 × π × 40² × 9.0³ = 2.20 × 10⁶ W
  3. (c) 36.4 %; the air must keep moving after the blades (it cannot be brought to rest), and there are friction and electrical losses.
    B1 0.80 × 10⁶/2.20 × 10⁶ × 100 = 36.4 %; B1 air still has KE after passing the blades / friction / heating in generator
Q29Power & efficiency · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A cyclist and bicycle of total mass 85 kg travel up a straight road inclined at 4.0° to the horizontal at a constant speed of 5.0 m s⁻¹ in the Murree hills. The total resistive force from air and friction is 22 N.

  1. (a) Calculate the useful power output of the cyclist. [3]
  2. (b) On level ground the cyclist produces the same power. The resistive force is proportional to the square of the speed and is still 22 N at 5.0 m s⁻¹. Determine the constant speed of the cyclist on level ground. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 401 W
    component of weight down slope = 85 × 9.81 × sin 4.0° = 58.2 N (C1); total force = 58.2 + 22 = 80.2 N (C1); P = Fv = 80.2 × 5.0 = 401 W (A1)
  2. (b) 7.69 m s⁻¹
    F = kv² with k = 22/5.0² = 0.88 N s² m⁻² (C1); P = Fv = kv³ (C1); v = (401/0.88)^(1/3) = 7.69 m s⁻¹ (A1)
Q30Power & efficiency · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A farmer in Punjab uses an electric pump to lift water from a well. The water is raised through 18 m and leaves the pipe at a speed of 6.0 m s⁻¹. The pump delivers 0.025 m³ of water per second. The density of water is 1000 kg m⁻³.

  1. (a) Calculate the useful power output of the pump. [3]
  2. (b) The pump is 65 % efficient. Calculate the electrical input power. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the electrical energy used in 2.0 hours of pumping, in kW h. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4860 W
    mass per second = 25 kg s⁻¹ (C1); GPE per second = 25 × 9.81 × 18 = 4415 W and KE per second = ½ × 25 × 6.0² = 450 W (C1); total = 4860 W (A1)
  2. (b) 7480 W
    4860/0.65 = 7480 W (B1)
  3. (c) 15.0 kW h
    E = Pt = 7480 W × 2.0 h (C1) = 15.0 kW h (A1)
