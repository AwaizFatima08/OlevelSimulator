Q22Kinetic theory & diffusion · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement is NOT an assumption of the kinetic theory of an ideal gas? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D The volume of the molecules is a large fraction of the volume of the container.
    An ideal gas assumes the molecules themselves have negligible volume. A, B and C are all genuine assumptions; C (no forces) is a common distractor because real gases do have forces.
Q23Kinetic theory & diffusion · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement explains why a gas exerts a pressure on the walls of its container? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The gas molecules collide with the walls and exert a force on them.
    Gas molecules move randomly and constantly; their collisions with the container walls give a force per unit area, which is the pressure. Gas molecules are not attracted to walls (A), not at rest (C), and expansion (D) describes the result of motion, not the cause of pressure.

  1. (a) Explain why a gas has no fixed volume and fills the whole of any container it is placed in. [2]

Q27Kinetic theory & diffusion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Use the kinetic theory to explain why the pressure of a gas in a sealed container increases when it is heated. [2]
  2. (b) Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia is placed at one end of a 100 cm glass tube and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid at the other end. A white ring forms after a few minutes. State where the ring forms and explain why. [3]
Show answer

Q28Kinetic theory & diffusion · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Hydrogen (Mr = 2.0) and oxygen (Mr = 32.0) are at the same temperature. Which statement is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C Both gases have the same average kinetic energy, but hydrogen molecules move faster.
    The average kinetic energy of gas molecules depends only on absolute temperature, so it is the same for both gases. For equal kinetic energies, ½mv² is the same, so the lighter hydrogen molecules must move faster. A and B are wrong because kinetic energy does not depend on the molar mass at the same temperature; D reverses the speed relationship.
Q29Kinetic theory & diffusion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Cotton wool soaked in concentrated ammonia and cotton wool soaked in concentrated hydrochloric acid are placed at opposite ends of a 100.0 cm glass tube. A white ring of ammonium chloride forms inside the tube. State which end of the tube the ring is nearer to. [1]
  2. (b) Use Graham's law to calculate the distance from the ammonia end to the white ring. (Mr: NH₃ = 17.0; HCl = 36.5) [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The ring is nearer to the hydrochloric acid end (HCl end).
    NH₃ (Mr 17.0) is lighter and diffuses faster than HCl (Mr 36.5), so it travels further before meeting the HCl; the ring forms nearer the HCl end.
  2. (b) 59.4 cm
    M1 rate ratio: d(NH3)/d(HCl) = √(Mr HCl / Mr NH3) = √(36.5/17.0) = 1.465; M1 d(NH3) + d(HCl) = 100.0 cm, so d(NH3) = 100.0 × 1.465 / 2.465; A1 = 59.4 cm.
Q30Kinetic theory & diffusion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) A plunger of a gas syringe is pushed in slowly at constant temperature. Use kinetic theory to explain why the pressure of the gas increases. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The same number of molecules is in a smaller volume, so the molecules hit the walls and plunger more often per unit area. The total force per unit area (pressure) increases.
    1 mark more collisions per unit area per second (smaller volume); 1 mark force per unit area (pressure) therefore increases.
