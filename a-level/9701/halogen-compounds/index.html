Q1SN1/SN2 & reactivity · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State which mechanism, SN1 or SN2, is mainly followed in the hydrolysis of (i) 1-bromobutane (ii) 2-bromo-2-methylpropane. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why tertiary halogenoalkanes react mainly by the SN1 mechanism. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) SN2 (ii) SN1
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Tertiary carbocation is the most stable because three alkyl groups donate electrons (positive inductive effect) and stabilise the positive charge; the three bulky alkyl groups hinder (steric hindrance) attack by a nucleophile from the side opposite the halogen, so SN2 is difficult.
    B1 tertiary carbocation stable; B1 alkyl groups electron-donating/inductive effect; B1 steric hindrance prevents SN2 attack.
Q2SN1/SN2 & reactivity · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

1-bromobutane is warmed with aqueous sodium hydroxide.

  1. (a) Describe the SN2 mechanism for the reaction of 1-bromobutane with hydroxide ions. Describe in words where each curly arrow starts and ends, and describe the transition state. [4]
  2. (b) State the rate equation for this reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Arrow from a lone pair on O of OH⁻ to the δ+ carbon bonded to Br; arrow from the C–Br bond to Br; transition state [HO···C···Br]⁻ with partial bonds and a negative charge, OH and Br on opposite sides of carbon; products butan-1-ol and Br⁻.
    B1 C–Br dipole (Cδ+, Brδ−); B1 arrow from OH⁻ lone pair to C; B1 arrow from C–Br bond to Br; B1 transition state with partial bonds and overall negative charge.
  2. (b) rate = k[CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Br][OH⁻]
    B1.
Q3SN1/SN2 & reactivity · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

An isomer of C₄H₉Br reacts with aqueous hydroxide ions. Initial rates at 25 °C:Expt 1: [C₄H₉Br] = 0.050, [OH⁻] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, rate = 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Expt 2: [C₄H₉Br] = 0.100, [OH⁻] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, rate = 7.2 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Expt 3: [C₄H₉Br] = 0.050, [OH⁻] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³, rate = 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

  1. (a) Deduce the orders with respect to C₄H₉Br and to OH⁻, and write the rate equation. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate constant, with units. [1]
  3. (c) Deduce the mechanism and hence the structural formula of this isomer of C₄H₉Br. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) First order in C₄H₉Br, zero order in OH⁻; rate = k[C₄H₉Br]
    B1 orders from data; B1 rate equation.
  2. (b) 0.0072 s⁻¹
    B1 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.050.
  3. (c) SN1, so the isomer is tertiary: (CH₃)₃CBr, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane
    M1 rate independent of [OH⁻] → SN1; A1 tertiary isomer (CH₃)₃CBr.
Q4SN1/SN2 & reactivity · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which halogenoalkane is most likely to undergo hydrolysis by an SN1 mechanism?A CH₃CH₂CH₂ClB CH₃CHClCH₃C (CH₃)₃CClD CH₃Cl [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C (CH₃)₃CCl
    (CH₃)₃CCl is tertiary: it forms the most stable carbocation and is sterically hindered towards SN2. B is secondary and can react by either route, but less readily by SN1.
Q5SN1/SN2 & reactivity · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

2-bromobutane, CH₃CHBrCH₂CH₃, can undergo nucleophilic substitution with hydroxide ions.

  1. (a) Explain why 2-bromobutane is chiral. [1]
  2. (b) A single enantiomer of 2-bromobutane reacts with OH⁻ by the SN2 mechanism. State and explain whether the butan-2-ol formed rotates plane-polarised light. [2]
  3. (c) If the same reaction occurred by SN1, the product would not rotate plane-polarised light. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Carbon 2 is bonded to four different groups (H, Br, CH₃, C₂H₅).
    B1.
  2. (b) Yes – OH⁻ attacks from the side opposite Br, so the configuration is inverted and a single enantiomer of butan-2-ol forms, which is optically active.
    B1 attack opposite to leaving group/inversion; B1 single enantiomer so optically active.
  3. (c) The carbocation intermediate is planar, so the nucleophile can attack equally from either side; a racemic mixture (50 : 50 enantiomers) forms and the rotations cancel.
    B1 planar carbocation, attack from both faces; B1 racemic mixture, no net rotation.
Q6SN1/SN2 & reactivity · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Three test-tubes each contain 1 cm³ of ethanol, 1 cm³ of aqueous silver nitrate and three drops of a halogenoalkane, warmed in a water bath at 50 °C: 1-chlorobutane, 1-bromobutane and 1-iodobutane.Bond energies / kJ mol⁻¹: C–Cl 340; C–Br 280; C–I 240

  1. (a) Predict the order in which precipitates appear, fastest first, and state the colour of each precipitate. [2]
  2. (b) Use the bond energies to explain the order of reactivity. [2]
  3. (c) A student predicts that 1-chlorobutane should react fastest because the C–Cl bond is the most polar. Explain why this prediction is wrong. [1]
  4. (d) Explain the purpose of the ethanol. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1-iodobutane (yellow AgI) first, then 1-bromobutane (cream AgBr), then 1-chlorobutane (white AgCl) last.
    B1 order I > Br > Cl; B1 all colours.
  2. (b) C–I is the weakest bond (240 kJ mol⁻¹), so it breaks most easily; C–Cl is the strongest (340 kJ mol⁻¹), so the chloroalkane is hydrolysed most slowly.
    B1 bond strength decreases C–Cl > C–Br > C–I; B1 weaker bond broken more easily so faster rate.
  3. (c) Bond strength, not bond polarity, is the dominant factor controlling the rate of hydrolysis.
    B1.
  4. (d) It is a solvent in which both the halogenoalkane and aqueous silver nitrate dissolve (they mix).
    B1.

    B1.
  3. (c) Base (removes H⁺ from the carbon next to the C–Br carbon).
    B1.

Q9Elimination · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

2-bromobutane is heated with potassium hydroxide in ethanol. Three different alkenes are formed.

  1. (a) Name all three alkenes formed. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why there are two products with the formula CH₃CH=CHCH₃. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) but-1-ene; cis-but-2-ene (Z); trans-but-2-ene (E)
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Restricted rotation about the C=C bond; and each carbon of the double bond carries two different groups (H and CH₃), so cis/trans (geometrical) isomers exist.
    B1 restricted rotation; B1 two different groups on each C of C=C.
Q10Elimination · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Name the organic product and write an equation when 1-bromopropane is heated with (i) NaOH(aq) (ii) NaOH in ethanol. [4]
  2. (b) Explain the different behaviour of the hydroxide ion in (i) and (ii). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) propan-1-ol: CH₃CH₂CH₂Br + NaOH → CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + NaBr (ii) propene: CH₃CH₂CH₂Br + NaOH → CH₃CH=CH₂ + NaBr + H₂O
    1 mark for each product name, 1 mark for each equation.
  2. (b) In water OH⁻ acts mainly as a nucleophile, attacking the δ+ carbon (substitution); in ethanol it acts mainly as a base, removing H⁺ (elimination).
    B1 nucleophile in aqueous; B1 base in ethanolic.
Q11Elimination · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

There are four structural isomers with formula C₄H₉Br. Each is heated with ethanolic potassium hydroxide.

  1. (a) Give the names of the four structural isomers of C₄H₉Br. [2]
  2. (b) Identify which isomer(s) give(s) only 2-methylpropene on elimination. [2]
  3. (c) Identify the isomer whose elimination products include a pair of stereoisomers. [1]
  4. (d) Identify the isomer that gives only an unbranched alkene with the C=C at the end of the chain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1-bromobutane; 2-bromobutane; 1-bromo-2-methylpropane; 2-bromo-2-methylpropane
    B2 all four (B1 for three).
  2. (b) 1-bromo-2-methylpropane and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane
    B1 each.
  3. (c) 2-bromobutane
    B1 gives cis- and trans-but-2-ene.
  4. (d) 1-bromobutane (gives but-1-ene only)
    B1.
Q12Halogenoarenes vs halogenoalkanes · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why chlorobenzene does not undergo nucleophilic substitution with NaOH(aq) under normal laboratory conditions. [3]
  2. (b) Predict what is seen when chlorobenzene and 1-chlorobutane are each warmed with ethanolic silver nitrate. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A lone pair (p-orbital) on Cl overlaps with the delocalised π-electrons of the ring; so the C–Cl bond has partial double-bond character and is stronger/harder to break; the electron-rich ring also repels nucleophiles.
    B1 p-orbital overlap with π system; B1 stronger C–Cl bond; B1 π electron cloud repels OH⁻.
  2. (b) Chlorobenzene: no precipitate; 1-chlorobutane: white precipitate (slowly).
    B1 each.
Q13Halogenoarenes vs halogenoalkanes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Consider three chlorine compounds: benzoyl chloride (C₆H₅COCl), 1-chlorobutane (CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂Cl) and chlorobenzene (C₆H₅Cl).

  1. (a) Put the three compounds in order of ease of hydrolysis, most easily hydrolysed first. [1]
  2. (b) Describe what is observed when each compound is added to water containing AgNO₃(aq) at room temperature. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why benzoyl chloride is hydrolysed most easily. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) benzoyl chloride > 1-chlorobutane > chlorobenzene
    B1.
  2. (b) C₆H₅COCl: immediate white precipitate (and steamy fumes of HCl); 1-chlorobutane: no precipitate at room temperature (slow, faint on warming); C₆H₅Cl: no precipitate.
    B1 immediate precipitate with acyl chloride; B1 none/very slow for the other two.
  3. (c) The carbonyl carbon is attached to both O and Cl, two electronegative atoms, so it is strongly δ+; the planar C=O carbon is easily attacked by water (addition–elimination).
    B1 two electronegative atoms make C very δ+; B1 attack by water easy (lone pair of H₂O).
Q14Halogenoarenes vs halogenoalkanes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Methylbenzene reacts with chlorine under two different sets of conditions.

  1. (a) Give the conditions needed to make (i) 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene (and its 2-isomer) (ii) (chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl, from methylbenzene and chlorine. [2]
  2. (b) Name the type of mechanism in each case. [2]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the formation of the electrophile in (i). [1]

  1. (a) (i) AlCl₃ (or FeCl₃) catalyst, room temperature, no UV (ii) UV light (or boiling)
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) (i) electrophilic substitution (ii) free-radical substitution
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) Cl₂ + AlCl₃ → Cl⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
    B1.
Q15Halogenoarenes vs halogenoalkanes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Equal amounts of bromobenzene, 1-bromobutane and 2-bromo-2-methylpropane are added to separate tubes of warm aqueous ethanolic silver nitrate.

  1. (a) Predict the order in which a cream precipitate appears in the three tubes, fastest first. [1]
  2. (b) Explain your order for the two halogenoalkanes. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why bromobenzene gives no precipitate. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2-bromo-2-methylpropane, then 1-bromobutane; bromobenzene gives no precipitate
    B1.
  2. (b) 2-bromo-2-methylpropane is tertiary and reacts by SN1 via a stable tertiary carbocation, faster than the primary 1-bromobutane (SN2).
    B1 tertiary via SN1/carbocation; B1 faster than primary.
  3. (c) The C–Br bond is strengthened by overlap of a Br lone pair with the ring π system, and the π electrons repel the nucleophile, so no Br⁻ is released.
    B1 stronger C–Br bond (delocalisation); B1 nucleophile repelled.
Q16Halogenoarenes vs halogenoalkanes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give the number of structural isomers of C₇H₇Cl that contain a benzene ring and name the one with Cl on the side chain. [2]
  2. (b) Describe a chemical test to distinguish (chloromethyl)benzene from 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene. Give the results. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why dilute nitric acid is added before the silver nitrate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4; (chloromethyl)benzene
    B1 4 (three ring isomers + one side-chain); B1 name.
  2. (b) Warm each with NaOH(aq); acidify with dilute HNO₃; add AgNO₃(aq). (Chloromethyl)benzene gives a white precipitate; 1-chloro-4-methylbenzene gives no precipitate.
    B1 warm NaOH(aq) then HNO₃ then AgNO₃; B1 white ppt with side-chain compound; B1 no ppt with ring-substituted.
  3. (c) To neutralise excess OH⁻, which would otherwise form a brown precipitate of silver oxide.
    B1.
Q17Reactions of halogenoalkanes (reagents & conditions) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Bromoethane, C₂H₅Br, is a useful starting material in synthesis.

  1. (a) Give the reagent and conditions to convert bromoethane into (i) ethanol (ii) propanenitrile (iii) ethylamine. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) NaOH(aq), heat under reflux (ii) KCN in ethanol, heat under reflux (iii) excess concentrated NH₃ in ethanol, heat in a sealed tube (under pressure)
    1 mark for each reagent and 1 mark for each set of conditions.

Q19Reactions of halogenoalkanes (reagents & conditions) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give reagents and conditions for each step of a two-step synthesis of butanoic acid from 1-bromopropane, naming the intermediate. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why this route is useful in synthesis. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: KCN in ethanol, heat under reflux → butanenitrile; Step 2: dilute HCl(aq) (or H₂SO₄), heat under reflux → butanoic acid.
    B1 KCN/ethanol/heat; B1 intermediate butanenitrile; B1 aqueous acid; B1 reflux.
  2. (b) It increases the length of the carbon chain by one carbon atom.
    B1.
Q20Reactions of halogenoalkanes (reagents & conditions) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write the equation for the reaction of 1-chloropropane with excess ammonia to form propylamine. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why ammonia is used in large excess. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the reaction is carried out in a sealed tube. [1]
  4. (d) Give the structural formula of the secondary amine that could form as a by-product. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Cl
    B1.
  2. (b) Propylamine has a lone pair and is itself a nucleophile, so it can react with more 1-chloropropane to form secondary and tertiary amines (and quaternary salts); excess NH₃ makes collision of the halogenoalkane with NH₃ more likely.
    B1 amine product also nucleophile/further substitution; B1 excess NH₃ reduces this.
  3. (c) Ammonia is volatile; the sealed tube keeps it in (under pressure) when heated.
    B1.
  4. (d) (CH₃CH₂CH₂)₂NH
    B1.
Q21Nucleophilic substitution mechanism · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the term nucleophile. [1]
  2. (b) From the list, identify all the nucleophiles: OH⁻, H⁺, CN⁻, Br₂, NH₃, NO₂⁺, H₂O, AlCl₃ [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbon atom bonded to the halogen in a halogenoalkane is attacked by nucleophiles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A species that donates a lone pair of electrons (to an electron-deficient carbon) to form a new covalent bond.
    B1.
  2. (b) OH⁻, CN⁻, NH₃, H₂O
    B2 all four and no extras (B1 three correct).
  3. (c) The halogen is more electronegative than carbon, so the C–X bond is polar and the carbon is δ+.
    B1.
Q22Nucleophilic substitution mechanism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe, with curly arrows described in words, the SN1 mechanism for the hydrolysis of 2-chloro-2-methylpropane by water. [4]
  2. (b) Identify the rate-determining step. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: C–Cl bond breaks heterolytically (arrow from C–Cl bond to Cl) to give (CH₃)₃C⁺ and Cl⁻; Step 2: a lone pair on the O of H₂O attacks the carbocation (arrow from O lone pair to C⁺); Step 3: loss of H⁺ from the –OH₂⁺ group gives (CH₃)₃COH.
    B1 heterolytic fission arrow; B1 carbocation and Cl⁻; B1 arrow from O lone pair to C⁺; B1 loss of H⁺ to give 2-methylpropan-2-ol.
  2. (b) Step 1 – formation of the carbocation.
    B1.
Q23Nucleophilic substitution mechanism · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Bromomethane reacts with hydroxide ions by the SN2 mechanism. Which statement describes the transition state?A It is a carbocation, CH₃⁺.B It has a partial C–O bond and a partial C–Br bond and carries a negative charge.C It contains a full C–O bond and a full C–Br bond.D It is uncharged, with OH and Br on the same side of the carbon atom. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B It has a partial C–O bond and a partial C–Br bond and carries a negative charge.
    In SN2, bond-making and bond-breaking happen together: [HO···CH₃···Br]⁻ with OH and Br opposite each other. A describes the SN1 intermediate, not a transition state.
Q24Nucleophilic substitution mechanism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the mechanism of the reaction between bromoethane and ammonia to form ethylamine. Describe each curly arrow in words and show the intermediate ion. [4]
  2. (b) Name the type of mechanism. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lone pair on N of NH₃ attacks δ+ C of C–Br (arrow N lone pair → C); C–Br bond breaks (arrow C–Br → Br), giving CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺ and Br⁻; a second NH₃ uses its lone pair to remove H⁺ from CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺ (arrow from N lone pair to H, arrow from N–H bond to N) giving CH₃CH₂NH₂ and NH₄⁺.
    B1 dipole and attack by N lone pair; B1 C–Br breaks, Br⁻; B1 intermediate CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺; B1 deprotonation by second NH₃.
  2. (b) Nucleophilic substitution (SN2)
    B1.

  3. (c) Orange to green

Q28Preparation of halogenoalkanes · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give a reagent for converting propan-2-ol into 2-chloropropane, and write the equation. [2]
  2. (b) Give the reagent and conditions for making bromoethane from (i) ethene (ii) ethane. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why method (ii) is not a good way to make pure bromoethane. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) PCl₅ (or SOCl₂, or PCl₃ with heat, or conc HCl with ZnCl₂): CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + PCl₅ → CH₃CHClCH₃ + POCl₃ + HCl
    B1 reagent; B1 matching balanced equation.
  2. (b) (i) HBr(g), room temperature (ii) Br₂, ultraviolet light
    B1 HBr; B1 Br₂; B1 UV light.
  3. (c) Free-radical substitution gives a mixture of multi-substituted products (e.g. dibromoethanes).
    B1.
Q29Preparation of halogenoalkanes · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

2-chloro-2-methylpropane is prepared by shaking 7.40 g of 2-methylpropan-2-ol with concentrated hydrochloric acid in a separating funnel at room temperature. The upper organic layer is washed with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution, then with water, dried with anhydrous sodium sulfate and distilled. 6.94 g of product is collected.

  1. (a) Explain the purpose of shaking with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution and how the pressure in the funnel is controlled. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why anhydrous sodium sulfate is added. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage yield. [Mr: (CH₃)₃COH 74.0; (CH₃)₃CCl 92.5] [2]
  4. (d) Suggest one safety precaution specific to this experiment. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Removes/neutralises the remaining HCl acid; CO₂ is produced so the tap must be opened regularly (with the funnel inverted) to release pressure.
    B1 neutralise acid; B1 release CO₂ pressure.
  2. (b) It is a drying agent that removes traces of water from the organic layer.
    B1.
  3. (c) 75.0 %
    M1 theoretical 7.40/74.0 × 92.5 = 9.25 g; A1 6.94/9.25 × 100.
  4. (d) Use concentrated HCl in a fume cupboard / wear gloves (corrosive, gives off HCl fumes); keep the volatile, flammable product away from flames.
    B1.
