Q1Nitrogen oxides, pollution & catalytic converters · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which pollutant is removed from car exhaust gases by reduction in a catalytic converter?A carbon monoxideB unburned hydrocarbonsC nitrogen monoxideD carbon dioxide [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C nitrogen monoxide
    NO is reduced to N₂ (e.g. 2NO + 2CO → N₂ + 2CO₂). CO and hydrocarbons (A, B) are oxidised, not reduced; CO₂ (D) is not removed at all.
Q2Nitrogen oxides, pollution & catalytic converters · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Traffic on the main roads of Lahore releases oxides of nitrogen.

  1. (a) Explain why nitrogen monoxide, NO, is formed inside a car engine although nitrogen and oxygen do not react in the air at room temperature. [2]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the reaction of NO in the air to form NO₂. [1]
  3. (c) State one natural source of oxides of nitrogen. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The very high temperature in the engine (spark/combustion) provides enough energy to break the strong N≡N triple bond (overcome the high activation energy): N₂ + O₂ → 2NO.
    B1 high temperature in engine. B1 enough energy to break N≡N / overcome high Ea (equation accepted).
  2. (b) 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂
    B1
  3. (c) Lightning (or volcanic activity / bacterial action in soil).
    B1

Q7Nitrogen oxides, pollution & catalytic converters · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write two equations to show how NO₂ catalyses the oxidation of SO₂ in the atmosphere. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how your equations show that NO₂ acts as a catalyst. [1]
  3. (c) Give the oxidation number of nitrogen in NO₂ and in NO. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why this reaction makes the problem of acid rain worse. [1]

    B1 each.

    B1

Q8Nitrogen oxides, pollution & catalytic converters · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A rickshaw engine without a catalytic converter releases 1.50 m³ of exhaust gas (measured at room conditions) for each kilometre travelled. The exhaust contains 0.12 % of NO by volume.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of NO, in dm³, released per kilometre. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of NO released per kilometre. (molar gas volume 24.0 dm³ at room conditions; Mr NO = 30.0) [2]
  3. (c) If a catalytic converter were fitted, state the volume of CO needed to remove all of this NO and the volume of N₂ formed, both at room conditions. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.80 dm³
    1.50 m³ = 1500 dm³; 0.12 % of 1500 = 1.80 dm³
  2. (b) 2.25 g
    M1 mol NO = 1.80 ÷ 24.0 = 0.0750. A1 × 30.0 = 2.25 g
  3. (c) CO 1.80 dm³; N₂ 0.90 dm³
    2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂: CO : NO = 1 : 1 and N₂ : NO = 1 : 2.

Q12Nitrogen oxides, pollution & catalytic converters · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

N₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g) ΔH = +180 kJ mol⁻¹At 2000 K, Kc = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴.

  1. (a) Use Le Chatelier's principle to explain why more NO is present at the high temperatures inside an engine. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why changing the pressure has no effect on the position of this equilibrium. [1]
  3. (c) Write the expression for Kc and calculate the equilibrium concentration of NO at 2000 K when [N₂] = 4.8 × 10⁻³ and [O₂] = 1.2 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The forward reaction is endothermic, so increasing the temperature shifts the position of equilibrium to the right, increasing the yield of NO.
    M1 forward reaction endothermic. A1 equilibrium moves right at high T.
  2. (b) There are equal numbers of moles of gas on each side (2 and 2).
    B1
  3. (c) Kc = [NO]² ÷ ([N₂][O₂]); [NO] = 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³
    B1 expression. M1 [NO]² = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 4.8 × 10⁻³ × 1.2 × 10⁻³ = 2.30 × 10⁻⁹. A1 [NO] = 4.8 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³
Q13Nitrogen oxides, pollution & catalytic converters · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe, in terms of adsorption, how the metal catalyst in a catalytic converter speeds up the reaction between CO and NO. [3]
  2. (b) Some 'lean-burn' engines run with a large excess of air. Suggest why a normal catalytic converter removes NO less effectively from these engines. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why leaded petrol must not be used in cars with catalytic converters. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gas molecules are adsorbed onto the metal surface (weak bonds form to active sites); bonds within the molecules are weakened and the molecules are held close together in a favourable orientation, lowering Ea; the products (CO₂, N₂) are desorbed, freeing the sites.
    B1 adsorption onto surface/active sites. B1 bonds weakened / molecules held close, so lower activation energy. B1 desorption of products.
  2. (b) The excess O₂ oxidises CO and hydrocarbons on the catalyst, so little CO/hydrocarbon is left to reduce the NO to N₂.
    B1 O₂ reacts with CO/hydrocarbons. B1 so not enough reducing agent for NO.
  3. (c) Lead is adsorbed strongly onto the active sites and poisons the catalyst.
    B1

Q15Ammonia & ammonium ion · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe the bonding in the ammonium ion, NH₄⁺, and explain how it forms from NH₃ and H⁺. [2]
  2. (b) Give the H–N–H bond angle in NH₃ and in NH₄⁺, and explain the difference. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Four covalent N–H bonds; the fourth is a dative (coordinate) bond formed when the lone pair on N is donated to H⁺. All four bonds are then identical.
    B1 dative/coordinate bond. B1 lone pair on N donated to H⁺.
  2. (b) NH₃ 107°, NH₄⁺ 109.5°. NH₃ has a lone pair, which repels more strongly than bonding pairs, pushing the N–H bonds closer; NH₄⁺ has four bonding pairs only.
    B1 both angles. B1 lone pair–bond pair repulsion greater than bond pair–bond pair.

Q22Ammonia & ammonium ion · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Ammonia is made at a fertiliser plant near Daharki by the Haber process:N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹In one equilibrium mixture at a total pressure of 20.0 MPa the mole fractions are N₂ 0.20, H₂ 0.60 and NH₃ 0.20.

  1. (a) Write the expression for Kp and calculate its value, with units, to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the plant runs at about 450 °C even though a lower temperature gives a higher equilibrium yield. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kp = p(NH₃)² ÷ (p(N₂) × p(H₂)³) = 2.31 × 10⁻³ MPa⁻²

  2. (b) The forward reaction is exothermic so a low temperature favours yield, but the rate would be too slow; 450 °C is a compromise giving acceptable yield at a fast enough rate (with an iron catalyst).
    B1 lower T gives higher yield (exothermic). B1 rate too slow at low T; compromise.

The Thar coalfield in Sindh supplies power stations that burn lignite.

  1. (a) Explain how sulfur dioxide is released when coal is burned in a power station. [1]
  2. (b) Write two equations to show how SO₂ is converted into sulfuric acid in the atmosphere. [2]
  3. (c) State two harmful effects of acid rain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Coal contains sulfur compounds, which burn to form SO₂ (S + O₂ → SO₂).
    B1
  2. (b) SO₂ + ½O₂ → SO₃; SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄
    B1 each (accept SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃ then oxidation).
  3. (c) Any two: kills fish/aquatic life in lakes; damages trees/leaches nutrients from soil; corrodes limestone/marble buildings and metal structures; releases toxic Al³⁺ into water.
    1 mark each for any two.

  1. (a) Calculate the amount, in moles, of sulfur in 1.00 tonne (1.00 × 10⁶ g) of coal. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of SO₂, in m³ at room conditions, formed when 1.00 tonne of coal burns. (molar gas volume 24.0 dm³) [1]
  3. (c) The SO₂ is removed by powdered calcium carbonate: CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂. Calculate the mass of CaCO₃, in kg, needed per tonne of coal. (Mr CaCO₃ = 100.1) [2]
  4. (d) Suggest one disadvantage of this method of removing SO₂. [1]
Show answer

    1.8 % of 10⁶ g = 18 000 g; ÷ 32.1 = 561 mol
  2. (b) 13.5 m³
    560.7 × 24.0 dm³ = 13458 dm³ = 13.5 m³
  3. (c) 56.1 kg
    M1 mol CaCO₃ = mol SO₂ = 560.7. A1 × 100.1 = 56131 g = 56.1 kg
  4. (d) It releases CO₂ (a greenhouse gas) / limestone must be quarried / large amounts of solid waste.
    B1

Q27Sulfur dioxide & acid rain · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

To monitor sulfur dioxide near a brick kiln, 50.0 m³ of air is bubbled through excess hydrogen peroxide solution, which converts all the SO₂ to sulfuric acid. The solution needs 18.60 cm³ of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH for neutralisation. (Mr SO₂ = 64.1)

  1. (a) Write the equation for the reaction of SO₂ with H₂O₂ and give the change in oxidation number of sulfur. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of SO₂ in the air sample, in µg, to 3 significant figures. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the SO₂ concentration in µg m⁻³ and state whether it is above a guideline limit of 40 µg m⁻³ (24-hour average). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) SO₂ + H₂O₂ → H₂SO₄; S +4 → +6
    B1 equation. B1 +4 to +6.
  2. (b) 5960 µg (5.96 mg)
    M1 mol NaOH = 1.86 × 10⁻⁴. M1 mol H₂SO₄ = mol SO₂ = 9.30 × 10⁻⁵. A1 × 64.1 = 0.00596 g = 5960 µg
  3. (c) 119 µg m⁻³; above the limit
    5960 ÷ 50.0 = 119 µg m⁻³
Q28Nitrogen: unreactivity · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why nitrogen gas is very unreactive. [2]
  2. (b) Give one use of nitrogen that depends on its lack of reactivity. [1]
  3. (c) The bond energy of O=O is 496 kJ mol⁻¹ and that of N≡N is 944 kJ mol⁻¹. Oxygen is far more reactive than nitrogen. Use the bond energies to explain this difference. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The N≡N triple bond is very strong (high bond energy), and the molecule is non-polar, so it does not attract electrophiles or nucleophiles.
    B1 strong triple bond / high bond energy. B1 non-polar molecule.
  2. (b) Filling packets of food (e.g. crisps) to prevent oxidation / an inert atmosphere for welding or storing chemicals.
    B1
  3. (c) Much less energy is needed to break O=O than N≡N, so reactions of O₂ have lower activation energies.
    B1
Q29Nitrogen: unreactivity · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Bond energies / kJ mol⁻¹: N≡N 944; H–H 436; N–H 388

  1. (a) Use the bond energies to calculate the enthalpy change for N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. [3]
  2. (b) The reaction is exothermic, yet nitrogen and hydrogen do not react at room temperature. Explain this and state how industry overcomes it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) −76 kJ mol⁻¹
    M1 bonds broken: 944 + 3(436) = 2252. M1 bonds made: 6(388) = 2328. A1 2252 − 2328 = −76 kJ mol⁻¹
  2. (b) The activation energy is very high because the N≡N bond must be broken first; an iron catalyst (and a raised temperature) is used to give a reasonable rate.
    B1 high activation energy (N≡N). B1 iron catalyst / high temperature.

  2. (b) Give the oxidation number of each nitrogen atom in ammonium nitrate. [1]
  3. (c) In the factory, half of the ammonia is first converted into nitric acid, which then reacts with the rest of the ammonia. Calculate the maximum mass of ammonium nitrate, in tonnes, made from 1.00 tonne of ammonia. (Mr NH₃ = 17.0; Mr NH₄NO₃ = 80.0) [3]
