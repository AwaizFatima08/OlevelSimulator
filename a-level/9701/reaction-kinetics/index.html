Q1Rate equations & orders · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A and B react in solution. Initial rates were measured at constant temperature.Expt 1: [A] = 0.10, [B] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, rate = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Expt 2: [A] = 0.20, [B] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, rate = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Expt 3: [A] = 0.10, [B] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³, rate = 1.8 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

  1. (a) Deduce the order of reaction with respect to A and with respect to B. Explain your reasoning. [2]
  2. (b) Write the rate equation and calculate the rate constant, k, including its units. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the initial rate when [A] = 0.25 mol dm⁻³ and [B] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) First order in A; second order in B
    B1 expts 1→2: [A] doubles, rate doubles → first order in A; B1 expts 1→3: [B] ×3, rate ×9 → second order in B.
  2. (b) rate = k[A][B]²; k = 0.20 dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹
    B1 rate equation; M1 k = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ (0.10 × 0.10²) = 0.20; A1 units dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 8.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹
    B1 0.20 × 0.25 × 0.040².
Q2Rate equations & orders · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

For the reaction X + 2Y → Z, the rate equation is rate = k[X][Y]².

  1. (a) State the order of reaction with respect to X, with respect to Y, and overall. [2]
  2. (b) State the factor by which the rate changes when the concentrations of both X and Y are doubled. [1]
  3. (c) State the factor by which the rate changes if [X] is tripled and [Y] is halved. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) X: 1; Y: 2; overall 3
    B1 X and Y; B1 overall.
  2. (b) 8 times
    B1 2 × 2² = 8.
  3. (c) 0.75 (rate falls to three-quarters)
    B1 3 × (½)² = 0.75.
Q3Rate equations & orders · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Propanone reacts with iodine in acid solution:CH₃COCH₃ + I₂ → CH₃COCH₂I + HIThe rate equation is rate = k[CH₃COCH₃][H⁺]. With [CH₃COCH₃] = 0.40 mol dm⁻³, [H⁺] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³ and [I₂] = 0.0020 mol dm⁻³, the initial rate is 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.

  1. (a) State what the rate equation tells you about the role of iodine in the rate-determining step. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate constant and give its units. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the initial rate when [CH₃COCH₃] = 0.60 mol dm⁻³, [H⁺] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³ and [I₂] is doubled. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest how the rate of this reaction could be followed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Iodine is not involved in the rate-determining step (it reacts in a later, fast step).
    B1 zero order → I₂ not in / after the RDS.
  2. (b) k = 4.25 × 10⁻⁴ dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
    M1 k = 3.4 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (0.40 × 0.20) = 4.25 × 10⁻⁴; A1 units dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 2.55 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹
    M1 [I₂] has no effect; A1 4.25 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.60 × 0.10 = 2.55 × 10⁻⁵.
  4. (d) Use a colorimeter to measure the decrease in the brown/yellow iodine colour over time.
    B1 colorimetry (or titrating quenched samples with thiosulfate).
Q4Rate equations & orders · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give the units of the rate constant for a reaction that is (i) zero order overall (ii) first order overall (iii) second order overall. [3]
  2. (b) Describe the shape of a graph of concentration of reactant against time for a reaction that is zero order with respect to that reactant. [1]
  3. (c) Describe the shape of a graph of initial rate against concentration of a reactant for a reaction that is second order with respect to it. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ (ii) s⁻¹ (iii) dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) A straight line with negative gradient (constant rate).
    B1.
  3. (c) A curve upwards through the origin (rate ∝ [X]², a parabola).
    B1.
Q5Rate equations & orders · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Nitrogen monoxide reacts with oxygen: 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g). The rate equation is rate = k[NO]²[O₂].Expt 1: [NO] = 0.020, [O₂] = 0.010 mol dm⁻³, rate = 6.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹Expt 2: [NO] = 0.010, [O₂] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³, rate = XExpt 3: [NO] = Y, [O₂] = 0.010 mol dm⁻³, rate = 0.054 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

  1. (a) Calculate the rate constant, k, with units, using experiment 1. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the missing value X (rate in experiment 2). [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the missing value Y ([NO] in experiment 3). [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the rate equation suggests the reaction might occur in a single step, and why this is unlikely in practice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 1500 dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹
    M1 6.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ (0.020² × 0.010) = 1500; A1 units.
  2. (b) X = 0.0060 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹
    B1 1500 × 0.010² × 0.040.
  3. (c) Y = 0.060 mol dm⁻³
    M1 [NO]² = 0.054 ÷ (1500 × 0.010) = 0.00360; A1 [NO] = 0.060.
  4. (d) Rate eq matches the stoichiometry 2NO + O₂ (a termolecular collision); a three-particle collision with correct orientation and energy is very improbable.
    B1 orders match equation coefficients; B1 simultaneous collision of three particles very unlikely.
Q6Rate equations & orders · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 8 marks

A student investigates the reaction S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂ using an iodine clock. Each mixture contains 10.0 cm³ of K₂S₂O₈(aq), 5.0 cm³ of Na₂S₂O₃(aq), 1 cm³ of starch, a volume V of KI(aq) and enough water to make the total volume 26.0 cm³. The time t for the blue-black colour to appear is recorded.V / cm³: 10, 8, 6, 4, 2t / s: 22.0, 27.5, 36.7, 55.0, 110.0

  1. (a) Explain why the total volume of each mixture is kept the same. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate 1000/t for each experiment, giving values to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why 1/t can be used as a measure of the initial rate. [1]
  4. (d) Use your values to deduce the order of reaction with respect to iodide ions. Explain your answer. [2]
  5. (e) State two variables, other than total volume, that must be kept constant. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) So that the volume of KI solution is proportional to [I⁻] in the mixture.
    B1.
  2. (b) 1000/t = 45.5, 36.4, 27.2, 18.2, 9.09 s⁻¹
    B2 all five correct (B1 for three or four).
  3. (c) Each run stops when the same fixed amount of iodine has been formed (just enough to use up all the thiosulfate), so rate ∝ amount/t ∝ 1/t.
    B1 same (fixed) amount of I₂ formed/reacted before the blue-black colour appears in every run.
  4. (d) First order
    M1 1/t is directly proportional to volume of KI (e.g. halving volume halves 1/t; graph straight line through origin); A1 first order.
  5. (e) Temperature; concentration/volume of peroxodisulfate (and of thiosulfate and starch).
    1 mark each.
Q7Half-life & first-order kinetics · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

The decomposition of dinitrogen pentoxide, 2N₂O₅(g) → 4NO₂(g) + O₂(g), is first order with a constant half-life of 24 minutes at a certain temperature.

  1. (a) Calculate the fraction of the original N₂O₅ remaining after 96 minutes. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate constant, in min⁻¹, using k = 0.693/t½. [1]
  3. (c) State how the half-life would change if the initial concentration of N₂O₅ were doubled. Explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1/16
    B1 96/24 = 4 half-lives; (½)⁴.
  2. (b) 0.0289 min⁻¹
    B1 0.693/24.
  3. (c) No change – the half-life of a first-order reaction is independent of the starting concentration.
    B1.
Q8Half-life & first-order kinetics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Hydrogen peroxide decomposes in the presence of a catalyst. The concentration of H₂O₂ is measured over time.t / s: 0, 100, 200, 300, 400, 600[H₂O₂] / mol dm⁻³: 0.800, 0.566, 0.400, 0.283, 0.200, 0.100

  1. (a) Use the data to show that the reaction is first order with respect to H₂O₂. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate constant, k, using k = 0.693/t½, and give its units. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the initial rate of reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Successive half-lives are constant: 0.800→0.400 in 200 s, 0.400→0.200 in 200 s, 0.200→0.100 in 200 s.
    M1 two or more half-lives found from data; A1 they are equal (200 s), so first order.
  2. (b) 3.47 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹ (accept 3.46 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹)
    M1 k = 0.693/200 = 0.003465; A1 3.47 × 10⁻³ (0.00346–0.00347 accepted) with units s⁻¹.
  3. (c) 2.77 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ (accept 2.78 × 10⁻³)
    B1 rate = k[H₂O₂] = 0.003465 × 0.800 = 2.77 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.
Q9Half-life & first-order kinetics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

2-chloro-2-methylpropane is hydrolysed by water in a first-order reaction: rate = k[(CH₃)₃CCl], with k = 4.62 × 10⁻⁴ s⁻¹ at 25 °C.

  1. (a) Calculate the half-life of the reaction. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the time taken for the concentration of 2-chloro-2-methylpropane to fall to 12.5 % of its initial value. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the concentration of water does not appear in the rate equation, even though water is a reactant. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1500 s
    B1 0.693 ÷ 4.62 × 10⁻⁴.
  2. (b) 4500 s
    B1 three half-lives.
  3. (c) Water is the solvent and in large excess, so its concentration is effectively constant; and/or it is not involved in the slow step (SN1 – RDS is ionisation of the C–Cl bond).
    B1 water in large excess/constant; B1 RDS involves only the halogenoalkane.
Q10Half-life & first-order kinetics · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Ammonia decomposes on the surface of a hot tungsten wire: 2NH₃(g) → N₂(g) + 3H₂(g).t / s: 0, 100, 200, 300, 400[NH₃] / 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³: 2.00, 1.60, 1.20, 0.80, 0.40

  1. (a) Deduce the order of reaction with respect to ammonia. Explain your answer. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate constant and give its units. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the time for the first half-life and the time for the next half-life (from 1.00 × 10⁻³ to 0.50 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³). [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the rate does not depend on the concentration of ammonia in this experiment. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Zero order
