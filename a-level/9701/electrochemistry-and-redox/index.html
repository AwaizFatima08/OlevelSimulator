Q2Standard electrode potentials & cells · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A cell is made from a Zn²⁺(aq)/Zn half-cell and an Ag⁺(aq)/Ag half-cell under standard conditions.E°: Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn −0.76 V; Ag⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Ag +0.80 V

  1. (a) Calculate the standard cell potential, E°cell. [1]
  2. (b) State which electrode is the negative electrode and give the direction of electron flow in the external circuit. [2]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the overall cell reaction. [1]
  4. (d) The salt bridge is made from filter paper soaked in saturated potassium nitrate, not potassium chloride. Explain the purpose of the salt bridge and why KCl is not suitable here. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) +1.56 V
    E°cell = E°(right/positive) − E°(left/negative) = +0.80 − (−0.76) = +1.56 V
  2. (b) The zinc electrode is negative; electrons flow from zinc to silver through the wire.
    B1 zinc is negative (more negative E°). B1 electrons from Zn to Ag in the external circuit.
  3. (c) Zn + 2Ag⁺ → Zn²⁺ + 2Ag
    B1 balanced, correct species (2 Ag⁺ per Zn).
  4. (d) It completes the circuit by allowing ions to move between the half-cells (keeps each solution neutral); Cl⁻ would precipitate Ag⁺ as AgCl, lowering [Ag⁺] and changing the potential.
    B1 allows movement of ions / completes circuit without mixing solutions. B1 KCl gives AgCl precipitate with Ag⁺(aq).
Q3Standard electrode potentials & cells · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

E°: MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ ⇌ Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O +1.52 V; Cl₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2Cl⁻ +1.36 V

  1. (a) Use the data to calculate E°cell for the reaction between acidified MnO₄⁻ and Cl⁻ ions. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why dilute hydrochloric acid is not used to acidify potassium manganate(VII) in a titration with iron(II) ions. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why dilute sulfuric acid is suitable. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) +0.16 V; the reaction is feasible
    M1 E°cell = 1.52 − 1.36. A1 +0.16 V (positive, so feasible).
  2. (b) MnO₄⁻ would oxidise some Cl⁻ to Cl₂, so extra MnO₄⁻ is used and the titre is too large (toxic chlorine is also released).
    B1 Cl⁻ is oxidised by MnO₄⁻ (E°cell positive). B1 titre larger than it should be / results inaccurate.
  3. (c) SO₄²⁻ ions are not oxidised by MnO₄⁻ (sulfur is already in its highest oxidation state, +6).
    B1

    B1

  1. (a) Standard electrode potentials: Mg²⁺(aq)/Mg(s) E° = −2.37 V; Ag⁺(aq)/Ag(s) E° = +0.80 V. Calculate E°cell for a cell made from these two half-cells. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Write the overall equation for the spontaneous cell reaction. [1]
  3. (c) State the direction of electron flow in the external circuit. [1]
Show answer

  3. (c) From the magnesium electrode to the silver electrode
    Electrons leave the negative electrode (Mg) and travel through the wire to the positive electrode (Ag).

Q9Standard electrode potentials & cells · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

E°: H₂O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2H₂O +1.77 V; O₂ + 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ H₂O₂ +0.68 V

  1. (a) Use the data to show that hydrogen peroxide can disproportionate under standard conditions. Calculate E°cell. [2]
  2. (b) Write the equation for the disproportionation. [1]
  3. (c) A bottle of 6% hydrogen peroxide from a pharmacy can be kept for months. Explain why this does not contradict your answer to (a). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) H₂O₂ acts as both oxidising agent (+1.77 V couple) and reducing agent (+0.68 V couple): E°cell = 1.77 − 0.68 = +1.09 V, positive so feasible.
    M1 identifies both couples involving H₂O₂. A1 +1.09 V.
  2. (b) 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂
    B1
  3. (c) E° shows only that the reaction is energetically feasible; it says nothing about rate. The activation energy is high, so the reaction is very slow without a catalyst (e.g. MnO₂).
    B1 E° values give no information on rate/kinetics. B1 high activation energy / reaction very slow at room temperature.

Q12Standard electrode potentials & cells · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A solution of yellow VO₂⁺ ions in acid is reduced in steps.E°: VO₂⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ VO²⁺ + H₂O +1.00 V; VO²⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ V³⁺ + H₂O +0.34 V; V³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ V²⁺ −0.26 V; Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn −0.76 V; Sn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Sn −0.14 V

  1. (a) Explain, using E°cell values, why zinc reduces VO₂⁺ all the way to V²⁺. [2]
  2. (b) Tin metal is added instead of zinc. Deduce the final oxidation state of vanadium. Show your working. [3]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the reduction of VO²⁺ to V³⁺ by tin. [1]
Show answer
