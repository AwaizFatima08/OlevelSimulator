Q58Entropy changes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Ammonium nitrate decomposes: NH₄NO₃(s) → N₂O(g) + 2H₂O(g). ΔfH° (kJ mol⁻¹): NH₄NO₃(s) −365.6; N₂O(g) +82.1; H₂O(g) −241.8. S° (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): NH₄NO₃(s) 151.1; N₂O(g) 220.0; H₂O(g) 188.8. Calculate ΔH°, ΔS° and ΔG° at 298 K. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why this decomposition is feasible at all temperatures, and why it is explosive in practice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ΔH° = -35.9 kJ mol⁻¹; ΔS° = +446.5 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹; ΔG° = -169.0 kJ mol⁻¹
    1 mark for ΔH°; 1 mark for ΔS° = 446.5; 1 mark for ΔG° = −35.9 − 298 × 0.4465 = −169.0 kJ mol⁻¹.
  2. (b) ΔH° is negative and ΔS° is positive, so ΔG° = ΔH° − TΔS° is negative at every temperature; the reaction is feasible at all T. It is explosive in practice because a large volume of gas and heat is produced very rapidly once started.
    1 mark for ΔG° negative from both terms; 1 mark for the large gas formation and exothermic reaction causing explosion.
Q59Entropy changes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain, in terms of the sign of ΔS_total, why ΔG° < 0 corresponds to a feasible process, and why ΔG° = 0 at equilibrium. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) ΔG° = −TΔS_total (at constant T and P). ΔS_total > 0 gives ΔG° < 0, so the process is feasible. At equilibrium, ΔS_total = 0 and ΔG° = 0.
    1 mark for ΔG = −TΔS_total relation; 1 mark for positive ΔS_total giving negative ΔG; 1 mark for zero at equilibrium.
