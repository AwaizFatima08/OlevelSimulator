Q1Multi-step synthesis routes · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Ethanoic acid can be made from ethene in two steps via ethanol. Give the reagents and conditions for each step. [4]
  2. (b) State the colour change seen in step 2. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: steam with H₃PO₄ catalyst, about 300 °C and 6–7 MPa (high pressure). Step 2: acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄), heat under reflux
    B1 steam + acid catalyst (H₃PO₄). B1 high temperature and pressure. B1 K₂Cr₂O₇ + H⁺. B1 reflux (not distil).
  2. (b) orange to green
    B1.
Q2Multi-step synthesis routes · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which sequence of reagents converts bromoethane into ethanoic acid?A NaOH(aq), heat; then K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, heat under refluxB NaOH in ethanol, heat; then K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, distilC KCN in ethanol, heat; then dilute HCl, heat under refluxD NH₃ in ethanol, heat; then K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, heat under reflux [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A NaOH(aq), heat; then K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, heat under reflux
    Aqueous NaOH substitutes OH to give ethanol, which is fully oxidised under reflux. B gives ethene (elimination). C gives propanoic acid, with one more carbon – the main distractor.
Q3Multi-step synthesis routes · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) 1-bromobutane is converted into pentanoic acid in two steps. Give the reagents and conditions for each step and the structure of the intermediate. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why this route is useful to a chemist designing a synthesis. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: KCN in ethanol, heat under reflux → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN (pentanenitrile). Step 2: dilute HCl (or H₂SO₄), heat under reflux
    B1 step 1. B1 intermediate. B1 step 2.
  2. (b) It increases the length of the carbon chain by one carbon atom
    B1.
Q4Multi-step synthesis routes · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Benzoic acid can be made from benzene in two steps via methylbenzene. Give the reagents and conditions for each step. [4]
  2. (b) Name the type of reaction in step 1. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: CH₃Cl with AlCl₃ catalyst (anhydrous), warm. Step 2: hot alkaline KMnO₄, heat under reflux, then acidify with dilute H₂SO₄ (or HCl)
    B1 CH₃Cl. B1 AlCl₃ (anhydrous). B1 KMnO₄ hot, alkaline. B1 acidify afterwards.
  2. (b) electrophilic substitution (Friedel–Crafts alkylation)
    B1.
Q5Multi-step synthesis routes · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Propene is converted into propanone in two steps. Give the structure of the intermediate and the reagents and conditions for each step. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why propan-2-ol, not propan-1-ol, is the main product of step 1. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Intermediate propan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₃. Step 1: steam with H₃PO₄ catalyst (high temperature and pressure). Step 2: K₂Cr₂O₇ / dilute H₂SO₄, heat
    B1 propan-2-ol (Markovnikov product). B1 step 1. B1 step 2.
  2. (b) The secondary carbocation intermediate is more stable than the primary one, because of the electron-donating effect of two alkyl groups
    B1 secondary carbocation more stable. B1 inductive effect of two alkyl groups.
Q6Multi-step synthesis routes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Outline a three-step synthesis of 2-hydroxypropanoic acid, CH₃CH(OH)COOH, from ethanol. Give reagents, conditions and intermediates. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why ethanal must be distilled off as soon as it forms in step 1. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 ethanol → ethanal: K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, warm and distil off the aldehyde as it forms. 2 ethanal → CH₃CH(OH)CN: HCN with KCN catalyst. 3 hydrolysis: dilute HCl, heat under reflux
    B1 oxidation reagent. B1 distil (to stop further oxidation). B1 HCN/KCN. B1 intermediate 2-hydroxypropanenitrile. B1 acid hydrolysis under reflux.
  2. (b) Otherwise it is oxidised further to ethanoic acid
    B1.
Q7Multi-step synthesis routes · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A three-step synthesis has yields of 80 %, 65 % and 90 % for the individual steps. What is the overall percentage yield?A 46.8 %B 53.0 %C 65.0 %D 78.3 % [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 46.8 %
    Overall yield = 0.80 × 0.65 × 0.90 × 100 = 46.8 %. D is the mean of the three yields, which is not how yields combine.
Q8Multi-step synthesis routes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A student wants to make ethyl ethanoate using ethanol as the only organic starting material. Describe the two stages, giving reagents and conditions. [4]
  2. (b) The student starts with 9.20 g of ethanol, uses half for each stage, and the overall yield is 60.0 %. Calculate the mass of ethyl ethanoate (Mr 88.0) obtained. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stage 1: oxidise half the ethanol to ethanoic acid with excess K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, heat under reflux. Stage 2: heat the ethanoic acid with the rest of the ethanol and a few drops of concentrated H₂SO₄ under reflux (then distil)
    B1 split the ethanol. B1 oxidation under reflux with excess oxidant. B1 esterification with ethanol. B1 conc H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat.
  2. (b) 5.28 g
    M1 n(ethanol) = 9.20 ÷ 46.0 = 0.200 mol; 0.100 mol of each reactant → 0.100 mol ester at 100 %. A1 0.100 × 88.0 × 0.600 = 5.28 g.
Q9Multi-step synthesis routes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) 1-phenylethanol, C₆H₅CH(OH)CH₃, can be made from benzene in two steps. Give the reagents and conditions for each step and the intermediate. [4]
  2. (b) Name the type of reaction in each step. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: CH₃COCl with anhydrous AlCl₃ → phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃. Step 2: NaBH₄ (in water/methanol) or LiAlH₄ in dry ether
    B1 CH₃COCl. B1 AlCl₃. B1 intermediate C₆H₅COCH₃. B1 reducing agent.
  2. (b) step 1 electrophilic substitution (Friedel–Crafts acylation); step 2 reduction (nucleophilic addition)
    B1 each.
Q10Multi-step synthesis routes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) (Chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl, is converted into 2-phenylethylamine, C₆H₅CH₂CH₂NH₂, in two steps. Give the reagents and conditions for each step and the intermediate. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why chlorobenzene, C₆H₅Cl, could not be used instead in step 1. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: KCN in ethanol, heat under reflux → C₆H₅CH₂CN. Step 2: LiAlH₄ in dry ether (or H₂ / Ni)
    B1 step 1. B1 intermediate. B1 step 2.
  2. (b) In chlorobenzene the C–Cl bond is strengthened because a lone pair on Cl overlaps with the ring π system; the C–Cl bond is not broken by nucleophiles under these conditions
    B1 lone pair of Cl delocalised into ring. B1 C–Cl bond stronger / partial double-bond character – no nucleophilic substitution.

Q12Multi-step synthesis routes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) N-phenylethanamide, C₆H₅NHCOCH₃, is made from benzene in three steps. Give the reagents and conditions for each step and the intermediates. [5]
  2. (b) 7.80 g of benzene gave 8.10 g of N-phenylethanamide (Mr 135.0). Calculate the overall percentage yield. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 conc HNO₃ + conc H₂SO₄, below 55 °C → nitrobenzene. 2 Sn + conc HCl, reflux, then NaOH(aq) → phenylamine. 3 ethanoyl chloride, room temperature → N-phenylethanamide
    B1 nitration reagents. B1 nitrobenzene. B1 reduction reagents. B1 phenylamine. B1 CH₃COCl.
  2. (b) 60.0 %
    M1 n(benzene) = 7.80 ÷ 78.0 = 0.100 mol; theoretical mass = 13.5 g. A1 8.10 ÷ 13.5 × 100 = 60.0 %.
Q13Multi-step synthesis routes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) 4-aminobenzoic acid, H₂N–C₆H₄–COOH (1,4), is made from methylbenzene in three steps: nitration, oxidation and reduction. Give the reagents and conditions for each step. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the oxidation must be carried out before the reduction. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why nitration gives a mixture of products. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nitration: conc HNO₃ + conc H₂SO₄ (about 30 °C), separate the 4-isomer. Oxidation: hot alkaline KMnO₄ under reflux, then acidify. Reduction: Sn + conc HCl, reflux, then neutralise
    B1 nitration. B1 separating 4-nitromethylbenzene from the 2-isomer. B1 oxidation. B1 reduction.
  2. (b) If the NO₂ were reduced first, the NH₂ group would be oxidised / destroyed by KMnO₄
    B1 order. B1 amino group is oxidised by KMnO₄.
  3. (c) The CH₃ group directs substitution to the 2- and 4-positions
    B1.
Q14Multi-step synthesis routes · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Propanoic acid is to be made from ethene in three steps (no other organic reagent may be used). Give the reagents, conditions and intermediates for each step. [5]
  2. (b) Suggest one reason why the overall yield of a multi-step synthesis is often low. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 HBr(g), room temperature → bromoethane. 2 KCN in ethanol, heat under reflux → propanenitrile. 3 dilute HCl, heat under reflux → propanoic acid
    B1 HBr. B1 bromoethane. B1 KCN/ethanol/reflux. B1 propanenitrile. B1 acid hydrolysis under reflux.
  2. (b) Losses at each step (incomplete reactions, side reactions, losses in purification) multiply together
    B1.
Q15Multi-step synthesis routes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) 2-hydroxy-2-phenylethanoic acid, C₆H₅CH(OH)COOH, is made from benzaldehyde, C₆H₅CHO, in two steps. Give the reagents and conditions. [3]
  2. (b) 10.6 g of benzaldehyde (Mr 106.0) gave 9.12 g of the acid (Mr 152.0). Calculate the percentage yield. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) The acid is then heated with methanol and concentrated sulfuric acid. Give the structure of the product and state whether the product mixture would rotate plane-polarised light. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: HCN with KCN catalyst → C₆H₅CH(OH)CN. Step 2: dilute HCl, heat under reflux
    B1 step 1. B1 intermediate. B1 step 2.
  2. (b) 60.0 %
    M1 0.100 mol → theoretical 15.2 g. A1 9.12 ÷ 15.2 × 100 = 60.0 %.
  3. (c) C₆H₅CH(OH)COOCH₃; no – the acid was made as a racemic mixture, and the chiral centre is unchanged by esterification
    B1 structure. B1 no rotation – racemic.
Q16Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which reagent gives a positive result with propanal but not with propanone?A Tollens' reagentB 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazineC sodium carbonate solutionD sodium metal [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Tollens' reagent
    Aldehydes are oxidised by Tollens' reagent (silver mirror); ketones are not. B is the main distractor: 2,4-DNPH gives an orange precipitate with both aldehydes and ketones. Neither compound reacts with sodium carbonate or with sodium (no acidic or OH group).
Q17Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe a simple chemical test to distinguish ethanol from ethanoic acid. Give the result for each. [2]
  2. (b) Describe a chemical test to distinguish propan-2-ol from 2-methylpropan-2-ol. Give the result for each. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Add sodium carbonate solution (or solid): effervescence (CO₂) with ethanoic acid; no change with ethanol
    B1 reagent. B1 both results.
  2. (b) Warm with acidified potassium dichromate(VI): propan-2-ol turns it from orange to green; 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tertiary) – stays orange
    B1 reagent and warm. B1 orange → green for secondary. B1 no change for tertiary.
Q18Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe a test to distinguish hexane from hex-1-ene. Give the result for each. [2]
  2. (b) 1-chlorobutane and 1-iodobutane are each warmed with aqueous silver nitrate in ethanol. Describe and explain the differences seen. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shake with bromine water: hex-1-ene decolourises it (orange → colourless); hexane – no change (stays orange)
    B1 reagent. B1 results.
  2. (b) 1-iodobutane gives a yellow precipitate (AgI) quickly; 1-chlorobutane gives a white precipitate (AgCl) slowly; the C–I bond is weaker than C–Cl, so it is hydrolysed faster
    B1 colours. B1 iodo faster. B1 C–I bond weaker / lower bond energy.
Q19Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which compound gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-dinitrophenylhydrazine AND a yellow precipitate when warmed with alkaline aqueous iodine?A butanalB butanoneC ethanolD pentan-3-one [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B butanone
    Butanone is a carbonyl compound with a CH₃CO– group, so both tests are positive. C (ethanol) gives tri-iodomethane but does not react with 2,4-DNPH; D has no CH₃CO– group.
Q20Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Four unlabelled bottles contain propan-1-ol, propanal, propanone and propanoic acid. Describe a sequence of test-tube tests that would identify each liquid, giving the observations expected. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 Na₂CO₃(aq): only propanoic acid fizzes (CO₂ turns limewater milky). 2 2,4-DNPH on the other three: orange precipitate with propanal and propanone, none with propan-1-ol. 3 Tollens' reagent (warm) on the two carbonyls: silver mirror with propanal, no change with propanone. (Alternatively Fehling's: red-brown precipitate with propanal.)
    B1 test for acid. B1 result. B1 2,4-DNPH. B1 result separating the alcohol. B1 Tollens'/Fehling's. B1 result. Accept acidified dichromate for alcohol and aldehyde if the logic is complete.
Q21Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe a test to distinguish phenol from ethanol. Give the result for each. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how you could distinguish ethanoyl chloride from chloroethane using aqueous silver nitrate. Explain the difference. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Add bromine water: phenol decolourises it and gives a white precipitate (2,4,6-tribromophenol); ethanol – no change
    B1 reagent. B1 results (white precipitate and decolourisation with phenol; no change with ethanol).
  2. (b) Ethanoyl chloride gives an immediate white precipitate (AgCl) at room temperature (with steamy fumes as it hydrolyses); chloroethane gives a white precipitate only slowly on warming; the carbonyl carbon in the acyl chloride is very electron-deficient (bonded to O and Cl) so it is attacked rapidly by water
    B1 immediate ppt for acyl chloride. B1 slow / needs warming for chloroalkane. B1 reason.
Q22Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Compound X, C₄H₈O, gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH, does not react with Tollens' reagent, and gives a yellow precipitate with alkaline aqueous iodine. Identify X and explain each result. [4]
  2. (b) Give the structures of two isomers of X that would give a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent, and state what the yellow precipitate in (a) is. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) X is butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃. Orange ppt: carbonyl group. No Tollens' reaction: ketone, not aldehyde. Yellow ppt (CHI₃): CH₃CO– group present
    B1 butanone. B1 B1 B1 for each result explained.
  2. (b) CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO (butanal) and (CH₃)₂CHCHO (2-methylpropanal); tri-iodomethane, CHI₃
    B1 each isomer. B1 CHI₃.
Q23Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Four colourless liquids are ethanoyl chloride, ethanamide solution, ethyl ethanoate and ethanoic acid. Suggest simple tests that would identify each one. Give the observation for each test. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Add a few drops to water: ethanoyl chloride reacts violently, giving steamy fumes of HCl. Add Na₂CO₃(aq) to the rest: ethanoic acid effervesces. Warm the other two with NaOH(aq): ethanamide gives ammonia, which turns damp red litmus blue; ethyl ethanoate gives no ammonia (sweet smell disappears as it hydrolyses)
    B1 B1 acyl chloride test + observation. B1 B1 acid test + observation. B1 B1 amide test + observation; ester identified by elimination.
Q24Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol are three isomers of C₄H₁₀O. Describe how acidified potassium dichromate(VI), followed by a further test on the products, can distinguish all three. [4]
  2. (b) Give a single test, using a different reagent, that gives a positive result with butan-2-ol only. State the observation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Warm each with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ and distil: 2-methylpropan-2-ol – stays orange (tertiary). Butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol turn it green. Test the distillates with Tollens' reagent: butanal (from butan-1-ol) gives a silver mirror; butanone (from butan-2-ol) does not
    B1 tertiary not oxidised. B1 the other two turn green. B1 distil and test distillate with Tollens'/Fehling's. B1 correct results.
  2. (b) warm with alkaline aqueous iodine; yellow precipitate (tri-iodomethane) – butan-2-ol has a CH₃CH(OH)– group
    B1 reagent. B1 yellow ppt.
Q25Deducing structures from data · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Compound Y contains 54.5 % carbon, 9.1 % hydrogen and 36.4 % oxygen by mass. Calculate its empirical formula. [2]
  2. (b) The mass spectrum of Y has its molecular ion peak at m/e 88. Deduce the molecular formula. [1]
  3. (c) Y effervesces with aqueous sodium carbonate. Its IR spectrum has a very broad absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and a strong one at about 1710 cm⁻¹. Give the structures of the two possible compounds Y could be. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₂H₄O
    M1 C 4.54 : H 9.10 : O 2.27. A1 ÷ 2.27 → 2 : 4 : 1.
  2. (b) C₄H₈O₂
    B1 88 ÷ 44.0 = 2.
  3. (c) CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH (butanoic acid) or (CH₃)₂CHCOOH (2-methylpropanoic acid)
    B1 each.
Q26Deducing structures from data · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Alkene P, C₅H₁₀, is heated with hot, concentrated acidified KMnO₄. The products are propanone and ethanoic acid. Deduce the structure and name of P. [2]
  2. (b) P reacts with steam in the presence of H₃PO₄. The major product Q is not oxidised by acidified dichromate(VI). Give the structure of Q and explain why it is not oxidised. [2]
  3. (c) Does P show cis/trans isomerism? Explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃; 2-methylbut-2-ene
    B1 structure. B1 name.
  2. (b) (CH₃)₂C(OH)CH₂CH₃ (2-methylbutan-2-ol); tertiary alcohol – no H on the carbon bearing the OH
    B1 structure. B1 tertiary explanation.
  3. (c) No – one carbon of the C=C carries two identical CH₃ groups
    B1.
Q27Deducing structures from data · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Q, C₉H₁₀O₂, is a neutral, sweet-smelling liquid containing a benzene ring. When Q is heated under reflux with aqueous NaOH, it gives ethanol and R, C₇H₅O₂Na. Acidifying R gives a white solid S, C₇H₆O₂. Identify Q, R and S. [3]
  2. (b) Isomer T of Q is also an ester. Alkaline hydrolysis of T followed by acidification gives methanol and 4-methylbenzoic acid. Give the structure of T. [1]
  3. (c) Isomer U of Q is hydrolysed by NaOH(aq) to give sodium ethanoate and a compound that, on acidification, gives a white precipitate with bromine water. Suggest a structure for U and explain the bromine water result. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Q ethyl benzoate, C₆H₅COOCH₂CH₃; R sodium benzoate, C₆H₅COONa; S benzoic acid, C₆H₅COOH
    B1 each.
  2. (b) CH₃–C₆H₄–COOCH₃ (methyl 4-methylbenzoate)
    B1.
  3. (c) CH₃COO–C₆H₄–CH₃ (e.g. 4-methylphenyl ethanoate); hydrolysis forms a phenol (4-methylphenol), and phenols react with bromine water to give a white precipitate
    B1 ester of ethanoic acid with a methylphenol. B1 phenol + bromine water → white ppt.
Q28Deducing structures from data · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) 0.290 g of compound Z, which contains C, H and O only, is burned completely in oxygen, forming 0.660 g of CO₂ and 0.270 g of H₂O. Calculate the empirical formula of Z. [3]
  2. (b) The Mr of Z is 58. Z gives an orange precipitate with 2,4-DNPH, does not react with Fehling's solution, and gives a yellow precipitate with alkaline aqueous iodine. Identify Z. [2]
  3. (c) An isomer of Z gives a red-brown precipitate with Fehling's solution. Give its structure. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₃H₆O
    M1 mass C = 0.660 × 12.0/44.0 = 0.180 g; mass H = 0.270 × 2.0/18.0 = 0.0300 g. M1 mass O = 0.0800 g. A1 mol C 0.0150 : H 0.0300 : O 0.00500 = 3 : 6 : 1.
  2. (b) propanone, CH₃COCH₃
    B1 propanone. B1 reasoning: carbonyl, not aldehyde, CH₃CO– group.
  3. (c) CH₃CH₂CHO (propanal)
    B1.
Q29Functional group identification · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Compound V has the structure CH₃COCH₂CH(OH)CH₂CHO. Name the three functional groups present. [3]
  2. (b) Give the structure of the organic product when V is heated under reflux with excess acidified potassium dichromate(VI). [2]
  3. (c) Give the structure of the product when V reacts with excess NaBH₄. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ketone (C=O within chain); secondary alcohol (hydroxyl); aldehyde (CHO)
    B1 each; 'carbonyl' alone scores for only one of ketone/aldehyde.
  2. (b) CH₃COCH₂COCH₂COOH
    B1 secondary alcohol → ketone. B1 aldehyde → carboxylic acid (ketone unchanged).
  3. (c) CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₂OH
    B1 both carbonyl groups reduced.
Q30Functional group identification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Compound W, a drug intermediate, has the structure HO–C₆H₄–CH₂–CH(NH₂)–COOCH₃ (the OH is attached directly to the benzene ring). Name the three functional groups in W, other than the benzene ring. [3]
  2. (b) Give the structure of the organic product formed when W is heated with excess aqueous sodium hydroxide. [2]
  3. (c) Give the structure of the organic product formed when W reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid at room temperature. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) phenol; primary amine; ester
    B1 each.
  2. (b) ⁻O–C₆H₄–CH₂–CH(NH₂)–COO⁻ (with Na⁺ ions) and methanol
    B1 ester hydrolysed to carboxylate (+ CH₃OH). B1 phenol deprotonated to phenoxide.
  3. (c) HO–C₆H₄–CH₂–CH(NH₃⁺)–COOCH₃ Cl⁻
    B1 amine protonated only.
