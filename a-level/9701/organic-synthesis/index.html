  2. (b) State the colour change seen in step 2. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Step 1: steam with H₃PO₄ catalyst, about 300 °C and 6–7 MPa (high pressure). Step 2: acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄), heat under reflux
    B1 steam + acid catalyst (H₃PO₄). B1 high temperature and pressure. B1 K₂Cr₂O₇ + H⁺. B1 reflux (not distil).
  2. (b) orange to green
    B1.

Q8Multi-step synthesis routes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A student wants to make ethyl ethanoate using ethanol as the only organic starting material. Describe the two stages, giving reagents and conditions. [4]
  2. (b) The student starts with 9.20 g of ethanol, uses half for each stage, and the overall yield is 60.0 %. Calculate the mass of ethyl ethanoate (Mr 88.0) obtained. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stage 1: oxidise half the ethanol to ethanoic acid with excess K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄, heat under reflux. Stage 2: heat the ethanoic acid with the rest of the ethanol and a few drops of concentrated H₂SO₄ under reflux (then distil)
    B1 split the ethanol. B1 oxidation under reflux with excess oxidant. B1 esterification with ethanol. B1 conc H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat.
  2. (b) 5.28 g

    B1.

Q16Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark

Q24Distinguishing tests for functional groups · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which compound reacts with sodium metal but does NOT react with aqueous sodium hydroxide? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B ethanol
    Ethanol has an O–H group so it gives hydrogen with sodium, but it is too weak an acid to react with NaOH(aq). Phenol and ethanoic acid react with both. Ethyl ethanoate has no O–H, so it does not react with sodium (it is hydrolysed by NaOH).

  2. (b) Give the structures of the products when ibuprofen reacts with (i) ethanol and concentrated sulfuric acid, heated, and (ii) LiAlH₄ in dry ether. [2]

    B1 alkene unchanged.

Q59Functional group identification · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Aspirin is 2-ethanoyloxybenzoic acid: a benzene ring with –COOH on carbon 1 and –OCOCH₃ on carbon 2.

  1. (a) Name the functional groups in aspirin. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why one mole of aspirin reacts with three moles of hot aqueous sodium hydroxide. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the volume of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ NaOH needed to react completely with 0.360 g of aspirin when heated. (Mr 180.2) Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Carboxylic acid; ester (and benzene ring/arene)
    B1 each of acid and ester.
  2. (b) One mole for the COOH group; the ester is hydrolysed, giving ethanoate and a phenol OH, and the phenol then reacts with a further mole of NaOH (so ester hydrolysis + phenol uses two moles).
    M1 COOH uses 1. M1 ester hydrolysis gives ethanoate and phenol, phenol also neutralised.
  3. (c) 30.0 cm³
    M1 0.360 ÷ 180.2 = 1.998 × 10⁻³ mol × 3 = 5.99 × 10⁻³ mol. A1 ÷ 0.200 = 30.0 cm³.
Q60Functional group identification · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) 4-aminophenol, H₂N–C₆H₄–OH, is converted into paracetamol by reaction with ethanoyl chloride. Name the two functional groups in 4-aminophenol and the new functional group formed. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why, with one mole of ethanoyl chloride, the –NH₂ group reacts rather than the –OH group. [1]
  3. (c) Give the structure of the product formed if excess ethanoyl chloride is used. [1]
  4. (d) Give the formulae of the products when paracetamol, HO–C₆H₄–NHCOCH₃, is heated with dilute hydrochloric acid. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Amine (primary aromatic amine); phenol; amide formed
    B1 each.
  2. (b) The nitrogen lone pair is a better nucleophile (N less electronegative than O, lone pair more available), so the amine reacts faster.
    B1
  3. (c) CH₃COO–C₆H₄–NHCOCH₃ (both groups acylated: ester and amide)
    B1
  4. (d) HO–C₆H₄–NH₃⁺ Cl⁻ and CH₃COOH
    B1 each.
