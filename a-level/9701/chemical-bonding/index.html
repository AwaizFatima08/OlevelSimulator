Q1Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State the type of hybridisation of each carbon atom in ethene, C₂H₄, and the H–C–H bond angle. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the σ bond and the π bond between the two carbon atoms in ethene are formed. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why there is no free rotation about the C=C bond in ethene. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) sp²; 120°
    B1 sp²; B1 120° (accept 117–121°).
  2. (b) σ: end-on (head-on) overlap of sp² hybrid orbitals, electron density on the internuclear axis. π: sideways overlap of the unhybridised p orbitals, electron density above and below the plane of the molecule.
    B1 σ by end-on/axial overlap (of sp² orbitals); B1 π by sideways overlap of p orbitals; B1 π electron density above and below the plane / not on the axis.
  3. (c) Rotation would break the sideways overlap of the p orbitals (the π bond).
    B1 rotation would destroy the p-orbital overlap / break the π bond.
Q2Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In which molecule is a carbon atom sp hybridised? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A CO₂
    In CO₂ the carbon forms two double bonds and no lone pairs, two regions of electron density, linear → sp. CH₃CHO has sp² (C=O carbon) and sp³ carbons but no sp carbon; C₂H₄ is sp² only; CH₃OH is sp³.
Q3Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement about a π bond between two carbon atoms is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B It is formed by sideways overlap of two p orbitals, with electron density above and below the bond axis.
    A π bond forms by sideways overlap of unhybridised p orbitals; its electron density lies above and below the internuclear axis. A describes a σ bond (end-on overlap). The π bond is weaker than the σ bond (less overlap), and it prevents rotation, so C and D are wrong.
Q4Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Methanal, H₂C=O, is used to make resins for plywood. State the hybridisation of the carbon atom in methanal and the approximate H–C–H bond angle. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the σ bond and the π bond between the carbon atom and the oxygen atom are formed. [2]
  3. (c) State the number of σ bonds and the number of π bonds in one molecule of methanal. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) sp²; about 120°
    B1 sp²; B1 120° (trigonal planar: three bonding regions, no lone pair on C).
  2. (b) σ: end-on (head-on) overlap of an sp² orbital of C with an orbital of O; π: sideways overlap of the unhybridised p orbital on C with a p orbital on O.
    B1 σ bond = end-on/axial overlap of orbitals along the C–O axis; B1 π bond = sideways overlap of p orbitals, electron density above and below the axis.
  3. (c) 3 σ bonds and 1 π bond
    Two C–H σ + one C=O σ = 3 σ; the second bond of C=O is the π bond.
Q5Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Buta-1,3-diene, CH₂=CH–CH=CH₂, is used to make synthetic rubber for car tyres. Calculate the number of σ bonds and the number of π bonds in one molecule. [2]
  2. (b) State the hybridisation of each carbon atom in buta-1,3-diene. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest the approximate H–C–H bond angle at a terminal CH₂ group. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9 σ bonds; 2 π bonds
    C–H bonds: 6 (σ). C–C bonds: 3 σ (one in each C=C plus the central C–C). Total σ = 9 (B1). Each C=C has one π: 2 π (B1).
  2. (b) All four carbon atoms are sp²
    Each carbon forms three σ bonds and one π bond, so sp² (B1).
  3. (c) about 120°
    Three regions of electron density around an sp² carbon: trigonal planar, 120°.
Q6Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Propenenitrile, CH₂=CH–C≡N, is used to make acrylic fibres for winter shawls. How many σ bonds and how many π bonds are there in one molecule of propenenitrile? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 6 σ and 3 π
    σ bonds: 3 C–H, 1 C–C and one σ in each of C=C and C≡N → 6. π bonds: 1 in C=C + 2 in C≡N → 3. Option C counts every line of every multiple bond as σ; a multiple bond contains only one σ bond.
Q7Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give the hybridisation of each carbon atom, C1 to C5, in pent-3-en-1-yne, HC≡C–CH=CH–CH₃ (C1 is the carbon of HC≡, C5 is the CH₃ carbon). [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the total number of σ bonds and of π bonds in one molecule. [2]
  3. (c) State the bond angle around C2 and around C5. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C1 sp, C2 sp, C3 sp², C4 sp², C5 sp³
    B1 C1 and C2 sp; B1 C3 and C4 sp²; B1 C5 sp³.
  2. (b) 10 σ bonds and 3 π bonds
    6 C–H σ bonds + 4 C–C σ bonds (one in each carbon–carbon link) = 10 σ (B1). π: 2 in C≡C + 1 in C=C = 3 (B1).
  3. (c) C2 180°; C5 about 109.5°
    B1 both.
Q8Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In which molecule are ALL the carbon atoms sp² hybridised? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A benzene, C₆H₆
    Each carbon in benzene forms three σ bonds (two C–C, one C–H) and uses its remaining p orbital in the delocalised π system, so all six are sp². Propene is the main distractor: its CH₃ carbon is sp³. Cyclohexane carbons are all sp³; ethyne carbons are sp.
Q9Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Urea, (NH₂)₂CO, is made in Pakistan on a large scale as a nitrogen fertiliser. Its structure has a C=O group with each NH₂ group joined to the carbon atom. Calculate the number of σ bonds and π bonds in one urea molecule. [2]
  2. (b) State the hybridisation of the carbon atom and the approximate O=C–N bond angle. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbon atom and the three atoms bonded to it lie in one plane. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7 σ bonds; 1 π bond
    4 N–H + 2 C–N + 1 C=O σ = 7 σ (B1); 1 π in C=O (B1).
  2. (b) sp²; about 120°
    B1 sp² (three σ bonds, one π); B1 about 120° (trigonal planar).
  3. (c) The three sp² hybrid orbitals of carbon lie in one plane at 120° and point towards O and the two N atoms.
    B1 the three sp² orbitals are coplanar (trigonal planar arrangement); the unhybridised p orbital is perpendicular to this plane and is used in the π bond.
Q10Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Vinegar contains ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH. State the hybridisation of the carbon atom in the CH₃ group and of the carbon atom in the COOH group. [2]
  2. (b) Give the approximate H–C–H bond angle in the CH₃ group and the O=C–O bond angle in the COOH group. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the total number of σ bonds and π bonds in one molecule of ethanoic acid. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃ carbon: sp³; COOH carbon: sp²
    B1 sp³ (four σ bonds); B1 sp² (three σ bonds and one π bond).
  2. (b) about 109.5°; about 120°
    B1 tetrahedral 109.5°; B1 trigonal planar 120°.
  3. (c) 7 σ; 1 π
    3 C–H, 1 C–C, 1 C=O σ, 1 C–O, 1 O–H = 7 σ (B1); 1 π in C=O (B1).
Q11Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Average bond energies (kJ/mol): C–C 350; C=C 610; C≡C 840.

  1. (a) Use the data to estimate the energy of the π bond in C=C, and the average energy of each π bond in C≡C. [2]
  2. (b) Explain, in terms of orbital overlap, why a π bond is weaker than a σ bond. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why alkenes are much more reactive than alkanes even though the C=C bond energy is greater than the C–C bond energy. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) π in C=C ≈ 260 kJ/mol; each π in C≡C ≈ 245 kJ/mol
    M1 subtract σ (C–C) energy: 610 − 350 = 260; A1 (840 − 350)/2 = 245 kJ/mol.
  2. (b) Sideways overlap of p orbitals is less effective than end-on overlap, so electron density between the nuclei is lower and the electrons are less strongly attracted by both nuclei.
    B1 sideways overlap is smaller/less effective than end-on; B1 electron density lies away from the internuclear axis, so weaker attraction to the nuclei.
  3. (c) Only the weaker π bond needs to be broken in addition reactions; the exposed π electron density above and below the plane attracts electrophiles.
    B1 only the π bond (≈260 kJ/mol) breaks; B1 π electrons are exposed/high electron density so electrophiles attack.
Q12Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the bonding between the two carbon atoms in ethyne, C₂H₂, in terms of hybrid and unhybridised orbitals. State the H–C–C bond angle. [3]
  2. (b) The C–C bond lengths in ethane, ethene and ethyne are 0.154 nm, 0.134 nm and 0.120 nm. Explain this trend. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each C is sp hybridised; one σ bond from end-on overlap of sp orbitals; two π bonds from sideways overlap of the two pairs of unhybridised p orbitals, at right angles to each other; 180°.
    B1 sp hybridisation and σ from sp–sp overlap; B1 two π bonds from two sets of p orbitals at 90° to each other; B1 180°.
  2. (b) More shared electron pairs between the carbons (1, 2, 3) → greater electron density between the nuclei → nuclei pulled closer; also more s character in the hybrid orbitals (sp³ → sp² → sp).
    B1 bond order increases / more electrons shared between nuclei; B1 stronger attraction pulls nuclei closer (credit increasing s character).
Q13Sigma & pi bonds / hybridisation · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Propadiene (allene), H₂C=C=CH₂, has two C=C bonds that share the central carbon atom. State the hybridisation of the central carbon atom and of each end carbon atom. [2]
