Q1Proton (1H) NMR · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

¹H chemical shifts (δ/ppm): R–CH₃, R–CH₂–R 0.9–1.7; H–C–C=O or H–C attached to a benzene ring 2.0–3.0; H–C–O (alcohols, ethers, esters) 3.3–4.8; H–C–Cl 3.0–4.2; R–OH 0.5–6.0 (variable); benzene ring H 6.0–9.0; –CHO 9.3–10.5; –COOH 9.0–13.0.

  1. (a) Ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃, is the fruity solvent in nail-polish remover. Predict its ¹H NMR spectrum: for each peak give the approximate δ, the relative number of protons and the splitting pattern. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the splitting of the CH₂ peak. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃–C=O: δ ≈ 2.0, 3H, singlet; O–CH₂: δ ≈ 4.1, 2H, quartet; CH₃ of ethyl: δ ≈ 1.2, 3H, triplet
    B1 each peak with δ range, integration and splitting.
  2. (b) It is next to a CH₃ group with 3 H; n + 1 rule → 4 lines (quartet).
    B1.
Q2Proton (1H) NMR · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State why tetramethylsilane, Si(CH₃)₄, is used as the reference in ¹H NMR. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why samples are dissolved in CDCl₃ rather than CHCl₃. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) All 12 H are equivalent giving one strong sharp peak, defined as δ = 0, upfield of most organic protons; it is inert, non-toxic and volatile (easily removed).
    B1 single peak at δ 0 (12 equivalent H); B1 any one property: inert / volatile / non-toxic.
  2. (b) CDCl₃ contains no ¹H, so the solvent gives no peak that would interfere.
    B1.

Q4Proton (1H) NMR · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

¹H chemical shifts (δ/ppm): R–CH₃, R–CH₂–R 0.9–1.7; H–C–C=O or H–C attached to a benzene ring 2.0–3.0; H–C–O (alcohols, ethers, esters) 3.3–4.8; H–C–Cl 3.0–4.2; R–OH 0.5–6.0 (variable); benzene ring H 6.0–9.0; –CHO 9.3–10.5; –COOH 9.0–13.0.

  1. (a) Compound K, C₃H₆O, shows a single peak at δ 2.1 in its ¹H NMR spectrum. Identify K and explain. [2]
  2. (b) Isomer L shows three peaks with relative areas 1 : 2 : 3; the 1H peak is at δ 9.8. Identify L. [1]
  3. (c) Predict the splitting of the CH₃ peak of L. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Propanone, CH₃COCH₃: both CH₃ groups are equivalent, next to C=O (δ 2.0–3.0), and have no H on the adjacent carbon so the peak is a singlet.
    B1 propanone; B1 one environment, singlet, δ consistent with H–C–C=O.
  2. (b) Propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO
    B1.
  3. (c) Triplet (next to CH₂)
    B1.
Q5Proton (1H) NMR · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The ¹H NMR spectrum of ethanol shows peaks at δ 1.2 (3H), δ 2.6 (1H) and δ 3.7 (2H). Assign each peak and give its splitting pattern. [3]
  2. (b) A few drops of D₂O are added and the spectrum is run again. State and explain the change. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the O–H peak is not split, and does not split the CH₂ peak. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) δ 1.2 CH₃ triplet; δ 2.6 O–H singlet; δ 3.7 CH₂ (H–C–O) quartet
    B1 each.
  2. (b) The δ 2.6 peak disappears: the O–H proton exchanges with deuterium (CH₃CH₂OH + D₂O ⇌ CH₃CH₂OD + HOD) and D does not absorb in this region.
    B1 OH peak disappears; B1 exchange with D, which gives no ¹H signal.
  3. (c) The O–H proton exchanges rapidly between molecules, so no coupling is seen.
    B1.
Q6Proton (1H) NMR · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

¹H chemical shifts (δ/ppm): R–CH₃, R–CH₂–R 0.9–1.7; H–C–C=O or H–C attached to a benzene ring 2.0–3.0; H–C–O (alcohols, ethers, esters) 3.3–4.8; H–C–Cl 3.0–4.2; R–OH 0.5–6.0 (variable); benzene ring H 6.0–9.0; –CHO 9.3–10.5; –COOH 9.0–13.0.

  1. (a) Ester N, C₄H₈O₂, has peaks at δ 1.1 (triplet, 3H), δ 2.3 (quartet, 2H) and δ 3.7 (singlet, 3H). Identify N, explaining each peak. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why ethyl ethanoate, also C₄H₈O₂, would give a quartet at a different δ value. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Methyl propanoate, CH₃CH₂COOCH₃. δ 3.7 singlet 3H: CH₃–O with no neighbouring H. δ 2.3 quartet 2H: CH₂ next to C=O and next to CH₃. δ 1.1 triplet 3H: CH₃ next to CH₂.
    B1 identity; B1 each peak assigned (×3).
  2. (b) In ethyl ethanoate the CH₂ is bonded to O (H–C–O, δ ≈ 4.1), not to C=O.
    B1.

Q8Proton (1H) NMR · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

¹H chemical shifts (δ/ppm): R–CH₃, R–CH₂–R 0.9–1.7; H–C–C=O or H–C attached to a benzene ring 2.0–3.0; H–C–O (alcohols, ethers, esters) 3.3–4.8; H–C–Cl 3.0–4.2; R–OH 0.5–6.0 (variable); benzene ring H 6.0–9.0; –CHO 9.3–10.5; –COOH 9.0–13.0.The integration trace of a ¹H NMR spectrum gives step heights of 2.4 cm (δ 2.1), 1.6 cm (δ 2.5) and 2.4 cm (δ 1.1).

    B1 three peaks; B1 splittings.

Q11Proton (1H) NMR · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

    B1 identity; B1 COOH; B1 para-disubstituted ring from two 2H peaks; B1 CH₃ on ring.

Q12Mass spectrometry · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) The mass spectrum of an organic compound has two molecular ion peaks, at m/e 122 and 124, of almost equal height. Which element does the compound contain?A bromineB chlorineC sulfurD nitrogen [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A bromine
    ⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br occur in about a 1 : 1 ratio, giving M and M+2 peaks of equal height. Chlorine (³⁵Cl : ³⁷Cl ≈ 3 : 1) would give M+2 one-third the height of M.
Q13Mass spectrometry · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Gallium, used in LED lights, has isotopes ⁶⁹Ga (60.1 %) and ⁷¹Ga (39.9 %). Calculate the relative atomic mass of gallium to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) The mass spectrum of gallium shows peaks at m/e 69 and 71. Give the ratio of their heights as a ratio of small whole numbers. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 69.8
    M1 (69 × 60.1 + 71 × 39.9)/100; A1 69.8.
  2. (b) 3 : 2
    B1 60.1 : 39.9 = 1.51 : 1 ≈ 3 : 2.
Q14Mass spectrometry · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) In the mass spectrum of a hydrocarbon the molecular ion peak has a relative height of 42.0 and the M+1 peak has a relative height of 1.85. How many carbon atoms are in one molecule?A 4B 2C 3D 5 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 4
    n = (100 × 1.85)/(1.1 × 42.0) = 4.00 ≈ 4. Three carbons would give an M+1 height of only about 1.39, and five about 2.31.

  1. (a) Butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃, gives peaks at m/e 72, 57, 43 and 29. Give the formula of the ion responsible for each peak. [4]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the fragmentation of the molecular ion that gives the peak at m/e 43. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 72: [CH₃COCH₂CH₃]⁺ (M⁺); 57: CH₃CH₂CO⁺; 43: CH₃CO⁺; 29: CH₃CH₂⁺
    B1 each (ions must show + charge).
  2. (b) [CH₃COCH₂CH₃]⁺ → CH₃CO⁺ + •CH₂CH₃
    B1.
Q16Mass spectrometry · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) The mass spectrum of ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH, has a large peak at m/e 31. Which ion causes it?A CH₂OH⁺B CH₃CH₂⁺C CH₃O⁻D C₂H₇⁺ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A CH₂OH⁺
    Breaking the C–C bond of the molecular ion gives CH₂OH⁺ (12 + 2 + 16 + 1 = 31). CH₃CH₂⁺ has m/e 29; CH₃O⁻ is a negative ion, which is not detected. D also has m/e 31 but would need 7 H atoms; ethanol has only 6, so it cannot form.
Q17Mass spectrometry · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Give the m/e values of the three molecular ion peaks of dichloromethane, CH₂Cl₂. (C = 12, H = 1, Cl = 35 or 37) [1]
  2. (b) Show that the relative heights of these peaks are 9 : 6 : 1, given that ³⁵Cl : ³⁷Cl = 3 : 1. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 84, 86, 88
    B1.
  2. (b) P(35,35) = 9/16; P(35,37 or 37,35) = 2 × 3/16 = 6/16; P(37,37) = 1/16 → 9 : 6 : 1
    M1 (3/4)² = 9/16; M1 2 × (3/4)(1/4) = 6/16; A1 (1/4)² = 1/16 → 9 : 6 : 1.
Q18Mass spectrometry · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A compound contains carbon, hydrogen and bromine only. Its mass spectrum shows peaks at m/e 136 and 138 of equal height (relative height 100 each), and an M+1 peak at m/e 137 of relative height 4.4.

  1. (a) Explain what the two molecular ion peaks of equal height tell you. [1]
  2. (b) Use the M+1 peak to calculate the number of carbon atoms. [1]
  3. (c) Deduce the molecular formula, showing that it fits m/e 136. [2]
  4. (d) A large peak at m/e 57 is present. Identify the ion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The compound contains one bromine atom (⁷⁹Br and ⁸¹Br, 1 : 1).
    B1.
  2. (b) 4
    B1 n = 100 × 4.4/(1.1 × 100) = 4.0 → 4.
  3. (c) C₄H₉Br: 4 × 12 + 9 × 1 + 79 = 136 (and 138 with ⁸¹Br)
    M1 C₄ + Br = 127, so 9 H; A1 C₄H₉Br.
  4. (d) C₄H₉⁺
    B1.

IR absorption ranges (cm⁻¹): O–H in alcohols 3200–3600 (broad); O–H in carboxylic acids 2500–3000 (very broad); C–H 2850–2950; C=O 1670–1740; C=C 1620–1680; C–O 1040–1300.

Q21Infrared spectroscopy · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

IR absorption ranges (cm⁻¹): O–H in alcohols 3200–3600 (broad); O–H in carboxylic acids 2500–3000 (very broad); C–H 2850–2950; C=O 1670–1740; C=C 1620–1680; C–O 1040–1300.

  1. (a) Ethanol is heated under reflux with acidified potassium dichromate(VI). Describe how the IR spectrum of the organic product differs from that of ethanol. [3]
  2. (b) If the product is instead distilled off as it forms, ethanal is obtained. State how the IR spectrum of ethanal differs from that of ethanoic acid. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Product ethanoic acid: new strong C=O absorption at 1670–1740 cm⁻¹; the broad alcohol O–H at 3200–3600 is replaced by a very broad acid O–H at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹.
    B1 product named; B1 C=O appears; B1 O–H band changes to 2500–3000.
  2. (b) No broad O–H absorption at all (C=O still present).
    B1.
Q22Infrared spectroscopy · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain what happens at the molecular level when a molecule absorbs infrared radiation. [2]
  2. (b) Carbon dioxide and methane are greenhouse gases but nitrogen is not. Suggest why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bonds vibrate (stretch/bend) at particular frequencies; radiation of matching frequency is absorbed, increasing the vibrational energy, provided the vibration changes the dipole moment.
    B1 bond vibration energy increases; B1 specific frequencies / change in dipole.
  2. (b) CO₂ and CH₄ have polar bonds whose vibrations change the dipole, so they absorb IR emitted by the Earth. N₂ is a non-polar diatomic; its vibration causes no dipole change.
    B1 CO₂/CH₄ polar bonds, dipole change, absorb IR; B1 N≡N non-polar, no dipole change.
Q23Infrared spectroscopy · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

IR absorption ranges (cm⁻¹): O–H in alcohols 3200–3600 (broad); O–H in carboxylic acids 2500–3000 (very broad); C–H 2850–2950; C=O 1670–1740; C=C 1620–1680; C–O 1040–1300.

  1. (a) Compound R, C₃H₆O₂, shows a strong absorption at 1740 cm⁻¹ and an absorption at 1200 cm⁻¹, but no broad absorption above 2500 cm⁻¹ except C–H near 2900 cm⁻¹. Identify the functional group and give two possible structures. [3]
  2. (b) Propanoic acid is also C₃H₆O₂. State how its IR spectrum differs from that of R. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest a different technique that could distinguish the two esters in (a). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ester (C=O and C–O, no O–H). Methyl ethanoate CH₃COOCH₃ or ethyl methanoate HCOOCH₂CH₃
    B1 ester with evidence; B1 each structure (max 2).
  2. (b) Very broad O–H band at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹.
    B1.
  3. (c) ¹H NMR (or mass spectrometry fragments).
    B1.
Q24Chromatography (TLC, GC) · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A food-safety officer in Karachi runs a TLC plate of a red sauce colouring. The solvent front moves 8.0 cm and the red spot moves 3.2 cm from the baseline.

  1. (a) Calculate the Rf value of the red dye. [1]
  2. (b) The permitted dye E129 has Rf 0.40 and the banned dye Sudan I has Rf 0.70 in this solvent. Calculate the distance Sudan I would travel and state which dye is present. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the result should be confirmed with a second solvent. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.40
    B1 3.2/8.0.
  2. (b) 5.6 cm; the dye is E129 (Rf matches 0.40)
    B1 0.70 × 8.0 = 5.6 cm; B1 E129.
  3. (c) Two different dyes could have the same Rf in one solvent.
    B1.
Q25Chromatography (TLC, GC) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) In TLC, name the stationary phase and the mobile phase, and explain how the components of a mixture separate. [3]
  2. (b) Amino acids are colourless. Describe how their spots can be made visible. [1]
  3. (c) Why must the baseline be drawn in pencil and be above the solvent level? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stationary: thin layer of silica (or alumina) on a plate; mobile: solvent. Components have different affinities: those more strongly adsorbed on the polar stationary phase move more slowly; those more soluble in the solvent move further.
    B1 phases; B1 different adsorption/affinity for stationary phase; B1 different solubility in solvent → different distances.
  2. (b) Spray with ninhydrin (and warm) or use a UV lamp/iodine.
    B1.
  3. (c) Ink would dissolve and separate; spots below the solvent would dissolve into the solvent instead of moving up.
    B1 either reason.
Q26Chromatography (TLC, GC) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A fruit-flavouring mixture is analysed by gas chromatography. Assume peak area is proportional to the amount of each component.ethyl ethanoate: retention time 2.1 min, peak area 1.8 cm²propyl ethanoate: 3.4 min, 5.4 cm²butyl ethanoate: 5.0 min, 4.8 cm²

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of each ester in the mixture. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why butyl ethanoate has the longest retention time. [2]
  3. (c) Name a suitable carrier gas. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ethyl ethanoate 15.0 %; propyl ethanoate 45.0 %; butyl ethanoate 40.0 %
    M1 total area = 12.0; A1 each ÷ total × 100.
  2. (b) It has the highest boiling point (largest molecule, strongest id–id forces), so it spends more time dissolved in the liquid stationary phase.
    B1 highest bp / least volatile; B1 more time in (more soluble in) stationary phase.
  3. (c) Helium (or nitrogen)
    B1.
Q27Chromatography (TLC, GC) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A student runs a TLC plate of a mixture of glycine and alanine. In solvent 1 one spot appears with Rf 0.30. In solvent 2 the solvent front travels 10.0 cm; spots are at 2.6 cm (glycine) and 4.5 cm (alanine).

  1. (a) Explain why the two amino acids in a mixture give overlapping spots in solvent 1 but are separated in solvent 2. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the Rf of each amino acid in solvent 2. [2]
  3. (c) In gas chromatography a sample is too involatile to be analysed. Suggest one reason, and suggest an alternative. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In solvent 1 they have the same (very similar) Rf because their relative affinities for stationary and mobile phase are similar; in solvent 2 their solubilities differ, so they travel different distances.
    B1 same Rf in solvent 1; B1 different relative solubility/affinity in solvent 2.
  2. (b) glycine 0.26; alanine 0.45
    B1 each.
  3. (c) It would decompose / not vaporise at the column temperature (e.g. has strong hydrogen bonding, high bp); use TLC (or HPLC).
    B1 reason; B1 alternative.

Q30Carbon-13 NMR · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Ester S, C₄H₈O₂, gives four ¹³C peaks at δ 14, 21, 60 and 171. Its ¹H NMR spectrum includes a singlet (3H) at δ 2.0.¹³C shifts (δ/ppm): C–C (alkyl) 5–40; C–O 50–90; C=O in esters and acids 160–185.

  1. (a) Assign each ¹³C peak to a carbon atom and identify S. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how the ¹H NMR information rules out methyl propanoate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃: 171 C=O; 60 O–CH₂; 21 CH₃–C=O; 14 CH₃ of ethyl.
    B1 identity; B1 C=O at 171; B1 60 = C–O; B1 21 and 14 alkyl assigned.
  2. (b) Methyl propanoate has a CH₃O singlet at about δ 3.7, not a CH₃C=O singlet at δ 2.0 (its CH₃CO-side carbon is a CH₂).
    B1.
