Q19Halogen trends (volatility, bond strength, oxidising power) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The table gives data for the first four halogens.Halogen | Mr | Boiling point / °CF₂ | 38.0 | −188Cl₂ | 70.9 | −34Br₂ | 159.8 | 59I₂ | 253.8 | 184State the trend in boiling point from F₂ to I₂. [1]
  2. (b) Explain this trend in terms of the forces between molecules. [3]
  3. (c) Calculate the amount, in mol, of iodine in 25.0 g of I₂ (Mr = 253.8). Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Boiling point increases down the group, from −188 °C to 184 °C.
    1 mark for the trend, with the direction stated.
  2. (b) The molecules are non-polar, so the forces between them are instantaneous induced dipole–induced dipole (London/van der Waals) forces. Larger halogen molecules have more electrons and larger electron clouds, so these forces are stronger. More energy is needed to separate the molecules, so the boiling point is higher.
    1 mark: the forces are induced dipole–induced dipole (van der Waals) forces. 1 mark: larger molecules have more electrons, so the forces are stronger. 1 mark: stronger forces need more energy to overcome, so boiling points rise.
  3. (c) 0.0985 mol
    25.0 ÷ 253.8 = 0.0985 mol.
Q20Halogen trends (volatility, bond strength, oxidising power) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Bond energies / kJ/mol: H–Cl 431; H–Br 366; H–I 299; Cl–Cl 242; Br–Br 193; I–I 151.

  1. (a) Describe and explain the trend in the H–X bond energies. [2]
  2. (b) Use these values to explain the trend in thermal stability of the hydrogen halides. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the Cl–Cl bond is stronger than the I–I bond. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bond energy decreases from H–Cl to H–I because the halogen atom is larger, so the bond is longer and the shared electrons are further from the halogen nucleus (weaker attraction).
    B1 decreases; B1 larger atom / longer bond / weaker attraction of bonding pair
  2. (b) Thermal stability decreases from HCl to HI because less energy is needed to break the weaker bond.
    B1
