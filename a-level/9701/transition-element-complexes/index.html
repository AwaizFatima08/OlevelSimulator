Q8Ligand exchange & stability constants · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Aqueous potassium thiocyanate is added to aqueous iron(III) chloride. Write an equation for the formation of the blood-red complex [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ from [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺. [1]
  2. (b) When aqueous sodium fluoride is added to the blood-red solution, the colour fades to almost colourless as [FeF(H₂O)₅]²⁺ forms. What does this show about the stability constants of the two complexes? Explain. [2]
  3. (c) Write the Kstab expression for [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ and give its units. [2]

    B1

    B1 Kstab(F complex) larger; B1 competing equilibria / ligand exchange to more stable complex

    B1 expression; B1 units
Q9Ligand exchange & stability constants · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Aqueous cobalt(II) chloride contains the pink ion [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺. Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added until the solution turns blue, due to the equilibrium: [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O. State the change in coordination number and in shape of cobalt. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why diluting the blue solution with water turns it pink again. [2]
  3. (c) Explain the sign of the entropy change, ΔS, for the forward reaction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Coordination number falls from 6 to 4; shape changes from octahedral to tetrahedral.
    1 mark for coordination number. 1 mark for shape.
  2. (b) Adding water increases the concentration of H₂O, so the equilibrium moves to the left (Le Chatelier's principle). The pink [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ ion forms again.
    1 mark: water is a product, so adding water shifts the equilibrium left. 1 mark: pink [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ reforms.
  3. (c) ΔS is positive. The forward reaction increases the number of particles from 5 to 7, which increases disorder.
    1 mark: ΔS positive. 1 mark: 5 particles change to 7 particles (more disorder).
Q10Ligand exchange & stability constants · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

lg Kstab: [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ 8.6; [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ 18.3. Both N–Ni bonds are similar in strength.

  1. (a) Write an equation for the exchange of all the NH₃ ligands in [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ by 1,2-diaminoethane, en. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the equilibrium constant for this exchange. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) ΔH for the exchange is close to zero. Explain, in terms of entropy, why the reaction is very favourable. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6NH₃
    B1
  2. (b) 5.0 × 10⁹
    M1 K = Kstab([Ni(en)₃]²⁺) ÷ Kstab([Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺); lg K = 18.3 − 8.6 = 9.7; A1 K = 10^9.7 = 5.01 × 10⁹
  3. (c) Four particles on the left become seven on the right, so the disorder (entropy) increases greatly; ΔS is positive so ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is negative.
    B1 4 → 7 particles, entropy increases; B1 ΔG negative / feasible because ΔH ≈ 0
Q11Ligand exchange & stability constants · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Kstab of [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ is 1.7 × 10⁷ dm⁶ mol⁻².

  1. (a) Write the expression for Kstab of [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. [1]
  2. (b) In a solution, the equilibrium concentration of free ammonia is 0.50 mol/dm³. Calculate the ratio [Ag(NH₃)₂⁺] : [Ag⁺]. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Hence calculate the fraction of the silver present as free Ag⁺(aq) ions. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [1]
  4. (d) Use your answer to explain why silver chloride dissolves in aqueous ammonia. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kstab = [Ag(NH₃)₂⁺] / ([Ag⁺][NH₃]²)
    B1
  2. (b) 4.25 × 10⁶ : 1

  4. (d) Free [Ag⁺] becomes so small that [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] falls below the solubility product, so more AgCl dissolves.
    B1

Q15Ligand exchange & stability constants · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 2S₂O₃²⁻ ⇌ [Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻ + 2NH₃. Write the expression for the equilibrium constant, K, of this ligand exchange. [1]
  2. (b) lg Kstab values: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ = 7.2; [Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻ = 13.5. Calculate K for the exchange. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) State what the value of K tells you about the position of the equilibrium, and why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) K = [Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻ × [NH₃]² ÷ ([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ × [S₂O₃²⁻]²)
    Products over reactants, with each concentration raised to the power of its coefficient.
  2. (b) 2.0 × 10⁶
    K = Kstab([Ag(S₂O₃)₂]³⁻) ÷ Kstab([Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺) = 10^(13.5 − 7.2) = 10^6.3 = 2.0 × 10⁶.
  3. (c) K is much greater than 1, so the equilibrium lies to the right: the thiosulfate complex is much more stable than the ammonia complex.
    1 mark: K ≫ 1, so products are favoured. 1 mark: the thiosulfate complex has a much larger stability constant.

Q60Colour & d-orbital splitting · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ is diamagnetic (no unpaired electrons), but [CoF₆]³⁻ is paramagnetic with four unpaired electrons. Explain this difference, referring to the ligand field strength, the size of the splitting Δ and the pairing energy. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) NH₃ is a strong-field ligand, so Δ is larger than the pairing energy. The d⁶ electrons pair in the lower t₂g orbitals (low spin), giving no unpaired electrons. F⁻ is a weak-field ligand, so Δ is smaller than the pairing energy. Electrons occupy the higher eg orbitals before pairing (high spin, t₂g⁴e_g²), giving four unpaired electrons.
    1 mark: strong field gives Δ greater than pairing energy, so electrons pair (low spin). 1 mark: weak field gives Δ smaller than pairing energy, so electrons occupy eg first (high spin). 1 mark: correct counts of unpaired electrons.
