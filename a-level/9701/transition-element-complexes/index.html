  2. (b) Write an equation for the formation of the precipitate from [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺. [1]
  3. (c) Write an equation for the ligand exchange that forms the deep blue complex from [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A pale blue precipitate forms first; it dissolves in excess ammonia to give a deep (dark) blue solution.
    B1 pale blue precipitate; B1 dissolves to deep blue solution
  2. (b) [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄ + 2H₂O (accept Cu²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → Cu(OH)₂)
    B1
  3. (c) [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O
    B1
Q2Ligand exchange & stability constants · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate. Give the formula of the complex formed and its colour. [2]
  2. (b) State the shape and coordination number of the complex in (a) and of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the coordination number changes. [1]
  4. (d) State what is seen when the solution from (a) is diluted with plenty of water, and explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) [CuCl₄]²⁻; yellow (yellow-green solution as some aqua complex remains).
    B1 formula; B1 colour
  2. (b) [CuCl₄]²⁻: tetrahedral, 4; [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺: octahedral, 6.
    1 mark each
  3. (c) Chloride ions are larger than water molecules, so fewer fit around the Cu²⁺ ion.
    B1
  4. (d) It turns blue again; the equilibrium shifts back to [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ as water is in large excess.
    B1
Q3Ligand exchange & stability constants · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define the term stability constant, Kstab, of a complex ion. [2]
  2. (b) Write the expression for Kstab of [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ formed from [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, and give its units. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the concentration of water does not appear in the expression. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The equilibrium constant for the formation of the complex ion in a solvent from its constituent ions or molecules (the aqueous metal ion and the ligands).
    B1 equilibrium constant; B1 for formation of complex from metal ion and ligands (in water)
  2. (b) Kstab = [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂²⁺] / ([Cu(H₂O)₆²⁺][NH₃]⁴); units dm¹² mol⁻⁴.
    B1 expression (water omitted); B1 units
  3. (c) Water is the solvent, in large excess, so its concentration is effectively constant.
    B1
Q4Ligand exchange & stability constants · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

lg Kstab values: [CuCl₄]²⁻ 5.6; [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ 13.1; [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ 18.8.

  1. (a) Predict what happens when excess aqueous ammonia is added to a solution of [CuCl₄]²⁻. Explain using the data. [2]
  2. (b) EDTA⁴⁻ solution is added to the product of (a). Write an equation for the reaction and explain why it happens. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why lg Kstab values are often quoted rather than Kstab. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The solution turns deep blue: [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ forms, because its Kstab is much larger, so NH₃ replaces Cl⁻.
    B1 deep blue / ammine complex forms; B1 larger Kstab, more stable
  2. (b) [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 4NH₃ + 2H₂O; [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ has the largest Kstab, so it is the most stable complex.
    B1 equation; B1 explanation
  3. (c) Kstab values are very large and vary over many powers of ten, so logarithms are easier to compare.
    B1

  1. (a) Concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to a pink solution containing [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺. Which row describes the new complex?A [CoCl₄]²⁻, blue, tetrahedralB [CoCl₆]⁴⁻, blue, octahedralC [CoCl₄]²⁻, pink, square planarD [Co(H₂O)₄Cl₂], green, octahedral [1]

Q6Ligand exchange & stability constants · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Aqueous potassium thiocyanate is added to aqueous iron(III) chloride. Write an equation for the formation of the blood-red complex [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ from [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺. [1]
  2. (b) When aqueous sodium fluoride is added to the blood-red solution, the colour fades to almost colourless as [FeF(H₂O)₅]²⁺ forms. What does this show about the stability constants of the two complexes? Explain. [2]
  3. (c) Write the Kstab expression for [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ and give its units. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + SCN⁻ ⇌ [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺ + H₂O
    B1
  2. (b) Kstab of [FeF(H₂O)₅]²⁺ is larger than that of [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅]²⁺; F⁻ competes for Fe³⁺ and the equilibrium favours the more stable complex.
    B1 Kstab(F complex) larger; B1 competing equilibria / ligand exchange to more stable complex
  3. (c) Kstab = [Fe(SCN)(H₂O)₅²⁺] / ([Fe(H₂O)₆³⁺][SCN⁻]); dm³ mol⁻¹.
    B1 expression; B1 units
Q7Ligand exchange & stability constants · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

lg Kstab: [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ 8.6; [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ 18.3. Both N–Ni bonds are similar in strength.

  1. (a) Write an equation for the exchange of all the NH₃ ligands in [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ by 1,2-diaminoethane, en. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the equilibrium constant for this exchange. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) ΔH for the exchange is close to zero. Explain, in terms of entropy, why the reaction is very favourable. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ + 6NH₃
    B1
  2. (b) 5.0 × 10⁹
    M1 K = Kstab([Ni(en)₃]²⁺) ÷ Kstab([Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺); lg K = 18.3 − 8.6 = 9.7; A1 K = 10^9.7 = 5.01 × 10⁹
  3. (c) Four particles on the left become seven on the right, so the disorder (entropy) increases greatly; ΔS is positive so ΔG = ΔH − TΔS is negative.
    B1 4 → 7 particles, entropy increases; B1 ΔG negative / feasible because ΔH ≈ 0
Q8Ligand exchange & stability constants · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Kstab of [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ is 1.7 × 10⁷ dm⁶ mol⁻².

  1. (a) Write the expression for Kstab of [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺. [1]
  2. (b) In a solution, the equilibrium concentration of free ammonia is 0.50 mol/dm³. Calculate the ratio [Ag(NH₃)₂⁺] : [Ag⁺]. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Hence calculate the fraction of the silver present as free Ag⁺(aq) ions. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [1]
  4. (d) Use your answer to explain why silver chloride dissolves in aqueous ammonia. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kstab = [Ag(NH₃)₂⁺] / ([Ag⁺][NH₃]²)
    B1
  2. (b) 4.25 × 10⁶ : 1
    M1 ratio = Kstab × [NH₃]²; A1 = 1.7 × 10⁷ × 0.25 = 4.25 × 10⁶
  3. (c) 2.4 × 10⁻⁷
    B1 1 ÷ (1 + 4.25 × 10⁶) = 2.4 × 10⁻⁷
  4. (d) Free [Ag⁺] becomes so small that [Ag⁺][Cl⁻] falls below the solubility product, so more AgCl dissolves.
    B1
Q9Ligand exchange & stability constants · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Carbon monoxide is dangerous because it binds to the Fe²⁺ ion in haemoglobin. Which statement explains this?A CO forms a weaker bond to Fe²⁺ than O₂, so O₂ cannot bindB The complex with CO has a larger stability constant than the complex with O₂, so the exchange is effectively irreversibleC CO oxidises Fe²⁺ to Fe³⁺, which cannot carry oxygenD CO is a bidentate ligand that replaces two water molecules [1]

Q11Complex ions: ligands, shapes & coordination number · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Q18Complex ions: ligands, shapes & coordination number · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Chromium(III) chloride hexahydrate, CrCl₃·6H₂O, exists in forms with different colours. A solution containing 0.0100 mol of the pale green form is treated with excess aqueous silver nitrate at once; 2.87 g of silver chloride (Mr 143.4) is precipitated. Chromium has coordination number 6 in all forms.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of moles of chloride ions precipitated per mole of complex. [2]
  2. (b) Deduce the formula of the complex ion and the full formula of the compound, explaining your reasoning. [2]
  3. (c) State the type of bond between the chloride ligand and the chromium ion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.00 ≈ 2
    M1 n(AgCl) = 2.87 ÷ 143.4 = 0.0200 mol; A1 ratio 0.0200 ÷ 0.0100 = 2
  2. (b) [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]²⁺; [Cr(H₂O)₅Cl]Cl₂·H₂O. Only free Cl⁻ ions (not ligands) precipitate, so two Cl⁻ are outside and one is a ligand; coordination number 6 is completed by five H₂O.
    B1 complex ion; B1 full formula with reasoning
  3. (c) Dative covalent (coordinate) bond.
    B1
Q19Complex ions: ligands, shapes & coordination number · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

25.0 cm³ of a solution of Ni²⁺ ions is titrated with 0.0500 mol/dm³ EDTA⁴⁻. 18.60 cm³ is needed. The ions react in a 1 : 1 ratio.

  1. (a) State the coordination number of nickel in [Ni(EDTA)]²⁻ and explain how one EDTA⁴⁻ ion achieves this. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of Ni²⁺ in the solution, in mol/dm³, to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) The solution was made by dissolving a nickel alloy sample in acid and making it up to 250 cm³. Calculate the mass of nickel in the sample, to 3 significant figures. (Ar Ni = 58.7) [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6; EDTA⁴⁻ is hexadentate, forming dative bonds from two N atoms and four O⁻ atoms.
    B1 6; B1 six donor atoms (2 N, 4 O)
  2. (b) 0.0372 mol/dm³
    M1 n(EDTA) = 0.01860 × 0.0500 = 0.000930 mol = n(Ni²⁺) (1 : 1); A1 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0372
  3. (c) 0.546 g
    M1 n in 250 cm³ = 10 × 0.000930 = 0.00930 mol; A1 × 58.7 = 0.546 g
Q20Isomerism in complexes · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the two stereoisomers of square planar [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]. [2]
  2. (b) State the type of stereoisomerism shown. [1]
  3. (c) The cis isomer, cisplatin, is an anticancer drug. Explain how it acts. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cis: the two Cl ligands are next to each other (90° apart); trans: the two Cl ligands are opposite each other (180° apart).
    B1 cis; B1 trans
  2. (b) Geometric (cis/trans) isomerism.
    B1
  3. (c) Its two Cl ligands are replaced by nitrogen atoms of guanine bases on DNA; this binds adjacent bases, distorts the DNA and stops the cancer cell dividing.
    B1 ligand exchange: binds to N on DNA bases; B1 prevents DNA replication / cell division
Q21Isomerism in complexes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Octahedral [Ni(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ has two geometric isomers. Describe the arrangement of the water ligands in each. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why [Ni(NH₃)₅(H₂O)]²⁺ has no geometric isomers. [1]
  3. (c) State whether either isomer in (a) is optically active and explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cis: two water ligands adjacent (90°); trans: two water ligands opposite (180°).
    1 mark each
  2. (b) With only one water ligand, every position is equivalent; all arrangements are identical.
    B1
  3. (c) No; each has a plane of symmetry, so its mirror image is superimposable.
    B1
Q22Isomerism in complexes · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which complex ion shows optical isomerism?A [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺B trans-[Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺C [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻D [CuCl₄]²⁻ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C [Cr(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻
    Three bidentate ethanedioate ligands give a chiral octahedral complex. The trans complex has a plane of symmetry; tetrahedral [CuCl₄]²⁻ with four identical ligands is not chiral.
Q23Isomerism in complexes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) For each complex, state whether it shows geometric isomerism, optical isomerism, or neither. Give a reason in each case.(i) tetrahedral [Zn(NH₃)₂Cl₂](ii) octahedral [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺(iii) octahedral [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻(iv) octahedral [Cr(H₂O)₄Cl₂]⁺ [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) neither: in a tetrahedron all positions are adjacent. (ii) neither: only one Cl, all positions equivalent. (iii) optical: three bidentate ligands, non-superimposable mirror images. (iv) geometric: Cl ligands can be cis (90°) or trans (180°).
    1 mark each, with reason
Q24Isomerism in complexes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ shows optical isomerism. [2]
  2. (b) State the total number of stereoisomers of [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺. Explain. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest how the two optical isomers could be distinguished. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The three bidentate ligands make a propeller shape with no plane of symmetry; the complex and its mirror image are non-superimposable.
    B1 no plane/centre of symmetry; B1 non-superimposable mirror images
  2. (b) Three: the trans isomer (Cl ligands opposite) has a plane of symmetry and is not chiral; the cis isomer is chiral, giving a pair of optical isomers.
    B1 three; B1 trans optically inactive; B1 cis exists as two enantiomers
  3. (c) They rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions.
    B1
Q25Isomerism in complexes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Two different compounds of formula [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] exist. Explain how this shows that the complex is square planar and not tetrahedral. [2]
  2. (b) One isomer has a dipole moment and the other does not. Identify which, and explain. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest which isomer is more soluble in water, and why. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A tetrahedral MA₂B₂ complex has all positions adjacent, so only one arrangement exists; two isomers are possible only for square planar (cis and trans).
    B1 tetrahedral: one form only; B1 square planar gives cis and trans
  2. (b) Cis is polar: the Pt–Cl bond dipoles do not cancel; trans is non-polar: the bond dipoles are opposite and cancel.
    B1 cis polar; B1 dipoles cancel in trans
  3. (c) Cis, because it is polar and interacts more strongly with polar water molecules.
    B1
Q26Colour & d-orbital splitting · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is coloured. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why [Zn(H₂O)₆]²⁺ and [Sc(H₂O)₆]³⁺ are colourless. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ligands split the d orbitals into two energy levels; an electron absorbs visible light and moves to a higher d orbital (energy ΔE = hf); the light not absorbed (complementary colour, blue) is transmitted/seen.
    B1 d orbitals split by ligands; B1 electron promoted absorbing visible light of energy ΔE; B1 remaining colour transmitted
  2. (b) Zn²⁺ is 3d¹⁰ (full: no space to promote an electron); Sc³⁺ is 3d⁰ (no d electrons to promote).
    1 mark each
Q27Colour & d-orbital splitting · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name the d orbitals that are raised in energy in an octahedral complex and explain why. [3]
  2. (b) Describe how the splitting in a tetrahedral complex differs from that in an octahedral complex. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) dx²−y² and dz²; their lobes point along the axes directly at the ligands; electrons in them are repelled more by the ligand lone pairs.
    B1 names; B1 lobes along axes / towards ligands; B1 greater repulsion
  2. (b) Reversed: dxy, dxz, dyz are higher and dx²−y², dz² lower; the energy gap is smaller.
    B1 reversed pattern (three up, two down); B1 smaller ΔE
Q28Colour & d-orbital splitting · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Excess ammonia is added to aqueous [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺, forming deep blue [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺. Which statement is correct?A The d-orbital splitting decreases, so light of longer wavelength is absorbedB The d-orbital splitting increases, so light of shorter wavelength is absorbedC The d orbitals are no longer splitD The oxidation state of copper changes from +2 to +1 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The d-orbital splitting increases, so light of shorter wavelength is absorbed
    NH₃ splits the d orbitals more than H₂O, so ΔE is larger and higher-energy (shorter-wavelength) light is absorbed, deepening the blue. A reverses the trend; the oxidation state stays +2.
Q29Colour & d-orbital splitting · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is pink but [CoCl₄]²⁻ is blue. Give two reasons why the colours differ. [2]
  2. (b) [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is pale blue but [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ is deep blue, although both contain Cu²⁺ in an octahedral complex. Explain why their colours differ. [2]
  3. (c) State one other factor that affects the colour of a transition element complex. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Different ligands (H₂O v Cl⁻) and different coordination number/shape (octahedral v tetrahedral) change the size of ΔE, so a different wavelength is absorbed.
    B1 ligand; B1 coordination number/geometry; (both linked to ΔE)
  2. (b) NH₃ ligands split the d orbitals by a different (larger) amount than H₂O, so ΔE is different and light of a different wavelength (frequency) is absorbed; the transmitted colour changes.
    B1 different ligands give different ΔE; B1 different wavelength absorbed, so different complementary colour seen
  3. (c) Oxidation state of the metal (or identity of the metal).
    B1
Q30Colour & d-orbital splitting · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The complex [Ti(H₂O)₆]³⁺ has one absorption peak in the visible region, at 500 nm.

  1. (a) Calculate the energy gap ΔE between the split d orbitals, in J per ion, to 3 significant figures. (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s; c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s) [2]
  2. (b) Convert your answer to kJ/mol, to 3 significant figures. (L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹) [1]
  3. (c) Light of 500 nm is green-blue. Suggest the colour of the solution. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the ion is coloured although titanium has only one d electron in this ion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.98 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
    M1 ΔE = hc/λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 500 × 10⁻⁹; A1 = 3.978 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
  2. (b) 239 kJ/mol
    B1 3.978 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 1000 = 239 kJ/mol
  3. (c) Purple/violet (red-purple).
    B1 complementary colour, purple/violet
  4. (d) The single 3d electron can be promoted from a lower to a higher d orbital, absorbing light.
    B1
