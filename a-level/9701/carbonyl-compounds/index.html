Q55Carbonyl structure & reactions · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the C=O bond is polar. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the C=O group of an aldehyde is attacked by nucleophiles, but the C=C group of an alkene is attacked by electrophiles. [3]
  3. (c) State the shape around the carbonyl carbon and explain why this makes attack from either face of the molecule equally likely. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, so the bonding electrons are pulled towards O, leaving C δ+ and O δ−.
    B1
  2. (b) The carbonyl carbon is δ+ (electron-poor), so it attracts nucleophiles, which donate a lone pair to it. The C=C bond is non-polar but has a region of high electron density in its π bond, which attracts electrophiles (electron-pair acceptors).
    M1 C of C=O is δ+; M1 nucleophiles attracted to δ+ C/donate electron pair; A1 C=C electron-rich π bond attracts electrophiles.
  3. (c) Trigonal planar (120°); the nucleophile can approach from above or below the plane of the C=O group with equal ease.
    B1 trigonal planar; B1 planar group accessible equally from both sides.
