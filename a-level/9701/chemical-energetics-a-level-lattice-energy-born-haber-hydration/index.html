Q9Lattice energy & Born–Haber cycles · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Lattice energies (kJ/mol): NaF −918; NaCl −787; NaBr −747.

  1. (a) Explain the trend in lattice energy from NaF to NaBr. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the lattice energy of MgO (−3845 kJ/mol) is much more exothermic than that of NaF. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The anion radius increases from F⁻ to Br⁻, so the distance between ion centres increases and the electrostatic attraction becomes weaker; the lattice energy becomes less exothermic.
    M1 anion size increases (same charge)M2 weaker attraction, less exothermic
  2. (b) Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have double the charges of Na⁺ and F⁻ (and Mg²⁺ is smaller), so the attraction between the ions is much stronger.
    M1 higher charges on both ionsM2 stronger electrostatic attraction (Mg²⁺ smaller)

Q17Enthalpies of solution & hydration · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

  2. (b) F⁻ is very small, so the lattice energy of LiF is very exothermic; the large hydration enthalpies of Li⁺ and F⁻ only just balance it (ΔHsol slightly positive). For LiCl the lattice energy falls more (larger Cl⁻) than the hydration enthalpy, so ΔHsol is negative.
    M1 LiF lattice energy very large (small ions)M2 for LiCl lattice energy decreases more than hydration enthalpy, so dissolving is more favourable

  2. (b) Use the data to explain why the solubility of the Group 2 sulfates decreases down the group. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) MgSO₄ −146 kJ/mol; BaSO₄ +50 kJ/mol
    B1 2833 − 1920 − 1059 = −146B1 2469 − 1360 − 1059 = +50
