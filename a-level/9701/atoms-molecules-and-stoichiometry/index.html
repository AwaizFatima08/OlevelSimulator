Q1Titration calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A student in a Lahore college laboratory pipettes 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution into a conical flask and titrates it with 0.100 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid using phenolphthalein. The mean titre is 21.35 cm³.

  1. (a) Write the equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of sodium hydroxide with sulfuric acid. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the amount, in mol, of sulfuric acid used in the mean titre. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration, in mol/dm³, of the sodium hydroxide solution. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2NaOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l)
    B1 correct formulae, balancing and state symbols.
  2. (b) 0.00214 mol
    0.100 × 21.35 ÷ 1000 = 0.002135 mol (B1).
  3. (c) 0.171 mol/dm³
    M1 mol NaOH = 2 × 0.002135 = 0.004270 mol; A1 ÷ 0.0250 dm³ = 0.171 mol/dm³.

  1. (a) 25.0 cm³ of sodium carbonate solution is titrated with 0.150 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid using methyl orange. The end point is reached after 20.0 cm³ of acid.Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂What is the concentration of the sodium carbonate solution? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 0.0600 mol/dm³
    mol HCl = 0.150 × 0.0200 = 0.00300; mol Na₂CO₃ = 0.00150; ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0600 mol/dm³. C ignores the 1:2 ratio.

Show answer
  1. (a) B 0.150 mol/dm³
    KOH + HNO₃ react 1:1. n(KOH) = 0.120 × 0.0250 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol = n(HNO₃); c = 3.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0200 = 0.150 mol/dm³. C and D come from inverting the division or using the wrong volume ratio.

Show answer

Q6Titration calculations · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A food-quality analyst tests a brand of white vinegar sold in Karachi. 25.0 cm³ of the vinegar is diluted to 250 cm³ with distilled water in a volumetric flask. 25.0 cm³ portions of the diluted solution are titrated with 0.0950 mol/dm³ NaOH(aq).Titre results (cm³): rough 19.10; run 1 18.60; run 2 18.95; run 3 18.70.

  1. (a) Identify the concordant titres and calculate the mean titre. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration, in mol/dm³, of ethanoic acid in the diluted solution. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the original vinegar, in g/dm³. [Mr of CH₃COOH = 60.0] [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the vinegar was diluted before titration. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18.60 and 18.70 cm³; mean = 18.65 cm³
    B1 uses only titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other (rough and 18.95 cm³ excluded).
  2. (b) 0.0709 mol/dm³
    M1 mol NaOH = 0.0950 × 18.65/1000 = 0.001772 mol = mol CH₃COOH (1:1); A1 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0709 mol/dm³.
  3. (c) 42.5 g/dm³
    M1 dilution factor 250/25.0 = 10, so 0.709 mol/dm³; A1 × 60.0 = 42.5 g/dm³.
  4. (d) So that a sensible titre (within one burette filling) is obtained / the undiluted acid would need too large a volume of NaOH.
    B1 any one: titre would exceed 50 cm³ burette; reduces percentage error from a very large titre.
Q7Titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Sodium carbonate solution of concentration 0.0850 mol/dm³ is prepared. 25.00 cm³ of it is titrated with hydrochloric acid. 18.60 cm³ of acid is needed for complete reaction.

  1. (a) Write the equation for the reaction between sodium carbonate and hydrochloric acid. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of the hydrochloric acid, in mol/dm³, to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂
    Balanced equation; the mole ratio Na₂CO₃ : HCl is 1 : 2.
  2. (b) 0.228 mol/dm³
    M1 n(Na₂CO₃) = 0.0850 × 25.00 ÷ 1000 = 2.125 × 10⁻³ mol. M1 n(HCl) = 2 × 2.125 × 10⁻³ = 4.25 × 10⁻³ mol. A1 c = 4.25 × 10⁻³ ÷ (18.60 ÷ 1000) = 0.228 mol/dm³.
Q8Titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Ethanedioic acid dihydrate, H₂C₂O₄·2H₂O (Mr = 126.0), is used as a primary standard. 0.630 g of it is dissolved and made up to 250.0 cm³. 25.0 cm³ of this solution is titrated with sodium hydroxide solution, and 22.50 cm³ is needed for complete neutralisation.

  1. (a) Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide solution, in mol/dm³, to 3 significant figures. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0444 mol/dm³
    M1 n(H₂C₂O₄·2H₂O) in 250.0 cm³ = 0.630 ÷ 126.0 = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol. M1 n in 25.0 cm³ = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol. M1 n(NaOH) = 2 × 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.00 × 10⁻³ mol (H₂C₂O₄ + 2NaOH). A1 c = 1.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.02250 = 0.0444 mol/dm³.
Q9Titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

25.0 cm³ of iron(II) sulfate solution of unknown concentration is acidified with dilute sulfuric acid and titrated with 0.0200 mol/dm³ potassium manganate(VII). 18.75 cm³ of the manganate(VII) solution is needed.

  1. (a) Explain why no separate indicator is needed for this titration. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration of Fe²⁺(aq), in mol/dm³, in the 25.0 cm³ sample. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The manganate(VII) ion is intensely purple and is reduced to almost colourless Mn²⁺; the first excess drop gives a permanent pale pink, so it acts as its own indicator.
    1 mark: MnO₄⁻ is purple and Mn²⁺ is almost colourless. 1 mark: the first excess manganate(VII) gives a permanent pink colour at the end point.
  2. (b) 0.0750 mol/dm³
    M1 n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 18.75 ÷ 1000 = 3.75 × 10⁻⁴ mol. M1 n(Fe²⁺) = 5 × 3.75 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.875 × 10⁻³ mol. A1 c = 1.875 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0750 mol/dm³.
Q10Titration calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A barium hydroxide solution of concentration 0.0500 mol/dm³ is used to neutralise 25.0 cm³ of nitric acid of concentration 0.120 mol/dm³.

  1. (a) Write the equation for the reaction of barium hydroxide with nitric acid. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the volume of barium hydroxide solution needed, in cm³, to neutralise the acid completely. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ba(OH)₂ + 2HNO₃ → Ba(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O
    Balanced; two moles of HNO₃ per mole of Ba(OH)₂.
  2. (b) 30.0 cm³
    M1 n(HNO₃) = 0.120 × 25.0 ÷ 1000 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol. M1 n(Ba(OH)₂) = 1.50 × 10⁻³ mol. A1 V = 1.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0500 = 0.0300 dm³ = 30.0 cm³.
Q11Titration calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A 0.500 g antacid tablet bought from a pharmacy in Peshawar contains calcium carbonate as its only base. The tablet is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid (an excess). When reaction stops, the excess acid needs 22.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH(aq) for neutralisation. [Mr of CaCO₃ = 100.1]

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of HCl added to the tablet and the amount left over after the reaction. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage by mass of calcium carbonate in the tablet. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why a back titration is used here rather than titrating the tablet directly with acid. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) added 0.0100 mol; left over 0.00224 mol
    B1 0.200 × 50.0/1000 = 0.0100 mol; B1 excess = 0.100 × 22.40/1000 = 0.00224 mol (NaOH:HCl 1:1).
  2. (b) 77.7 %
    M1 HCl reacted = 0.007760 mol; M1 CaCO₃ = ÷2 = 0.003880 mol (CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂); A1 mass = 0.388 g → 77.7 %.
  3. (c) CaCO₃ is insoluble and reacts slowly, so the end point of a direct titration would be unclear.
    B1 insoluble solid / slow reaction / no sharp end point.
Q12Titration calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

An iron supplement tablet is dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and the solution made up to 100 cm³. 25.0 cm³ of this solution needs 14.60 cm³ of 0.00500 mol/dm³ potassium manganate(VII) for complete reaction.MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of MnO₄⁻ ions used in the titration. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of iron, in mg, in one tablet. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [Ar of Fe = 55.8] [3]
  3. (c) State why no indicator is added to this titration. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.30 × 10⁻⁵ mol
    B1 0.00500 × 14.60/1000.
  2. (b) 81.5 mg
    M1 mol Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 7.30 × 10⁻⁵ = 3.65 × 10⁻⁴; M1 × 100/25.0 = 0.00146 mol; A1 × 55.8 × 1000 = 81.5 mg.
  3. (c) MnO₄⁻ is purple and Mn²⁺ is almost colourless, so the first permanent pale pink colour shows the end point.

Show answer
  1. (a) x = 9
    M1 n(Fe) = 0.993 ÷ 55.8 = 0.0178 mol, so n(Fe₂(SO₄)₃) = 0.00890 mol. M1 Mr of the whole salt = 5.00 ÷ 0.00890 = 562. M1 Mr Fe₂(SO₄)₃ = 399.9, so water = 162.1. A1 x = 162.1 ÷ 18.0 = 9.

  1. (a) Deduce the oxidation state of chromium in K₂Cr₂O₇ and of manganese in K₂MnO₄. Use the fact that the oxidation states of potassium (+1) and oxygen (−2) are fixed. [3]

Q135Formulae of ions & compounds · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Aluminium and chlorine form an ionic compound. [Ar: Al 27.0, Cl 35.5]

  1. (a) Write the formula of aluminium chloride. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate its relative formula mass. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of chloride ions in 26.7 g of aluminium chloride. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) AlCl₃
    1 mark: Al³⁺ and Cl⁻ give AlCl₃.
  2. (b) 133.5
    Mr = 27.0 + 3 × 35.5 = 133.5.
  3. (c) 21.3 g
    M1 n(AlCl₃) = 26.7 ÷ 133.5 = 0.200 mol; n(Cl⁻) = 0.600 mol. A1 mass = 0.600 × 35.5 = 21.3 g.
