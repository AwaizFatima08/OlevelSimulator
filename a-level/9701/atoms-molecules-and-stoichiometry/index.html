Q1Titration calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A student in a Lahore college laboratory pipettes 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution into a conical flask and titrates it with 0.100 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid using phenolphthalein. The mean titre is 21.35 cm³.

  1. (a) Write the equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of sodium hydroxide with sulfuric acid. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the amount, in mol, of sulfuric acid used in the mean titre. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration, in mol/dm³, of the sodium hydroxide solution. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2NaOH(aq) + H₂SO₄(aq) → Na₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l)
    B1 correct formulae, balancing and state symbols.
  2. (b) 0.00214 mol
    0.100 × 21.35 ÷ 1000 = 0.002135 mol (B1).
  3. (c) 0.171 mol/dm³
    M1 mol NaOH = 2 × 0.002135 = 0.004270 mol; A1 ÷ 0.0250 dm³ = 0.171 mol/dm³.
Q2Titration calculations · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) 25.0 cm³ of sodium carbonate solution is titrated with 0.150 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid using methyl orange. The end point is reached after 20.0 cm³ of acid.Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂What is the concentration of the sodium carbonate solution?A 0.0300 mol/dm³B 0.0600 mol/dm³C 0.120 mol/dm³D 0.240 mol/dm³ [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 0.0600 mol/dm³
    mol HCl = 0.150 × 0.0200 = 0.00300; mol Na₂CO₃ = 0.00150; ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0600 mol/dm³. C ignores the 1:2 ratio.
Q3Titration calculations · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A food-quality analyst tests a brand of white vinegar sold in Karachi. 25.0 cm³ of the vinegar is diluted to 250 cm³ with distilled water in a volumetric flask. 25.0 cm³ portions of the diluted solution are titrated with 0.0950 mol/dm³ NaOH(aq).Titre results (cm³): rough 19.10; run 1 18.60; run 2 18.95; run 3 18.70.

  1. (a) Identify the concordant titres and calculate the mean titre. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration, in mol/dm³, of ethanoic acid in the diluted solution. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid in the original vinegar, in g/dm³. [Mr of CH₃COOH = 60.0] [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the vinegar was diluted before titration. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18.60 and 18.70 cm³; mean = 18.65 cm³
    B1 uses only titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other (rough and 18.95 cm³ excluded).
  2. (b) 0.0709 mol/dm³
    M1 mol NaOH = 0.0950 × 18.65/1000 = 0.001772 mol = mol CH₃COOH (1:1); A1 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0709 mol/dm³.
  3. (c) 42.5 g/dm³
    M1 dilution factor 250/25.0 = 10, so 0.709 mol/dm³; A1 × 60.0 = 42.5 g/dm³.
  4. (d) So that a sensible titre (within one burette filling) is obtained / the undiluted acid would need too large a volume of NaOH.
    B1 any one: titre would exceed 50 cm³ burette; reduces percentage error from a very large titre.
Q4Titration calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A 0.500 g antacid tablet bought from a pharmacy in Peshawar contains calcium carbonate as its only base. The tablet is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid (an excess). When reaction stops, the excess acid needs 22.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH(aq) for neutralisation. [Mr of CaCO₃ = 100.1]

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of HCl added to the tablet and the amount left over after the reaction. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage by mass of calcium carbonate in the tablet. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why a back titration is used here rather than titrating the tablet directly with acid. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) added 0.0100 mol; left over 0.00224 mol
    B1 0.200 × 50.0/1000 = 0.0100 mol; B1 excess = 0.100 × 22.40/1000 = 0.00224 mol (NaOH:HCl 1:1).
  2. (b) 77.7 %
    M1 HCl reacted = 0.007760 mol; M1 CaCO₃ = ÷2 = 0.003880 mol (CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂); A1 mass = 0.388 g → 77.7 %.
  3. (c) CaCO₃ is insoluble and reacts slowly, so the end point of a direct titration would be unclear.
    B1 insoluble solid / slow reaction / no sharp end point.
Q5Titration calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

An iron supplement tablet is dissolved in dilute sulfuric acid and the solution made up to 100 cm³. 25.0 cm³ of this solution needs 14.60 cm³ of 0.00500 mol/dm³ potassium manganate(VII) for complete reaction.MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of MnO₄⁻ ions used in the titration. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of iron, in mg, in one tablet. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [Ar of Fe = 55.8] [3]
  3. (c) State why no indicator is added to this titration. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.30 × 10⁻⁵ mol
    B1 0.00500 × 14.60/1000.
  2. (b) 81.5 mg
    M1 mol Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 7.30 × 10⁻⁵ = 3.65 × 10⁻⁴; M1 × 100/25.0 = 0.00146 mol; A1 × 55.8 × 1000 = 81.5 mg.
  3. (c) MnO₄⁻ is purple and Mn²⁺ is almost colourless, so the first permanent pale pink colour shows the end point.
    B1 KMnO₄ acts as its own indicator.

Q7Mole calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Aluminium sulfate is used as a coagulant at water treatment plants such as those supplying Islamabad.

  1. (a) Calculate the relative formula mass of aluminium sulfate, Al₂(SO₄)₃. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of 0.0350 mol of aluminium sulfate. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the number of sulfate ions in 0.0350 mol of aluminium sulfate. [L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹] [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 342.3
    B1 2(27.0) + 3(32.1) + 12(16.0).
  2. (b) 12.0 g
    B1 0.0350 × 342.3.
  3. (c) 6.32 × 10²²
    B1 0.0350 × 3 × 6.02 × 10²³.
Q8Mole calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

2.50 g of hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·5H₂O, is dissolved in water and made up to 250 cm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the concentration, in mol/dm³, of copper(II) sulfate in the solution. [Mr of CuSO₄·5H₂O = 249.6] [2]
  2. (b) 10.0 cm³ of this solution is diluted to 50.0 cm³. Calculate the new concentration of Cu²⁺ ions. [1]
  3. (c) State the total concentration of all ions in the original solution. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0401 mol/dm³
    M1 2.50/249.6 = 0.0100 mol; A1 ÷ 0.250 = 0.0401 mol/dm³.
  2. (b) 0.00801 mol/dm³
    B1 × 10.0/50.0.
  3. (c) 0.0801 mol/dm³
    B1 Cu²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ = 2 × concentration.
Q9Mole calculations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Caffeine is found in tea, a favourite drink across Pakistan.

  1. (a) Calculate the relative molecular mass of caffeine, C₈H₁₀N₄O₂. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass, in grams, of one caffeine molecule. [L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹] [1]
  3. (c) A cup of tea contains 95 mg of caffeine. Calculate the number of nitrogen atoms in the caffeine in the cup. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 194.0
    B1 8(12.0)+10(1.0)+4(14.0)+2(16.0).
  2. (b) 3.22 × 10⁻²² g
    B1 194.0 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³.
  3. (c) 1.18 × 10²¹
    M1 mol caffeine = 0.095/194.0 = 4.90 × 10⁻⁴; A1 × 4 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.18 × 10²¹.
Q10Mole calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

One sachet of oral rehydration salts (ORS), widely used in Pakistan for treating diarrhoea, is dissolved in water to make 1.00 dm³ of solution. It contains: 2.6 g NaCl, 1.5 g KCl, 2.9 g trisodium citrate dihydrate (Na₃C₆H₅O₇·2H₂O) and 13.5 g glucose.

  1. (a) Calculate the concentration, in mmol/dm³, of Na⁺ ions in the solution. [Mr: NaCl 58.5; Na₃C₆H₅O₇·2H₂O 294.0] [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the concentration, in mmol/dm³, of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration, in mmol/dm³, of Cl⁻ ions in the solution. [Mr KCl = 74.6] [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 74.0 mmol/dm³
    M1 NaCl: 2.6/58.5 = 0.0444 mol; M1 citrate: 2.9/294.0 = 0.00986 mol → Na⁺ = 3 × that = 0.0296 mol; A1 total 74.0 mmol/dm³.
  2. (b) 75.0 mmol/dm³
    B1 13.5/180.0 × 1000.
  3. (c) 64.6 mmol/dm³
    M1 Cl⁻ from both NaCl and KCl: 0.0444 + 0.0201; A1 64.6 mmol/dm³.
Q11Relative masses (Ar, Mr, isotopic mass) · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A sample of bromine contains two isotopes: ⁷⁹Br (isotopic mass 78.92, abundance 50.69 %) and ⁸¹Br (isotopic mass 80.92, abundance 49.31 %).

  1. (a) Define the term relative atomic mass. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the relative atomic mass of bromine. Give your answer to 4 significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The weighted average mass of the atoms of an element compared with 1/12 of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
    B1 weighted mean/average mass of atoms (of naturally occurring isotopes); B1 relative to 1/12 the mass of a ¹²C atom.
  2. (b) 79.91
    M1 (78.92 × 50.69 + 80.92 × 49.31)/100; A1 79.91.
Q12Relative masses (Ar, Mr, isotopic mass) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Gallium, used in LEDs, has two stable isotopes, ⁶⁹Ga and ⁷¹Ga. A sample has relative atomic mass 69.72.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage abundance of ⁶⁹Ga in the sample. Take the isotopic masses as 69.0 and 71.0. [2]
  2. (b) State one way in which the atoms of ⁶⁹Ga and ⁷¹Ga are the same and one way in which they differ. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 64.0 %
    M1 69.0y + 71.0(100 − y) = 6972; A1 y = 64.0 %.
