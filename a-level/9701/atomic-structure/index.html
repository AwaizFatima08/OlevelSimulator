Q4Ionisation energies · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

First ionisation energies in kJ/mol: hydrogen 1312, helium 2372, lithium 520.

  1. (a) Write an equation, with state symbols, for the first ionisation energy of lithium. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the first ionisation energy of helium is greater than that of hydrogen. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the first ionisation energy of lithium is much lower than that of helium. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Li(g) → Li⁺(g) + e⁻
    B1 correct species and state symbols (g) for Li and Li⁺.
  2. (b) He has two protons (greater nuclear charge) and its electrons are in the same 1s orbital/shell at a similar distance, with no extra shielding.
    M1 helium has more protons / greater nuclear charge. M1 electron removed from the same shell (1s) with no inner shielding, so attraction is greater (the small repulsion between the two 1s electrons does not outweigh this).
  3. (c) Lithium's outer electron is in the 2s orbital, further from the nucleus and shielded by the two 1s electrons, so it is held less strongly despite the extra proton.
    M1 outer electron in a new shell (2s), further from nucleus. M1 shielded by the full 1s shell, so weaker attraction outweighs the increase in nuclear charge.
Q5Ionisation energies · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

The first ionisation energies of the Period 3 elements, in kJ/mol, are:Na 496, Mg 738, Al 578, Si 787, P 1012, S 1000, Cl 1251, Ar 1521.

  1. (a) Explain why there is a general increase in first ionisation energy from sodium to argon. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the first ionisation energy of aluminium is lower than that of magnesium. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the first ionisation energy of sulfur is lower than that of phosphorus. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nuclear charge increases across the period while shielding stays roughly the same (electrons added to the same shell), so the outer electron is held more strongly / atomic radius decreases.
    M1 nuclear charge (number of protons) increasesM2 similar shielding / same shell, so greater attraction for outer electrons
  2. (b) In Al the electron is removed from a 3p orbital, which is higher in energy (further from the nucleus) and is shielded by the 3s electrons, so less energy is needed.
    M1 Al outer electron in 3p (Mg in 3s)M2 3p is higher in energy / further from nucleus / extra shielding by 3s²
  3. (c) In S the electron is removed from a 3p orbital containing a pair of electrons; repulsion between the paired electrons makes it easier to remove. P has three unpaired 3p electrons.
    M1 S has a paired electron in one 3p orbital (3p⁴); P is 3p³ with no pairsM2 spin-pair repulsion makes the paired electron easier to remove

Q15Ionisation energies · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

  1. (a) Explain why the first ionisation energy of gallium is lower than that of calcium, even though gallium has eleven more protons. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the first ionisation energy of selenium is slightly lower than that of arsenic. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the energy, in J, needed to remove one electron from one gaseous bromine atom. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹) [2]
Show answer

    M1 Se has a paired 4p electron / As has three unpaired 4p electrons. M1 spin-pair repulsion makes removal easier.
  3. (c) 1.89 × 10⁻¹⁸ J
    M1 1140 × 1000 J/mol. A1 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.89 × 10⁻¹⁸ J.
