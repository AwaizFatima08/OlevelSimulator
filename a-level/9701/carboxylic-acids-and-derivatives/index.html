Q1Acyl chlorides · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name three reagents that convert ethanoic acid into ethanoyl chloride. [3]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the reaction of ethanoic acid with SOCl₂. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why SOCl₂ is often preferred for this preparation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) phosphorus(V) chloride, PCl₅; phosphorus(III) chloride, PCl₃ (with heat); sulfur dichloride oxide (thionyl chloride), SOCl₂
    B1 each
  2. (b) CH₃COOH + SOCl₂ → CH₃COCl + SO₂ + HCl
    B1
  3. (c) The by-products SO₂ and HCl are gases and escape, so the acyl chloride is easy to separate.
    B1

  1. (a) Give the organic product of the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with each of: (i) water (ii) ethanol (iii) ammonia (iv) methylamine. [4]
  2. (b) Write an equation for reaction (ii). [1]
  3. (c) State what is seen when ethanoyl chloride is added to water. [1]
Show answer

    B1 each
  2. (b) CH₃COCl + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + HCl
    B1

    B1
Q3Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Name the type of mechanism for the reaction between ethanoyl chloride and ammonia. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the mechanism in words, stating where each pair of electrons moves. (You may describe curly arrows in words.) [4]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbonyl carbon of ethanoyl chloride is attacked much more readily than the carbon of chloroethane. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nucleophilic addition–elimination
    B1
  2. (b) 1 The lone pair on the N of NH₃ attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon.2 At the same time the π electrons of C=O move onto the oxygen, giving a tetrahedral intermediate with O⁻ and N⁺.3 The lone pair on O⁻ reforms the C=O and the C–Cl bond pair moves onto Cl, which leaves as Cl⁻.4 Cl⁻ (or another NH₃) removes H⁺ from the N, giving CH₃CONH₂ and HCl (NH₄Cl).
    M1 N lone pair attacks C of C=OM1 C=O π pair to O / intermediate with O⁻ and N⁺M1 C=O reforms and Cl⁻ leavesM1 loss of H⁺ from N
  3. (c) It is bonded to two electronegative atoms (O and Cl), making it strongly δ+, and it is planar/unhindered so the nucleophile can approach easily.
    B1
Q4Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of phenol with benzoyl chloride, C₆H₅COCl. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why phenyl benzoate is made from benzoyl chloride and not by heating benzoic acid with phenol and an acid catalyst. [1]
  3. (c) 2.35 g of phenol is reacted with excess benzoyl chloride. The yield of phenyl benzoate is 78.0%. Calculate the mass of phenyl benzoate obtained. [Mr: phenol 94.0; phenyl benzoate 198.0] [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₆H₅OH + C₆H₅COCl → C₆H₅COOC₆H₅ + HCl
    B1
  2. (b) Phenol reacts only very slowly with carboxylic acids (its lone pair is delocalised into the ring, so it is a poor nucleophile); the acyl chloride is much more reactive.
    B1
  3. (c) 3.86 g
    M1 n(phenol) = 2.35/94.0 = 0.0250 molM1 theoretical mass = 0.0250 × 198.0 = 4.95 gA1 × 0.780 = 3.86 g
Q5Acyl chlorides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A few drops of each of ethanoyl chloride, chloroethane and chlorobenzene are added to separate test-tubes containing aqueous silver nitrate in ethanol at room temperature.

  1. (a) Describe what you would see in each tube. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the difference in reactivity between chloroethane and chlorobenzene. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ethanoyl chloride: white precipitate immediately (with steamy fumes). Chloroethane: white precipitate forms slowly (on warming). Chlorobenzene: no precipitate.
    B1 each
  2. (b) In chlorobenzene a lone pair on Cl overlaps with the delocalised π system of the ring, giving the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character so it is stronger and harder to break; the ring electrons also repel nucleophiles.
    M1 Cl lone pair delocalised into ringM2 C–Cl bond stronger / partial double bond

Q10Relative acidity (acids, phenols, alcohols) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why phenol is a stronger acid than ethanol. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why ethanol is a weaker acid than water. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why ethanoic acid is a stronger acid than phenol. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) In the phenoxide ion the negative charge on O is delocalised into the benzene ring (a p orbital on O overlaps with the π system), which stabilises the anion. The ethoxide ion has no delocalisation.
    M1 charge on phenoxide delocalised into ringM2 anion stabilised, so dissociation more favourable; no delocalisation in ethoxide
  2. (b) The ethyl group is electron-donating, which increases the negative charge density on O in the ethoxide ion, destabilising it.
    B1
  3. (c) In the ethanoate ion the charge is delocalised over two electronegative oxygen atoms, which stabilises it more than delocalisation into a ring of carbon atoms.
    B1
Q11Relative acidity (acids, phenols, alcohols) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

pKa values at 298 K: CH₃COOH 4.76; ClCH₂COOH 2.87; Cl₂CHCOOH 1.29; Cl₃CCOOH 0.65.

  1. (a) Explain the trend in pKa values. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate Ka for chloroethanoic acid. [1]
  3. (c) Predict, with a reason, whether 2-chloropropanoic acid or 3-chloropropanoic acid is the stronger acid. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cl is electronegative and withdraws electron density (negative inductive effect) from the COO⁻ group, spreading the negative charge and stabilising the anion. More Cl atoms → greater stabilisation → more dissociation → stronger acid (lower pKa).
    M1 Cl electron-withdrawingM1 stabilises carboxylate anion / weakens O–HM1 effect increases with number of Cl
  2. (b) 1.35 × 10⁻³ mol/dm³
    B1 10⁻²·⁸⁷
  3. (c) 2-chloropropanoic acid: the Cl is closer to the COOH group, so its electron-withdrawing effect is greater.
    B1

  3. (c) 0.940 g of phenol reacts with excess sodium. Write the equation and calculate the volume of hydrogen produced at room conditions. [Mr: phenol, 94.0] [2]

Q13Relative acidity (acids, phenols, alcohols) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Three unlabelled bottles contain aqueous ethanoic acid, an aqueous solution of phenol and ethanol.

  1. (a) Describe how you could identify each liquid using no more than two reagents. Give the observations for each liquid. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why phenol does not produce carbon dioxide from sodium carbonate solution. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Add Na₂CO₃(aq) to each: only ethanoic acid effervesces (CO₂). Add bromine water to the other two: phenol decolourises the orange bromine water and gives a white precipitate; ethanol shows no change (stays orange). (Alternatively, neutral FeCl₃(aq) gives a violet colour with phenol and none with ethanol.)
    M1 Na₂CO₃ (or NaHCO₃) testA1 effervescence only with ethanoic acidM1 bromine water (or neutral FeCl₃) to separate phenol from ethanolA1 phenol: decolourised + white precipitate (or violet colour); ethanol: no change
  2. (b) Phenol is a weaker acid than carbonic acid (H₂CO₃ / HCO₃⁻), so it cannot protonate carbonate to release CO₂.
    B1
Q14Relative acidity (acids, phenols, alcohols) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

pKa of phenol = 9.95 at 298 K.

  1. (a) Calculate the pH of a 0.0500 mol/dm³ solution of phenol at 298 K. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why 4-nitrophenol (pKa 7.15) is a much stronger acid than phenol (pKa 9.95). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5.63
    M1 Ka = 10⁻⁹·⁹⁵ = 1.12 × 10⁻¹⁰M1 [H⁺] = √(1.12 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 0.0500) = 2.37 × 10⁻⁶A1 pH = 5.63
  2. (b) The NO₂ group is strongly electron-withdrawing, so it draws the negative charge of the phenoxide ion further away from O (through the ring), increasing delocalisation and stabilising the anion.
    M1 NO₂ electron-withdrawingM2 anion further stabilised / charge more delocalised
Q15Esters: formation & hydrolysis · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the name of the ester formed from propanoic acid and methanol?A propyl methanoateB methyl propanoateC methyl propanalD propyl methyl ether [1]
