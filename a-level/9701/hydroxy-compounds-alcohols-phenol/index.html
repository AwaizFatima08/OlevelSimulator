Q1Phenol: acidity & reactions · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reaction of phenol with aqueous sodium hydroxide. [1]
  2. (b) Place ethanol, phenol and water in order of increasing acidity. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why phenol is more acidic than ethanol. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅O⁻Na⁺ + H₂O
    B1
  2. (b) ethanol < water < phenol
    B1
  3. (c) In the phenoxide ion the negative charge on oxygen is delocalised into the benzene ring (a p-orbital lone pair overlaps with the π system), stabilising the anion; in ethoxide the ethyl group donates electrons, intensifying the charge on O; so phenol ionises more.
    B1 phenoxide charge delocalised into ring; B1 ethoxide charge concentrated / alkyl group electron-donating; B1 more stable anion means equilibrium further to the right / more H⁺ released

Show answer
  1. (a) C ethanol, CH₃CH₂OH

Q5Phenol: acidity & reactions · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain why phenol is a stronger acid than ethanol. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The phenoxide ion's negative charge is delocalised into the benzene ring, so the ion is stabilised. In ethanol, the ethoxide ion's charge is localised on O and the electron-donating alkyl group destabilises it.
    1 mark: delocalisation of the negative charge into the ring stabilises phenoxide; 1 mark: ethoxide charge is localised and destabilised by alkyl donation.

Q15Phenol: acidity & reactions · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Phenol has Ka = 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³ at 298 K. Calculate Kb for the phenoxide ion, C₆H₅O⁻, giving your answer in mol dm⁻³ to 2 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the pH of a 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ solution of sodium phenoxide. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Kb = 7.7 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³
    Kb = Kw / Ka = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ = 7.7 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.
