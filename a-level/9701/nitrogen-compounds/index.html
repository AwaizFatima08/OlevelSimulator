Q1Phenylamine & azo dyes · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give the reagents and conditions for the two stages needed to convert nitrobenzene into phenylamine. [3]
  2. (b) Phenylamine is shaken with bromine water at room temperature. Describe what is seen and name the organic product. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stage 1: tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid, heat under reflux. Stage 2: add aqueous sodium hydroxide (excess)
    B1 Sn + conc HCl. B1 heat/reflux. B1 then NaOH(aq) to release the free amine from its salt.
  2. (b) orange/brown bromine water decolourised and a white precipitate forms; 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine
    B1 decolourises + white ppt. B1 name.
Q2Phenylamine & azo dyes · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Phenylamine reacts with nitrous acid to form benzenediazonium chloride. State the reagents used to produce nitrous acid in the flask and the temperature needed. [2]
  2. (b) Give the formula of the benzenediazonium ion. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the temperature must be kept low, and write an equation for what happens if the solution is warmed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) sodium nitrite (NaNO₂) and dilute hydrochloric acid; below 10 °C (ice bath)
    B1 NaNO₂ + HCl. B1 temperature below 10 °C (accept 0–10 °C).
  2. (b) C₆H₅N₂⁺ (C₆H₅–N≡N⁺)
    B1.
  3. (c) Diazonium ion decomposes above about 10 °C; C₆H₅N₂⁺ + H₂O → C₆H₅OH + N₂ + H⁺
    B1 decomposes / unstable at higher temperature. B1 equation (phenol and nitrogen formed).
Q3Phenylamine & azo dyes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A solution of benzenediazonium chloride is added to a cold solution of phenol in aqueous sodium hydroxide. A yellow-orange precipitate forms. Give the structural formula of the product and name the type of reaction. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the product is coloured. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one use of compounds of this type. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4-hydroxyazobenzene: C₆H₅–N=N–C₆H₄–OH with the OH para (4-position) to the azo group; electrophilic substitution (coupling)
    B1 –N=N– linking two benzene rings. B1 OH in 4-position. B1 electrophilic substitution / coupling.
  2. (b) The –N=N– group links two benzene rings so there is an extended delocalised system; it absorbs light in the visible region
    B1 extended delocalisation / conjugation. B1 absorbs some visible wavelengths; the colour seen is the light not absorbed.
  3. (c) dyes (for fabric, e.g. cotton in Faisalabad mills) / food colouring / indicators
    B1.
Q4Phenylamine & azo dyes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Write an equation for the reduction of nitrobenzene to phenylamine using [H] to represent the reducing agent. [1]
  2. (b) 12.3 g of nitrobenzene gave 6.51 g of pure phenylamine. Calculate the percentage yield. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (Mr: C₆H₅NO₂ 123.0, C₆H₅NH₂ 93.0) [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₆H₅NO₂ + 6[H] → C₆H₅NH₂ + 2H₂O
    B1.
  2. (b) 70.0 %
    M1 n(nitrobenzene) = 12.3 ÷ 123.0 = 0.100 mol. M1 theoretical mass = 0.100 × 93.0 = 9.30 g. A1 6.51 ÷ 9.30 × 100 = 70.0 %.
Q5Phenylamine & azo dyes · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why phenylamine reacts with aqueous bromine at room temperature without a catalyst, whereas benzene does not react. [3]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the reaction and calculate the maximum mass of organic product from 2.79 g of phenylamine. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. (Mr: C₆H₅NH₂ 93.0, product 329.7) [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The lone pair on N is delocalised into the ring; this increases the electron density of the ring (especially at 2, 4, 6 positions); the ring is more easily attacked by electrophiles / Br₂ is polarised
    B1 lone pair on N overlaps with / delocalised into π system. B1 increased electron density in ring. B1 more susceptible to electrophilic attack, so no halogen carrier needed.
  2. (b) C₆H₅NH₂ + 3Br₂ → C₆H₂Br₃NH₂ + 3HBr; 9.89 g
    B1 equation. M1 n = 2.79 ÷ 93.0 = 0.0300 mol. A1 × 329.7 = 9.89 g.
Q6Phenylamine & azo dyes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Outline a four-step synthesis of the azo dye formed between benzene-derived diazonium ions and naphthalen-2-ol, starting from benzene. For each step give the reagents and conditions and the organic product. [6]
  2. (b) Suggest why naphthalen-2-ol is dissolved in aqueous sodium hydroxide before coupling. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 benzene → nitrobenzene: conc HNO₃ + conc H₂SO₄, below 55 °C. 2 nitrobenzene → phenylamine: Sn + conc HCl, reflux, then NaOH(aq). 3 phenylamine → benzenediazonium chloride: NaNO₂ + HCl, below 10 °C. 4 couple with naphthalen-2-ol in aqueous NaOH, below 10 °C → azo dye
    B1 for each correct reagent set (4) and B1 for correct temperatures in steps 1 and 3; B1 all intermediates named correctly. Max 6.
  2. (b) To form the naphthoxide (phenoxide-type) ion, which is more strongly activated / soluble in water
    B1 the anion makes the ring more electron-rich and dissolves the reagent.
Q7Phenylamine & azo dyes · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) 4-nitrophenylamine is diazotised and the product is coupled with phenol in alkaline solution. Give the structural formula and the molecular formula of the azo compound formed. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the Mr of the azo compound. [1]
  3. (c) A dye manufacturer wants to make the compound HO–C₆H₄–N=N–C₆H₄–CH₃ (both substituents in 4-positions). Identify the amine and the coupling agent that should be used, and explain why the coupling agent must contain an OH or NH₂ group. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) O₂N–C₆H₄–N=N–C₆H₄–OH (both groups at 4-positions); C₁₂H₉N₃O₃
    B1 structure. B1 molecular formula.
  2. (b) 243.0
    B1 12 × 12.0 + 9 × 1.0 + 3 × 14.0 + 3 × 16.0 = 243.0.
  3. (c) amine: 4-methylphenylamine (diazotised); coupling agent: phenol. The OH group activates the ring so the weakly electrophilic diazonium ion can substitute it
    B1 4-methylphenylamine. B1 phenol. B1 diazonium ion is a weak electrophile; it reacts only with activated rings.

Q11Amino acids, peptides & electrophoresis · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A mixture of lysine (isoelectric point 9.7), glycine (isoelectric point 6.0) and aspartic acid (isoelectric point 2.8) is placed in the centre of a strip of paper soaked in a buffer at pH 6.0. A voltage is applied. Predict and explain the movement of each amino acid. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest how the spots can be made visible after electrophoresis. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) lysine is positively charged at pH 6.0 and moves to the cathode (negative electrode); glycine is at its isoelectric point – zwitterion – does not move; aspartic acid is negatively charged and moves to the anode
    B1 each, with reason based on pH relative to isoelectric point.
  2. (b) spray with ninhydrin (or a locating agent) and warm
    B1 locating agent, e.g. ninhydrin.

Q13Amino acids, peptides & electrophoresis · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Electrophoresis is carried out at pH 1.0 on a mixture of glycine, the dipeptide Gly-Gly and lysine (which has two NH₂ groups). State the charge carried by each species at pH 1.0. [2]
  2. (b) Predict the order in which the three species are found from furthest to nearest from the starting point, towards the cathode. Explain your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) glycine +1; Gly-Gly +1; lysine +2
    B1 glycine and Gly-Gly +1 (amino group protonated, COOH un-ionised at pH 1.0). B1 lysine +2 (both amino groups protonated).
  2. (b) lysine (furthest) > glycine > Gly-Gly (nearest)
    B1 order. B1 lysine has the largest charge (charge/mass ratio highest). B1 glycine and Gly-Gly have the same charge but glycine is smaller, so moves further.
Q14Amino acids, peptides & electrophoresis · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A pentapeptide has the sequence Gly-Ala-Val-Ala-Gly. Calculate its Mr. (Mr: glycine 75.0, alanine 89.0, valine 117.0, water 18.0) [2]

  1. (a) 373.0

    B1 3. B1 glycine's α-carbon carries two H atoms.
  3. (c) Gly-Ala, Ala-Val, Val-Ala, Ala-Gly
    B1 for three correct. B1 all four with no extras.
Q15Amines: basicity & preparation · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Place ammonia, ethylamine and phenylamine in order of increasing base strength. [1]
  2. (b) Explain your order. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) phenylamine < ammonia < ethylamine
    B1.
  2. (b) Ethyl group is electron-donating so the lone pair on N is more available to accept H⁺; in phenylamine the lone pair is delocalised into the benzene ring, so it is less available
    B1 base strength depends on availability of N lone pair to accept a proton. B1 alkyl group pushes electron density onto N. B1 lone pair delocalised into ring in phenylamine.

  1. (a) Ethanamide is heated under reflux with dilute hydrochloric acid. Give the products. [2]
  2. (b) Ethanamide is heated with aqueous sodium hydroxide. Give the products and describe a test for the gas evolved. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH, and ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl
    B1 each.
  2. (b) sodium ethanoate, CH₃COONa, and ammonia; ammonia turns damp red litmus paper blue

Q23Amides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why ethanamide, CH₃CONH₂, is a much weaker base than ethylamine, CH₃CH₂NH₂. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why ethanamide is a solid at room temperature, whereas ethanoyl chloride is a liquid. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In ethanamide the lone pair on N is delocalised over the C=O group (the electronegative O withdraws electron density); the lone pair is much less available to accept a proton; in ethylamine the ethyl group donates electrons
    B1 lone pair delocalised with the C=O π system. B1 so less available to bond to H⁺. B1 ethyl group electron-donating in ethylamine.
  2. (b) Ethanamide molecules form hydrogen bonds (N–H…O=C); ethanoyl chloride molecules only have permanent dipole and London forces
    B1 hydrogen bonding in amide. B1 weaker forces in acyl chloride.
Q24Amides · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) N-methylpropanamide, CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃, is reduced by LiAlH₄ in dry ether. Give the structure of the product. [1]
  2. (b) 4.35 g of N-methylpropanamide (Mr 87.0) gave 2.92 g of the amine (Mr 73.0). Calculate the percentage yield. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest how the amide could be made from an acyl chloride. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CH₃CH₂CH₂NHCH₃
    B1.
  2. (b) 80.0 %
    M1 n = 4.35 ÷ 87.0 = 0.0500 mol; theoretical = 3.65 g. A1 80.0 %.
  3. (c) propanoyl chloride + methylamine
    B1 CH₃CH₂COCl + CH₃NH₂.
Q25Amides · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Paracetamol, HO–C₆H₄–NHCOCH₃ (substituents 1,4), is manufactured from 4-aminophenol. Name the two functional groups in paracetamol. [2]
  2. (b) Industry uses ethanoic anhydride: HO–C₆H₄–NH₂ + (CH₃CO)₂O → HO–C₆H₄–NHCOCH₃ + CH₃COOH. Calculate the atom economy of this reaction. (Mr: 4-aminophenol 109.0, ethanoic anhydride 102.0, paracetamol 151.0) [2]
  3. (c) Paracetamol is heated with excess aqueous sodium hydroxide. Give the structures of the organic products. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) phenol (OH on ring) and amide (secondary amide –NHCO–)
    B1 each.
  2. (b) 71.6 %
    M1 atom economy = Mr(desired product) ÷ ΣMr(reactants) × 100. A1 151.0 ÷ 211.0 × 100 = 71.6 %.
  3. (c) ⁻O–C₆H₄–NH₂ (sodium 4-aminophenoxide) and CH₃COO⁻Na⁺
    B1 amide hydrolysed to ethanoate. B1 4-aminophenoxide (phenol deprotonated by excess NaOH).
Q26Amides · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Three isomers, J, K and L, have the formula C₃H₇NO and are all amides. Each is heated with aqueous NaOH.J gives sodium propanoate and ammonia.K gives sodium ethanoate and methylamine.L gives sodium methanoate and dimethylamine, (CH₃)₂NH.Give the structures of J, K and L. [3]
  2. (b) Classify J, K and L as primary, secondary or tertiary amides. [1]
  3. (c) Only one of the three can be made from an acyl chloride and ammonia. Identify it and write the equation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) J CH₃CH₂CONH₂; K CH₃CONHCH₃; L HCON(CH₃)₂
    B1 each.
  2. (b) J primary; K secondary; L tertiary
    B1 all three.
  3. (c) J: CH₃CH₂COCl + NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CONH₂ + HCl
    B1 J. B1 equation.

Q29Amines & nitriles · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) 2-bromopropane is heated under reflux with KCN in ethanol. The organic product is then heated under reflux with dilute sulfuric acid. What is the final organic product?A butanoic acidB 2-methylpropanoic acidC propanoic acidD propan-2-ol [1]

  1. (a) B 2-methylpropanoic acid

Q30Amines & nitriles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Lactic acid, CH₃CH(OH)COOH, can be made from ethanal in two steps. Give the reagents and conditions for each step and the intermediate. [3]
  2. (b) Lactic acid made this way is not optically active, although its molecules are chiral. Explain why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 HCN with KCN catalyst (about 10–20 °C) → CH₃CH(OH)CN (2-hydroxypropanenitrile); 2 dilute HCl or H₂SO₄, heat under reflux → lactic acid
    B1 step 1. B1 intermediate. B1 step 2.
  2. (b) The C=O group in ethanal is planar; CN⁻ attacks equally from either side; a racemic mixture (50:50) of the two enantiomers forms, so the rotations cancel
    B1 planar carbonyl group. B1 equal chance of attack from above and below. B1 racemic mixture – no net rotation of plane-polarised light.
