Q1Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give the reagents and conditions for making chlorobenzene from benzene. [2]
  2. (b) Write equations to show how the catalyst forms the electrophile and how it is regenerated. [2]
  3. (c) Write the overall equation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Chlorine with a halogen carrier such as AlCl₃ (or FeCl₃ / iron); room temperature, anhydrous, in the dark
    B1 Cl₂; B1 AlCl₃/FeCl₃/Fe catalyst.
  2. (b) Cl₂ + AlCl₃ → Cl⁺ + AlCl₄⁻; H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl
    B1 each.
  3. (c) C₆H₆ + Cl₂ → C₆H₅Cl + HCl
    B1.
Q2Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Cyclohexene decolourises bromine water quickly at room temperature, but benzene does not. Explain. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why benzene undergoes substitution rather than addition reactions. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In cyclohexene the π electrons are localised in the C=C bond, giving high electron density that polarises Br₂. In benzene the π electrons are delocalised over six carbons, so electron density is lower and the ring is not attacked without a catalyst.
    B1 localised C=C high electron density; B1 delocalised π in benzene lower electron density.
  2. (b) Addition would destroy the delocalised π system, which gives extra stability; substitution keeps the delocalised ring intact.
    B1 delocalisation gives stability; B1 substitution retains it.
Q3Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) State the reagents and conditions for the nitration of benzene. [2]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the formation of the electrophile. [1]
  3. (c) Describe the mechanism of the reaction of benzene with this electrophile. Describe the curly arrows in words. [3]
  4. (d) Name this type of mechanism. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid; temperature below about 55 °C
    B1 both concentrated acids; B1 temperature (accept 25–55 °C).
  2. (b) HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻ + H₃O⁺
    B1 (accept HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O).
  3. (c) Curly arrow from the delocalised π ring to N of NO₂⁺; intermediate cation with a broken (horseshoe) delocalised system, positive charge, and C bearing H and NO₂; curly arrow from the C–H bond back into the ring, H⁺ lost (taken by HSO₄⁻), regenerating the aromatic ring.
    B1 arrow from π ring to NO₂⁺; B1 correct intermediate (partial delocalisation + charge); B1 C–H bond breaks, H⁺ lost, aromaticity restored.
  4. (d) Electrophilic substitution
    B1.
Q4Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Give the reagent and catalyst needed to convert benzene into ethylbenzene, and write the equation. [2]
  2. (b) Write an equation for the formation of the electrophile. [1]
  3. (c) Write an equation to show how the catalyst is regenerated. [1]
  4. (d) Ethylbenzene is made industrially as a step towards poly(phenylethene) packaging. Suggest why the AlCl₃ must be anhydrous. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Chloroethane with anhydrous AlCl₃ (heat); C₆H₆ + C₂H₅Cl → C₆H₅C₂H₅ + HCl
    B1 reagent + catalyst; B1 equation.
  2. (b) C₂H₅Cl + AlCl₃ → C₂H₅⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
    B1.
  3. (c) H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl
    B1.
  4. (d) Water would hydrolyse AlCl₃, so it could not accept a lone pair / generate the electrophile.
    B1.
Q5Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name the reagent and catalyst used to make phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃, from benzene, and write the equation. [2]
  2. (b) Give the formula of the electrophile. [1]
  3. (c) A student uses 7.80 g of benzene and obtains 8.40 g of phenylethanone. Calculate the percentage yield. (Mr: benzene 78.0; phenylethanone 120.0) [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ethanoyl chloride, CH₃COCl, with anhydrous AlCl₃; C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl
    B1; B1.
  2. (b) CH₃CO⁺
    B1 CH₃C⁺=O / CH₃CO⁺.
  3. (c) 70.0 %
    M1 theoretical = 0.100 mol × 120.0 = 12.0 g; A1 8.40/12.0 × 100 = 70.0 %.
Q6Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Methylbenzene reacts with bromine in the presence of FeBr₃ in the dark. Name the two main organic products and the mechanism. [3]
  2. (b) Methylbenzene reacts with bromine in ultraviolet light without a catalyst. Name the main organic product and the type of mechanism. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the conditions determine whether the ring or the side chain reacts. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1-bromo-2-methylbenzene and 1-bromo-4-methylbenzene; electrophilic substitution
    B1 each product (ring substitution at 2 and 4 positions); B1 electrophilic substitution.
  2. (b) (Bromomethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Br; free-radical substitution (in the side chain)
    B1 product; B1 free-radical substitution.
  3. (c) With a halogen carrier (FeBr₃), Br⁺ / polarised Br₂ is formed, an electrophile that attacks the electron-rich ring. In UV, Br₂ splits homolytically into Br• radicals, which abstract H from the CH₃ group.
    B1 catalyst makes an electrophile for the ring; B1 UV makes radicals that attack the side chain.
Q7Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Predict the main organic product(s) of mononitration of: (i) methylbenzene; (ii) nitrobenzene; (iii) benzoic acid. [3]
  2. (b) Classify the substituents CH₃, NO₂ and COOH as 2,4-directing or 3-directing. [1]
  3. (c) A chemist needs 3-nitrobenzoic acid. Suggest whether to nitrate methylbenzene and then oxidise the side chain, or oxidise first and then nitrate. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 1-methyl-2-nitrobenzene and 1-methyl-4-nitrobenzene; (ii) 1,3-dinitrobenzene; (iii) 3-nitrobenzoic acid
    B1 each.
  2. (b) CH₃ 2,4-directing; NO₂ and COOH 3-directing
    B1 all.
  3. (c) Oxidise first to benzoic acid, then nitrate: COOH is 3-directing so NO₂ goes to position 3. Nitrating methylbenzene first would put NO₂ at 2 or 4.
    B1 oxidise first; B1 reason based on directing effects.
Q8Arenes: electrophilic substitution · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the number of different mononitro products that can be formed from each dimethylbenzene: (i) 1,2-dimethylbenzene; (ii) 1,3-dimethylbenzene; (iii) 1,4-dimethylbenzene. Ignore any directing effects. [3]
  2. (b) A student mononitrates an unknown dimethylbenzene and obtains a single product. Identify it and give the name of the product. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 2; (ii) 3; (iii) 1
    B1 each. 1,2: positions 3 (=6) and 4 (=5). 1,3: positions 2, 4 (=6) and 5. 1,4: all four ring H equivalent.
  2. (b) 1,4-dimethylbenzene; product 1,4-dimethyl-2-nitrobenzene
    B1; B1.
Q9Alkenes: electrophilic addition · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the major product when propene reacts with hydrogen bromide?A 2-bromopropaneB 1-bromopropaneC 1,2-dibromopropaneD propan-2-ol [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 2-bromopropane
    H adds to the CH₂ end to give the more stable secondary carbocation CH₃C⁺HCH₃, which Br⁻ attacks → 2-bromopropane. 1-bromopropane would form via the less stable primary carbocation, so it is the minor product.
Q10Alkenes: electrophilic addition · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) In the reaction of ethene with bromine, how does the bromine molecule behave?A as an electrophile after a dipole is induced in itB as a nucleophile donating a lone pair to carbonC as a free radical formed by homolytic fissionD as a base accepting a proton from ethene [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A as an electrophile after a dipole is induced in it
    The high π electron density of C=C induces a dipole in Br₂; the δ+ Br accepts a pair of electrons, so Br₂ acts as an electrophile. A nucleophile would donate electrons, which is what the alkene does.
Q11Alkenes: electrophilic addition · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the mechanism of the reaction between ethene and bromine. Describe the curly arrows and intermediate in words. [4]
  2. (b) Name the mechanism. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The C=C π electrons induce a dipole in Br₂ (Brδ+–Brδ−). Curly arrow from the C=C to Brδ+, and from the Br–Br bond to Brδ−. A carbocation CH₂Br–CH₂⁺ and Br⁻ form. Curly arrow from a lone pair on Br⁻ to the C⁺, giving 1,2-dibromoethane.
    B1 induced dipole in Br₂; B1 arrows from C=C to Br and Br–Br bond to Br; B1 carbocation intermediate + Br⁻; B1 Br⁻ lone pair attacks C⁺.
  2. (b) Electrophilic addition
    B1.
Q12Alkenes: electrophilic addition · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the conditions for the industrial hydration of ethene and write the equation. [2]
  2. (b) But-1-ene is hydrated in the same way. Name the major and minor products. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why one product of (b) is formed in greater amount. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Steam, phosphoric acid catalyst, about 300 °C and 6–7 MPa; C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH
    B1 catalyst + two of T/p; B1 equation.
  2. (b) Major: butan-2-ol; minor: butan-1-ol
    B1 each.
  3. (c) It forms via the more stable secondary carbocation (Markovnikov addition).
    B1.
Q13Alkenes: electrophilic addition · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) 2-methylpropene, (CH₃)₂C=CH₂, reacts with HCl. Draw (describe) the two possible carbocation intermediates and classify each as primary, secondary or tertiary. [2]
  2. (b) Name the major product and explain why it is formed in greater amount. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (CH₃)₃C⁺ tertiary; (CH₃)₂CHCH₂⁺ primary
    B1 each with classification.
  2. (b) 2-chloro-2-methylpropane. The tertiary carbocation is more stable because three alkyl groups donate electrons (positive inductive effect) and spread the positive charge, so it forms faster/more readily.
    B1 product; B1 tertiary carbocation more stable; B1 alkyl groups electron-donating / inductive effect.
Q14Alkenes: electrophilic addition · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why but-2-ene gives only one structural isomer with HBr, while but-1-ene gives two. [2]
  2. (b) 2-bromobutane is formed as a racemic mixture. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) But-2-ene is symmetrical, so H adding to either carbon gives the same secondary carbocation and 2-bromobutane only. But-1-ene is unsymmetrical: primary or secondary carbocation → 1-bromobutane or 2-bromobutane.
    B1 symmetry of but-2-ene; B1 two different carbocations for but-1-ene.
  2. (b) C2 is a chiral centre; the carbocation is planar, so Br⁻ attacks from either side with equal probability, giving equal amounts of the two enantiomers.
    B1 planar carbocation attacked from both sides; B1 equal amounts of two optical isomers (optically inactive mixture).

Q20Alkenes: oxidation with KMnO4 · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) 2-methylbut-2-ene, (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃, is heated with hot, concentrated, acidified KMnO₄. What are the organic products?A propanone and ethanoic acidB propanone and ethanalC butanone and methanoic acidD 2-methylbutane-2,3-diol [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A propanone and ethanoic acid
    The C=C is broken. (CH₃)₂C= becomes (CH₃)₂C=O (propanone); =CHCH₃ becomes CH₃CHO, which is further oxidised to CH₃COOH under these conditions. Ethanal (B) is not the final product; the diol (D) forms only with cold dilute KMnO₄.

    B1.

Q29Arenes: structure & bonding · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe the bonding in a benzene molecule, including hybridisation and the arrangement of the π electrons. [3]
  2. (b) All C–C bonds in benzene are 0.139 nm long. Explain how this is evidence against a structure with alternating single and double bonds. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each C is sp² hybridised, forming σ bonds to two C and one H (planar, 120°). Each C has one unhybridised p orbital; these overlap sideways all round the ring to form a delocalised π system above and below the plane, containing 6 electrons.
    B1 sp², σ bonds, planar 120°; B1 p orbitals overlap sideways; B1 delocalised ring of 6 π electrons above and below plane.
  2. (b) Alternating bonds would have two different lengths (C–C ≈ 0.154 nm and C=C ≈ 0.134 nm); equal lengths show delocalisation.
    B1.

  1. (a) Use the enthalpy change for cyclohexene to predict the enthalpy change of hydrogenation of the Kekulé structure (cyclohexa-1,3,5-triene). [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the stabilisation (delocalisation) energy of benzene. [1]
  3. (c) Explain what this value shows about benzene. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the enthalpy change for cyclohexa-1,3-diene, −232 kJ/mol, is slightly less exothermic than 2 × −120 kJ/mol. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) −360 kJ/mol
    B1 3 × −120.
  2. (b) 152 kJ/mol
    B1 −208 − (−360) = 152.
  3. (c) Benzene is 152 kJ/mol more stable (lower in energy) than the Kekulé structure because the π electrons are delocalised.
    B1 more stable / less energy released; B1 due to delocalisation.
  4. (d) Its two double bonds are conjugated, giving some delocalisation and extra stability.
    B1.
