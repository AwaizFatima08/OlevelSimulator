Q1Redox titrations with transition-metal ions · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

25.0 cm³ of an acidified solution of iron(II) sulfate is titrated with 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ potassium manganate(VII). The mean titre is 22.40 cm³.

  1. (a) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. [1]
  2. (b) State the colour change at the end-point and explain why no indicator is needed. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the concentration of Fe²⁺ in the solution. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) MnO₄⁻ + 5Fe²⁺ + 8H⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O
    B1
  2. (b) From pale green/colourless to the first permanent pale pink; MnO₄⁻ is intensely purple and is decolourised until Fe²⁺ is used up, so it acts as its own indicator.
    B1 colour change. B1 self-indicating.
  3. (c) 0.0896 mol dm⁻³
    M1 mol MnO₄⁻ = 0.02240 × 0.0200 = 4.480 × 10⁻⁴; mol Fe²⁺ = × 5 = 0.00224. A1 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0896 mol dm⁻³

Show answer
  1. (a) 65.3 mg
    M1 mol MnO₄⁻ = 0.01170 × 0.00200 = 2.34 × 10⁻⁵. M1 mol Fe²⁺ in 25.0 cm³ = × 5 = 1.17 × 10⁻⁴; in 250 cm³ = × 10. A1 × 55.8 = 65.3 mg

Q11Redox titrations with transition-metal ions · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) Use electronic configurations to explain why zinc and scandium are d-block elements but not transition elements. [2]

Show answer
  1. (a) A d-block element that forms one or more stable ions with an incomplete d subshell.
    B1

Q20Transition element properties (variable oxidation states, catalysis) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Peroxodisulfate ions oxidise iodide ions: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂E°: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2SO₄²⁻ +2.01 V; Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺ +0.77 V; I₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2I⁻ +0.54 V

  1. (a) Both reactants are negative ions, which repel each other, so the activation energy is high.

    B1

Show answer
  1. (a) E° must be less positive than +2.01 V (so S₂O₈²⁻ can oxidise the lower oxidation state) and more positive than +0.54 V (so the higher oxidation state can oxidise I⁻).

Q28d-orbitals & electronic configuration of ions · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Third ionisation energies / kJ mol⁻¹: Mn 3248; Fe 2957

  1. (a) Give the electronic configurations of Mn²⁺ and Fe²⁺ in the form [Ar]3dⁿ. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the third ionisation energy of manganese is greater than that of iron. [3]
  3. (c) Use the data to explain why aqueous Fe²⁺ is easily oxidised to Fe³⁺ in air, while Mn²⁺ is much harder to oxidise to Mn³⁺. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mn²⁺ [Ar]3d⁵; Fe²⁺ [Ar]3d⁶
    B1
  2. (b) Mn's third electron is removed from a half-filled 3d⁵ subshell (all electrons unpaired); Fe's third electron is removed from 3d⁶ where one orbital holds a pair; repulsion between the paired electrons makes that electron easier to remove.
    B1 Mn: from 3d⁵ (half-filled, stable). B1 Fe: from a paired 3d orbital. B1 spin-pair repulsion lowers Fe's IE3.
  3. (c) Less energy is needed to remove the third electron from Fe (291 kJ mol⁻¹ less than for Mn), and Fe³⁺ gains the stable 3d⁵ configuration.
    B1
Q29d-orbitals & electronic configuration of ions · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) An ion M²⁺ has 25 electrons. Identify M, give the configuration of M²⁺ and the number of unpaired electrons. [3]
  2. (b) An ion Y³⁺ has the same number of electrons as V²⁺. Identify Y and give the configuration of Y³⁺. [2]
  3. (c) Copper(I) compounds such as CuCl are white, whereas copper(II) compounds are usually blue or green. Explain this in terms of electronic configuration. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) M is cobalt (Z = 27); Co²⁺ [Ar]3d⁷; 3 unpaired electrons
    B1 Co. B1 [Ar]3d⁷. B1 3.
  2. (b) Y is chromium (Z = 24); Cr³⁺ [Ar]3d³
    M1 V²⁺ has 23 − 2 = 21 electrons, so Y has 24 protons. A1 Cr³⁺ [Ar]3d³

    B1 Cu⁺ 3d¹⁰ full, no transition. B1 Cu²⁺ 3d⁹ allows d–d transition.
