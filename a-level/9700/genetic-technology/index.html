Q1Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Outline how bacteria can be genetically modified to produce human insulin. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) mRNA for insulin is extracted from pancreatic β-cells; reverse transcriptase makes cDNA (no introns); sticky ends added / DNA cut with a restriction enzyme; plasmids cut with the same restriction enzyme; insulin gene and plasmid joined by DNA ligase (recombinant plasmid); plasmids taken up by bacteria (transformation); bacteria with the plasmid identified using a marker gene; cultured in a fermenter; insulin extracted and purified
    1 mark each for any six of: mRNA from β-cells; reverse transcriptase → cDNA; restriction enzyme / complementary sticky ends; plasmid as vector cut with same enzyme; DNA ligase; promoter added; transformation (e.g. heat shock with Ca²⁺); marker gene (e.g. GFP) to identify transformed cells; cloning/culture in fermenter; extraction and purification.
Q2Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State the role of each of the following in gene transfer: (i) restriction enzyme, (ii) DNA ligase, (iii) plasmid, (iv) sticky ends. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why cDNA made from mRNA, rather than the gene cut from human chromosomes, is inserted into bacteria. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) cuts DNA at a specific base sequence (recognition site); (ii) joins sugar–phosphate backbones of DNA fragments by forming phosphodiester bonds; (iii) vector that carries the gene into the host cell (and replicates there); (iv) short single-stranded overhangs that pair with complementary sticky ends by hydrogen bonding, so fragments join
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Human genes contain introns; bacteria cannot remove introns (no splicing), so the gene would not be expressed correctly; cDNA from mature mRNA has no introns
    1 mark: genomic gene contains introns / cDNA does not. 1 mark: bacteria cannot splice out introns.
Q3Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the gene for green fluorescent protein (GFP) can be used as a marker to identify bacteria that have taken up a recombinant plasmid. [3]
  2. (b) Antibiotic-resistance genes were once widely used as markers. Suggest two reasons why fluorescent markers are now often preferred. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) GFP gene is placed in the plasmid alongside the desired gene; bacteria that take up the plasmid express GFP; under ultraviolet (blue) light these colonies fluoresce green, so they can be identified and selected
    1 mark each: GFP gene inserted with/next to the gene of interest (same plasmid); transformed bacteria make GFP; glow green under UV light / can be picked out.
  2. (b) Avoids spreading antibiotic resistance genes to other (pathogenic) bacteria; cells can be identified without killing non-transformed cells / no need for antibiotics; results seen quickly and easily
    1 mark each for any two sensible reasons.
Q4Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The restriction enzyme EcoRI recognises the sequence GAATTC and cuts between G and A on each strand, leaving sticky ends. One strand of a 43-nucleotide linear DNA molecule is:5′ ATCGGAATTCTAGCCTAGGCATTACGGAATTCAGTTCAGCATG 3′

  1. (a) State the number of fragments produced when this DNA is cut with EcoRI. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the length, in nucleotides, of each fragment of the strand shown. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the same restriction enzyme must be used to cut the plasmid and the DNA containing the gene. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3
    Two recognition sites (GAATTC at positions 5–10 and 27–32), so a linear molecule gives 3 fragments.
  2. (b) 5, 22 and 16 nucleotides
    Cuts after the G of each site (after nucleotides 5 and 27): 5, 27 − 5 = 22 and 43 − 27 = 16. 1 mark for two correct; 2 marks all three.
  3. (c) It produces complementary sticky ends on both; the sticky ends can base pair (hydrogen bond) with each other so ligase can join gene and plasmid
    1 mark: same/complementary sticky ends. 1 mark: base pairing allows fragments to join before ligase seals them.
Q5Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Bt cotton, widely grown in Pakistan, contains a gene from the bacterium Bacillus thuringiensis that codes for a protein toxic to caterpillars such as bollworms. Explain two benefits of growing Bt cotton. [3]
  2. (b) Discuss two concerns about growing Bt cotton. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Plants are protected from insect pests so yield increases; less insecticide spraying needed, which reduces costs for farmers and harm to the farmer's health and to non-target insects/environment; toxin is specific to certain insects
    1 mark each for any three of: reduced crop damage/higher yield; less pesticide spraying; lower cost; less exposure of farm workers to sprays; fewer non-target organisms killed (beneficial insects); toxin is specific.
  2. (b) Pests may evolve resistance to the Bt toxin (selection), especially if refuges are not used; the gene may spread to wild relatives by pollen; seed is costly and must often be bought each year; other (secondary) pests not affected may increase; effects on non-target insects
    1 mark each for any three points, at least one explained (e.g. resistance: individuals with resistance alleles survive and reproduce, so allele frequency rises).
Q6Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Golden rice has been genetically modified to produce β-carotene in its endosperm (the part of the grain that is eaten). The body converts β-carotene to vitamin A. Explain why the inserted genes are joined to a promoter that is active only in endosperm cells. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why golden rice was developed and discuss one objection that has been raised against it. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A promoter is needed for RNA polymerase to bind and transcribe the gene; an endosperm-specific promoter means the genes are expressed only in the grain (the eaten part), not wasting resources in leaves/roots
    1 mark: promoter allows transcription (RNA polymerase binding). 1 mark: expression limited to endosperm/edible part.
  2. (b) Vitamin A deficiency causes blindness and weak immunity in children in countries where rice is the staple food; golden rice provides vitamin A cheaply in the normal diet. Objections: a varied diet/supplements could solve the problem; possible gene flow to other rice; concerns about control by companies or farmers' acceptance; unknown long-term effects
    1 mark: vitamin A deficiency/blindness in rice-dependent populations. 1 mark: provides vitamin A in staple food. 1 mark: one objection explained.
Q7Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A GM Atlantic salmon contains a growth hormone gene from another salmon species, joined to a promoter from a fish called the ocean pout. In one trial the GM salmon reached market mass in 18 months, compared with 36 months for non-GM salmon.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage reduction in the time to reach market mass. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the growth hormone gene was joined to a promoter from the ocean pout rather than its own promoter. [2]
  3. (c) The GM salmon are all sterile females and are farmed only in tanks on land. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 50 %
    (36 − 18) ÷ 36 × 100 = 50 %.
  2. (b) The normal promoter is only active in certain seasons/conditions; the ocean pout promoter is active all year, so growth hormone is produced continuously and the fish grow faster
    1 mark: promoter controls when the gene is transcribed. 1 mark: pout promoter switched on all year → continuous growth hormone production.
  3. (c) To prevent GM fish escaping and breeding with wild salmon; this avoids spreading the transgene into wild populations / GM fish outcompeting wild fish
    1 mark: sterile so cannot reproduce if they escape. 1 mark: land tanks prevent escape; protects wild populations from gene flow/competition.
Q8Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Outline how the CRISPR–Cas9 system can be used to inactivate (knock out) a specific gene in a plant. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest how CRISPR–Cas9 could instead be used to insert a corrected allele. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A guide RNA is made with a base sequence complementary to part of the target gene; it is combined with the Cas9 enzyme (a nuclease); the guide RNA base pairs with the target DNA, so Cas9 cuts both strands at that point; the cell repairs the break, often with errors (insertions/deletions), causing a frameshift so the gene no longer codes for a functional protein
    1 mark each: guide RNA complementary to target sequence; Cas9 nuclease guided to site; cuts both DNA strands (double-strand break); repair introduces mutation / frameshift, inactivating gene.
  2. (b) Supply a DNA template containing the desired allele with sequences matching either side of the cut; the cell uses this template to repair the break, so the new sequence is incorporated
    1 mark: provide repair template with the correct sequence. 1 mark: template has ends matching the DNA around the cut, so it is used in repair.
Q9Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A plasmid carries two antibiotic-resistance genes: one for ampicillin and one for tetracycline. A human gene is inserted into the middle of the tetracycline-resistance gene. After transformation, bacteria are spread on agar containing ampicillin and 200 colonies grow. The colonies are then copied, in the same positions, onto agar containing tetracycline; 46 colonies grow.

  1. (a) Explain how growing the colonies on the two types of agar allows bacteria containing the recombinant plasmid to be identified. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of colonies on the ampicillin plate that contain the recombinant plasmid. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Only bacteria with a plasmid (either type) grow on ampicillin, because all plasmids carry the ampicillin-resistance gene; inserting the gene into the tetracycline-resistance gene inactivates it; so bacteria with recombinant plasmids grow on ampicillin but not on tetracycline, while those with the original plasmid grow on both
    1 mark each: ampicillin selects bacteria that took up any plasmid; insert disrupts tetracycline resistance gene; recombinant colonies are those that grow on ampicillin but fail to grow on tetracycline (replica plating).
  2. (b) 77 %
    M1: 200 − 46 = 154 colonies did not grow on tetracycline. A1: 154 ÷ 200 × 100 = 77 %.
Q10Recombinant DNA & GM organisms · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Erythropoietin (EPO) is a human glycoprotein hormone. It is produced commercially using genetically modified mammalian cells grown in culture rather than bacteria. Suggest why. [3]
  2. (b) Before GM bacteria were used, diabetics were treated with insulin extracted from pigs and cattle. Give three advantages of insulin made by GM bacteria. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) EPO has carbohydrate chains added after translation (in the Golgi body / ER); bacteria have no Golgi or ER so cannot add these chains; without them the protein may be inactive or quickly removed; mammalian cells modify the protein like human cells
    1 mark each: EPO needs glycosylation/post-translational modification; bacteria lack ER/Golgi (cannot glycosylate); mammalian cells can produce the correctly modified/functional protein.
  2. (b) Identical to human insulin so fewer immune/allergic reactions; can be produced in large quantities to meet demand; cheaper/faster; no ethical or religious objections to animal source; no risk of transmitting animal diseases; consistent quality
    1 mark each for any three.
Q11Gene therapy & genetic screening · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Severe combined immunodeficiency (SCID) caused by a faulty gene for the enzyme ADA has been treated by gene therapy. Describe how this is done. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why gene therapy in humans is carried out on body (somatic) cells but not on egg, sperm or embryo cells. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stem cells (or T-lymphocytes) are taken from the patient's bone marrow/blood; a normal allele of the ADA gene is inserted into a virus vector (e.g. a retrovirus/lentivirus); the virus infects the cells and inserts the gene into their DNA; the modified cells are returned to the patient, where they produce ADA
    1 mark each for any four of: cells removed from patient (bone marrow stem cells/T-cells); normal allele/functional gene; viral vector (retrovirus/lentivirus); gene inserted into cells' DNA; cells cultured and returned; cells now produce ADA so immune system works.
  2. (b) Germ-line changes would be passed to future generations; who could not consent, and long-term effects are unknown (ethical concerns; not permitted in most countries)
    1 mark: germ-line changes inherited by offspring. 1 mark: ethical issue – no consent / unknown effects / 'designer babies'.
Q12Gene therapy & genetic screening · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by genetic screening. [1]
  2. (b) β-thalassaemia is a recessive genetic disorder that is common in Pakistan. Suggest two ways in which genetic screening could be used to reduce the number of children born with β-thalassaemia. [2]
  3. (c) Give two ethical concerns about genetic screening. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Testing a person's (or embryo's) DNA to find out whether they carry a particular allele, e.g. one that causes a genetic disease
    Testing DNA for presence of specific alleles/mutations.
  2. (b) Screening couples before marriage/pregnancy to find carriers so they can make informed decisions; prenatal testing of a fetus; screening embryos from IVF (pre-implantation genetic diagnosis) so only unaffected embryos are implanted
    1 mark each for any two: carrier screening of couples/relatives; prenatal diagnosis; embryo (PGD) screening with IVF.
  3. (c) Decisions to terminate pregnancies; stress/anxiety from results; confidentiality and discrimination (e.g. employers, insurance, marriage prospects); results may reveal information about relatives; could lead to selecting embryos for non-medical traits
    1 mark each for any two.
Q13Gene therapy & genetic screening · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Compare the use of viruses and liposomes as vectors for gene therapy. [4]
  2. (b) Gene therapy for cystic fibrosis using liposomes in an inhaled spray must be repeated regularly. Suggest why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Viruses enter cells efficiently and some insert genes into host DNA, so the effect is long-lasting; but they may trigger an immune response and may insert into/disrupt another gene. Liposomes (lipid spheres) fuse with cell surface membranes, cause little immune response and cannot cause infection; but they are less efficient at delivering genes and the gene is not integrated, so the effect is temporary
    Max 4: virus – efficient entry; may integrate (long-lasting); immune response; risk of insertional mutation. Liposome – fuses with membrane; little immune response/safe; less efficient; temporary expression.
  2. (b) The gene is delivered to epithelial cells lining the airways; these cells are short-lived and are replaced by new cells that do not carry the gene; the gene is not inserted into stem cells/host chromosomes
    1 mark: airway epithelial cells are constantly replaced. 1 mark: new cells lack the gene / gene not integrated or passed on.
Q14Gene therapy & genetic screening · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Screening shows that a husband and wife are both carriers of β-thalassaemia (heterozygous). Calculate the probability that their first child will have β-thalassaemia. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the probability that their first two children will both be unaffected. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why marriages between close relatives (for example, first cousins) increase the chance of a child having a recessive disorder such as β-thalassaemia. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.25 (25 %, 1 in 4)
    Tt × Tt gives 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt, so P(tt) = 0.25.
  2. (b) 0.56
    M1: P(unaffected) = 0.75 for each child (independent events). A1: 0.75 × 0.75 = 0.5625 → 0.56.
  3. (c) Relatives share many alleles inherited from common ancestors; so both partners are more likely to carry the same recessive allele, increasing the chance of a homozygous recessive child
    1 mark: shared alleles from common ancestor. 1 mark: both more likely to be heterozygous for the same allele → more homozygous recessive offspring.
Q15Gene therapy & genetic screening · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) In early gene therapy trials for one form of SCID, a retrovirus vector was used. Some of the treated children later developed leukaemia. Suggest how the gene therapy could have caused leukaemia. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two ways in which the risks of this type of gene therapy could be reduced. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Retroviruses insert their DNA at random positions in host chromosomes; in some cells the vector inserted next to/within a gene controlling cell division (proto-oncogene); this activated the gene (or disrupted a tumour suppressor gene), so the cell divided uncontrollably
    1 mark each: random insertion into host DNA; inserted near a proto-oncogene/tumour suppressor gene; abnormal activation/disruption → uncontrolled mitosis (cancer).
  2. (b) Use vectors that do not integrate (e.g. AAV or liposomes) or that integrate more safely (modified lentiviruses with self-inactivating promoters); use gene editing to correct the gene at its normal position; screen treated cells before returning them; monitor patients
    1 mark each for any two sensible suggestions.
Q16Gene therapy & genetic screening · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) In one study, women with a harmful BRCA1 allele had a 72 % lifetime risk of breast cancer, compared with 12 % for women in the general population. Calculate how many times greater the risk was for women with the BRCA1 allele. [1]
  2. (b) Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of offering screening for harmful BRCA1 alleles to women whose relatives have had breast cancer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6 times
    72 ÷ 12 = 6.
  2. (b) Advantages: women with the allele can have more frequent checks so cancer is found early; can choose preventive treatment (e.g. surgery); relatives can be tested. Disadvantages: a positive result does not mean cancer will develop (risk not certainty), causing anxiety; a negative result does not rule out cancer; possible discrimination (insurance/employment/marriage); results affect relatives who did not choose testing; costly
    Max 3 for one side; need both sides for 4 marks. 1 mark each for valid points.
Q17PCR, electrophoresis & sequencing · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the steps of one cycle of the polymerase chain reaction (PCR), giving the approximate temperature of each step. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why Taq polymerase, from a bacterium living in hot springs, is used in PCR. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Denaturation at about 95 °C: hydrogen bonds break and the DNA strands separate; annealing at about 55–65 °C: primers bind (base pair) to complementary sequences at each end of the target sequence; extension at about 72 °C: Taq polymerase adds free nucleotides to the primers to build new complementary strands
    1 mark each for any four of: 95 °C (90–98 °C) denaturation; strands separate/H bonds broken; 50–65 °C annealing of primers to complementary sequences; 72 °C extension; Taq polymerase adds nucleotides from primer.
  2. (b) It is thermostable – it is not denatured at 95 °C; so it does not need to be replaced after every cycle, and its optimum temperature is about 72 °C
    1 mark: not denatured at high (95 °C) temperature. 1 mark: does not need to be added each cycle / works at 72 °C.
Q18PCR, electrophoresis & sequencing · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A forensic scientist has a sample containing 50 copies of a target DNA sequence from a hair root found at a crime scene in Islamabad.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of copies of the target DNA after 25 cycles, assuming the number doubles in every cycle. Give your answer in standard form to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Each cycle takes 3 minutes. Calculate the total time for 25 cycles. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why two different primers are needed. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.68 × 10⁹
    M1: 50 × 2²⁵. A1: 50 × 33 554 432 = 1 677 721 600 = 1.68 × 10⁹.
  2. (b) 75 minutes
    25 × 3 = 75 min.
  3. (c) Each primer is complementary to the 3′ end of one of the two strands of the target sequence; DNA polymerase needs a primer to start adding nucleotides; using one for each strand means both strands are copied and only the region between them is amplified
    1 mark: one primer per strand (strands have different sequences/are antiparallel). 1 mark: polymerase needs a primer to start / defines the region copied.
Q19PCR, electrophoresis & sequencing · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A DNA profile was used in a family court case. Bands (fragment lengths in kilobases, kb) were:child: 1.2, 2.5, 3.1, 4.0, 5.6mother: 1.2, 3.1, 4.8, 6.0man X: 2.5, 4.0, 5.6, 7.2man Y: 2.5, 3.9, 6.0, 8.1

  1. (a) Explain how gel electrophoresis separates DNA fragments. [3]
  2. (b) State which man could be the biological father. Explain your answer. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why DNA sequencing of a disease-causing gene can give more information than a DNA profile. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) DNA is negatively charged (phosphate groups), so in an electric field it moves towards the positive electrode (anode); the gel acts as a sieve/mesh; smaller fragments move more easily and travel further in a given time
    1 mark each: negative charge on phosphates; moves to anode/positive terminal; smaller fragments move faster/further through gel pores.
  2. (b) Man X; the child's bands that are not from the mother (2.5, 4.0 and 5.6 kb) all match bands of man X, but man Y lacks the 4.0 and 5.6 kb bands
    1 mark: man X. 1 mark: all non-maternal bands of the child are present in man X (man Y lacks two of them).
  3. (c) Sequencing shows the exact order of bases, so a specific mutation (e.g. a single base change) can be identified; a profile only shows fragment lengths
    Exact base sequence revealed / identifies specific mutations.
Q20PCR, electrophoresis & sequencing · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A DNA ladder was run alongside a PCR product. Distances moved by the ladder bands from the well:1000 bp: 12 mm | 500 bp: 24 mm | 250 bp: 36 mm | 125 bp: 48 mmThe PCR product moved 30 mm.

  1. (a) Use the ladder to estimate the size of the unknown fragment. Give your answer to the nearest base pair. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why a ladder of fragments of known size is run in a separate lane of the same gel. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 354 bp
    M1: each extra 12 mm halves the size (size = 1000 ÷ 2^((d − 12)/12)). M1: unknown at 30 mm is 18 mm beyond the 1000 bp band, so 1000 ÷ 2^1.5. A1: 1000 ÷ 2.828 = 353.6 → 354 bp. (Allow 350–360 bp read from a semi-log graph for 2 marks.)
  2. (b) Distance moved depends on conditions (voltage, time, gel concentration), so the ladder must experience the same conditions; it gives a calibration to compare the unknown with
    1 mark: same conditions/same gel and run time. 1 mark: allows size of unknown to be determined by comparison.
Q21Microarrays & bioinformatics · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a microarray can be used to compare the genes being expressed in cancer cells and in normal cells from the same tissue. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) mRNA is extracted from each type of cell; reverse transcriptase makes cDNA, labelled with a different fluorescent dye for each sample (e.g. red for cancer, green for normal); the cDNA is added to the microarray, where it hybridises (base pairs) with complementary DNA probes for thousands of genes; unbound cDNA is washed off; the array is scanned and the colour/intensity of each spot shows which genes are expressed in which sample
    1 mark each for any four of: mRNA extracted; converted to cDNA; fluorescent labels (two colours); hybridisation to complementary probes on the chip; washing; scanning – colour/intensity shows expression level.
Q22Microarrays & bioinformatics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Researchers compared gene expression in leaves of drought-stressed and well-watered wheat plants using a microarray. cDNA from drought-stressed plants was labelled with a red dye and cDNA from watered plants with a green dye. Mean intensities (arbitrary units):gene P: red 1200, green 300gene Q: red 400, green 400gene R: red 150, green 600

  1. (a) Calculate the ratio of red to green intensity for each gene. [2]
  2. (b) Using your ratios, describe the expression of each gene in the drought-stressed plants compared with the watered plants. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the experiment should be repeated with samples from several plants. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = 4.0; Q = 1.0; R = 0.25
    1200 ÷ 300 = 4.0; 400 ÷ 400 = 1.0; 150 ÷ 600 = 0.25. 2 marks all three, 1 mark for two.
  2. (b) P is expressed four times more in drought-stressed plants (switched on/up-regulated); Q is unchanged; R is expressed four times less (down-regulated)
    1 mark: P up-regulated, R down-regulated. 1 mark: Q unchanged, with use of the factor of four.
  3. (c) Individual plants vary; repeats show whether the differences are consistent/reliable (and allow statistical testing)
    Natural variation between plants; check reliability.
Q23Microarrays & bioinformatics · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A short gene sequence of 60 bases from a newly discovered bacterium was compared with the matching sequence of a known species using a database search. The sequences differed at 9 positions. Calculate the percentage identity of the two sequences. [1]
  2. (b) Explain what is meant by bioinformatics and outline how databases of DNA and protein sequences can be used to identify an unknown pathogen. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why comparing a longer sequence would give a more reliable result. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 85 %
    (60 − 9) ÷ 60 × 100 = 85 %.
  2. (b) Bioinformatics is the collection, storage and analysis of biological data (e.g. sequences) using computers; the sequence of the unknown organism is entered into a search program; it is compared/aligned with sequences in the database; the most similar sequences suggest the identity or closest relatives of the pathogen
    1 mark: definition (computer storage/analysis of biological information). 1 mark: unknown sequence compared/aligned with database sequences. 1 mark: closest match identifies organism/relationship.
  3. (c) A short sequence may match several species by chance / gives fewer differences to compare; longer sequences give more data so the comparison is more accurate
    More bases compared, chance matches less important.
Q24Skill: statistics (SD, SE, CI, t-test, Spearman, chi-squared) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

An agricultural researcher in Punjab compared seed-cotton yield per plant (g) for Bt cotton and non-Bt cotton grown in the same field.Bt cotton: n = 10, mean = 92.4 g, standard deviation = 8.1 gnon-Bt cotton: n = 10, mean = 81.7 g, standard deviation = 9.3 g

  1. (a) State a null hypothesis for this investigation. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the value of t, using t = (x̄₁ − x̄₂) ÷ √(s₁²/n₁ + s₂²/n₂). Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [3]
  3. (c) The critical value of t at p = 0.05 for 18 degrees of freedom is 2.10. Explain what your result shows. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) There is no significant difference between the mean yield per plant of Bt cotton and non-Bt cotton
    Must refer to no (significant) difference in mean yield between the two varieties.
  2. (b) t = 2.74
    M1: s₁²/n₁ = 65.61 ÷ 10 = 6.561; s₂²/n₂ = 86.49 ÷ 10 = 8.649. M1: √15.21 = 3.90. A1: 10.7 ÷ 3.90 = 2.74.
  3. (c) 2.74 is greater than 2.10, so the null hypothesis is rejected; there is a significant difference in mean yield (the difference is unlikely to be due to chance, p < 0.05)
    1 mark: compares calculated t with critical value (greater). 1 mark: reject null hypothesis / difference is significant (not due to chance).
Q25Skill: calculations & data processing · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A student transformed E. coli with 0.05 µg of plasmid carrying a GFP gene and an ampicillin-resistance gene. After heat shock, the bacteria were mixed with nutrient broth to a total volume of 1000 µl. 100 µl of this mixture was spread on an agar plate containing ampicillin, and 380 glowing colonies grew.

  1. (a) Calculate the total number of transformed cells produced in the 1000 µl mixture. [1]
  2. (b) Transformation efficiency is the number of transformed cells per µg of plasmid DNA. Calculate the transformation efficiency. Give your answer in standard form. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why only 100 µl of the mixture, rather than all of it, was spread on the plate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3800
    100 µl gave 380 colonies, so 1000 µl contains 380 × 10 = 3800.
  2. (b) 7.6 × 10⁴ transformants per µg
    M1: 3800 ÷ 0.05. A1: 76 000 = 7.6 × 10⁴ per µg.
  3. (c) So that colonies are separate/countable (not overlapping)
    Too many colonies would merge and could not be counted.
Q26Skill: conclusions & interpreting data · Standard · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

Three lines of GM rice (A, B and C) carrying genes for β-carotene synthesis were grown together with non-GM rice. β-carotene content of the grain was measured in three samples from each line (µg per g of grain):non-GM: 0.0, 0.0, 0.0 (mean 0.0)line A: 1.4, 1.9, 1.5 (mean 1.6)line B: 6.2, 7.5, 6.8 (mean 6.8)line C: 21.0, 28.5, 36.0 (mean 28.5)

  1. (a) A student concludes that line C is the best line to grow for vitamin A. Using the data, discuss the extent to which this conclusion is supported. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the non-GM rice was included. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Support: line C has the highest mean β-carotene (28.5 µg/g), about four times line B; its range does not overlap with A or B. Against: line C has the largest variation (range 21.0–36.0), so results are less consistent; only three samples per line; plants grown in one place/season only; β-carotene content is not the same as vitamin A delivered (losses in storage/cooking, absorption); yield and other traits not measured; no statistical test done
    1 mark: highest mean quoted. 1 mark: no overlap with other lines / clear difference. Max 2 limitations from: large spread in C; small sample (3); one site/season; β-carotene may be lost in storage/cooking; yield/taste not considered; no statistical test.
  2. (b) As a control to show that β-carotene is produced because of the inserted genes (non-GM rice has none)
    Control/comparison showing the effect is due to the genetic modification.
Q27Skill: explaining results with biology · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 5 marks

A student ran PCR to amplify one gene from the DNA of a chickpea plant, using different annealing temperatures. Products were run on a gel. Results:50 °C: strong band of correct size plus three other bands55 °C: one strong band of correct size60 °C: one strong band of correct size65 °C: one faint band of correct size70 °C: no bands

  1. (a) Explain why no product was formed at 70 °C. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why extra, unexpected bands appeared at 50 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest the best annealing temperature from these results and give a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) At high temperature the hydrogen bonds between primer and template are unstable / break, so the primers cannot remain base-paired (anneal) to the template; without primers Taq polymerase cannot start making new strands
    1 mark: primers cannot anneal / hydrogen bonds not stable at 70 °C. 1 mark: polymerase needs bound primer to begin extension.
  2. (b) At low temperature primers can bind to sequences that are only partly complementary (mismatches tolerated, some hydrogen bonds still form); so other regions of DNA are also copied, giving products of different lengths
    1 mark: primers bind to partly complementary/non-target sites. 1 mark: other DNA regions amplified, giving different-sized fragments.
  3. (c) 55–60 °C, because a single strong band of the correct size was produced
    55 or 60 °C (or between) with reason: single, strong correct band.
Q28Skill: errors, reliability & improvements · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A class in Peshawar used gel electrophoresis to compare DNA fragments from three plant samples. Several groups reported that: bands were smeared, some lanes were empty, the smallest fragments were not visible, and different groups obtained different band positions for the same sample. The method: wells were made with a comb before the gel had fully set; samples were added with a dropper; the gel was run at a high voltage for 2 hours; no DNA ladder was used.

  1. (a) Identify two sources of error in this procedure that could explain the poor results, and suggest an improvement for each. [4]
  2. (b) Explain the difference between repeatability and accuracy in this investigation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Samples loaded unevenly / overflowing wells → use a micropipette with fresh tips and load the same volume carefully; gel run too long so small fragments ran off → run for a shorter time / check with loading dye; voltage too high causing smearing/overheating → use a lower voltage; DNA degraded/contaminated → use fresh samples/sterile equipment; wells damaged → let the gel set fully before removing comb; no ladder → include a DNA ladder
    1 mark for each error linked to the observations (max 2) and 1 mark for each matching improvement (max 2).
  2. (b) Repeatability: getting the same band pattern when the procedure is repeated; accuracy: how close the estimated fragment sizes are to the true sizes
    1 mark each correct definition applied to the context.
Q29Skill: variables & controls · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A student plans to investigate how the duration of heat shock at 42 °C (0, 15, 30, 45, 60, 90 s) affects the uptake of a plasmid containing GFP and ampicillin-resistance genes by E. coli. Transformed bacteria are spread on ampicillin agar.

  1. (a) Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable, and state how the dependent variable would be measured. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how three other variables should be controlled. [3]
  3. (c) Describe a suitable control and explain its purpose. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Independent: duration of heat shock (s); dependent: transformation efficiency / number of colonies (glowing colonies) on the plate, counted after incubation (e.g. 24 h at 37 °C)
    1 mark: IV and DV correctly named. 1 mark: DV measured by counting (fluorescent) colonies after fixed incubation time.
  2. (b) Temperature of heat shock kept at 42 °C using a thermostatically controlled water bath; same mass/volume of plasmid DNA added (using a micropipette); same volume and concentration of bacteria (same culture, same optical density); same time on ice before and after; same volume spread and incubation temperature/time
    1 mark each for any three variables each with a method of control (variable alone = 0).
  3. (c) Bacteria treated in the same way but with no plasmid added; shows that colonies arise only from transformation (no growth on ampicillin/no glowing)
    No-plasmid (or no heat shock) control with purpose.
Q30Skill: hypotheses & predictions · Core · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

Ecologists want to know whether pollen from Bt maize, which drifts onto wild plants beside fields, harms the caterpillars of a butterfly that feeds on those plants. Caterpillars will be fed leaves dusted with Bt maize pollen, leaves dusted with non-Bt maize pollen, or leaves with no pollen, and survival after 7 days recorded.

  1. (a) State a hypothesis that the investigation could test. [1]
  2. (b) State the null hypothesis. [1]
  3. (c) Predict the results if Bt toxin in pollen harms these caterpillars, and explain why caterpillars fed on leaves with no pollen should also be included. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Caterpillars fed on leaves dusted with Bt maize pollen have a lower survival rate than those fed on leaves with non-Bt pollen (or: Bt maize pollen reduces survival of these caterpillars)
    Testable statement linking pollen type to survival.
  2. (b) There is no significant difference in survival between caterpillars fed on leaves with Bt pollen and those fed on leaves with non-Bt pollen
    No significant difference in survival between the groups.
  3. (c) Prediction: survival lower (and growth slower) in the Bt pollen group than in the non-Bt pollen group. The no-pollen group shows whether pollen itself (not the toxin) affects survival / gives a baseline
    1 mark: correct directional prediction. 1 mark: no-pollen control separates effect of pollen from effect of toxin.
