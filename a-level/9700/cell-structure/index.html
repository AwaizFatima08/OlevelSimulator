Practice questions

Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) An electron micrograph of a cell from the pancreas shows four structures, described below.P: a stack of flattened, curved membrane-bound sacs with small vesicles budding from the edgesQ: an organelle with a double membrane; the inner membrane is folded into shelvesR: a network of flattened membrane-bound sacs with many small dark granules attached to the outer surfaceS: a large structure bounded by two membranes with gaps (pores) in themName structures P, Q, R and S. [4]
  2. (b) State two functions of structure P. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P Golgi body (Golgi apparatus); Q mitochondrion; R rough endoplasmic reticulum; S nucleus (nuclear envelope)
    1 mark each. Accept Golgi complex for P. Do not accept 'ER' alone for R.
  2. (b) Any two: modifies proteins (e.g. adds carbohydrate to make glycoproteins); packages proteins into (secretory) vesicles; makes lysosomes
    1 mark each for any two of: modifies/processes proteins (or lipids); adds carbohydrate / forms glycoproteins; packages/sorts into vesicles for secretion (exocytosis); forms lysosomes.
Q2Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which structure is found in both plant and animal cells but is never found in prokaryotic cells?A 70S ribosomeB Golgi bodyC cell wallD circular DNA [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Golgi body
    The Golgi body is a membrane-bound organelle, found only in eukaryotic cells. Prokaryotes have 70S ribosomes, circular DNA and a cell wall (peptidoglycan), so A, C and D are found in prokaryotes; a cell wall is also absent from animal cells.
Q3Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A palisade mesophyll cell from a mango leaf is compared with a liver cell (hepatocyte). Name three structures found in the palisade mesophyll cell that are not found in the liver cell. [3]
  2. (b) Name one structure found in the liver cell that is not found in the palisade mesophyll cell. [1]
  3. (c) Liver cells contain a large amount of smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER). State two functions of SER. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cell wall; chloroplasts; large permanent vacuole (with tonoplast) — also accept plasmodesmata
    1 mark each for any three of: cellulose cell wall; chloroplast; large (permanent) vacuole / tonoplast; plasmodesmata.
  2. (b) Centrioles (centrosome)
    Centriole(s). Also accept microvilli. Do not accept lysosome or mitochondrion (both types of cell have these).
  3. (c) Synthesis of lipids (including steroids/phospholipids); detoxification of drugs/toxins (e.g. alcohol)
    1 mark each for any two of: lipid synthesis; steroid synthesis; detoxification; storage of calcium ions (in muscle).
Q4Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Name the organelle in which most ATP is produced in a cell respiring aerobically. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the structure of a mitochondrion as seen in an electron micrograph. [3]
  3. (c) The middle section of a human sperm cell is packed with mitochondria. Suggest why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mitochondrion
    Mitochondrion / mitochondria.
  2. (b) Surrounded by an envelope of two membranes; the inner membrane is folded into cristae; the inside is a fluid matrix; the matrix contains 70S ribosomes and circular DNA
    1 mark each for any three of: double membrane / envelope; inner membrane folded into cristae; matrix; 70S (small) ribosomes in matrix; circular DNA (loop) in matrix; intermembrane space.
  3. (c) The tail (flagellum) needs a lot of ATP for movement; mitochondria release energy/produce ATP by aerobic respiration close to where it is used
    1 mark: (aerobic) respiration produces large amounts of ATP. 1 mark: ATP needed for beating of the tail / swimming to the egg.
Q5Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the function of the nucleolus?A makes ribosomal RNA and assembles ribosome subunitsB makes proteins that are secreted from the cellC controls the movement of molecules into and out of the nucleusD stores the chromosomes during interphase [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A makes ribosomal RNA and assembles ribosome subunits
    The nucleolus is where rRNA is transcribed and combined with proteins to form ribosome subunits. Secreted proteins are made on the rough ER (B); the nuclear envelope and its pores control movement in and out (C).
Q6Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Chloroplasts contain two structures that are also found in prokaryotic cells. Name these two structures. [2]
  2. (b) Name the stacks of membranes inside a chloroplast and state their function. [1]
  3. (c) Name the fluid surrounding these stacks. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 70S ribosomes; circular DNA
    1 mark each: 70S/small ribosomes; circular DNA (loop of DNA).
  2. (b) Grana (stacks of thylakoids); site of the light-dependent stage / contain chlorophyll that absorbs light
    Grana/thylakoids AND absorb light / light-dependent reactions.
  3. (c) Stroma
    Stroma.
Q7Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Researchers in a laboratory in Karachi supplied pancreatic cells with radioactive amino acids for a short time and then followed where the radioactivity was found. The time after supply at which radioactivity was highest in each place is shown.rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER): 3 minGolgi body: 20 minsecretory vesicles: 40 minoutside the cell: 90 min

  1. (a) Explain the order in which the radioactivity peaks in the structures listed above. [4]
  2. (b) Name the process by which the protein leaves the cell. [1]
  3. (c) Vesicles move through the cytoplasm towards the cell surface membrane. Explain the role of microtubules in this movement. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Amino acids are joined into protein on ribosomes of the RER first; the protein is moved in vesicles to the Golgi body where it is modified and packaged; secretory vesicles carry it to the cell surface membrane; it is then released from the cell
    1 mark each: (1) protein synthesised by ribosomes on RER (first peak at 3 min); (2) transported in (transport) vesicles to Golgi body; (3) modified/packaged in Golgi, e.g. glycosylated, (peak 20 min); (4) secretory vesicles (40 min) move to and fuse with cell surface membrane, so protein appears outside (90 min).
  2. (b) Exocytosis
    Exocytosis.
  3. (c) Microtubules form tracks along which vesicles are carried; motor proteins (using ATP) move the vesicles along the microtubules
    1 mark: microtubules act as tracks/part of the cytoskeleton. 1 mark: vesicles moved along them by motor proteins / using ATP.
Q8Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Cells lining the bronchi of people living in a city with heavy winter smog have both cilia and microvilli. Describe two differences between cilia and microvilli in structure and function. [3]
  2. (b) Particles in smog can stop the cilia in the bronchi from beating. Suggest and explain one effect this has on the person. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cilia contain microtubules (9 + 2 arrangement) and are longer; microvilli are small finger-like folds of the cell surface membrane with no microtubules. Cilia beat to move mucus; microvilli increase the surface area for absorption
    Max 3: cilia contain microtubules, microvilli do not; cilia are larger/longer; cilia move/beat (to waft mucus), microvilli do not move; microvilli increase surface area (for absorption/exchange); both covered by cell surface membrane (no credit alone).
  2. (b) Mucus (with trapped dust and bacteria) is no longer moved up and out of the airways; it builds up in the bronchi, so the risk of infection increases / the person coughs to remove it
    1 mark: mucus not moved towards the throat / accumulates in airways. 1 mark: trapped bacteria/particles remain, so more infections (e.g. bronchitis) / coughing / narrowed airways.
Q9Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a lysosome. [1]
  2. (b) A neutrophil engulfs a bacterium. Explain how lysosomes are involved in destroying the bacterium. [3]
  3. (c) Lysosomal enzymes work best at about pH 5, but the cytoplasm is at about pH 7.2. Suggest how this protects the cell if a lysosome bursts. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A small vesicle surrounded by a single membrane, containing hydrolytic (digestive) enzymes
    Membrane-bound sac/vesicle containing hydrolytic enzymes.
  2. (b) The bacterium is enclosed in a phagocytic vacuole; lysosomes fuse with the vacuole; hydrolytic enzymes are released into it and digest (hydrolyse) the bacterium
    1 mark each: bacterium in phagocytic vacuole/phagosome; lysosome fuses with vacuole; enzymes (e.g. proteases, lysozyme) hydrolyse/digest the bacterium.
  3. (c) At pH 7.2 the enzymes have much lower activity (are partly denatured / active site shape is changed), so they do little damage to the cell's own molecules
    1 mark: enzymes are less active at cytoplasm pH. 1 mark: so cell components are not digested / cell protected from self-digestion.
Q10Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which organelles are surrounded by a double membrane?1 nucleus2 mitochondrion3 Golgi body4 chloroplastA 1, 2 and 4B 2 and 4 onlyC 1, 2 and 3D 2, 3 and 4 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 1, 2 and 4
    The nuclear envelope, mitochondrial envelope and chloroplast envelope each have two membranes. The Golgi body is made of single-membrane sacs, so any option containing 3 is wrong. B forgets that the nuclear envelope is a double membrane.
Q11Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure and function of plasmodesmata. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the large permanent vacuole helps to support a non-woody plant such as a spinach seedling. [2]
  3. (c) Root hair cells do not contain chloroplasts. Suggest why. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Narrow channels through the cell walls of adjacent plant cells, lined with cell surface membrane and containing cytoplasm; allow movement of substances (water, ions, sugars) / communication between cells
    1 mark structure: pores/channels through cell walls connecting cytoplasm of neighbouring cells. 1 mark function: transport of substances/communication between cells (symplast pathway).
  2. (b) The vacuole (cell sap) has a low water potential so water enters by osmosis; the vacuole swells and pushes the cytoplasm against the cell wall, making the cell turgid
    1 mark: water enters vacuole by osmosis (across tonoplast). 1 mark: cell becomes turgid, pressing on the cell wall, so tissues are firm.
  3. (c) They are underground / receive no light, so cannot photosynthesise
    Roots are in the dark so photosynthesis is not possible.
Q12Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Fish living in the sea lose water to the sea water and gain salts. 'Chloride cells' in their gills pump chloride ions out of the blood into the sea water, against a concentration gradient. Electron micrographs show that these cells contain very many mitochondria. Suggest why. [2]
  2. (b) The cell surface membrane of a chloride cell is highly folded. Suggest the advantage of this. [2]
  3. (c) Predict and explain the effect of a respiratory poison such as cyanide on the movement of chloride ions by these cells. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pumping ions against a concentration gradient is active transport, which needs ATP; mitochondria supply ATP by aerobic respiration
    1 mark: active transport (against gradient). 1 mark: requires ATP from (aerobic) respiration in mitochondria.
  2. (b) Increases the surface area of membrane; more carrier proteins/pumps can be present so more ions transported per unit time
    1 mark: larger surface area. 1 mark: more transport (carrier) proteins / faster rate of ion transport.
  3. (c) Movement of chloride ions out would (almost) stop, because no/little ATP is made, so the pumps cannot work
    1 mark: active transport stops/decreases. 1 mark: aerobic respiration inhibited so ATP not produced.
Q13Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Plasma cells develop from B-lymphocytes and secrete large quantities of antibody. Explain why plasma cells have an extensive rough endoplasmic reticulum. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why plasma cells also have a large Golgi body. [2]
  3. (c) In one study, RER made up 5 % of the cytoplasm volume in a resting B-lymphocyte and 35 % in a plasma cell. Calculate how many times greater the proportion of RER is in the plasma cell. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Antibodies are proteins; RER has ribosomes that synthesise proteins (and transports them / folds them) for secretion
    1 mark: antibodies are proteins (glycoproteins). 1 mark: ribosomes on RER synthesise proteins; RER transports/folds them.
  2. (b) The Golgi body modifies the antibody (adds carbohydrate to form glycoprotein) and packages it into secretory vesicles for exocytosis
    1 mark: modification/glycosylation. 1 mark: packaging into (secretory) vesicles.
  3. (c) 7 times
    35 ÷ 5 = 7.
Q14Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Leydig cells in the testis secrete the steroid hormone testosterone. Beta cells in the pancreas secrete the protein hormone insulin. Suggest and explain which organelle is likely to be more abundant in each cell type. [3]
  2. (b) Both cell types contain many secretory vesicles. Testosterone, however, can pass straight through membranes. Suggest why testosterone does not need to be released by exocytosis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Leydig cells: much smooth ER, because steroids are lipids made by SER. Beta cells: much rough ER (and Golgi), because insulin is a protein made by ribosomes on RER
    1 mark: Leydig – SER. 1 mark: Beta – RER (and Golgi). 1 mark: correct reason linking SER to lipid/steroid synthesis and RER to protein synthesis (both needed).
  2. (b) Steroids are lipid-soluble/non-polar, so they can diffuse through the phospholipid bilayer of the cell surface membrane
    1 mark: lipid-soluble/non-polar. 1 mark: diffuses through phospholipid bilayer.
Q15Cell ultrastructure & organelles · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Colchicine is a drug that stops tubulin molecules joining together to form microtubules. Predict and explain the effect of colchicine on a dividing cell in a root tip. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest the effect of colchicine on a pancreatic cell that is secreting enzymes. [2]
  3. (c) Centrioles are made of microtubules. State where centrioles are found in an animal cell. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Spindle cannot form (spindle fibres are microtubules), so chromosomes / chromatids cannot be separated and mitosis stops (at metaphase)
    1 mark: no spindle formed. 1 mark: chromatids not pulled apart / mitosis cannot be completed.
  2. (b) Secretion reduced, because vesicles from the Golgi are moved along microtubules to the cell surface membrane; without microtubules they are not transported
    1 mark: less exocytosis/secretion. 1 mark: vesicle transport depends on microtubules.
  3. (c) In the centrosome, near the nucleus
    Near the nucleus / in the centrosome (a pair at right angles).
Q16Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) An electron micrograph shows a mitochondrion with an image length of 24 mm. The magnification is ×12 000. Calculate the actual length of the mitochondrion in µm. [2]
  2. (b) Convert your answer to nm. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2 µm
    M1: 24 mm = 24 000 µm. A1: 24 000 ÷ 12 000 = 2 µm.
  2. (b) 2000 nm
    1 µm = 1000 nm, so 2 × 1000 = 2000 nm.
Q17Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A transmission electron microscope can show ribosomes but a light microscope cannot. What is the reason?A Electrons have a much shorter wavelength than light, so resolution is higher.B Electron microscopes have higher magnification, so small objects appear larger.C Ribosomes are stained only by heavy metals.D Light passes straight through ribosomes. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Electrons have a much shorter wavelength than light, so resolution is higher.
    Resolution depends on wavelength: the shorter wavelength of electrons lets two points closer together than about 200 nm be distinguished. Magnifying more (B) without better resolution only gives a larger, blurred image.
Q18Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between magnification and resolution. [2]
  2. (b) A red blood cell has a diameter of 7.5 µm. In a textbook drawing its diameter is 30 mm. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Magnification is how many times larger the image is than the actual object; resolution is the ability to distinguish two points close together as separate (the smallest distance between two points that can be seen as separate)
    1 mark each correct definition.
  2. (b) ×4000
    M1: 30 mm = 30 000 µm. A1: 30 000 ÷ 7.5 = ×4000.
Q19Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Standard · Paper 3 style · 6 marks

A student in Faisalabad looks at a leaf epidermis with a light microscope fitted with an eyepiece graticule. Using the ×40 objective lens, 40 eyepiece graticule units line up exactly with 25 divisions of a stage micrometer. Each stage micrometer division is 0.01 mm.

  1. (a) Calculate the length of one eyepiece graticule unit in µm. [2]
  2. (b) The lengths of five guard cells measured in eyepiece units were 11, 12, 10, 13 and 12. Calculate the mean actual length of the guard cells in µm. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the eyepiece graticule must be calibrated again if the student changes to the ×10 objective lens. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.25 µm
    M1: 25 divisions × 10 µm = 250 µm. A1: 250 ÷ 40 = 6.25 µm.
  2. (b) 72.5 µm
    M1: mean = 58 ÷ 5 = 11.6 eyepiece units. A1: 11.6 × 6.25 = 72.5 µm.
  3. (c) The eyepiece graticule stays the same size in the eyepiece, but the specimen's image size changes with magnification, so each eyepiece unit represents a different actual length
    1 mark: graticule scale does not change when objective changes. 1 mark: magnification of specimen changes so value of each unit changes.
Q20Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A micrograph of a leaf cell has a scale bar labelled 5 µm. The scale bar is 18 mm long on the micrograph. A chloroplast in the same micrograph is 27 mm long.

  1. (a) Calculate the magnification of the micrograph. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the actual length of the chloroplast in µm. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×3600
    M1: 18 mm = 18 000 µm. A1: 18 000 ÷ 5 = ×3600.
  2. (b) 7.5 µm
    M1: 27 mm = 27 000 µm. A1: 27 000 ÷ 3600 = 7.5 µm (allow ecf from a).
Q21Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A nucleus has a diameter of 8 µm. A student draws it at a magnification of ×2500. What is the diameter of the drawing?A 2.0 × 10¹ mmB 2.0 × 10⁰ mmC 3.2 × 10⁻³ mmD 2.0 × 10² mm [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 2.0 × 10¹ mm
    8 µm × 2500 = 20 000 µm = 20 mm = 2.0 × 10¹ mm. B comes from dividing by 10 000 instead of 1000 when converting µm to mm.
Q22Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A ribosome has a diameter of 25 nm. Calculate the diameter of its image in mm if it is viewed at a magnification of ×1500. [2]
  2. (b) The resolution of a light microscope is about 200 nm. Explain why ribosomes cannot be seen as separate structures with a light microscope, even though their image could be made larger by greater magnification. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one advantage of using a light microscope rather than an electron microscope to study malaria parasites inside red blood cells. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.0375 mm (3.75 × 10⁻² mm)
    M1: 25 × 1500 = 37 500 nm. A1: 37 500 nm = 37.5 µm = 0.0375 mm.
  2. (b) Ribosomes (25 nm) are much smaller than the resolution limit, and are closer together than 200 nm; the wavelength of light is too long, so they cannot be distinguished as separate points – increasing magnification only enlarges a blurred image
    1 mark: ribosomes smaller than/closer together than 200 nm limit (wavelength of light too long). 1 mark: magnifying beyond the resolution limit gives no more detail / image is blurred.
  3. (c) Living cells can be observed (e.g. to watch movement); or colour can be seen with stains; or cheaper/portable for field clinics
    Any one: living specimens can be viewed; natural/stained colour seen; quicker/simpler preparation; cheaper/portable.
Q23Microscopy, magnification & resolution · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) On an electron micrograph a bacterium that is 2 µm long has an image length of 50 mm. Calculate the magnification. [2]
  2. (b) A bacteriophage virus attached to the same bacterium has an image length of 2.5 mm. Calculate the actual length of the virus in nm. [2]
  3. (c) State one feature that would be visible in a transmission electron micrograph of the bacterium but would not be seen in a scanning electron micrograph. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×25 000
    M1: 50 mm = 50 000 µm. A1: 50 000 ÷ 2 = ×25 000.
  2. (b) 100 nm
    M1: 2.5 mm = 2500 µm ÷ 25 000 = 0.1 µm. A1: 0.1 µm × 1000 = 100 nm.
  3. (c) Internal structures, e.g. ribosomes / DNA (nucleoid) / cell wall in section
    SEM shows only the surface; TEM shows internal detail, e.g. ribosomes.
Q24Prokaryotes & viruses · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Vibrio cholerae is the bacterium that causes cholera. State four ways in which the structure of Vibrio cholerae differs from the structure of a cell lining the inside of the human cheek. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Vibrio: no nucleus (DNA free in cytoplasm); circular DNA (not linear chromosomes with histones); 70S ribosomes (not 80S); peptidoglycan cell wall (cheek cell has no wall); no membrane-bound organelles such as mitochondria/ER/Golgi; may have plasmids and a flagellum; much smaller
    1 mark each for any four comparative points: no nucleus/nuclear envelope; circular DNA not linear; DNA not associated with histones; 70S v 80S ribosomes; cell wall of peptidoglycan present; no membrane-bound organelles (named); plasmids present; smaller size (1–5 µm v about 50 µm).
Q25Prokaryotes & viruses · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which structure is present in all viruses?A a protein capsidB a phospholipid envelopeC ribosomesD DNA [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A a protein capsid
    Every virus has nucleic acid surrounded by a protein coat (capsid). Only some viruses, such as HIV and influenza, have an envelope (B); some viruses contain RNA instead of DNA (D), and no virus has ribosomes.
Q26Prokaryotes & viruses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why viruses are not considered to be cells. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the structure of an enveloped virus such as influenza. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) They have no cytoplasm, ribosomes or cell surface membrane of their own / no metabolism; they can only reproduce inside a host cell using the host's machinery
    1 mark each for any two of: no cytoplasm; no ribosomes/organelles; no cell surface membrane (envelope comes from host); cannot carry out metabolism/respire; only replicate inside host cells.
  2. (b) Nucleic acid (RNA in influenza) core; surrounded by a protein capsid; outer envelope of phospholipid (from host cell membrane) with (glyco)protein spikes / attachment proteins
    1 mark each: nucleic acid core (RNA or DNA); capsid made of protein (capsomeres); phospholipid envelope with surface glycoproteins.
Q27Prokaryotes & viruses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Cells of a Streptococcus bacterium have a diameter of 0.8 µm. Calculate how many of these cells could fit side by side in a line 1 mm long. [1]
  2. (b) Some antibiotics bind to 70S ribosomes. Explain why these antibiotics stop bacteria growing but have little effect on the cells of the person taking them. [2]
  3. (c) Name the main polymer in the cell wall of a bacterium. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1250
    1 mm = 1000 µm; 1000 ÷ 0.8 = 1250.
  2. (b) Bacteria have 70S ribosomes, so their protein synthesis is stopped; human cells make proteins in the cytoplasm on 80S ribosomes, which have a different shape and are not affected
    1 mark: bacterial protein synthesis inhibited at 70S ribosomes. 1 mark: human cytoplasmic ribosomes are 80S / different shape so antibiotic does not bind.
  3. (c) Peptidoglycan (murein)
    Peptidoglycan. Do not accept cellulose or chitin.
Q28Prokaryotes & viruses · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement about prokaryotic cells is correct?A The DNA is linear and enclosed by a nuclear envelope.B The ribosomes are 70S and are free in the cytoplasm.C The cell wall is made of cellulose.D Aerobic respiration takes place in mitochondria. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The ribosomes are 70S and are free in the cytoplasm.
    Prokaryotes have only 70S ribosomes, which are not attached to any membranes. Their DNA is circular and not enclosed (A), the wall is peptidoglycan (C) and they have no mitochondria (D).
Q29Prokaryotes & viruses · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Giardia is a single-celled organism that lives in the human gut and causes diarrhoea, often after drinking contaminated water. Studies of Giardia show:• it has two nuclei, each surrounded by a nuclear envelope• its DNA is linear and associated with proteins• it has 80S ribosomes and endoplasmic reticulum• it has no mitochondria• it has several flagellaA student says Giardia is a prokaryote because it is unicellular and has no mitochondria. Explain why the student is wrong. [3]
  2. (b) A Giardia cell is about 12 µm long. A typical gut bacterium is 1.5 µm long. Calculate how many times longer the Giardia cell is. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Giardia is a eukaryote: it has a nucleus with a nuclear envelope, linear DNA with proteins, 80S ribosomes and ER; prokaryotes never have these. Being unicellular and lacking mitochondria does not make it a prokaryote (many eukaryotes are unicellular)
    1 mark: has nuclei/nuclear envelope (prokaryotes have none). 1 mark: 80S ribosomes / ER / linear DNA with histones are eukaryotic features. 1 mark: eukaryotes can be unicellular / absence of mitochondria is not enough to classify it as prokaryotic.
  2. (b) 8 times
    12 ÷ 1.5 = 8.
Q30Prokaryotes & viruses · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A coronavirus particle has a diameter of 120 nm. In an electron micrograph its image diameter is 6 mm. Calculate the magnification. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why viruses can be seen only with an electron microscope. [1]
  3. (c) Patients with a viral infection sometimes ask doctors for antibiotics. Explain why antibiotics are not effective against viruses. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) ×50 000
    M1: 6 mm = 6 000 000 nm. A1: 6 000 000 ÷ 120 = ×50 000.
  2. (b) They are smaller than the resolution limit of a light microscope (about 200 nm); electron microscopes have much higher resolution
    Virus (120 nm) is below the 200 nm resolution of the light microscope.
  3. (c) Antibiotics act on bacterial structures/processes such as peptidoglycan cell walls or 70S ribosomes; viruses do not have these (they use the host cell's machinery)
    1 mark: antibiotics target bacterial cell walls/70S ribosomes/bacterial enzymes. 1 mark: viruses have no cell wall/ribosomes/metabolism of their own.
