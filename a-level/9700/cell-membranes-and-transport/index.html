Q1Membrane structure & cell signalling · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which components of the cell surface membrane are mainly involved in cell recognition? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B glycoproteins and glycolipids
    The carbohydrate chains of glycoproteins and glycolipids (the glycocalyx) act as recognition sites / antigens. Cholesterol (C) only regulates fluidity and stability; channel proteins (D) let ions and polar molecules through.
Q2Membrane structure & cell signalling · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which substance crosses the phospholipid bilayer of a cell surface membrane most rapidly by simple diffusion? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B carbon dioxide
    CO₂ is small and non-polar, so it passes between the hydrophobic fatty acid tails. Glucose and amino acids are polar and K⁺ is charged, so they need channel or carrier proteins.
Q3Membrane structure & cell signalling · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State the function of each of these components of a cell surface membrane:(i) channel protein(ii) carrier protein(iii) cholesterol(iv) glycolipid [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) forms a water-filled pore that lets specific ions/polar molecules through by facilitated diffusion; (ii) binds a specific molecule and changes shape to move it across (facilitated diffusion or active transport); (iii) regulates fluidity/stability of the membrane, reduces permeability to water and ions; (iv) cell recognition / acts as antigen / receptor, helps cells attach to each other.
    1 mark each: (i) hydrophilic pore for ions/polar molecules; (ii) binds specific solute, changes shape; (iii) controls fluidity / mechanical stability / reduces leakage of polar substances; (iv) recognition / antigen / cell adhesion / receptor.
Q4Membrane structure & cell signalling · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Membranes are found inside eukaryotic cells as well as around them. State three roles of membranes inside cells, giving an example of an organelle for each. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Compartmentalisation – keeps enzymes/reactions separate, e.g. lysosome keeps hydrolytic enzymes away from cytoplasm; site of attachment of enzymes / electron carriers, e.g. inner mitochondrial membrane (cristae) or thylakoid membranes in chloroplasts; transport/packaging of proteins in vesicles, e.g. rough ER and Golgi body; control of entry/exit of substances, e.g. nuclear envelope with pores.
    1 mark each for any three roles each with a correct example: separating reactions (lysosome/nucleus); holding enzymes or electron carriers (cristae/thylakoids); forming vesicles for transport (ER/Golgi); controlling what enters an organelle (nuclear envelope).
Q5Membrane structure & cell signalling · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the structure of the cell surface membrane is described as a 'fluid mosaic'. [2]
  2. (b) Fish living in very cold water under polar ice have cell membranes with a different composition from fish in tropical rivers such as the Indus. State the role of cholesterol in a cell membrane. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why sodium ions cannot cross the phospholipid bilayer by simple diffusion. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fluid: phospholipids (and many proteins) can move about / diffuse sideways within the bilayer. Mosaic: proteins are scattered among the phospholipids in a pattern, like tiles of a mosaic.
    1 mark: fluid – molecules move laterally within their layer. 1 mark: mosaic – proteins (of different kinds) scattered/embedded through the phospholipid bilayer.
  2. (b) Cholesterol regulates membrane fluidity: it stops phospholipids packing too closely at low temperature (keeps the membrane fluid) and restricts their movement at high temperature (keeps it stable); it also reduces permeability to ions/polar molecules.
    1 mark each for any two of: regulates/buffers fluidity; prevents tight packing / keeps membrane fluid at low temperature; reduces fluidity / adds stability at high temperature; reduces permeability to water-soluble ions/polar molecules; mechanical stability.
  3. (c) Sodium ions are charged (and surrounded by water molecules); the interior of the bilayer is made of hydrophobic fatty acid tails, which repel/do not let charged particles through, so they must use channel or carrier proteins.
    1 mark: ions are charged / hydrated / water-soluble. 1 mark: core of bilayer is hydrophobic (fatty acid tails), so ions need channel/carrier proteins.
Q6Membrane structure & cell signalling · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Proteins in the membrane of a human cell were labelled with a green fluorescent dye, and proteins of a mouse cell with a red dye. The two cells were fused. After 40 minutes at 37 °C the red and green labels were evenly mixed over the whole hybrid cell. At 5 °C the labels stayed in separate halves.What do these results show? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Membrane proteins can move sideways in the bilayer, and this movement is faster at higher temperature.
    Mixing shows lateral movement of proteins (the 'fluid' in fluid mosaic); it needs kinetic energy so it is slowed at 5 °C. C is wrong because the labels were on existing proteins; D is about phospholipids, which were not labelled.
Q7Membrane structure & cell signalling · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Wheat plants that survive cold winters in the north of Pakistan increase the proportion of cholesterol-like sterols and unsaturated fatty acids in their membranes in autumn. What is the main effect of this change at low temperatures? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The membrane stays fluid enough for membrane proteins to work.
    Kinks in unsaturated fatty acid tails stop phospholipids packing tightly, and sterols disrupt close packing at low temperature, so the membrane does not become a rigid gel and carriers/channels/enzymes can still move and function. A is the opposite effect; C and D are not caused by this change.
Q8Membrane structure & cell signalling · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Outline the stages of cell signalling in which a signalling molecule released by one cell causes a response in a target cell some distance away. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why a signalling molecule that is a small, lipid-soluble steroid does not need a receptor on the cell surface membrane. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Signalling cell releases the signal molecule (e.g. by exocytosis); it travels (by diffusion or in the blood) to the target cell; binds to a specific receptor (protein/glycoprotein) on the cell surface membrane with a complementary shape; receptor changes shape and activates a G protein/enzyme inside; a second messenger (e.g. cyclic AMP) is produced; this starts a cascade of enzyme activation that amplifies the signal and produces the response.
    1 mark each for any four: release by signalling cell; transport to target; binding to complementary receptor on cell surface membrane; change in receptor shape / G protein activated; second messenger formed (e.g. cAMP); amplification / enzyme cascade → specific response.
  2. (b) It is non-polar/lipid-soluble so it diffuses through the phospholipid bilayer; it binds to a receptor in the cytoplasm or nucleus instead.
    1: dissolves in / diffuses through bilayer; 1: receptor inside the cell (cytoplasm/nucleus).
Q9Membrane structure & cell signalling · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Barley seedlings were grown at 5 °C or at 25 °C. The fatty acids of the root cell membrane phospholipids were analysed.Growth temperature / °C: 5, 25Percentage of fatty acids that are unsaturated / %: 78, 54

  1. (a) Describe and explain how the composition of the membranes differs between the two groups of plants. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why it is important for root cells to keep their membranes fluid at low temperatures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Plants grown at 5 °C have 24 percentage points more unsaturated fatty acids (78 % v 54 %). Unsaturated fatty acid tails have C=C double bonds that make kinks, so the phospholipids cannot pack closely; this keeps the membrane fluid at low temperature.
    1: data quote / difference of 24 %; 1: C=C double bonds cause kinks; 1: phospholipids less tightly packed → membrane stays fluid at low temperature.
  2. (b) Membrane proteins (carriers, channels, pumps) must be able to change shape/move to take up ions and water; vesicles must be able to fuse with membranes; a rigid membrane may crack/leak.
    Any two: carrier/channel proteins can work (ion uptake); proteins can move within bilayer; endocytosis/exocytosis possible; avoids rigid membrane breaking.
Q10Membrane structure & cell signalling · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

In an electron micrograph of a liver cell, the cell surface membrane appears as two dark lines separated by a pale line. The total width of this 'railway track' on the photograph is 3.5 mm. The magnification is ×500 000.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual thickness of the membrane in nm. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the membrane appears as two dark lines with a pale line between them. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7 nm
    3.5 mm = 3.5 × 10⁶ nm (M1); 3.5 × 10⁶ ÷ 500 000 = 7 nm (A1).
  2. (b) The dark lines are the hydrophilic phosphate heads (which take up the heavy-metal stain) of the two layers of phospholipids; the pale line is the hydrophobic fatty acid tails in the middle of the bilayer, which do not take up stain.
    1: dark = phosphate heads/ hydrophilic regions stained; 1: pale = fatty acid tails / hydrophobic core.
Q11Membrane structure & cell signalling · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Outline the stages by which a water-soluble hormone such as glucagon, arriving in the blood, causes a specific response inside a liver cell. [4]
  2. (b) Steroid hormones, such as oestrogen, have receptors inside the cell rather than on the cell surface membrane. Suggest why. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why only some cells in the body respond to glucagon even though all cells are exposed to it in the blood. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Glucagon binds to a specific receptor on the cell surface membrane (complementary shape); binding changes the receptor's shape; this activates a G protein / transduction inside the cell; an enzyme makes a second messenger (cyclic AMP); the second messenger activates a chain of enzymes (amplification); the final enzyme causes the response (glycogen broken down to glucose).
    1 mark each for any four of: hormone (ligand) binds to specific receptor on cell surface membrane; receptor has complementary shape / only target cells have the receptor; receptor changes shape / signal transduction; G protein activated; second messenger (cAMP) produced; cascade/amplification by enzymes; specific response, e.g. glycogen breakdown.
  2. (b) Steroids are lipid-soluble (non-polar), so they can diffuse through the hydrophobic phospholipid bilayer and reach receptors in the cytoplasm or nucleus.
    1 mark: steroids are lipid-soluble / non-polar / hydrophobic. 1 mark: so they pass directly through the phospholipid bilayer (by simple diffusion).
  3. (c) Only target cells (e.g. liver cells) have receptors with a shape complementary to glucagon.
    1 mark: only target cells have the specific receptor.
Q12Membrane structure & cell signalling · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a phospholipid molecule and explain why phospholipids form a bilayer when mixed with water. [4]
  2. (b) A species of bacterium lives in hot springs at 80 °C. Its membrane phospholipids contain a higher proportion of saturated fatty acids than those of bacteria living at 25 °C. Suggest how this helps the bacterium to survive. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Glycerol joined to two fatty acids and a phosphate group; the phosphate head is hydrophilic (polar) and the fatty acid tails are hydrophobic (non-polar); in water the heads face the water on both sides and the tails point inwards, away from water, forming a bilayer.
    1 mark: glycerol + two fatty acids + phosphate (group). 1 mark: phosphate head hydrophilic/polar. 1 mark: fatty acid tails hydrophobic/non-polar. 1 mark: heads face aqueous surroundings, tails face inwards / away from water, so two layers form.
  2. (b) Saturated fatty acid tails are straight (no kinks), so they pack closely together; this reduces fluidity / keeps the membrane stable and not too leaky at high temperature.
    1 mark: saturated tails straight / no C=C kinks, pack tightly. 1 mark: membrane less fluid / more stable / less permeable at high temperature (does not become too leaky).
Q13Membrane structure & cell signalling · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In a historical experiment, all the lipids were extracted from 4.0 × 10⁹ red blood cells and spread on the surface of water as a layer one molecule thick. The lipid layer covered 1.10 m². The surface area of one red blood cell was taken to be 140 µm². (1 µm² = 1 × 10⁻¹² m²)

  1. (a) Calculate the total surface area of the red blood cells in m². [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the ratio of the area of the lipid layer to the surface area of the cells. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [1]
  3. (c) Explain what this result suggests about the structure of the cell surface membrane, and why red blood cells were a good choice for this experiment. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.56 m²
    140 × 10⁻¹² × 4.0 × 10⁹ (M1) = 0.56 m² (A1).
  2. (b) 1.96
    1.10 ÷ 0.56 = 1.96.
  3. (c) The lipid covers about twice the cell surface area, so the membrane is a lipid bilayer (two molecules thick) with heads facing water on both sides. Mammalian red blood cells have no nucleus or other membrane-bound organelles, so all the lipid comes from the cell surface membrane.
    1: ratio ≈ 2 → two layers / bilayer; 1: hydrophilic heads face the aqueous medium on each side / tails inward; 1: no nucleus/organelles so only the surface membrane contributes lipid.
Q14Membrane structure & cell signalling · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Liver cells respond to the hormone glucagon by breaking down glycogen. Liver cells were treated with a protease enzyme that digests proteins on the outer surface of the cells only, then washed. They were then exposed to either glucagon or to a lipid-soluble steroid hormone that also produces a response in liver cells.Treatment: no protease + glucagon → response; protease + glucagon → no response; protease + steroid → response

  1. (a) Explain the results. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the cells were washed after protease treatment and before the hormone was added. [1]

  1. (a) Predict the change in volume of the cells in each solution during the 10 minutes and explain your predictions. [4]
  2. (b) Describe a suitable control for this investigation and explain its purpose. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sodium chloride: the cells shrink, because the solution has a lower water potential than the cells, so water leaves by osmosis and the sodium chloride does not enter to balance it. Urea: little change at first, because the urea concentration is about isotonic with the cell contents; urea then enters the cells and water follows by osmosis, so the cells slowly swell.
    1 mark: sodium chloride cells shrink, water leaves by osmosis. 1 mark: NaCl cannot cross, so shrinkage continues. 1 mark: urea little change at first. 1 mark: urea enters over time and water follows, so swelling.
  2. (b) Cells in 0.15 mol dm⁻³ sodium chloride (about 0.9 %, isotonic with the cells): their volume should not change. This shows that the changes are caused by the solutes and not by handling.
    1 mark: cells in isotonic 0.15 mol dm⁻³ NaCl. 1 mark: no change shows the solute effects, not handling, cause the changes.
