Q1Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a peptide bond forms between two amino acids. [3]
  2. (b) Name the level of protein structure that is the sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide. [1]
  3. (c) State two types of bond, other than peptide bonds, that hold a protein molecule in its tertiary structure. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A condensation reaction between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amine group of the other; a molecule of water is removed; a C–N (peptide) bond forms, giving a dipeptide.
    1 mark each for: condensation; –COOH of one reacts with –NH₂ of other; water released; C–N bond / CONH link named.
  2. (b) Primary structure
    B1 primary.
  3. (c) Any two of: hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bonds, hydrophobic interactions.
    1 mark each for any two; do not credit 'peptide' or 'glycosidic'.
Q2Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A solution of the enzyme amylase is heated to 90 °C for ten minutes. Which bonds in the enzyme are least likely to be broken?A hydrogen bondsB ionic bondsC peptide bondsD hydrophobic interactions [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C peptide bonds
    Denaturation by heat breaks the weaker bonds holding the tertiary structure (hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, hydrophobic interactions); the covalent peptide bonds of the primary structure stay intact. Hydrogen bonds (A) are the first to break, so A is wrong.
Q3Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Haemoglobin is described as a globular protein with quaternary structure and a prosthetic group. Explain what is meant by each of the terms 'quaternary structure' and 'prosthetic group' for haemoglobin. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the structure of a haemoglobin molecule makes it soluble in water. [2]
  3. (c) State the role of the iron ion in each haem group. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Quaternary: the molecule is made of more than one polypeptide chain – four chains (two α and two β) held together. Prosthetic group: a non-protein group permanently attached to the protein – the haem group (containing Fe²⁺) on each chain.
    B1 four polypeptides/two α + two β; B1 non-protein (haem) group attached to each chain.
  2. (b) The polypeptides are folded so that hydrophobic R groups point into the centre of the molecule and hydrophilic R groups are on the outside, where they form hydrogen bonds with water.
    B1 hydrophobic R groups inside; B1 hydrophilic R groups outside/interact with water.
  3. (c) Each Fe²⁺ ion binds (reversibly) one oxygen molecule, so one haemoglobin carries up to four O₂.
    B1 binds O₂.
Q4Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Human adult haemoglobin contains two α-chains of 141 amino acids each and two β-chains of 146 amino acids each. Calculate the number of water molecules released when all four polypeptides are made from free amino acids. [2]
  2. (b) In a cytoplasmic enzyme from a thermophilic bacterium found in a hot spring, a cysteine at position 52 forms a disulfide bond with another cysteine. A mutation replaces this cysteine with alanine, whose R group is –CH₃. Suggest and explain how this mutation could affect the enzyme's activity at high temperatures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 570
    M1 total amino acids = 2×141 + 2×146 = 574; each chain of n amino acids has n − 1 peptide bonds, so water = 574 − 4 = 570 A1.
  2. (b) No disulfide bond can form (alanine has no –SH); the tertiary structure is less stable, so at high temperature the enzyme denatures more easily; the active site changes shape so fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form and activity falls.
    B1 disulfide bond lost; B1 tertiary structure less stable/denatures at lower temperature; B1 active site shape changes, fewer ES complexes.
Q5Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Collagen is the main protein in tendons, which join muscles to bones. Describe the structure of a collagen fibre, from a single polypeptide to the fibre. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why the staggered arrangement of collagen molecules increases the tensile strength of a tendon. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each polypeptide is a left-handed helix with glycine as every third amino acid; three polypeptides wind together into a triple helix (tropocollagen), held by hydrogen bonds; glycine is small so the chains pack closely; molecules lie side by side with ends staggered; covalent cross-links form between adjacent molecules, making fibrils that combine into fibres.
    1 mark each for any four of: glycine every third position; three chains/triple helix; H bonds between chains; staggered ends; covalent cross-links between molecules; fibrils → fibres.
  2. (b) The ends of the molecules (the weak points) do not line up in one plane, so there is no single line of weakness; the load is spread along the overlapping molecules.
    B1 no line of weakness/ends not in line; B1 force spread across many molecules/cross-links.
Q6Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A protein is made of three polypeptide chains containing a total of 450 amino acids. Two of the chains are joined to each other by one disulfide bond. How many peptide bonds are there in the protein?A 446B 447C 448D 449 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B 447
    Each chain of n amino acids has n − 1 peptide bonds, so three chains have 450 − 3 = 447. The disulfide bond is not a peptide bond. 449 (D) treats the protein as a single chain.
Q7Carbohydrates · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the difference between the structures of α-glucose and β-glucose. [1]
  2. (b) Name the bond that joins two α-glucose molecules to form maltose, and the type of reaction involved. [2]
  3. (c) State the name of the reaction that breaks maltose into glucose and name the reagent added in this reaction. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) In α-glucose the –OH group on carbon 1 is below the ring; in β-glucose it is above the ring (the H and OH on carbon 1 are reversed).
    B1 position of OH on carbon 1 reversed/below vs above the ring.
  2. (b) α-1,4 glycosidic bond; condensation reaction.
    B1 (α-1,4) glycosidic; B1 condensation.
  3. (c) Hydrolysis; water is added.
    B1 hydrolysis; B1 water.
Q8Carbohydrates · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which polysaccharide is made of β-glucose units joined only by 1,4 glycosidic bonds?A amyloseB amylopectinC celluloseD glycogen [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C cellulose
    Cellulose is an unbranched chain of β-glucose joined by β-1,4 bonds. Amylose (A) is also unbranched with 1,4 bonds but is made of α-glucose.
Q9Carbohydrates · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Potato tubers store starch while human liver cells store glycogen. Compare the structure of amylopectin with the structure of glycogen. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how the branched structure of glycogen suits it to be an energy store in an active animal. [2]
  3. (c) Give two features of starch and glycogen, other than branching, that make them suitable storage molecules. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Similarities: both made of α-glucose; both have α-1,4 glycosidic bonds in chains and α-1,6 bonds at branch points. Difference: glycogen is more highly branched (branches more frequent) and its chains between branches are shorter.
    1 mark each for any three of: both α-glucose; both 1,4 and 1,6 bonds; both branched; glycogen more branched/shorter chains.
  2. (b) Many branch ends (terminal glucose units) so enzymes can hydrolyse many glucose molecules at the same time – glucose is released quickly when needed; the molecule is compact so a lot is stored in a small space.
    B1 many ends for enzymes to act on/rapid hydrolysis; B1 compact.
  3. (c) Any two of: insoluble so no effect on water potential/osmosis; large so cannot leave the cell; easily hydrolysed to glucose; compact/coiled.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q10Carbohydrates · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The relative molecular mass (Mr) of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, is 180 and of water is 18. Calculate the Mr of a chain of ten glucose molecules joined by glycosidic bonds. [2]

  1. (a) Describe how a triglyceride is formed. [3]
  2. (b) Ghee is solid at room temperature in Lahore in winter, while sunflower oil is liquid. Explain this difference in terms of the fatty acids they contain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) One glycerol molecule joins to three fatty acids by condensation reactions; three ester bonds form; three molecules of water are released.

  2. (b) Explain why phospholipids form a bilayer when they are mixed with water. [3]
Show answer
