Q1Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a peptide bond forms between two amino acids. [3]
  2. (b) Name the level of protein structure that is the sequence of amino acids in a polypeptide. [1]
  3. (c) State two types of bond, other than peptide bonds, that hold a protein molecule in its tertiary structure. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A condensation reaction between the carboxyl group of one amino acid and the amine group of the other; a molecule of water is removed; a C–N (peptide) bond forms, giving a dipeptide.
    1 mark each for: condensation; –COOH of one reacts with –NH₂ of other; water released; C–N bond / CONH link named.
  2. (b) Primary structure
    B1 primary.
  3. (c) Any two of: hydrogen bonds, ionic bonds, disulfide bonds, hydrophobic interactions.
    1 mark each for any two; do not credit 'peptide' or 'glycosidic'.
Q2Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark

Show answer
  1. (a) B It differs between amino acids and gives each amino acid its particular properties.
    The 20 amino acids share the amine and carboxyl groups and differ only in R. A is wrong: the peptide bond forms between the amine group of one amino acid and the carboxyl group of another; only cysteine (and methionine) R groups contain sulfur; the water removed comes from the –OH of the carboxyl and an H of the amine group.
Q3Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the general structure of an amino acid. Name the groups attached to the central carbon atom. [3]
  2. (b) Glycine is the smallest amino acid. State what its R group is. [1]
  3. (c) When proteins are digested in the small intestine, peptide bonds are broken. Name this type of reaction and state the molecule that must be added for each bond broken. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A central carbon atom bonded to an amine group (–NH₂), a carboxyl group (–COOH), a hydrogen atom and an R group (side chain).
    1 mark each: amine group –NH₂; carboxyl group –COOH; H atom and R group on the same central (α) carbon.
  2. (b) A single hydrogen atom (–H).
    B1 hydrogen atom.
  3. (c) Hydrolysis; one molecule of water per peptide bond.
    B1 hydrolysis; B1 water (one molecule per bond).
Q4Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A technician in a hospital laboratory in Karachi compares haemoglobin and collagen. Give four differences between the two proteins, using these headings: shape of molecule, solubility in water, function, presence of a prosthetic group. [4]
  2. (b) State two places in the human body where collagen is found. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shape: haemoglobin globular (compact, roughly spherical); collagen fibrous (long, rope-like). Solubility: haemoglobin soluble; collagen insoluble. Function: haemoglobin transports oxygen (metabolic role); collagen gives strength/structural support. Prosthetic group: haemoglobin has haem groups; collagen has none.
    1 mark per correct comparison under each heading (both proteins needed for each mark).
  2. (b) Any two of: skin, tendons, ligaments, bone, cartilage, walls of arteries, cornea.
    Both needed for 1 mark.

Q6Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Haemoglobin is described as a globular protein with quaternary structure and a prosthetic group. Explain what is meant by each of the terms 'quaternary structure' and 'prosthetic group' for haemoglobin. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the structure of a haemoglobin molecule makes it soluble in water. [2]
  3. (c) State the role of the iron ion in each haem group. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Quaternary: the molecule is made of more than one polypeptide chain – four chains (two α and two β) held together. Prosthetic group: a non-protein group permanently attached to the protein – the haem group (containing Fe²⁺) on each chain.
    B1 four polypeptides/two α + two β; B1 non-protein (haem) group attached to each chain.
  2. (b) The polypeptides are folded so that hydrophobic R groups point into the centre of the molecule and hydrophilic R groups are on the outside, where they form hydrogen bonds with water.
    B1 hydrophobic R groups inside; B1 hydrophilic R groups outside/interact with water.
  3. (c) Each Fe²⁺ ion binds (reversibly) one oxygen molecule, so one haemoglobin carries up to four O₂.
    B1 binds O₂.
Q7Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Human adult haemoglobin contains two α-chains of 141 amino acids each and two β-chains of 146 amino acids each. Calculate the number of water molecules released when all four polypeptides are made from free amino acids. [2]
  2. (b) In a cytoplasmic enzyme from a thermophilic bacterium found in a hot spring, a cysteine at position 52 forms a disulfide bond with another cysteine. A mutation replaces this cysteine with alanine, whose R group is –CH₃. Suggest and explain how this mutation could affect the enzyme's activity at high temperatures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 570
    M1 total amino acids = 2×141 + 2×146 = 574; each chain of n amino acids has n − 1 peptide bonds, so water = 574 − 4 = 570 A1.
  2. (b) No disulfide bond can form (alanine has no –SH); the tertiary structure is less stable, so at high temperature the enzyme denatures more easily; the active site changes shape so fewer enzyme–substrate complexes form and activity falls.
    B1 disulfide bond lost; B1 tertiary structure less stable/denatures at lower temperature; B1 active site shape changes, fewer ES complexes.
Q8Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A small polypeptide hormone is made of 85 amino acids. The mean relative molecular mass (Mr) of the free amino acids is 128, and the Mr of water is 18. Calculate the Mr of the polypeptide. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why replacing one amino acid in the primary structure of this polypeptide with a different amino acid may stop the hormone binding to its receptor. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9368
    85 × 128 = 10880 (M1); 84 peptide bonds, so subtract 84 × 18 = 1512; Mr = 9368 (A1).
  2. (b) The new R group may form different bonds (hydrogen, ionic, disulfide bonds or hydrophobic interactions) or fail to form the original ones, so the folding/tertiary structure changes; the shape of the part that binds to the receptor changes, so it is no longer complementary to the receptor.
    1 mark each for any three of: different R group / different properties (e.g. charge, polarity); different bonds formed or bonds lost between R groups; changed tertiary structure / folding; binding site no longer complementary in shape to receptor.
Q9Proteins (incl. haemoglobin & collagen) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Blood of a healthy adult contains 150 g of haemoglobin per dm³. The Mr of haemoglobin is 64500, and each haemoglobin molecule can carry up to four molecules of oxygen. One mole of oxygen occupies 24000 cm³ at room temperature. Calculate the maximum volume of oxygen, in cm³, that can be carried by the haemoglobin in 1 dm³ of blood. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how the structure of haemoglobin allows each molecule to carry four oxygen molecules. [2]
  3. (c) Haem is described as a prosthetic group. State what this means. [1]
