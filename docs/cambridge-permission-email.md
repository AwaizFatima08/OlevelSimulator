# Email draft — permission request to Cambridge (Publisher Relations)

**To:** Publisher.Relations@cambridge.org
**Subject:** Permission enquiry: non-profit study app using Cambridge past paper questions (O Level, IGCSE, AS & A Level)

---

Dear Publisher Relations team,

I run HomiLabs, a small independent developer in Pakistan, and I would like to ask whether Cambridge would consider granting permission for a free, non-profit study app to show selected past paper questions.

**About the project**
"Question Simulator" (website: https://cambridgesimulator.homilabs.org; Android app on Google Play) helps O Level, IGCSE and AS & A Level students practise by topic. At the moment it shows only our own analysis: for each syllabus topic it lists where questions appeared (for example "Cambridge IGCSE Physics (0625) Paper 42 Q7, November 2024"), how many marks they carried and how often the topic is examined, and it links students to the official past papers on cambridgeinternational.org. It does not contain any question papers, mark schemes or examiner reports.

The app is free, has no advertising, collects no personal data, and is not affiliated with Cambridge. Every page and the store listing say so.

**What we would like to do**
Under each topic, show the relevant past paper questions so that students can practise one topic at a time. We would follow your published conditions for past paper use:

- only questions from papers set in 2018 or later;
- no more than 70% of the questions from any one paper;
- questions from only one variant per exam series;
- questions reproduced exactly as published, as page images, with no alteration, abridgement or translation;
- each question followed by the acknowledgement in your required format, e.g. "Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (0580) Paper 32 Q5 a, b & c, November 2021", with a link to the official past papers page;
- no mark schemes, examiner reports or multiple-choice papers unless you advise that these can be included;
- any material containing third-party copyright (texts, photographs, maps) left out;
- prompt removal of any item on request.

**Our questions**
1. Is permission of this kind available for a free, non-profit digital app and website, and is the Copyright Application form the right route, given that the content is updated twice a year rather than published once?
2. Given the non-profit purpose, would Cambridge consider waiving or reducing the administration fee?
3. If a full application is needed, how should we scope it (per syllabus, per release, or per question set), and is there any material you would exclude?

I would be glad to send screenshots of the app, a list of the syllabuses involved, or anything else that would help. Thank you for considering this request.

Kind regards,

Humayun Shahzad
HomiLabs
homilabs.smc@gmail.com
https://homilabs.org

Notes for Homi (not part of the email):

- Cambridge's guidance says formal applications must be signed, list each question in order, include copies of the

  £600 (101+) per application, charged even if refused; processing takes about 3 weeks. This email is the step before that.

## Reply (received by 25 Oct 2026) — permission refused

From Jayne Neale, Support Officer, Publisher Relations, Cambridge University Press & Assessment. Summary: permission is
not granted for the reproduction of Cambridge past paper questions within the app or website. The permissions process
is mainly for approved extracts in published educational resources; permission is not normally granted for stand-alone
digital products or platforms whose primary purpose is to reproduce, organise, deliver or provide access to collections
of Cambridge examination questions.

Consequences: no question text or images, ever; references + HomiLabs-original practice questions only; links go to
cambridgeinternational.org only (never to mirror sites). Consider removing "Cambridge"/"IGCSE" from the product name.
