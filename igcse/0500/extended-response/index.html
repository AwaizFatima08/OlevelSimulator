Read the text carefully, then answer the question. Paragraph numbers are shown in brackets.Text: First night at sea(1) Bilal was fifteen when his uncle finally agreed to take him out on the boat. They left Gwadar harbour as the sun was setting, the engine coughing and then settling into a steady growl, and Bilal stood at the bow feeling the spray on his face and trying to look as though he did this every day.(2) The sea was calm at first, a vast sheet of beaten copper that turned purple, then black, as the light drained away. The coast shrank behind them to a string of yellow beads. Bilal had never known darkness like it: the stars were not scattered but poured across the sky like spilled sugar, and the wake behind the boat glittered with tiny sparks of green light.(3) Around midnight they put out the nets. His uncle and the two crewmen worked without talking, their hands moving as if they belonged to one body, while Bilal fumbled with ropes that were wet, heavy and seemed determined to tangle. His palms burned. Nobody shouted at him, which somehow made it worse.(4) Then the wind changed. It came from nowhere, slapping the boat sideways, and the sea, so obedient an hour before, began to heave and snarl. Waves rose like dark hills and broke over the side, drenching him. The deck tilted beneath his feet and his stomach lurched with it. He clung to the mast, sick and terrified, certain the boat would roll over and swallow them all. Through the roar he heard his uncle laughing. "This is nothing!" he shouted. "This is the sea saying hello."(5) By dawn the wind had dropped, and the nets came up silver and thrashing with fish. Bilal hauled with the others until his arms shook. When the harbour appeared again, low and pale on the horizon, he realised he was no longer pretending.
