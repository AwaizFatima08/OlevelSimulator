Music thudded from loudspeakers somewhere to the east. Above us the sky was already freckled with kites: paper diamonds in lime green and hot pink, swooping and stalling like nervous birds, their strings glinting in the low sun. The air smelled of fried pakoras, diesel and the dusty sweetness of marigolds strung along the parapet.(3) Grandfather handed me the reel as if it were something precious. "Let the wind do the work," he said. "You only listen." My kite, purple with a white tail, rode higher and higher until it was a tiny flickering stamp against the blue. I watched my kite drift away over the rooftops, turning slowly, already somebody else's prize, while shouts of triumph rose from a roof three streets away. Something hot and heavy climbed into my throat.(5) I expected to be laughed at. Instead, Grandfather sat down beside me on an upturned bucket and began to tell me about the first kite he had lost, sixty years earlier, on the same roof. By the time he finished, my tears had dried, and he had quietly tied a new kite to my reel.

Gulnaz waited for him to go out, then followed.(4) The moon was almost full, and the pasture was silver and black. At the edge of the pen, on a boulder, stood a shape she had heard about all her life but never seen: lean, grey and motionless, its eyes glinting green when the torchlight swept across it. The wolf did not look frightened. It looked at the sheep the way a shopper looks at a market stall. The dogs barked furiously but held back, their hackles raised. Then it turned, unhurried, and vanished into the darkness.(5) Neither of them slept again that night. They sat by the pen until dawn, wrapped in blankets, while the sheep slowly settled. When the sun rose over the ridge, her father counted the flock twice. Every animal was there.

  1. (a) Using your own words, explain what the writer means by the word "restless" (paragraph 2). [1]
  2. (b) Using your own words, explain what the writer means by the word "motionless" (paragraph 4). [1]
  3. (c) Using your own words, explain what the writer means by the word "unhurried" (paragraph 4). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Unable to stay still or calm; uneasy and moving about.
    1 mark for uneasy / agitated / fidgety / unsettled. Not 'tired' or 'not resting'.
  2. (b) Completely still; not moving at all.
    1 mark for perfectly still / without moving.
  3. (c) Slow and calm, without any sign of rushing or fear.
    1 mark for leisurely / slow / relaxed / calm.
