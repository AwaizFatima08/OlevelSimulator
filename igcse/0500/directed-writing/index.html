Q1Formal letter · Core · Paper 2 style · 40 marks

Read Text A and Text B, then answer the question.Text A: Phones off, minds on (from a parents' newsletter)Our school should follow the example of many others and ban mobile phones completely during the school day. Phones would be handed in at the gate each morning and returned at home time.The reasons are obvious to anyone who has watched a group of teenagers at break. Instead of talking, playing or simply resting their minds, they sit hunched over screens. Teachers tell us that notifications interrupt lessons, and that some pupils secretly message friends or play games under their desks.Phones also bring the worst of the internet into school. Arguments that begin online continue in the corridors, and cruel photographs can be shared within seconds. A ban would give pupils seven hours a day of freedom from this pressure.Some parents worry about contacting their children in an emergency. But the school office has always been able to pass on messages, just as it did before phones existed. A short period of adjustment would be worth the calmer, more focused school that would result.Text B: Don't treat us like children (a student's blog)Every few months, someone suggests banning phones in school, and every time I want to ask: has anyone asked us?My phone is not just for games. I use it to check my timetable, photograph the board when I can't copy fast enough, look up words in English lessons and use the calculator in science. During the last power cut, it was the only torch in our classroom.Banning phones will not stop online bullying. Most of it happens in the evening, at home, where the school has no control. What would actually help is teaching us how to use phones responsibly and how to report problems, because we will have phones for the rest of our lives.There is also the journey to and from school. Many of us travel by bus or rickshaw, and our parents feel safer knowing they can reach us. Handing phones in at the gate means queues and lost or damaged devices.A better rule would be simple: phones switched off in lessons unless the teacher asks for them. Trust us, and most of us will respect it.

You decide to write a letter to the headteacher.In your letter you should:• evaluate the views given in both texts about banning mobile phones in school• give your own views, based on what you have read.Begin your letter, 'Dear Headteacher'.Base your answer on what you have read in both texts, but be careful to use your own words. Address both of the bullet points. Write about 250 to 350 words. Up to 15 marks are available for the content of your answer and up to 25 marks for the quality of your writing (exam total 40). [40]
Show answer
  1. (a) Model plan: Opening – purpose (respond to proposal; student perspective). Para 2 – accept Text A's concerns about distraction and the cruelty of shared images, but challenge the claim that a ban gives 'freedom from pressure', since Text B shows bullying mostly happens at home. Para 3 – weigh Text B's educational uses against Text A's evidence of misuse: uses are real but could be supervised. Para 4 – safety on journeys (B) versus office messages (A): office cannot help on the bus. Para 5 – own view: a compromise (phones off and in bags in lessons; used only when teacher permits; clear sanctions; lessons on online safety). Close formally, offering student-council help.
    Ideas to evaluate: Text A: phones distract at break and in lessons; secret messaging/games; online arguments and cruel photos spread; ban gives relief from pressure; office can pass emergency messages; adjustment worth it. Text B: phones used for timetable, photos of the board, dictionary, calculator, torch; bullying mainly happens out of school; better to teach responsible use; safety on journeys; queues and damage at gate; alternative rule – off in lessons unless asked. Evaluation points: A relies on observation but generalises; B's practical uses could be replaced by school devices; both care about well-being; compromise possible. In any case, a fixed early start that leaves pupils exhausted makes no sense if our aim is learning rather than routine.Changing timetables is difficult, but schools exist for pupils' benefit. School buses and vans that serve several schools would have to change their routes, and costs would rise.I also doubt that teenagers would use the extra hour to sleep.
