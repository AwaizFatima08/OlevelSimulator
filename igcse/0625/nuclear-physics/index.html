Original questions written by HomiLabs in the style of the exam, with answers and mark schemes. They are not Cambridge past paper questions and have not been reviewed by Cambridge. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Radioactivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Compare alpha, beta and gamma emissions. For each, state its nature, its relative charge and what is needed to stop most of it. [6]
  2. (b) State which of the three is the most strongly ionising. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) alpha: helium nucleus (2 protons + 2 neutrons), charge +2, stopped by a sheet of paper (or a few cm of air); beta: high-speed electron, charge −1, stopped by a few mm of aluminium; gamma: electromagnetic radiation, no charge, reduced significantly only by several cm of lead or thick concrete
    For each emission: nature and charge both correct (B1); absorber correct (B1); 2 marks × 3 emissions = 6
  2. (b) alpha
    B1
Q2Radioactivity · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which type of radiation is the least penetrating but the most ionising? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A alpha
    Alpha particles are large and doubly charged, so they ionise strongly and lose energy quickly, being stopped by paper. Gamma (C) is the most penetrating and least ionising.
Q3Radioactivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State three ways in which a laboratory worker can reduce the dose received from a radioactive source. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why an alpha source is particularly dangerous if it is swallowed or breathed in, even though it is easily stopped outside the body. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) reduce the time of exposure; increase the distance from the source (use tongs); use shielding (lead screen / store in lead box)
    1 mark each
  2. (b) inside the body all the alpha particles are absorbed by nearby cells; alpha is highly ionising, so it causes severe local damage to cells (mutation, cancer)
    absorbed by tissue inside body (B1); strongly ionising damages cells (B1)
Q4Radioactivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by radioactive decay. [2]
  2. (b) Radioactive decay is described as random and spontaneous. Explain what these two words mean. [2]
  3. (c) Describe what is meant by ionisation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) a change in an unstable nucleus that results in the emission of alpha particles, beta particles or gamma radiation
    unstable nucleus (B1); emits α, β or γ (B1)
  2. (b) random: it is not possible to predict which nucleus will decay next or when; spontaneous: decay is not affected by external conditions such as temperature or pressure
    B1 each
  3. (c) the removal (or addition) of electrons from atoms, making them charged ions
    B1
Q5Radioactivity · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
