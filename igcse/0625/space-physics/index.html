Q3Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Lahore is in the northern hemisphere. Its summers are hot and its winters are cool.

  1. (a) Explain why the Earth has seasons. [2]
  2. (b) State the month in which Lahore has its summer, and explain why it is warmer then. [2]
  3. (c) State what the season is in Australia when it is summer in Lahore. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) the Earth's axis is tilted relative to the plane of its orbit; as the Earth orbits the Sun, each hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun for part of the year and away from it for another part
    tilted axis (B1); hemisphere tilted towards/away from Sun at different times of year (B1)
  2. (b) around June (June–August); the northern hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, so the Sun is higher in the sky, its radiation is more concentrated, and days are longer
    June (B1); any one valid reason (B1)
  3. (c) winter
    B1

Q5Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark

Q7Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The Earth turns once on its axis in 24 hours. Calculate the angle the Earth turns through in one hour. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the Sun appears to rise in the east and set in the west. [2]
  3. (c) Karachi is about 23° of longitude west of Dhaka. Calculate how many minutes later the Sun rises in Karachi than in Dhaka on the same day (assume the two cities are at the same latitude). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 15°
    360 ÷ 24 = 15° (B1)
  2. (b) the Earth spins on its axis from west to east (anticlockwise seen from above the North Pole); so the Sun appears to move across the sky from east to west
    B1 Earth rotates on axis; B1 direction (west to east) gives apparent east to west motion
  3. (c) 92 minutes (about 1.5 hours)
    Earth turns 15° per hour, so 23 ÷ 15 = 1.53 h (C1) = 92 min (B1)

Q9Earth & Solar System · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The gravitational field strength g due to the Earth at different distances from the Earth's centre (R = radius of the Earth):at R: 9.8 N/kgat 2R: 2.45 N/kgat 3R: 1.09 N/kgat 4R: 0.61 N/kg

  1. (a) Describe how g changes with distance from the centre of the Earth. [1]
  2. (b) A satellite has a mass of 50 kg. Calculate the gravitational force on it at a distance of 2R. [2]
  3. (c) State the mass of the satellite at a distance of 3R. [1]

  2. (b) about 120 N (122.5 N)

  3. (c) 50 kg
    B1 mass does not change

Q11Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The Earth orbits the Sun in an approximately circular orbit of radius 1.5 × 10¹¹ m. One orbit takes 365 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the orbital speed of the Earth in m/s. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) State the direction of the gravitational force on the Earth due to the Sun. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3.0 × 10⁴ m/s
    T = 365 × 24 × 3600 = 3.15 × 10⁷ s (C1); v = 2πr/T (C1) = 2π × 1.5 × 10¹¹ ÷ 3.15 × 10⁷ = 3.0 × 10⁴ m/s (A1)
  2. (b) towards the centre of the Sun
    B1
Q12Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The Moon orbits the Earth in an approximately circular orbit of radius 3.84 × 10⁸ m, taking 27.3 days for one orbit.

  1. (a) Calculate the orbital speed of the Moon. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why we see the Moon go through phases, such as crescent and full moon, during each orbit. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.0 × 10³ m/s
    T = 27.3 × 24 × 3600 = 2.36 × 10⁶ s (C1); v = 2πr/T (C1) = 1.0 × 10³ m/s (A1)
  2. (b) the Moon does not give out its own light but reflects sunlight; as it orbits the Earth we see different amounts of its sunlit half
    reflects light from the Sun (B1); different portion of lit half visible as it orbits (B1)
Q13Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark

Data for four planets:Mercury: mean distance from Sun 58 million km, orbital period 88 daysVenus: 108 million km, 225 daysMars: 228 million km, 687 daysJupiter: 778 million km, 4330 days

  1. (a) Which statement is supported by the data? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The orbital speed decreases as the distance from the Sun increases.
    Using v = 2πr/T: Mercury 48 km/s, Venus 35 km/s, Mars 24 km/s, Jupiter 13 km/s. D is wrong: Jupiter is 13 times further than Mercury but its period is about 49 times longer.
Q14Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

The four planets closest to the Sun are small and rocky. The four outer planets are much larger and are made mostly of gases.

  1. (a) Name the model that describes how the Solar System formed from a cloud of gas and dust. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how the planets formed according to this model. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the inner planets are rocky while the outer planets are mainly gaseous. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) the accretion model
    B1
  2. (b) a rotating cloud of interstellar gas and dust collapsed under gravity; most material formed the Sun at the centre and the rest formed a flat spinning disc; within the disc, particles collided and stuck together (accreted) under gravity to form larger bodies, eventually planets
    collapse of cloud under gravity (B1); disc around the Sun (B1); particles accrete into planets (B1)
  3. (c) near the Sun the temperature was too high for light substances (gases, ices) to condense, so only rocky/metallic material remained to form planets; further out it was cold enough for gases and ices to collect, forming large gas giants
    high temperature near Sun leaves only rock/metal (B1); colder further out so gases collect (B1)
Q15Earth & Solar System · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

An astronaut has a mass of 72 kg. The gravitational field strength at the surface of Mars is 3.7 N/kg and at the cloud tops of Jupiter it is 24.8 N/kg.

  1. (a) Calculate the astronaut's weight on Mars and on Jupiter. [2]
  2. (b) State the astronaut's mass on Jupiter. [1]
  3. (c) State two factors that determine the gravitational field strength at the surface of a planet. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 266 N on Mars; 1790 N (1786 N) on Jupiter
    W = mg: 72 × 3.7 = 266 N (B1); 72 × 24.8 = 1786 N (B1)
