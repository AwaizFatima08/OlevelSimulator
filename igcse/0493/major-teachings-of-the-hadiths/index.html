Q1Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that every action is valued according to the intention behind it, and each person receives the result of what they truly meant to do.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how Muslims can put this teaching into practice in their daily lives. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Deeds are judged by the intention in the heart; God rewards sincerity, so the same outward act can be worthy or worthless depending on its purpose; a believer's actions should be done for God's pleasure.
    1 mark each for any four of: intention (niyya) is the basis of every deed; God knows what is in the heart; reward depends on sincerity, not outward appearance; good acts done for show or worldly gain lose their reward; ordinary acts can become worship with a good intention; each person is responsible for their own intention.
  2. (b) By renewing a sincere intention before actions — study, work, charity, worship — so that they are done for God rather than praise, and by checking their motives.
    Up to 4 marks: 1 per practical action, +1 for development, e.g.: giving charity quietly rather than posting about it online (1+1); studying with the intention of serving the community makes study an act of worship (1+1); making intention before prayer and fasting (1); self-checking motives when helping others (1).
Q2Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that God does not look at people's bodies, looks or wealth; rather He looks at their hearts and their deeds.

  1. (a) What does this Hadith teach Muslims about how God judges people? [4]
  2. (b) How can Muslims act on this teaching in a society that values looks and money? Give examples. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) God judges by inner faith, sincerity and actions, not by appearance, race, wealth or status; all people are equal before Him and differ only in piety and deeds.
    1 mark each for any four of: outward appearance is not the basis of God's judgement; wealth and status do not earn God's favour; God sees the heart / intentions; deeds matter; equality of all people; piety (taqwa) is the true measure.
  2. (b) By treating people equally whatever their appearance or income, focusing on improving their own character rather than image, and not judging others by clothes or wealth.
    Up to 4 marks for developed examples, e.g.: respecting a cleaner or driver as much as a rich guest (1+1); not judging classmates by brands or phones (1); spending more effort on good deeds than on appearance or social-media image (1+1).
Q3Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that true richness does not come from having many possessions; real richness is contentment of the soul.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how this teaching could help a young Muslim today. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Wealth is not measured by possessions but by contentment; a person who is satisfied with what God has given is truly rich, while a greedy person stays poor however much he has.
    1 mark each for any four of: material wealth does not bring real richness; contentment (qana'a) is true wealth; greed leaves a person always wanting more; gratitude for God's provision; inner peace matters more than possessions; wealth is a test from God.
  2. (b) It helps them avoid comparison and envy, especially on social media, spend wisely, be grateful and find peace in faith and relationships rather than possessions.
    Up to 4 marks for developed points, e.g.: not envying friends' expensive phones or holidays (1+1); avoiding debt and wasteful spending (1); valuing family, faith and good deeds above possessions (1).
Q4Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that purity (cleanliness) is half of faith.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) How can Muslims put this teaching into practice at home and in their community? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cleanliness of body, clothes and surroundings, and purity of the heart, are a central part of faith; physical purity is needed for worship, and inner purity means freedom from sin and bad intentions.
    1 mark each for any four of: cleanliness is a major part of faith; physical purity — body, clothes, place; wudu and ghusl are needed for prayer; inner purity — heart free of pride, envy and sin; cleanliness pleases God; hygiene protects health.
  2. (b) By keeping themselves and their homes clean, performing wudu carefully, keeping streets, mosques and public places free of litter and working on purifying their character.
    Up to 4 marks for developed examples, e.g.: regular washing and clean clothes (1); careful wudu before prayer (1); community clean-up days in a neighbourhood in Lahore or a park in Manchester (1+1); purifying the heart from envy and grudges (1).
Q5Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): When asked about the highest level of faith, the Prophet (pbuh) explained that it is to worship God as though you can see Him, and although you cannot see Him, to know that He certainly sees you.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith about ihsan (excellence in faith). [4]
  2. (b) Explain how this teaching can improve a Muslim's prayer and behaviour. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ihsan is the highest level of faith: worshipping with full awareness of God's presence, as if seeing Him; since He sees everything, the believer acts sincerely and carefully in public and private.
    1 mark each for any four of: ihsan is the highest stage of religion (after islam and iman); worship with complete focus and devotion; awareness that God is always watching; this produces sincerity and humility; excellence in all actions, not only prayer; part of the well-known Hadith in which Jibril questioned the Prophet (pbuh).
  2. (b) Awareness of God's presence brings concentration and humility in prayer, and makes the believer honest and careful even when no one else is watching.
    Up to 4 marks: focus and calm in salat, avoiding rushing or distraction by the phone (1+1); honesty in private, e.g. not cheating in an online test (1); doing work to the best standard because God sees it (1).
Q6Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): A man asked the Prophet (pbuh) whether he should leave his camel untied and trust in God, or tie it and trust in God. The Prophet (pbuh) told him to tie it and then trust in God.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Give two examples of how a Muslim can act on this Hadith today, explaining each. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Trust in God (tawakkul) does not mean doing nothing; a Muslim must take sensible practical steps and then rely on God for the result.
    1 mark each for any four of: tawakkul is part of faith; it must be combined with effort; people must take reasonable precautions; the result is in God's hands; laziness is not trust; balance between action and reliance on God.
  2. (b) E.g. a student revises thoroughly and then prays for success, accepting the result; a family in a flood-prone area in Sindh prepares and follows warnings while trusting God; a traveller locks the car and trusts God.
    2 marks per example: 1 for the action (effort / precaution), 1 for explaining how trust in God is added (prayer, calm acceptance of outcome).
Q7Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that none of you truly has faith until he wants for his brother what he wants for himself.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) How can Muslims show this teaching in action? Give examples. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Complete faith includes caring for others; a believer should wish good for others as for himself, avoid jealousy and selfishness, and see other believers as brothers and sisters.
    1 mark each for any four of: faith is not complete without love for others; wish the same good for others as for yourself; removes envy and selfishness; brotherhood / sisterhood among believers; many scholars extend this to all humanity; faith shown in attitudes and conduct towards others.
  2. (b) By sharing, helping others succeed, being happy at their success and treating others as they would like to be treated.
    Up to 4 marks for developed examples, e.g.: sharing notes with a classmate before exams instead of hiding them (1+1); congratulating a colleague's promotion without envy (1); giving good-quality items in charity, the kind one would want oneself (1+1).
Q8Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that whoever believes in God and the Last Day should either say something good or remain silent.

  1. (a) Explain what this Hadith teaches about the link between belief and speech. [4]
  2. (b) How can Muslims apply this teaching to their use of social media? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Belief in God and the Day of Judgement should show in how a Muslim speaks; since words are recorded and will be judged, the believer speaks only what is good and otherwise stays silent.
    1 mark each for any four of: true faith affects behaviour; speech is an important part of conduct; belief in the Last Day — words will be accounted for; say only what is good, true and useful; avoid lies, backbiting, insults, gossip; silence is better than harmful speech.
  2. (b) By posting and commenting only what is true and beneficial, not spreading rumours or insulting people, and staying silent rather than joining arguments or gossip.
    Up to 4 marks: checking facts before sharing (1+1); avoiding hurtful comments, mockery or backbiting online (1); using platforms to spread useful knowledge or kindness (1); stepping away from heated arguments (1).
Q9Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that God divided mercy into one hundred parts; He kept ninety-nine with Himself and sent down one part to the earth, and it is from that one part that all creatures show mercy to one another, even an animal lifting its hoof so as not to harm its young.

  1. (a) Explain what this Hadith teaches about God's mercy. [4]
  2. (b) 'Knowing that God is so merciful may make people careless about sin.' Evaluate this statement. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) God's mercy is immense, far greater than all the mercy found in creation; every act of kindness on earth comes from a small portion of it; He keeps the greater share for His servants in the Hereafter, which gives hope of forgiveness.
    1 mark each for any four of: mercy is one of God's greatest attributes; all compassion in creation comes from Him; the mercy seen on earth is only a tiny share; ninety-nine parts are kept for the Day of Judgement; even animals' care for their young is from God's mercy; believers should never despair of God's mercy.
  2. (b) Disagree mostly: God's mercy should lead to gratitude, hope and repentance rather than carelessness; Muslims balance hope in mercy with fear of accountability, and mercy also calls them to be merciful to others.
    Up to 4 marks: a clear judgement (1); mercy brings hope and encourages repentance, not complacency (1); balance of hope and fear / accountability on the Day of Judgement (1); believers are expected to reflect this mercy to others, with example (1).
Q10Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) said that a hypocrite has three signs: when he speaks he lies, when he makes a promise he breaks it, and when he is trusted he betrays the trust.

  1. (a) Explain what this Hadith teaches about the qualities expected of a true believer. [4]
  2. (b) To what extent is keeping promises the most important of these three qualities in modern society? Give reasons. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A believer's faith must be matched by honesty, keeping promises and faithfulness to trusts; lying, breaking promises and betraying trust are signs of hypocrisy, where outward faith hides an inner fault.
    1 mark each for any four of: faith must be shown in character; truthfulness is essential; promises must be kept; trusts (amanah) must be honoured, including property, secrets and responsibilities; these three faults are signs of hypocrisy (nifaq); a believer should examine himself for them.
  2. (b) A reasoned judgement, e.g. all three are linked — business contracts, family commitments and public office all depend on promises, but honesty underlies keeping promises and trusts, so truthfulness may be the foundation.
    Up to 4 marks: judgement (1); reasoned support for promises, e.g. contracts, deadlines, marriage (1); comparison with truthfulness or trust, e.g. public officials handling funds (1); modern example and conclusion (1).
Q11Set Hadiths: teachings about belief · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that this world is like a prison for the believer and like a paradise for the disbeliever.

  1. (a) Explain the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how this teaching can help Muslims cope with hardship and temptation. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The believer restrains desires and follows God's limits in this life, so it feels restricting compared with the reward awaiting him; the disbeliever treats this world as his only enjoyment, and it is the best he will have compared with what follows; this life is temporary and a test.
    1 mark each for any four of: this world is temporary; it is a test; the believer accepts limits set by God (lawful and unlawful); the believer's true home and reward are in the Hereafter; compared with Paradise, worldly life is restricted; for one who rejects the Hereafter, worldly pleasure is all there is; it does not mean believers must be unhappy or reject lawful enjoyment.
  2. (b) It reminds them that difficulties and self-restraint are temporary and will be rewarded, so they stay patient in hardship and resist temptations that bring short pleasure but long regret.
    Up to 4 marks: patience in illness, poverty or loss knowing it is temporary (1+1); resisting unlawful income or pleasures by keeping the Hereafter in mind (1+1).
Q12Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that the strong person is not the one who defeats others in wrestling; the strong person is the one who controls himself when he is angry.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain practical ways a Muslim can act on this Hadith. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Real strength is self-control, not physical power; a Muslim should control anger and not act on it through violence or harsh words.
    1 mark each for any four of: true strength is moral / inner strength; physical strength is not the measure of a person; anger must be controlled; acting in anger leads to harm and regret; self-discipline is a sign of faith; patience is praised in Islam.
  2. (b) By staying calm in arguments, keeping quiet, changing position, making wudu, seeking refuge in God from Satan and forgiving others.
    Up to 4 marks: 1 per method, +1 for development, e.g.: staying silent or walking away during a quarrel (1+1); sitting down if standing, making wudu (1); seeking refuge in God (1); calm driving in Karachi traffic instead of road rage (1+1); forgiving instead of taking revenge (1).
Q13Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that whoever among you sees something wrong should change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue; and if he cannot, then in his heart, and that is the weakest level of faith.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Give two examples of how a Muslim today can act on this Hadith at different levels. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Muslims have a duty to oppose wrong in the way they are able: by action if they have the authority, by speaking out if not, and at least by hating it in the heart; it is part of faith to care about wrongdoing in society.
    1 mark each for any four of: duty to forbid wrong; three levels — action, speech, heart; according to one's ability and authority; changing by hand means proper action by those responsible, not violence by individuals; disliking wrong in the heart is the minimum; faith is linked to social responsibility.
  2. (b) E.g. a teacher stops bullying in her class (hand); a student speaks up politely when friends mock someone (tongue); a person who cannot act stays away from a gathering where backbiting takes place and prays for change (heart).
    2 marks per example: 1 for a suitable wrong and action, 1 for linking it to the correct level and to the person's ability.
Q14Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that smiling at your brother is an act of charity.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how Muslims can act on this teaching in a busy city such as Karachi or New York. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Charity is not only giving money; simple kind acts such as a smile are rewarded; Muslims should be cheerful and friendly, spreading happiness and brotherhood.
    1 mark each for any four of: charity (sadaqa) has many forms; even small acts are rewarded; a smile spreads love and goodwill; it costs nothing, so everyone can give; good manners are part of faith; strengthens brotherhood.
  2. (b) By greeting neighbours, shopkeepers and colleagues warmly, welcoming newcomers at the mosque and being cheerful with family, which builds friendship in places where people often feel isolated.
    Up to 4 marks for developed examples: greeting and smiling at colleagues and staff (1+1); welcoming new students or neighbours (1); positive manner with family after a stressful day (1).
Q15Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that each of you is a shepherd and each of you is responsible for those in his care: the ruler for his people, a man for his family, a woman for her husband's home and children, and a servant for his master's property.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how this Hadith can guide two different people in Pakistan today, such as a government official and a parent. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Everyone has responsibilities over others or over trusts given to them, and will be asked about how they carried them out; this applies to rulers, parents, spouses and workers.
    1 mark each for any four of: everyone is a guardian / shepherd; responsibility is a trust (amanah); rulers responsible for citizens; husbands for families; wives for the home and children; employees for property in their care; all will be questioned by God; no one is without responsibility.
  2. (b) The official must serve citizens honestly, avoid corruption and use public funds properly; the parent must provide, educate and give a good example to children, knowing both will be asked by God.
    2 marks per person: 1 for a relevant responsibility, 1 for development (e.g. official — fair service, no bribes; parent — religious and academic education, time and love).
Q16Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that God is gentle and loves gentleness in every matter.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how Muslims can show gentleness in family life and at work. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gentleness is one of God's qualities and He loves it in people; Muslims should be kind and soft in manner in all situations, avoiding harshness, which spoils things.
    1 mark each for any four of: gentleness (rifq) is loved by God; it applies to all matters; harshness is disliked; gentleness achieves what harshness cannot; it is part of good character; the Prophet (pbuh) himself was gentle with people.
  2. (b) By speaking softly to children and elderly parents, correcting mistakes kindly, being patient with colleagues and customers and treating animals with care.
    Up to 4 marks for developed examples: correcting a child's mistake calmly rather than shouting (1+1); patience with ageing parents (1); a manager giving feedback respectfully (1); kindness to animals (1).
Q17Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) instructed that a worker should be given his wages before his sweat has dried.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) How can this Hadith be put into practice by employers today? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Employers must pay workers fully and promptly; delaying wages is injustice; workers' rights and dignity must be respected; fair dealing is part of faith.
    1 mark each for any four of: pay wages promptly; pay the full agreed amount; delay or cheating is oppression; respect the dignity of labour; workers depend on wages for their families; fairness in business is required by Islam.
  2. (b) By paying salaries on time, honouring agreed wages, giving fair pay to domestic workers and daily-wage labourers, and providing safe working conditions.
    Up to 4 marks: monthly salaries paid on the agreed date (1); daily labourers in construction paid at the end of the day (1); fair wages and no unfair deductions for domestic workers (1+1); safe conditions and respect (1).
Q18Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that the believers, in their love, mercy and kindness towards one another, are like one body: when one part is in pain, the whole body suffers with sleeplessness and fever.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how Muslims can act on this teaching when people are suffering in another part of the world or in their own city. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Muslims form one community bound by love and mercy; they should feel each other's suffering and respond to help; unity and care for others are signs of faith.
    1 mark each for any four of: unity of the ummah; mutual love, mercy and compassion; sharing in each other's pain; responsibility to help those suffering; the community is weakened if any member suffers; selfishness has no place among believers.
  2. (b) By giving money and supplies to relief efforts, praying for those affected, volunteering locally, and raising awareness, as Muslims did after floods in Pakistan or the earthquake in Türkiye.
    Up to 4 marks for developed points: donations to disaster relief (1+1); volunteering locally, e.g. food for the homeless (1); prayer and raising awareness (1); visiting the sick in one's own community (1).
Q19Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that a true Muslim is one from whose tongue and hand other people are safe.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) Give two examples of how a Muslim can act on this Hadith today, explaining each. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Being a Muslim means not harming others by words or actions; faith shows in the safety and peace others feel around you.
    1 mark each for any four of: Islam means peace / safety; the tongue can harm through lies, insults and backbiting; the hand can harm through violence, theft or cheating; a believer must not harm others; good conduct is a sign of true faith; this includes harm through writing / online.
  2. (b) E.g. refusing to join in mocking someone in a WhatsApp group (tongue); driving carefully and not damaging public property (hand).
    2 marks per example: 1 for the action, 1 for showing how it keeps others safe from harm by tongue or hand.
Q20Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that faith has many branches; the highest is to declare that there is no god but God, the lowest is to remove something harmful from the road, and modesty is also a branch of faith.

  1. (a) Explain what this Hadith teaches about the nature of faith. [4]
  2. (b) Evaluate the importance of small acts, such as removing harm from a road, in a Muslim's faith. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Faith is not only belief but includes many actions, from the declaration of God's oneness to small public services; even removing an obstacle from a path is part of faith, and good character such as modesty is included.
    1 mark each for any four of: faith (iman) has many parts / branches; belief in tawhid is the highest; small acts of service are also faith; caring for public safety is part of religion; modesty (haya') is a branch; faith covers belief, speech and action.
  2. (b) They are very important because they show faith in everyday life, protect others and are rewarded; but they should come with correct belief, the highest branch, so both together make complete faith.
    Up to 4 marks: judgement (1); small acts protect the community — e.g. removing glass from a road, reporting an open manhole (1); they keep faith active every day (1); balanced against belief and obligatory worship (1).
Q21Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) instructed his followers to make things easy and not difficult for people, and to give good news and not drive people away.

  1. (a) Describe the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) A new Muslim in London finds some members of the mosque critical of her mistakes in prayer. Explain how the community should act on this Hadith. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Islam is a religion of ease; Muslims should present faith positively, avoid making religion harsh or burdensome and encourage people with hope rather than fear and blame.
    1 mark each for any four of: make things easy; do not make religion hard for others; give glad tidings / be positive; do not repel people by harshness; teaching should be gentle and encouraging; applies to teachers, preachers, parents and leaders.
  2. (b) They should teach her gently and patiently, praise her efforts, introduce practices step by step and make her feel welcome so she is encouraged rather than driven away.
    Up to 4 marks: gentle correction in private (1); encouragement and praise for effort (1); gradual teaching — essentials first (1); welcoming support such as classes or a friend to guide her (1).
Q22Set Hadiths: putting teachings into action · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Hadith (meaning in plain English): The Prophet (pbuh) taught that the upper hand is better than the lower hand: the upper hand is the one that gives, and the lower hand is the one that receives.

  1. (a) Explain the teaching of this Hadith. [4]
  2. (b) A young man in Lahore can work but relies on relatives for money. Evaluate how this Hadith applies to his situation. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Giving is better than receiving; Muslims should work to support themselves and others, be generous, and avoid begging when they can earn; but it does not look down on genuine need.
    1 mark each for any four of: generosity is praised; giving is more honourable than taking; encourages hard work and self-reliance; discourages begging without need; wealth is to be shared; those in genuine need still have a right to help; giving should be done with dignity for the receiver.
  2. (b) He should seek work and become self-reliant so he can support himself and later help others; relatives may help in the short term, and if he truly cannot work, accepting help is not shameful; the aim is to become a giver.
    Up to 4 marks: judgement (1); encourages him to earn and avoid dependence (1); recognises genuine hardship / temporary help is allowed (1); aims to become a giver — family, charity (1).
Q23Set Hadiths essay: personal and communal guidance · Core · Paper 2 style · 14 marks
  1. (a) Using Hadiths you have studied, describe how Muslims should treat other people. [10]
  2. (b) Explain why good treatment of others is an important part of a Muslim's faith. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Wish for others what you wish for yourself; keep others safe from your tongue and hand; be gentle; smile, which is charity; show mercy; make things easy; speak good or stay silent; pay workers promptly.
