Spinner A is numbered 1, 2, 3. Spinner B is numbered 1, 2, 3, 4. Both are fair. Each spinner is spun once and the two numbers are added.

  1. (a) Complete a sample space diagram showing all the possible totals. How many outcomes are there? [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the total is 5. [1]
  3. (c) Find the probability that the total is more than 5. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12 outcomes; totals: 2,3,4,5 / 3,4,5,6 / 4,5,6,7
    B2
  2. (b) 3/12 = 1/4

    (2,4), (3,3), (3,4) B1

Q15Combined events (trees, tables, Venn) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · calculator · 5 marks

A bag contains n balls, of which 3 are green. Two balls are taken at random without replacement. The probability that both are green is 1/15.

  1. (a) Show that n² − n − 90 = 0. [3]
  2. (b) Solve the equation to find n. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3/n × 2/(n − 1) = 1/15, so 6 × 15 = n(n − 1), giving n² − n − 90 = 0
    Product of probabilities M1; 90 = n(n − 1) M1; rearranged A1
  2. (b) n = 10
    (n − 10)(n + 9) = 0 M1; n = 10 (n = −9 rejected) A1

Show answer
  1. (a) 12 balls, 4 red

  2. (b) Find the probability that the dice shows an odd number. [2]

Q28Relative & expected frequency · Challenge · Paper 2 style · no calculator · 4 marks

  1. (a) How many faulty bulbs would you expect in a batch of 4000? [1]
  2. (b) In a batch of 2400 bulbs, 102 were faulty. Find the relative frequency of a faulty bulb in this batch. [2]
  3. (c) Does this batch suggest the factory's figure of 0.035 is too low? Explain your answer. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 140
    0.035 × 4000 B1
  2. (b) 0.0425
    102 ÷ 2400 M1; 0.0425 A1
