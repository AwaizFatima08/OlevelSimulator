Q1Recounting a recent experience · Core · Paper 1 style · 15 marks
  1. (a) You recently took part in a cooking competition at your school.Write an email to a friend about the competition.In your email:• Explain why you decided to enter the competition.• Describe what you cooked and how you prepared it.• Say what you learned from the experience.Write about 120 to 160 words. You will receive up to 6 marks for the content of your email, and up to 9 marks for the style and accuracy of your language. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Model answer (one of many acceptable): Hi Ali, Guess what? Last Friday I entered the school cooking competition! I only signed up because my mum said my chicken karahi was better than hers, and I wanted to see if the judges agreed. We had just forty-five minutes to cook. I chopped onions, tomatoes and green chillies as fast as I could, then fried the chicken with ginger and garlic. Halfway through I realised I'd forgotten the salt, so I was really nervous when the judges tasted it! In the end I came third, which I'm proud of. The biggest lesson was that planning matters: if I'd written a list of steps, I wouldn't have panicked. Next year I'm going to practise with a timer. Why don't you enter with me? Write soon, Hamza
    Possible content: Bullet 1: reason for entering, e.g. loves cooking, a teacher or relative encouraged them, wanted a challenge. Bullet 2: the dish and steps, e.g. ingredients, time limit, a moment of panic. Bullet 3: a lesson, e.g. time management, staying calm, teamwork, try new recipes. (a) Recently your school has had a problem that has affected students and teachers.Write an email to a friend about the problem.In your email:• Describe the problem and how it started.• Explain the effects it has had on students and teachers.• Say what has been done so far and whether you think it is enough.Write about 120 to 160 words. You will receive up to 6 marks for the content of your email, and up to 9 marks for the style and accuracy of your language. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Model answer (one of many acceptable): Dear Hafsa, Our school has been in chaos this month. During the first heavy monsoon storm, part of the science block roof started leaking, and within a week water had damaged the ceiling in three labs. Now the labs are closed for safety, which is a disaster for those of us taking practical exams next term. Our chemistry classes are being held in the hall, where it's noisy and we can't do any experiments. The teachers are stressed too; Miss Fariha said she has had to rewrite all her lesson plans. So far, the school has put buckets and plastic sheets everywhere and arranged extra theory lessons. Honestly, I don't think that's enough. They need to repair the roof properly before the next rains, or we'll fall even further behind. Do you have any ideas? Love, Mehwish
    Possible content: Bullet 1: problem: broken air-conditioning/fans, leaking roof, teacher shortage, flooding, internet failure; how it began. Bullet 2: effects on learning, health, mood, exams. Bullet 3: action so far and evaluation with a reason. Content (6 marks): 5–6 = all three bullets covered and well developed, sense of audience and purpose fully clear, friendly informal register kept throughout; 3–4 = all bullets covered but some development thin, register mostly suitable; 1–2 = one bullet missing or barely touched, little development; 0 = nothing relevant. Language (9 marks): 8–9 = wide range of vocabulary used precisely, varied and complex sentences, very few errors, ideas linked smoothly in clear paragraphs; 6–7 = good range with some less common words, mostly accurate complex sentences, clear organisation; 4–5 = adequate everyday vocabulary, mainly simple sentences, errors do not block meaning; 2–3 = limited range, frequent errors that sometimes obscure meaning; 1 = very basic; 0 = not readable. A missing bullet caps content at 3; a much shorter answer limits development.
Q30Describing a problem and its effects · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 15 marks
  1. (a) Recently you had a problem while travelling on a journey.Write an email to a friend about what happened.In your email:• Describe the journey and the problem that occurred.• Explain how the problem affected your plans.• Say how the problem was finally solved and what you learned from it.Write about 120 to 160 words. You will receive up to 6 marks for the content of your email, and up to 9 marks for the style and accuracy of your language. [15]
Show answer
  1. (a) Model answer (one of many acceptable): Hi Usama, I've just survived the worst journey of my life! I was travelling by bus from Lahore to Peshawar for my cousin's engagement. About halfway, near a small town, the bus broke down with a loud bang, and smoke poured out of the engine. The driver said a mechanic would come within an hour, but we waited for nearly four, in the heat, with no shop nearby. I missed the engagement ceremony completely, which was really disappointing, and my phone battery died, so I couldn't even tell my family I was safe. Eventually the bus company sent a replacement bus, and a kind passenger lent me her power bank. I arrived just in time for dinner! Lesson learned: always carry a charged power bank, water and some snacks on long journeys. Take care, Shahbaz
    Possible content: Bullet 1: journey (train, bus, flight) and problem: delay, breakdown, lost luggage, missed connection. Bullet 2: effects: missed event, extra cost, waiting, worry. Bullet 3: resolution and lesson (keep a charger, check times, stay calm, carry snacks). Content (6 marks): 5–6 = all three bullets covered and well developed, sense of audience and purpose fully clear, friendly informal register kept throughout; 3–4 = all bullets covered but some development thin, register mostly suitable; 1–2 = one bullet missing or barely touched, little development; 0 = nothing relevant.
