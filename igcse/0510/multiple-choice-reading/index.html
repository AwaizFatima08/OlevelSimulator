Q1Multiple-choice questions on an article · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read the article about a young chess champion from Lahore. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.The girl who beat the grandmasterWhen twelve-year-old Mehak Rizvi sat down opposite a visiting grandmaster at a Lahore hotel last March, few people in the room expected the game to last long. Two hours later, the grandmaster stood up, shook her hand and resigned.Mehak learned the game at the age of six from her grandfather, who used to play with her every evening after dinner. 'He never let me win,' she says. 'That made me angry at first, but it taught me that I had to earn every victory.' By the time she was nine, she was beating everyone in her family, and her school chess teacher suggested she join a club in the city.Her parents were not sure at first. The club met three evenings a week, and her father worried that chess would take time away from her studies. However, her marks actually improved, and her mother noticed that she had become much more patient when doing her homework.Mehak now trains for about two hours a day. She spends one hour solving puzzles and the other playing online against opponents from other countries. She says that the hardest part is not learning new openings but staying calm when she makes a mistake. 'In the past, one bad move would ruin the whole game for me, because I couldn't stop thinking about it. Now I take a deep breath and focus on the next move.'Her dream is to become Pakistan's first female grandmaster. To reach that title, she will need to play in international tournaments, which are expensive to travel to. A local company has offered to pay for her trip to a youth championship in Malaysia next year, and she is already preparing.

  1. (a) Who first taught Mehak to play chess?A her school chess teacherB a coach at a city clubC her grandfather [1]
  2. (b) What did Mehak learn from the way her grandfather played?A that she should not get angryB that practice every evening is necessaryC that winning has to be deserved [1]
  3. (c) Why were Mehak's parents unsure about the club?A Her mother thought she was not patient enough.B Her father thought it might affect her schoolwork.C The club was too far from their home. [1]
  4. (d) How does Mehak spend half of her daily training?A learning new openingsB playing opponents abroad onlineC playing her family [1]
  5. (e) What does Mehak find most difficult?A remembering openingsB keeping calm after a mistakeC playing for two hours [1]
  6. (f) What will happen next year?A Mehak will travel to Malaysia with a company's help.B Mehak will become a grandmaster.C A company will organise a tournament in Lahore. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C her grandfather
    She learned at six from her grandfather. The school teacher only suggested she join a club (A).
  2. (b) C that winning has to be deserved
    'He never let me win… I had to earn every victory'. She did get angry (A), but that was not the lesson.
  3. (c) B Her father thought it might affect her schoolwork.
    Her father worried chess would take time from her studies. Patience (A) was something her mother noticed later as an improvement.
  4. (d) B playing opponents abroad online
    One hour puzzles, one hour online against opponents from other countries. Openings (A) are mentioned as not the hardest part.
  5. (e) B keeping calm after a mistake
    'the hardest part is not learning new openings but staying calm when she makes a mistake'.
  6. (f) A Mehak will travel to Malaysia with a company's help.
    A local company will pay for her trip to a youth championship in Malaysia. Becoming a grandmaster (B) is only her dream.
Q2Multiple-choice questions on an article · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read the article about a school that grows its own food. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Lessons in the vegetable patchBehind the classrooms of a girls' secondary school in Faisalabad, what used to be a dusty car park is now a thriving garden. Rows of spinach, onions and coriander grow alongside lemon trees and a small greenhouse built from recycled plastic bottles.The idea came from the biology teacher, Mrs Nasreen Akhtar, three years ago. 'I was teaching photosynthesis, and I realised many of my students had never actually grown a plant,' she says. She asked the head teacher for a corner of land, and a group of twenty students volunteered to help clear it.The first season was not a success. The soil was full of stones and the tomatoes died in the summer heat. Instead of giving up, the students asked local farmers for advice. They learned to add compost made from kitchen scraps and to plant crops that could cope with high temperatures.Today, the garden produces enough vegetables to supply the school canteen twice a week. Any extra is sold to parents at a small stall on Fridays, and the money is used to buy seeds and tools. Teachers of other subjects have started to use the garden too: maths classes measure the growth of plants, and art students sketch the flowers.For many students, the biggest change is in their attitude to food. 'I never ate spinach before,' says fifteen-year-old Hira. 'But when you've grown it yourself, you want to taste it.' Mrs Akhtar now hopes to help other schools in the city start their own gardens.

  1. (a) What was the garden area used for before?A a car parkB a sports groundC a farm [1]
  2. (b) Why did Mrs Akhtar start the garden?A Many students had no experience of growing plants.B The canteen needed cheaper vegetables.C The head teacher asked her to. [1]
  3. (c) What went wrong in the first season?A The students lost interest.B The farmers gave poor advice.C Some crops died because of the heat. [1]
  4. (d) How is the money from the Friday stall used?A to improve the canteenB to pay for seeds and toolsC to buy a greenhouse [1]
  5. (e) How do maths classes use the garden?A They sketch the flowers.B They calculate the stall's profits.C They measure how plants grow. [1]
  6. (f) What does Hira say about spinach?A Growing it made her want to eat it.B She has always liked it.C It is the easiest crop to grow. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A a car park
    'what used to be a dusty car park'.
  2. (b) A Many students had no experience of growing plants.
    She realised many students had never grown a plant.
  3. (c) C Some crops died because of the heat.
    The tomatoes died in the summer heat; the farmers' advice helped later (B).
  4. (d) B to pay for seeds and tools
    Money used to buy seeds and tools. Greenhouse (C) was built from bottles.
  5. (e) C They measure how plants grow.
    Maths classes measure the growth of plants; art students sketch (A).
  6. (f) A Growing it made her want to eat it.
    'when you've grown it yourself, you want to taste it'.

    A business pays for fuel; she is still looking for help with driving.

Read the article about a family-run sweet shop. For each question, choose the correct answer, A, B or C.Sweet traditionsIn a narrow lane in old Hyderabad, Sindh, a small sweet shop has been open for more than ninety years. It was started by Abdul Ghani in 1934, and today it is run by his great-grandson, Faisal.The shop is famous for its sohan halwa, a rich sweet made from milk, sugar, wheat and plenty of ghee, topped with almonds and pistachios. Faisal still uses his great-grandfather's recipe, which has never been written down. 'My father taught me by letting me watch him for years before I was allowed to cook it myself,' he says.Making the halwa is hard work. The mixture must be stirred for several hours over a fire in large metal pans, and if the cook stops even for a minute, it can burn. Faisal starts at four in the morning so that the halwa is ready when the shop opens at ten.Customers come from all over the country, especially before weddings and religious festivals. Some people living abroad ask relatives to bring tins of halwa back with them. Recently, Faisal started selling online, and he now sends orders to customers in other cities.Faisal's son is only eight, but he already likes helping in the shop. 'I hope he will continue,' Faisal says. 'But I won't force him. He must choose for himself.'

  1. (a) Who started the shop?A Faisal's great-grandfatherB Faisal's fatherC Faisal [1]

  5. (e) What has Faisal started doing recently?A opening a second shopB exporting tins abroadC selling online [1]
  6. (f) What does Faisal say about his son?A His son should decide his own future.B His son must take over the shop.C His son is too young to help. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Faisal's great-grandfather
    Started by Abdul Ghani; run by his great-grandson Faisal.
  2. (b) C by watching his father for years
    His father let him watch for years; never written down.
  3. (c) B so that it does not burn
    If the cook stops even for a minute, it can burn.
  4. (d) A at four in the morning
    Starts at four; shop opens at ten (B).
  5. (e) C selling online
    Recently started selling online; relatives (not Faisal) take tins abroad.
  6. (f) A His son should decide his own future.
    'I won't force him. He must choose for himself.'
