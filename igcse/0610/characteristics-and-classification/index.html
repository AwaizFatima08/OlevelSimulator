Q2Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A wildlife survey in the Margalla Hills records a common leopard and a rose-ringed parakeet.

  1. (a) State three features that all mammals have but birds do not have. [3]
  2. (b) State two features that birds have that mammals do not have. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fur / hair on the skin; mammary glands (produce milk); external ears (pinnae).
    1 mark each for any three of: fur/hair; mammary glands / feed young on milk; external ear (pinna); accept: give birth to live young (most), diaphragm, different types of teeth. Do not accept 'warm-blooded' (birds are too).
  2. (b) Feathers; a beak (and lay hard-shelled eggs).
    1 mark each for any two of: feathers; beak; wings (forelimbs as wings); lay eggs with hard shells.

  2. (b) A woodlouse has seven pairs of legs and two pairs of antennae. Name its arthropod group. [1]
  3. (c) A centipede has a long body made of many similar segments, each with one pair of legs. Name its arthropod group. [1]

  1. (a) Insects
    B1 insects.
  2. (b) Crustaceans
    B1 crustaceans.
  3. (c) Myriapods
    B1 myriapods.
  4. (d) Jointed legs / an exoskeleton / a segmented body.
    B1 any one of: jointed legs (limbs); exoskeleton; segmented body.
Q4Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Name the kingdom to which each organism belongs: (i) Chlorella, a single-celled organism with a nucleus and chloroplasts that lives in ponds; (ii) a bacterium that lives in soil; (iii) a mushroom. [3]
  2. (b) State one feature found in all prokaryote cells that is not found in protoctist cells. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Protoctists; (ii) Prokaryotes; (iii) Fungi.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) No nucleus – DNA is a circular chromosome free in the cytoplasm (plasmids may be present).
    B1 no nucleus / circular DNA / plasmids. Do not accept 'cell wall' (some protoctists have walls).
Q5Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature is shared by organisms in the fungus kingdom and the prokaryote kingdom but NOT by organisms in the plant kingdom? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B cell walls are not made of cellulose
    Fungi and prokaryotes both have cell walls, but neither wall is made of cellulose, whereas plant walls are cellulose. A is wrong because plants also have cell walls; prokaryotes have no nucleus (C).
Q6Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A frog and a monitor lizard are both vertebrates. Describe two differences between the skin and eggs of amphibians and reptiles. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why amphibians usually need to return to water or very damp places to breed. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Skin: amphibians have moist, smooth skin without scales; reptiles have dry skin covered in scales. Eggs: amphibians lay eggs without shells (in water / jelly); reptiles lay eggs with soft, leathery shells (on land).
    B1 amphibian skin moist / no scales; B1 reptile skin dry / scaly; B1 amphibian eggs no shell / laid in water; B1 reptile eggs leathery / rubbery shell / laid on land. Comparative pairs needed for full marks.
  2. (b) Their eggs have no shell, so they would dry out (lose water) on land; the eggs must be fertilised and develop in water.
    B1 eggs lack a shell / waterproof covering so dry out; accept larvae (tadpoles) live in water.
Q7Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A farmer in Sindh grows wheat and maize. Both are monocotyledons. State two features of the leaves or flowers that show they are monocotyledons. [2]
  2. (b) Mustard and chickpea plants are dicotyledons. Give two ways their leaves or seeds differ from those of monocotyledons. [2]
  3. (c) Ferns are also in the plant kingdom. State how the reproduction of ferns differs from that of flowering plants. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Leaves have parallel veins; flower parts are in threes (or multiples of three).
    1 mark each for any two of: parallel veins; long narrow leaves; flower parts in threes/multiples of three; seed has one cotyledon.
  2. (b) Dicot leaves have a branching network of veins (not parallel); dicot seeds have two cotyledons (not one).
    1 mark each for any two comparative points: net/branched veins vs parallel; two cotyledons vs one; flower parts in fours or fives vs threes; leaves usually broad vs narrow.
  3. (c) Ferns do not produce flowers or seeds; they reproduce using spores.
    B1 spores (no flowers / no seeds).

  3. (c) State one feature that the student should look for, using a hand lens, to confirm that a preserved specimen is a mammal rather than a bird. [1]

  2. (b) R: reptile. S: bird.
    B1 R reptile; B1 S bird.
  3. (c) Hair / fur on the skin (or external ears, mammary glands / nipples).
    B1 hair/fur; accept pinna, nipples/mammary glands.

  2. (b) A student observes an animal on a beach in Gwadar. It has a hard, chalky exoskeleton, ten jointed legs, two pairs of antennae and compound eyes on stalks. Identify its group and give two reasons for your answer. [3]

Q10Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement about viruses is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B They contain genetic material inside a protein coat and can only reproduce inside living cells.
    A virus is a protein coat around genetic material (DNA or RNA); it has no cell structures, so it reproduces only inside host cells. Viruses are not placed in any of the five kingdoms (D) and have no ribosomes or membrane of their own.
Q11Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Yeast is placed in the fungus kingdom even though it is single-celled. State two features of fungal cells that yeast shows. [2]
  2. (b) Most fungi, such as bread mould, are made of thread-like structures. Name these structures. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why fungi are not classified as plants, even though both have cell walls and do not move from place to place. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A nucleus; a cell wall that is not made of cellulose; (stores carbohydrate as glycogen; no chloroplasts).
    1 mark each for any two of: has a nucleus; cell wall not made of cellulose (accept chitin); no chloroplasts / does not photosynthesise; stores glycogen.
  2. (b) Hyphae (forming a mycelium).
    B1 hyphae.
  3. (c) Fungi do not have chloroplasts so cannot photosynthesise; they feed by saprophytic / parasitic nutrition (secreting enzymes and absorbing the products); their cell walls are not made of cellulose; they store glycogen, not starch.
    1 mark each for any three of: no chloroplasts/chlorophyll; cannot photosynthesise / heterotrophic, feed on organic matter (saprophytic/parasitic); cell wall not cellulose; store glycogen not starch.
Q12Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

During an outbreak of dengue fever in Karachi, a microbiology student compared the dengue virus with a bacterium that causes a skin infection.

  1. (a) Compare the structure of a bacterial cell with that of a virus particle. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why viruses are not placed in any of the five kingdoms. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bacterium: cell with cytoplasm, cell membrane, cell wall, ribosomes, circular DNA and (often) plasmids. Virus: no cell structure – only genetic material (DNA or RNA) surrounded by a protein coat; no cytoplasm, membrane or ribosomes; much smaller.
    1 mark each for any four comparative points: bacterium has cytoplasm/virus does not; cell membrane; cell wall vs protein coat; ribosomes; circular DNA + plasmids vs DNA or RNA; bacteria are cells/viruses are not cells; viruses much smaller.
  2. (b) They are not made of cells and do not show all the characteristics of living organisms (e.g. no respiration, nutrition or excretion) on their own; they can only reproduce inside a host cell.
    B1 not cells / no cell structure; B1 do not carry out (all) life processes, e.g. respiration / only reproduce inside other cells.
Q13Features of kingdoms, groups & viruses · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student collected three arthropods from leaf litter in Murree and recorded these features.

  1. (a) Use the information to identify the group to which each animal belongs: Animal 1 – 3 pairs of legs, 2 pairs of wings, 1 pair of antennae, 3 body sections. Animal 2 – 4 pairs of legs, 0 antennae, 2 body sections. Animal 3 – about 60 segments, 2 pairs of legs on most segments, 1 pair of antennae. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the student should count legs on both sides of the body rather than on one side only and then doubling. [1]
  3. (c) State one other feature, visible with a hand lens, that would help to confirm that Animal 1 is an insect. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1: insect; 2: arachnid; 3: myriapod.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Some legs may be missing / damaged, so counting both sides gives an accurate number.
    B1 legs may be lost or damaged on preserved/live specimens; idea of accuracy.
  3. (c) Compound eyes / mouthparts / spiracles / wings attached to the thorax.
    B1 any sensible visible insect feature, e.g. wings on thorax, compound eyes.
Q14Classification, species & binomial system · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The scientific name of the domestic cat is Felis catus. Which statement is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Felis is the genus and catus is the species.
    In the binomial system the first name (capital letter) is the genus, the second (small letter) is the species. A reverses them.
Q15Classification, species & binomial system · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the term species. [2]
  2. (b) A horse and a donkey can mate and produce a mule. Mules are infertile. Explain whether horses and donkeys belong to the same species. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.
    B1 group of organisms that can (inter)breed / reproduce; B1 to produce fertile offspring.
  2. (b) They are different species, because although they can breed, the offspring (mule) are not fertile.
    B1 different species; B1 offspring infertile / cannot reproduce.
Q16Classification, species & binomial system · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The lion (Panthera leo), tiger (Panthera tigris) and leopard (Panthera pardus) are in the genus Panthera. The cheetah is Acinonyx jubatus. Which pair would be expected to share the MOST similar DNA base sequences? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Panthera pardus and Panthera tigris
    Organisms in the same genus share a more recent common ancestor, so their DNA base sequences are more similar. Cheetah is in a different genus (A).
Q17Classification, species & binomial system · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the binomial system of naming organisms. [2]
  2. (b) The mango tree is Mangifera indica. Explain why scientists use this name rather than a local name such as 'aam'. [2]
  3. (c) Write the name correctly as it would appear in a printed textbook. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) An internationally agreed system in which each species is given a two-part scientific name: the genus name followed by the species name.
    B1 two names / two parts; B1 genus then species; accept internationally agreed / in Latin.
  2. (b) Local names differ between languages and regions; the scientific name is the same worldwide, so scientists everywhere know exactly which species is meant (avoids confusion).
    B1 common names vary between countries/languages; B1 binomial name is universal / avoids confusion.
  3. (c) Mangifera indica, in italics, with a capital M for the genus and a small i for the species.
    B1 italics (or underlined if hand-written) and capital for genus only.
Q18Classification, species & binomial system · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

Dolphins and sharks have a similar streamlined shape with fins, but dolphins are mammals and sharks are fish.

  1. (a) State the main purpose of classification systems. [1]
  2. (b) Traditionally organisms were classified using morphology (body form) and anatomy. Suggest one reason why classification using DNA sequences may be more reliable. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) To group organisms according to the features they share, so that they reflect evolutionary relationships.
    B1 group by shared features / reflect evolutionary relationships.
  2. (b) Organisms that look similar may not be closely related (similar features can evolve separately in different environments); DNA shows actual genetic relatedness / common ancestry.
    B1 similar appearance may be due to similar environment, not relatedness; B1 DNA directly reflects how recently species shared a common ancestor.
Q19Classification, species & binomial system · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Part of the same gene was sequenced in four species of wild cat (12 bases shown):Species A: ATGCCTAGGTCASpecies B: ATGCATAGCTGASpecies C: ATGCCTAGGTCCSpecies D: TTGACTAAGTGA

  1. (a) State which species is most closely related to species A. Explain your answer. [2]
  2. (b) State the number of positions at which species D differs from species A. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the sequence of bases in DNA is useful for classifying organisms. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Species C; its sequence differs from A at only 1 of the 12 positions, fewer differences than any other species.
    B1 species C; B1 fewest differences in base sequence (1 difference).
  2. (b) 4
    B1 4.
  3. (c) Organisms that share a more recent common ancestor have more similar base sequences, so the degree of similarity shows evolutionary relationships.
    B1 more similar sequence = more closely related; B1 shared (recent) common ancestor / reflects evolutionary relationships.
Q20Classification, species & binomial system · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A sequence of 40 DNA bases from the same gene was compared in three species of freshwater fish. Species W and species X differ at 6 positions.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the 40 bases that are the same in species W and species X. [2]
  2. (b) Species W and Y differ at 14 of the 40 positions. Calculate the percentage similarity. [1]
  3. (c) State and explain which species, X or Y, shares a more recent common ancestor with W. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 85 %
    M1 (40 − 6) ÷ 40 × 100; A1 85 %.
  2. (b) 65 %
    B1 (40 − 14)/40 × 100 = 65 %.
  3. (c) X; it has a higher percentage of identical bases (85 % against 65 %), so fewer mutations have accumulated since they separated.
    B1 X; B1 more similar sequence / fewer differences.

Q24Characteristics of living organisms · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A car engine takes in fuel and oxygen and gives out carbon dioxide and water. A student says this means the car is alive. Name two characteristics of living organisms that the car seems to show. [2]
  2. (b) Name three characteristics of living organisms that the car does not show. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nutrition (takes in fuel – a source of energy) and excretion-like release of waste / respiration-like release of energy; also movement.
    1 mark each for any two of: movement; 'respiration' (releases energy from fuel with oxygen); 'nutrition' (takes in fuel); 'excretion' (gives out waste gases).
  2. (b) Growth; reproduction; sensitivity (cannot respond to stimuli by itself).
    1 mark each for: growth; reproduction; sensitivity. Accept excretion/respiration only if not used in (a).
Q25Characteristics of living organisms · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the term reproduction. [1]
  2. (b) Define movement as a characteristic of living organisms. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how a rose plant, which is fixed in the soil, still shows movement. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The processes that make more of the same kind of organism.
    B1 processes that make more of the same kind of organism.
  2. (b) An action by an organism or part of an organism causing a change of position or place.
    B1 action by organism or part of organism; B1 causing change of position or place.
  3. (c) Parts of the plant move, e.g. leaves/flowers turn towards light, flowers open and close, or cytoplasm moves within cells.
    B1 any valid example of a part moving.

Q29Dichotomous keys · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student has four seeds:Kidney bean: 15 mm long, rounded, smooth, no grooveBroad bean: 22 mm long, flat, smooth, no grooveWheat: 6 mm long, oval, groove along one sideMustard: 2 mm long, round, no groove

Show answer
  1. (a) e.g. 1 seed longer than 10 mm → 2; 10 mm or shorter → 3. 2 seed flat → broad bean; seed rounded → kidney bean. 3 seed has a groove → wheat; no groove → mustard.

Q30Dichotomous keys · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A key to five freshwater fish species, A–E, from the Indus river:1 mouth sucker-shaped → 3; mouth not sucker-shaped → 22 adipose fin present → 4; adipose fin absent → species A3 barbels present → species D; barbels absent → species E4 body without scales → species B; body with scales → species C

  1. (a) Use the key to identify fish X, which has a sucker-shaped mouth and no barbels. Show the steps you used. [2]
  2. (b) Fish Y has a normal mouth, an adipose fin and scales absent. Identify Y. [1]
  3. (c) A student says step 2 of the key would be better written as 'large fish' or 'small fish'. Explain why this would be a poor change. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Species E: step 1 (mouth sucker-shaped) leads to step 3; no barbels leads to species E.
    B1 correct route 1 → 3; B1 E.
  2. (b) species B
    B1 B.
  3. (c) 'Large' and 'small' are relative/subjective and size changes with age, so different users would choose differently; a key needs clear, observable contrasting features.
    B1 subjective / no fixed boundary; B1 size varies with age / individual, so fish could be misidentified.
