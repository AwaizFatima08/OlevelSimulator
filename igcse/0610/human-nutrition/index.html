Q5Chemical digestion & enzymes · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the enzyme pepsin works in the stomach, where the pH is about 2, but salivary amylase does not work well there. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pepsin has its optimum pH near 2, so its active site matches its substrate best in the acidic stomach. Amylase has its optimum near pH 7; at pH 2 its active site changes shape (it is denatured), so it stops working.
    1 mark: pepsin has an optimum pH of about 2. 1 mark: amylase has an optimum near pH 7. 1 mark: at pH 2 the active site of amylase changes shape. 1 mark: the enzyme is denatured and stops working.
Q6Chemical digestion & enzymes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which row shows the places where starch is digested and the product of amylase action? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B mouth and small intestine; maltose
    Salivary amylase acts in the mouth and pancreatic amylase in the small intestine (duodenum); amylase produces maltose, which maltase then breaks down to glucose. Glucose is the product of maltase, not amylase; amylase does not act in the acid stomach.

Q10Chemical digestion & enzymes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which substance is produced when protein is partly digested by pepsin in the stomach? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D polypeptides (peptides)
    Pepsin is a protease that cuts protein into smaller polypeptide chains. Amino acids are only made later, when enzymes in the small intestine finish the job. Fatty acids and glycerol come from fats, and glucose from starch.
Q11Chemical digestion & enzymes · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement about the digestion of starch is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Maltase converts maltose into glucose in the small intestine
    Amylase breaks starch into maltose, and maltase in the small intestine converts maltose into glucose. Pepsin acts on protein, lipase acts on fats, and salivary amylase is made by the salivary glands, not the stomach.

Show answer

    1 mark: sodium pumped out by active transport. 1 mark: sodium diffuses back in down its gradient. 1 mark: glucose carried in with the sodium through a carrier protein. 1 mark: glucose diffuses from the cell into the blood.

    1 mark: fats are re-formed inside the epithelial cells. 1 mark: fat droplets are too large for the capillaries. 1 mark: lacteal is a lymph vessel that carries fat to the blood via the lymphatic system.
