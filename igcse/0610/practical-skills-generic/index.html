Q1Tables, graphs & calculations · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A student places cubes of starch–agar, stained blue-black with iodine, into amylase solution at five temperatures and times how long each cube takes to lose its blue-black colour. She writes her results in her notebook as: "20 degrees took 340 seconds, 30 degrees took 190 seconds, 40 degrees took 95 seconds, 50 degrees took 160 seconds, 60 degrees took 500 seconds".Describe how she should set out these results in a table. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) two columns (or rows): first 'temperature / °C' (independent variable), second 'time / s'; units only in the headings; numbers only in the body, in increasing order of temperature
    B1 independent variable (temperature) in first column; B1 headings with units (temperature / °C, time / s); B1 no units in the body of the table / all five results entered correctly.
Q2Tables, graphs & calculations · Core · Paper 5 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A student records the time taken for an enzyme reaction to finish at three concentrations of enzyme: 1 % – 125 s; 2 % – 80 s; 4 % – 50 s. Calculate the rate of reaction for each concentration using rate = 1000 ÷ time. Give each answer to one decimal place. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 %: 8.0; 2 %: 12.5; 4 %: 20.0
    B1 each.
Q3Tables, graphs & calculations · Core · Paper 6 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A thermometer is marked in 1 °C divisions with a small mark halfway between each. At the start of an experiment the liquid level is at the halfway mark between 23 °C and 24 °C. At the end it is exactly on 31 °C. State both readings and calculate the temperature change. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) start 23.5 °C; end 31.0 °C; change 7.5 °C
    B1 23.5 °C; B1 31.0 °C; B1 7.5 °C.
Q4Tables, graphs & calculations · Standard · Paper 6 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Three repeat readings for the time taken for a leaf disc to float were 42 s, 46 s and 61 s. Identify the anomalous result and calculate the mean of the other readings. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why carrying out repeats is useful. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) anomalous 61 s; mean 44 s
    B1 61 s; B1 (42 + 46) ÷ 2 = 44 s.
  2. (b) allows anomalous results to be identified (and a mean calculated)
    B1. Do not credit 'makes it accurate'.
Q5Tables, graphs & calculations · Standard · Paper 6 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A potato cylinder had a mass of 2.40 g before and 2.13 g after soaking in a sugar solution for 30 minutes. Calculate the percentage change in mass. Give your answer to two significant figures and show whether it is a gain or a loss. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) −11 % (a loss)
    M1 change = 2.13 − 2.40 = −0.27 g; M1 ÷ 2.40 × 100 (= −11.25); A1 −11 %.
Q6Tables, graphs & calculations · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

Time for a cut piece of beetroot to release enough pigment to colour the water a set shade:temperature / °C | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60time / s | 70 | 48 | 32 | 15 | 6

  1. (a) State which variable should be plotted on the x-axis and why. [1]
  2. (b) Assuming the change between 30 °C and 40 °C is linear, estimate the time at 35 °C. [1]
  3. (c) State two things a student should do when plotting this graph so that it gains full marks. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) temperature, because it is the independent variable (the one the student changed)
    B1.
  2. (b) 40 s
    B1 (48 + 32) ÷ 2 = 40 s.
  3. (c) linear scales using more than half the grid; axes labelled with units; points plotted accurately as small crosses; join points with ruled straight lines or a smooth curve; do not extrapolate beyond 20 °C or 60 °C
    1 mark each for any two.
Q7Tables, graphs & calculations · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A piece of Hydrilla pondweed is placed in hydrogencarbonate solution under a lamp. The gas given off is collected in an inverted syringe. In 5 minutes, 3.6 cm³ of gas is collected. Calculate the rate of gas production in cm³ per minute. [1]
  2. (b) The lamp is moved further away and 1.5 cm³ is collected in 5 minutes. Calculate the percentage decrease in rate. Give your answer to two significant figures. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.72 cm³ per min
    B1 3.6 ÷ 5.
  2. (b) 58 %
    M1 (3.6 − 1.5) ÷ 3.6 × 100 (or using rates 0.72 and 0.30); A1 58 %.
Q8Tables, graphs & calculations · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student has a 1.0 mol per dm³ sucrose solution and distilled water. He needs 20 cm³ of each of the concentrations 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 mol per dm³. Calculate the volumes of sucrose solution and distilled water for 0.2 and for 0.6 mol per dm³. [2]
  2. (b) State the piece of apparatus he should use to measure these volumes and explain your choice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.2: 4 cm³ sucrose + 16 cm³ water; 0.6: 12 cm³ sucrose + 8 cm³ water
    B1 each pair.
  2. (b) a graduated syringe (or 10 cm³ / 25 cm³ measuring cylinder) – measures small volumes precisely (a beaker is not precise)
    B1 syringe / small measuring cylinder; B1 precise / small graduations.
Q9Tables, graphs & calculations · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

Students measured the lengths of 36 earthworms collected from a school garden and grouped them:length / mm | 40–59 | 60–79 | 80–99 | 100–119 | 120–139number of earthworms | 3 | 8 | 13 | 9 | 3

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of earthworms in the 100–119 mm class. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why these data should be shown as a histogram rather than a line graph. [2]
  3. (c) State the modal class. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25 %
    M1 9 ÷ 36 × 100; A1 25 %.
  2. (b) length is continuous data grouped into classes; each class is a range, so bars of equal width that touch are used (a line graph is for showing how one variable changes with another)
    B1 continuous data in classes; B1 frequency of each class shown by touching bars.
  3. (c) 80–99 mm
    B1.
Q10Tables, graphs & calculations · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 3 marks

A student heats standard glucose solutions with Benedict's solution in a boiling water-bath and records the time for the first colour change from blue.glucose concentration / % | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.0time / s | 240 | 120 | 60A fruit-juice sample took 90 s.

  1. (a) Use the pattern in the results (time × concentration is constant) to estimate the glucose concentration of the fruit juice. Give your answer to two significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why it is better to estimate the concentration from a graph drawn through all the standard results than from one pair of standards. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.3 %
    M1 time × concentration = 120; M1/A1 120 ÷ 90 = 1.3 %.
  2. (b) the graph uses all the data, so the estimate is more reliable / reduces the effect of an error in one reading
    B1.
Q11Planning an investigation (6-mark plan) · Core · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation into the effect of sucrose concentration on the change in mass of cylinders of radish (mooli). You have a radish, a cork borer, a scalpel, a ruler, a balance, sucrose solutions of 0.0, 0.2, 0.4, 0.6 and 0.8 mol per dm³, boiling tubes and paper towels. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cut equal cylinders, blot and weigh, place in each concentration for a fixed time, blot and reweigh, calculate % change; repeat; control size, volume, time, temperature
    1 mark each for any six: use cork borer and scalpel to cut cylinders of the same diameter and length (e.g. 30 mm); remove skin / same part of radish; blot and weigh each cylinder before; place in same volume of each concentration (five concentrations); leave for the same time (e.g. 30 min) at the same temperature; remove, blot in the same way and reweigh; calculate percentage change in mass; repeat with three cylinders per concentration and find mean; safety: cut away from fingers on a tile.
Q12Planning an investigation (6-mark plan) · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find the effect of pH on the activity of amylase. You are provided with: starch solution, amylase solution, buffer solutions of pH 4, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9, iodine solution, a spotting tile, test-tubes, syringes, a stop-clock and a thermostatically controlled water-bath.Your plan should include what you would measure, the variables you would control and how you would make your results reliable. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mix fixed volumes of starch, buffer and amylase at each pH at 37 °C; test a drop with iodine every 30 s; record time until iodine stays orange-brown; repeat three times; control temperature, volumes, concentrations
    1 mark each for any six: independent variable pH using at least five buffers (4–9); same volume of buffer added to starch; same volume and concentration of starch; same volume and concentration of amylase; temperature kept constant with thermostatically controlled water-bath (e.g. 37 °C); solutions equilibrated to temperature before mixing; sample a drop onto iodine on the tile at regular intervals (e.g. every 30 s); record time until no blue-black colour (starch gone); rate = 1 ÷ time; repeat each pH at least three times and calculate a mean; control with boiled amylase; safety: eye protection with iodine.
Q13Planning an investigation (6-mark plan) · Standard · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how light intensity affects the rate of photosynthesis of Hydrilla. You may use a lamp, a metre rule, a beaker, sodium hydrogencarbonate solution, a stop-clock and any other normal apparatus. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hydrilla in hydrogencarbonate solution; lamp at five distances; acclimatise 5 min; count bubbles per minute or collect gas volume; heat shield; control temperature, CO₂, same piece of plant; repeat
    1 mark each for any six: place same piece of Hydrilla in a beaker / test-tube of sodium hydrogencarbonate solution; vary distance of lamp (at least five distances, e.g. 10–50 cm) measured with metre rule; allow time to adjust (e.g. 5 min) at each distance; count bubbles in a fixed time or collect gas in a syringe / measuring cylinder; keep temperature constant (heat filter / water bath / LED lamp); same concentration of hydrogencarbonate (CO₂ supply); darken the room so only the lamp gives light; repeat and calculate mean; safety: keep water away from mains lamp.
Q14Planning an investigation (6-mark plan) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how temperature affects the rate of anaerobic respiration of yeast. You may use any normal laboratory apparatus. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Yeast + glucose suspension in tubes in water-baths at 20–60 °C, oil layer, count bubbles or measure gas volume in a gas syringe/ inverted cylinder over a fixed time; acclimatise; repeat; control yeast mass, glucose volume and concentration
    1 mark each for any six: independent variable temperature, at least five values (e.g. 20, 30, 40, 50, 60 °C); thermostatically controlled water-baths; same mass / volume of yeast suspension; same volume and concentration of glucose; layer of oil / boiled glucose solution to keep conditions anaerobic; allow mixture to reach temperature before starting; measure volume of CO₂ (gas syringe / upturned measuring cylinder) or count bubbles over a fixed time (e.g. 5 min); calculate rate; repeat at least three times and calculate mean; control with no yeast / boiled yeast; safety: care with hot water.
Q15Planning an investigation (6-mark plan) · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation into the effect of air movement (wind speed) on the rate of water loss from a leafy shoot. You may use a cut leafy shoot, an electric fan, a balance, a tube of water, oil and any other normal laboratory apparatus. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shoot in water tube with oil layer on a balance; fan at different distances; measure mass loss over fixed time; same shoot or similar shoots; control light, temperature, humidity; repeat
    1 mark each for any six: shoot cut under water and placed in tube of water; oil layer to stop evaporation from the water surface; weigh apparatus at start; vary wind speed by placing fan at five distances (e.g. 25, 50, 75, 100, 125 cm) or fan speed settings, plus a no-fan control; measure mass after a fixed time (e.g. 30 min); calculate mass loss per hour; same shoot / shoots with similar leaf area; keep light intensity, temperature and humidity constant; repeat and calculate mean; safety: keep water away from the fan's electrics.

Show answer
  1. (a) draw large (fill most of the space); use clear, continuous lines with a sharp pencil; no shading or colouring; correct proportions; label lines ruled, touching the structure, without arrowheads
    1 mark each for any four.
Q17Biological drawing · Standard · Paper 6 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A guava cut in half across its middle shows a thin skin, a thick layer of firm flesh and a central pulpy region containing about 150 small, hard seeds. A tomato cut in the same way shows a thin skin, thinner flesh and three chambers of jelly, each containing about 20 flat seeds attached to a central column.State one similarity and two differences between the two fruits, using comparative language. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) similarity: both have a thin skin / both have seeds inside the fruit; differences: guava has more seeds than the tomato; guava has thicker flesh; guava seeds are in one central region whereas tomato seeds are in three chambers; tomato seeds are flatter
    B1 similarity; B1 each difference (max 2); differences must compare both fruits (not colour or overall size).
Q18Biological drawing · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student is asked to draw one cell from a slide of onion epidermis seen under high power. State three structures that should be drawn and labelled. [3]
  2. (b) The student's drawing of the cell is 60 mm long. The actual length of the cell is 0.3 mm. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) cell wall; cytoplasm; nucleus (also cell membrane closely against the wall; vacuole)
    1 mark each for any three.
  2. (b) ×200
    B1 60 ÷ 0.3 = ×200 (both lengths in mm).

Show answer

Q20Variables & controls · Core · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student places 10 cm³ of hydrogen peroxide in each of five test-tubes at 30 °C. She adds 1 g, 2 g, 3 g, 4 g or 5 g of chopped liver and measures the height of froth (oxygen bubbles) after 2 minutes.

  1. (a) State the independent variable and the dependent variable. [2]
  2. (b) State two variables that the student has kept constant. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one other variable that should be controlled. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) independent: mass of liver; dependent: height of froth
    B1 each.
  2. (b) volume of hydrogen peroxide; temperature; time (2 minutes)
    1 mark each for any two.
  3. (c) concentration of hydrogen peroxide / size of liver pieces (surface area) / diameter of test-tubes
    B1.
Q21Variables & controls · Standard · Paper 6 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) In an investigation of catalase activity, one tube contains liver that has been boiled and cooled. Explain why this tube is included. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) it is a control; it shows that the bubbles in the other tubes are due to the active enzyme (boiling denatures catalase) and not some other factor
    B1 control / comparison; B1 shows the result is due to enzyme activity.
Q22Variables & controls · Standard · Paper 5 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) In an osmosis experiment using potato cylinders in different salt solutions, explain why all cylinders must be cut to the same length and diameter. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the enzyme and substrate solutions in an enzyme experiment are placed in the water-bath separately for 5 minutes before mixing. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) so they have the same surface area (and mass / volume); surface area affects the rate of water movement, so comparisons are fair
    B1 same surface area; B1 surface area affects rate of osmosis / fair test.
  2. (b) so both reach the required temperature before the reaction starts; otherwise the reaction would begin at a different temperature
    B1 reach the set temperature; B1 reaction occurs at the intended temperature from the start.
Q23Variables & controls · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student investigates the effect of temperature on the activity of pectinase in releasing juice from chopped apple. He leaves beakers of apple pulp and enzyme on a laboratory bench, beside a sunny window, and in a refrigerator for 20 minutes, then filters the juice. Identify two weaknesses in how the independent variable was controlled and describe how to improve each. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) only three temperatures and they are not known – use at least five set temperatures measured with a thermometer; temperatures are not kept constant (sunlight varies) – use thermostatically controlled water-baths; enzyme and pulp not at temperature before mixing – equilibrate separately first
    Two weaknesses with matching improvement (2 × 2).
Q24Errors, improvements, reliability & safety · Core · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State one hazard and a precaution for each of these steps in food tests:(i) heating Benedict's solution in a water-bath(ii) cutting a potato with a scalpel [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) hot liquid may spit / scald – use a water-bath (not a direct flame), wear eye protection, use a test-tube holder; (ii) scalpel may cut fingers – cut on a tile, cut away from the body
    B1 hazard and B1 precaution for each.
Q25Errors, improvements, reliability & safety · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) In an experiment on vitamin C in fruit juices, a student adds juice drop by drop to a blue indicator (DCPIP) until the blue colour just disappears. Identify one source of error in judging the end point and suggest an improvement. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why drops from a pipette may give inaccurate volumes and how this could be improved. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) difficult to judge exactly when the colour disappears (subjective); compare against a colourless standard / white background / use a colorimeter
    B1 error; B1 matching improvement.
  2. (b) drop size varies; use a burette or graduated syringe to measure volume
    B1 error; B1 improvement.
Q26Errors, improvements, reliability & safety · Standard · Paper 5 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A student counts oxygen bubbles released from pondweed each minute. Explain why counting bubbles may give unreliable results, and describe a better method of measuring the gas produced. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) bubbles are not all the same size; some may be missed if they come fast; collect the gas in a gas syringe / inverted measuring cylinder and measure its volume
    B1 bubbles vary in size; B1 difficult to count fast bubbles accurately; B1 measure volume of gas (syringe / capillary tube / measuring cylinder).
Q27Errors, improvements, reliability & safety · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A student measured the change in mass of potato cylinders after 20 minutes in salt solutions. She blotted some cylinders firmly and others lightly, and timed using the classroom wall clock, starting the cylinders one after the other. Identify two sources of error and suggest how each could be reduced. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why each concentration should be tested with at least three cylinders. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) inconsistent blotting leaves different amounts of surface water – blot each cylinder the same way (e.g. roll once on paper towel); cylinders were in solution for different times – start each at a timed interval with a stop-clock and remove each after exactly 20 min
    Two errors each with matching improvement (2 × 2).
  2. (b) allows anomalous results to be identified and a mean to be calculated
    B1.
Q28Measurement, apparatus & observation · Core · Paper 6 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Name the most suitable apparatus to measure:(i) 7.5 cm³ of enzyme solution(ii) the temperature of a water-bath(iii) the length of a leaf [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) 10 cm³ syringe or 10 cm³ measuring cylinder; (ii) thermometer; (iii) ruler (in mm)
    B1 each. Do not accept a beaker for (i).
Q29Measurement, apparatus & observation · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student places a paper disc soaked in a garlic extract on an agar plate of bacteria. After 2 days a clear zone forms around the disc. She measures the clear zone as 14 mm across in one direction and 16 mm across at right angles. Calculate the mean diameter and then the area of the clear zone, using area = πr². Give the area to the nearest mm². [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the student measured two diameters at right angles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) mean diameter 15 mm; area 177 mm²
    B1 15 mm; M1 r = 7.5 mm, π × 7.5²; A1 177 mm².
  2. (b) the clear zone may not be a perfect circle, so a mean is more representative
    B1.
Q30Measurement, apparatus & observation · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A student records a reaction time from a digital stop-clock as 63.47 s. Explain why it is better to record this as 63 s. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why a 10 cm³ measuring cylinder is better than a 100 cm³ measuring cylinder for measuring 8 cm³ of liquid. [2]
  3. (c) When reading a measuring cylinder, state where the student should look and which part of the liquid surface should be read. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) human reaction time when starting and stopping the clock is much larger than 0.01 s, so the extra decimal places are not meaningful
    B1.
  2. (b) the 10 cm³ cylinder has smaller graduations (e.g. 0.2 cm³ instead of 1 cm³), so the reading is more precise; the error is a smaller fraction of the volume measured
    B1 smaller divisions / finer scale; B1 more precise / smaller percentage error.
  3. (c) eye level with the liquid surface; read the bottom of the meniscus
    B1 each.
