Q9Tables, graphs & calculations · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student measured the time taken for milk to clear at different temperatures in an experiment using a protease. The rate of reaction was calculated as 1000 ÷ time (in arbitrary units).Temperature (°C): 20, 30, 40, 50, 60. Time to clear (s): 84, 52, 33, 41, 118.

  1. (a) Calculate the rate of reaction at 30 °C. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) State the temperature at which the rate of reaction is highest. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the rate falls between 40 °C and 60 °C. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 19.2 (arbitrary units)
    1 mark: correct method, 1000 ÷ 52. 1 mark: 19.2.
  2. (b) 40 °C (time to clear is shortest, 33 s)
    1 mark: 40 °C has the shortest time, so the highest rate.
  3. (c) Above the optimum, the enzyme's active site changes shape (the enzyme is denatured), so fewer substrate molecules fit and the rate falls.
    1 mark: the enzyme denatures / active site changes shape. 1 mark: fewer enzyme-substrate complexes form, so the rate falls.
Q10Tables, graphs & calculations · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

Seeds were sown in three trays and the number that germinated was counted: tray 1: 14 of 20 seeds; tray 2: 18 of 25 seeds; tray 3: 9 of 30 seeds.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of seeds that germinated in each tray. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the overall percentage of seeds that germinated, using the total number of seeds. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the overall percentage should be calculated from the total seeds rather than by averaging the three percentages. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tray 1: 70 %; tray 2: 72 %; tray 3: 30 %
    1 mark: method (germinated ÷ sown × 100). 1 mark: all three values correct.
  2. (b) 54.7 % (41 of 75 seeds)
    1 mark: 41 ÷ 75 × 100 = 54.7 %.
  3. (c) Each tray has a different number of seeds, so the average of the percentages gives equal weight to each tray, which is not correct.
    1 mark: trays have different numbers of seeds, so the percentages must be weighted (use totals).

Q14Tables, graphs & calculations · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 4 marks

A mould colony growing on agar was measured at intervals. Diameter of the colony: 2 h: 5 mm; 3 h: 12 mm; 4 h: 30 mm. At 1 h the colony diameter was 2 mm.

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate of growth, in mm per hour, between 2 h and 4 h. [2]
  2. (b) State the time interval in which the colony grew fastest. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the rate of growth increases over time. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12.5 mm per hour
    1 mark: increase = 30 − 5 = 25 mm. 1 mark: 25 ÷ 2 h = 12.5 mm per hour.
  2. (b) Between 3 h and 4 h (18 mm in 1 h)
    1 mark: 3–4 h increase of 18 mm is the largest (1–2 h gives 3 mm; 2–3 h gives 7 mm).
  3. (c) The colony grows by repeated cell division, so the number of growing cells increases over time and the growth becomes faster.
    1 mark: repeated division increases the number of cells.
Q15Tables, graphs & calculations · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

A student measured the length of a plant stem (y, in mm) at different times (x, in days). The points were: (10, 4.1), (20, 8.3), (30, 12.0), (40, 15.9), (50, 27.5).

  1. (a) Identify the anomalous point. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the gradient of the line between (10, 4.1) and (40, 15.9). Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the anomalous point should not be included when drawing the line of best fit. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (50, 27.5)
    1 mark: it does not follow the pattern of the other four points.
  2. (b) 0.39 mm per day
    1 mark: (15.9 − 4.1) = 11.8 and (40 − 10) = 30. 1 mark: 11.8 ÷ 30 = 0.39.
