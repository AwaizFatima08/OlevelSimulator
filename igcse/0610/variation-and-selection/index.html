Q1Variation & mutation · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which is an example of discontinuous variation in humans? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C ABO blood group
    ABO blood group has a few distinct categories with no intermediates and is controlled by genes only. Height, mass and hand span show a continuous range.
Q2Variation & mutation · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe two differences between continuous and discontinuous variation. [2]
  2. (b) Give one example of each type of variation in plants. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) continuous shows a range of values with intermediates, discontinuous has a few distinct categories; continuous is caused by genes and environment, discontinuous mostly by genes only
    B1 range vs distinct groups; B1 genes and environment vs genes alone (or many genes vs one/few genes).
  2. (b) continuous: leaf length / plant height / mass of seeds; discontinuous: flower colour in some species / seed shape (round or wrinkled in peas)
    B1 each.
Q3Variation & mutation · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which is an example of continuous variation? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B the height of adults in a population
    Height varies across a range of values with no sharp categories, so it is continuous. Blood group, earlobe presence and flower colour are discrete (distinct categories).
Q4Variation & mutation · Core · Paper 3 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Give two causes of variation between individuals of the same species. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Genetic differences (different alleles); environmental factors such as diet or climate.
    1 mark each for any two valid causes.
Q5Variation & mutation · Core · Paper 3 style · 4 marks

A student counted the petals on 30 flowers of one plant species and measured the leaf length of the same plants. Every flower had either 5 or 6 petals. Leaf lengths ranged from 4 cm to 12 cm, with many different values.

  1. (a) Identify which trait is discontinuous and which is continuous. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how you decided which trait is discontinuous. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Petal number is discontinuous; leaf length is continuous.
    1 mark for each correct classification.
  2. (b) Petal number falls into distinct categories (5 or 6) with no intermediate values; leaf length shows a range of values with intermediates.
    1 mark: petal number has distinct categories with no intermediate values; 1 mark: leaf length shows a range of values with intermediates.
Q6Variation & mutation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement about mutation is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B A mutation is a genetic change, and its rate is increased by ionising radiation.
    A mutation is a genetic change; ionising radiation and some chemicals increase the rate. Mutations are random (C is wrong) and many are neutral or rarely beneficial (A is wrong).

Q10Variation & mutation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement about mutations is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B a mutation is a random change in the base sequence of DNA that can produce a new allele
    Mutations are random changes in DNA and can create new alleles. Many are harmful or neutral (so C is wrong), and mutations can occur in gametes (so D is wrong).
Q11Variation & mutation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a mutation can produce a new allele, and why most mutations are harmful or have no effect. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A change in the base sequence of a gene produces a different version of the gene, a new allele. Mutations are random, and a change to a protein that has been well adapted by natural selection is usually harmful or neutral; beneficial mutations are rare.
    1 mark: base sequence of a gene changes; 1 mark: new allele produced; 1 mark: mutations are random; 1 mark: most changes disrupt a well-adapted protein or have no effect, beneficial ones are rare.
Q12Variation & mutation · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

The rate of a particular gene mutation is 1 in 100 000 gametes.

  1. (a) Calculate the expected number of gametes with this mutation out of 500 000 gametes. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a mutation in a gamete is more likely to be inherited than a mutation in a body cell. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5
    500 000 ÷ 100 000 = 5. 1 mark method, 1 mark answer.
  2. (b) Gametes take part in sexual reproduction, so the mutated allele can be passed to offspring; mutations in body cells are not passed on to offspring.
    1 mark: gamete allele passed to offspring; 1 mark: body cell mutation is not inherited.
Q13Variation & mutation · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Define gene mutation. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why mutation is important for evolution. [2]
  3. (c) Name two processes, other than mutation, that produce genetic variation in sexually reproducing organisms. [2]
  4. (d) State one factor that increases the rate of mutation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) a random change in the base sequence of DNA (of a gene)
    B1.
  2. (b) mutation is the only source of new alleles; this creates new variation on which natural selection can act
    B1 new alleles; B1 variation for natural selection.
  3. (c) meiosis; random fertilisation (also random mating)
    B1 each.
  4. (d) ionising radiation (e.g. X-rays, gamma rays) / certain chemicals
    B1.
Q14Variation & mutation · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student measured the leaf lengths (mm) of identical plant clones grown in two places. Shade: 52, 48, 50, 46, 54. Sun: 62, 58, 60, 64, 56.

  1. (a) Calculate the mean leaf length in the shade and in the sun. [2]
  2. (b) Explain whether the difference between the means is caused by genetic or environmental variation. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shade: 50.0 mm; sun: 60.0 mm
    Shade: (52 + 48 + 50 + 46 + 54) ÷ 5 = 50.0 mm. Sun: (62 + 58 + 60 + 64 + 56) ÷ 5 = 60.0 mm. 1 mark for each correct mean.
  2. (b) Environmental: the plants are genetically identical clones, so the difference in mean leaf length is caused by the environment (the light intensity), not by genes.
    1 mark: clones have identical genes; 1 mark: difference due to environment (light); 1 mark: small differences within each group are environmental too.
Q15Variation & mutation · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why mutations are the ultimate source of new alleles, but why sexual reproduction is responsible for much of the variation seen in a population. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mutations create new alleles (new versions of genes). Sexual reproduction shuffles existing alleles into new combinations through independent assortment and crossing over in meiosis, and random fertilisation, so offspring differ from each other.
    1 mark: mutations create new alleles; 1 mark: sexual reproduction makes new combinations of existing alleles; 1 mark: independent assortment / crossing over in meiosis; 1 mark: random fertilisation gives different offspring.

Q19Adaptive features (xerophytes, hydrophytes) · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature of a xerophyte reduces water loss by trapping a layer of humid air next to the leaf surface? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B a dense covering of hairs on the leaf surface
    Hairs trap a layer of humid air, which lowers the water potential gradient out of the leaf, so less transpiration occurs. Many stomata and broad leaves increase water loss, and large air spaces are a hydrophyte (water plant) adaptation.

Q21Adaptive features (xerophytes, hydrophytes) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Cacti grow in the Thar desert, where rainfall is low and temperatures are high. Explain how three features of a cactus help it to survive in these conditions. [6]

Q22Adaptive features (xerophytes, hydrophytes) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The lotus is a hydrophyte rooted in the mud of ponds. Its leaf stalks and stems contain large air spaces. Explain two advantages of these air spaces. [2]
  2. (b) Hydrophytes have very little xylem tissue compared with land plants. Suggest why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) keep leaves buoyant / afloat so they reach the light for photosynthesis; allow oxygen to diffuse to roots and stems in the mud (which is low in oxygen)
    B1 each.
  2. (b) they are surrounded by water / supported by water, so need little xylem for support; water can be absorbed over the surface, so little transport of water is needed
    B1 support from water / little need for lignified support; B1 less need to transport water.
Q23Adaptive features (xerophytes, hydrophytes) · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A student peels the lower epidermis from a leaf and counts the stomata in the circular field of view of a microscope. The diameter of the field of view is 0.4 mm and 18 stomata are seen.Calculate the number of stomata per mm². Use area = πr². Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [3]

Q24Adaptive features (xerophytes, hydrophytes) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

  2. (b) Suggest one reason why a hydrophyte does not need a thick waxy cuticle. [1]

    B1.
Q25Adaptive features (xerophytes, hydrophytes) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature is an adaptation of a hydrophyte (water plant)? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C large air spaces in the leaves and stems that help the plant float
    Air spaces give buoyancy so the leaves float at the surface to receive light. Thick cuticles, sunken stomata and deep roots are xerophyte adaptations.
Q26Adaptive features (xerophytes, hydrophytes) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe three adaptations of a xerophyte that help it survive in a dry habitat, and explain how each reduces water loss. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Thick waxy cuticle: reduces evaporation from the leaf surface. Rolled leaves or hairs: trap a layer of humid air, reducing the water potential gradient. Sunken stomata or spines: reduce the air movement and leaf surface area for transpiration. (Accept any three with a correct explanation.)
    1 mark for each adaptation, plus 1 mark for each correct explanation of how it reduces water loss (max 3 marks).
