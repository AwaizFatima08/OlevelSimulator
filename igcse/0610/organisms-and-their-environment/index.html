Q1Food chains, webs & energy flow · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In the food chain mango leaves → caterpillar → myna bird → hawk, what do the arrows show? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A the direction in which energy is transferred
    Arrows in a food chain point from the organism eaten to the organism that eats it, showing the transfer of energy. They do not show size (B).
Q2Food chains, webs & energy flow · Core · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

A food web in a freshwater lake in Sindh is described below.Algae are eaten by snails and by mosquito larvae.Mosquito larvae are eaten by small fish.Snails are eaten by ducks.Small fish are eaten by kingfishers and herons.Herons also eat ducks.

  1. (a) Name the producer in this food web. [1]
  2. (b) Name one primary consumer and one secondary consumer. [2]
  3. (c) State the principal source of energy for this food web. [1]
  4. (d) Fishermen remove most of the small fish from the lake. Suggest and explain two effects on other organisms in the web. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Algae (water plants).
    B1.
  2. (b) Primary: snail or mosquito larva. Secondary: small fish (also duck, which eats snails).
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) The Sun (light energy).
    B1.
  4. (d) Any two, each with a reason: kingfishers decrease because they lose their only food; mosquito larvae increase because fewer are eaten by small fish; herons eat more ducks, so the duck population falls (and snails may then increase); algae decrease because the larger number of mosquito larvae eat more of them.
    For each of two effects: 1 mark effect, 1 mark matching explanation.
Q3Food chains, webs & energy flow · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In the food chain phytoplankton → zooplankton → small fish → river dolphin, which organism is a secondary consumer? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C small fish
    Phytoplankton is the producer; zooplankton is the primary consumer; small fish eat zooplankton, so they are the secondary consumer. The dolphin is the tertiary consumer, and phytoplankton is not a consumer.
Q4Food chains, webs & energy flow · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A food chain in the Cholistan desert: shrubs → desert gerbil → sand snake → eagle.

  1. (a) State the trophic level of the eagle in this chain. [1]
  2. (b) Name the primary consumer in this food chain. [1]
  3. (c) State the principal source of energy for this food chain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4 (fourth trophic level / tertiary consumer)
    Shrub is level 1 (producer), gerbil level 2, snake level 3, eagle level 4.
  2. (b) desert gerbil
    The primary consumer eats the producer.
  3. (c) sunlight (the Sun)
    Producers trap light energy by photosynthesis; this energy then passes along the chain.
Q5Food chains, webs & energy flow · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State two ways in which energy is lost from a food chain between trophic levels. [2]
  2. (b) State the form of energy in which most of the energy is lost. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) any two of: heat lost in respiration; faeces and urine (not digested or excreted); parts of the organism not eaten
    1 mark for each of any two correct ways.
  2. (b) heat (thermal energy)
    Most energy is lost as heat from respiration.
Q6Food chains, webs & energy flow · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In a pond food web, algae are eaten by water fleas and by tadpoles; water fleas are eaten by small fish; tadpoles are eaten by water beetles; small fish and water beetles are eaten by herons. Herons also eat tadpoles. At which trophic levels does the heron feed? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C third and fourth
    Algae (1st) → tadpole (2nd) → heron gives heron at the 3rd level; algae → water flea → small fish → heron or algae → tadpole → water beetle → heron gives the 4th level. The heron is a secondary and a tertiary consumer, so 'third only' is incomplete.
Q7Food chains, webs & energy flow · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why only a small proportion of the energy in one trophic level is transferred to the next. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why food chains rarely have more than five trophic levels. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Not all of the organism is eaten (e.g. roots, bones); not all that is eaten is digested and absorbed – some is lost in faeces; much energy is used in respiration and lost as heat (movement, keeping warm); energy remains in dead organisms and waste, passing to decomposers; energy is lost in excretory products such as urea.
    1 mark each for any four.
  2. (b) So much energy is lost at each transfer that after four or five levels there is too little energy left to support another population of animals.
    1 mark: energy lost at each level; 1 mark: too little left to support a further level.
Q8Food chains, webs & energy flow · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Define the terms producer and consumer. [2]
  2. (b) Define the term decomposer and state its role in an ecosystem. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why energy is described as flowing through an ecosystem whereas nutrients such as carbon are described as cycling. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Producer: an organism that makes its own organic nutrients, usually using energy from sunlight, through photosynthesis. Consumer: an organism that gets its energy by feeding on other organisms.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) An organism that gets its energy from dead or waste organic material; it breaks this down and releases nutrients (e.g. ammonium, carbon dioxide) back into the environment for recycling.
    1 mark: definition; 1 mark: recycles nutrients / releases CO₂ and mineral ions.
  3. (c) Energy enters as light and is eventually lost as heat, so it cannot be reused; carbon atoms are passed between organisms and the environment and used again and again.
    1 mark: energy enters from Sun and leaves as heat (not recycled); 1 mark: nutrients recycled.
Q9Food chains, webs & energy flow · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement about energy flow through a food web is correct? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B most energy passing to a trophic level is lost, mainly as heat, so less energy is available to higher levels
    Energy is lost at each level, mainly as heat from respiration and in waste and uneaten parts, so energy cannot be recycled (A). Energy flows one way from producers onwards (C is wrong). Nutrients such as carbon and nitrogen are recycled, unlike energy (D).
Q10Food chains, webs & energy flow · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Sunlight falling on a wheat field in Punjab provides 2 000 000 kJ per m² per year. The wheat stores 9000 kJ per m² per year in its biomass.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the light energy that is stored in the wheat biomass. [2]
  2. (b) Give two reasons why so little of the light energy is stored by the wheat. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.45 %
    9000 ÷ 2 000 000 × 100 = 0.45 %.
  2. (b) any two of: light is reflected or passes through the leaves; some light is the wrong wavelength; not all energy is used in photosynthesis; energy is lost to heat and respiration in the crop
    1 mark for each of any two correct reasons.
Q11Food chains, webs & energy flow · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A rice field in Sindh contains rice plants, snails, ducks, frogs, insects and birds. Rice plants are eaten by snails and by ducks. Snails are eaten by ducks. Insects are eaten by frogs. Ducks and frogs are eaten by birds.

  1. (a) Explain why a food web is more stable than a single food chain. [3]
  2. (b) Name one consumer in the rice field that is both a primary and a secondary consumer. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) in a food web an organism has several food sources, so if one species declines the consumers can switch to another; a single chain is broken if one link is lost
    1 mark: several food sources / alternative food. 1 mark: a single food chain is broken if one species is lost. 1 mark: a web can absorb change in one species.
  2. (b) duck
    Ducks eat rice (primary consumer) and snails (which are primary consumers, so the duck is also a secondary consumer).
Q12Food chains, webs & energy flow · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

In a mangrove ecosystem, dead mangrove leaves fall into the water. Crabs and bacteria feed on the leaves. Shrimps and small fish feed on the bacteria and on small pieces of leaf. Large fish such as groupers eat the crabs, shrimps and small fish.

  1. (a) Explain what would happen to the energy in the dead leaves of the mangroves if there were no crabs or decomposers. [2]
  2. (b) Mangrove forests along the coast near Karachi have been cut down. Suggest and explain the effect on the number of large fish caught offshore. [3]

  1. (a) A student says that water is used up by plants and animals in the water cycle. Evaluate this statement. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) the statement is mainly incorrect: water is returned to the environment by transpiration, excretion, and respiration; it is recycled continuously through the cycle rather than used up; a small amount is used in photosynthesis and other reactions
    1 mark: water is returned to the atmosphere or soil through transpiration and excretion. 1 mark: the water is recycled, not lost. 1 mark: a small amount is used in chemical reactions.
Q103Water cycle · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain how transpiration by the trees of the Amazon rainforest helps to maintain rainfall over the forest. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) transpiration releases large amounts of water vapour into the air; this vapour condenses to form clouds that produce rain over the forest; so water is recycled locally instead of all being lost to the sea
    1 mark: water vapour released by transpiration. 1 mark: condensation forms clouds. 1 mark: precipitation returns water to the forest.
Q104Water cycle · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A lake has a surface area of 50 km². Its water level falls by 1.5 m per year because of evaporation. Assume there is no inflow or outflow of water. Calculate the volume of water lost per year, in m³. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why water evaporates faster from a shallow lake in a hot climate than from a deep lake in a cool climate. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.5 × 10⁷ m³
    Area = 50 km² = 50 × 10⁶ m²; volume = 50 × 10⁶ × 1.5 = 7.5 × 10⁷ m³.
  2. (b) higher temperature increases the kinetic energy of water molecules so more escape as vapour; a larger surface area of shallow water exposes more water to the air
    1 mark: higher temperature increases evaporation. 1 mark: shallow lake has a larger area per volume (or more exposed surface).
Q105Water cycle · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the flow of the Indus River is highest in summer and lowest in winter. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) in summer, the monsoon rains and the melting of snow and glaciers in the mountains add large amounts of water to the river; in winter, precipitation is low and the snow is frozen, so less water runs into the river
    1 mark: monsoon rain in summer. 1 mark: snow and glacier melt in summer. 1 mark: frozen water in winter. 1 mark: less precipitation/runoff in winter.
