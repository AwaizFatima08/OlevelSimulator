Q1Kidney, nephron & urine · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which substances are all present in the filtrate in the Bowman's capsule of a healthy person? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B glucose, salts and urea
    Small molecules and ions (water, glucose, salts, urea) are filtered from the glomerulus into the Bowman's capsule. Proteins and red blood cells are too large to pass through the filter, so A, C and D are wrong; A is the main distractor because glucose and urea are filtered but protein is not.

Q5Kidney, nephron & urine · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Where in the kidney does filtration of blood take place? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A from the glomerulus into the Bowman's capsule
    Blood at high pressure in the glomerulus is filtered into the Bowman's capsule. The collecting duct carries urine towards the ureter (C) but no filtration occurs there.
Q6Kidney, nephron & urine · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which substance is completely reabsorbed from the filtrate in the proximal convoluted tubule of a healthy person? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B glucose
    All the filtered glucose is reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule (by active transport). Protein is not filtered at all, so it cannot be reabsorbed (C); water is reabsorbed in several regions and not completely (D).

Q9Kidney, nephron & urine · Standard · Paper 6 style · 8 marks

A teacher prepared three samples of artificial 'urine' (1, 2 and 3) to represent samples from three patients. A student tested each with Benedict's solution and with biuret solution.

Show answer

    1 mark: Benedict's solution; 1 mark: heat in water-bath; 1 mark: blue → green/yellow/orange/brick-red.

Q12Kidney, nephron & urine · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The cells lining the proximal convoluted tubule have many mitochondria. Explain why. [3]
  2. (b) These cells also have microvilli on the surface facing the filtrate. Suggest the advantage of this. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest what would happen to the glucose content of urine if a poison stopped respiration in these cells. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) These cells reabsorb glucose (and some ions) by active transport, against a concentration gradient; active transport needs energy; the energy is released by aerobic respiration in mitochondria.

  2. (b) Microvilli increase the surface area for reabsorption.
    B1.
  3. (c) Glucose would appear in the urine, because without energy it could not be reabsorbed by active transport.
    1 mark: glucose in urine; 1 mark: no energy → no active transport.

Q14Kidney, nephron & urine · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

In an adult, about 180 dm³ of filtrate is formed in the kidneys each day, but only about 1.5 dm³ of urine is produced.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the filtered water that is reabsorbed into the blood. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why it is important that so much of the filtered water is reabsorbed. [2]
  3. (c) On a very hot day a farm worker in Jacobabad sweats heavily and drinks little water. Describe and explain how the volume and concentration of the worker's urine change. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 99.2 %
    M1: (180 − 1.5) ÷ 180 × 100; A1: 99.2 %.
  2. (b) Otherwise the body would lose huge volumes of water each day and quickly become dehydrated; reabsorption keeps the water content (water potential) of the blood constant.
    1 mark: prevents dehydration / huge water loss; 1 mark: keeps blood water content/water potential constant.
  3. (c) Smaller volume of urine; more concentrated (darker); because water is lost in sweat, the kidneys reabsorb more water from the filtrate back into the blood.
    1 mark: smaller volume; 1 mark: more concentrated; 1 mark: more water reabsorbed in the kidney to conserve water.

Q16Kidney, nephron & urine · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

The table shows whether glucose was found in the urine of a person at different blood glucose concentrations.Blood glucose (mmol/dm³): 5, 8, 10, 12, 15Glucose in urine: none, none, none, small amount, large amount

  1. (a) Describe the relationship between blood glucose concentration and glucose in the urine. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest an explanation for these results in terms of how the nephron handles glucose. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why a person with untreated type 1 diabetes often produces large volumes of urine and feels very thirsty. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) No glucose appears in the urine until the blood glucose concentration rises above about 10 mmol/dm³; above this, the amount of glucose in the urine increases as blood glucose increases.
    1 mark: none up to 10 mmol/dm³; 1 mark: increasing glucose in urine above 10 / at 12 and 15.
  2. (b) All glucose in the blood is filtered into the nephron; reabsorption of glucose is by active transport with carrier proteins, which have a maximum rate; above about 10 mmol/dm³ more glucose is filtered than can be reabsorbed, so the excess stays in the filtrate and appears in urine.
    1 mark each: glucose filtered in proportion to blood concentration; reabsorption by active transport / carriers limited; excess not reabsorbed → in urine.
  3. (c) Glucose left in the filtrate lowers its water potential, so less water is reabsorbed by osmosis; large volumes of urine lose water, so the person becomes dehydrated and thirsty.
    1 mark: glucose in filtrate reduces water reabsorption (osmosis); 1 mark: more water lost → thirst.

Q18Kidney, nephron & urine · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State and explain two differences between the composition of blood in the renal artery and in the renal vein of a healthy person. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why the kidneys receive about a fifth of the blood pumped by the heart at rest, although they make up less than 1 % of body mass. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Urea concentration is lower in the renal vein, because urea is filtered out and not reabsorbed (removed in urine).     1 mark: blood needs to be filtered frequently / large volume processed; 1 mark: to keep removing urea / regulate water and ions.

    M1: (28.4 − 9.5) ÷ 9.5 × 100; A1: 199 %.
  2. (b) 0.24 g urea per g protein
    28.4 ÷ 120 = 0.24.
  3. (c) More protein eaten → more amino acids absorbed than the body needs; the excess amino acids cannot be stored and are deaminated in the liver; so more urea is formed and excreted by the kidneys.
    1 mark each: more amino acids absorbed than needed; excess deaminated in liver; more urea made → more excreted (urea roughly proportional to protein).
  4. (d) Body proteins are continually broken down / some amino acids are always deaminated, e.g. from the turnover of cells.
    B1.
Q29Liver, deamination & urea · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 9 marks

The enzyme urease breaks down urea into ammonia and carbon dioxide. A student mixed 5 cm³ of three urine samples (A, B and C) each with 1 cm³ of urease solution and three drops of phenol red indicator at 30 °C. Phenol red is yellow in acidic or neutral solution and turns pink in alkaline solution. The time for each mixture to turn pink was recorded.Sample A: 45 s; sample B: 120 s; sample C: 80 s

  1. (a) Calculate the rate of reaction for each sample, using rate = 1000 ÷ time. Give your answers to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) State which sample contained the most urea and explain your answer. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the indicator changed colour. [2]
  4. (d) State three variables that should be kept constant. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A: 22.2; B: 8.3; C: 12.5 (arbitrary units)
    2 marks all three correct; 1 mark two correct.
  2. (b) Sample A; it changed colour fastest, so ammonia (alkali) was produced fastest because there was most urea for the enzyme to act on.
    1 mark: A; 1 mark: shortest time / fastest rate because more substrate (urea) → more ammonia.
  3. (c) Urease breaks down urea into ammonia (and carbon dioxide); ammonia makes the solution alkaline, turning phenol red from yellow to pink.
    1 mark: urea → ammonia; 1 mark: alkaline → colour change.
  4. (d) Any three of: volume of urine sample; volume/concentration of urease; volume of indicator; temperature; pH at start.
    1 mark each for any three.
Q30Liver, deamination & urea · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A patient has severe liver damage. Tests show that the concentration of urea in their blood is lower than normal but the concentration of amino acids is higher than normal. Suggest an explanation. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the patient's urine contains less urea than normal even though their kidneys are healthy. [1]
  3. (c) Doctors advise this patient to eat only a moderate amount of protein. Suggest why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The damaged liver cannot deaminate amino acids efficiently; so amino acids build up in the blood; and less urea is formed.
    1 mark each: reduced deamination by liver; amino acids accumulate; less urea produced.
  2. (b) Less urea is made by the liver, so less is filtered into the urine.
    B1.
  3. (c) Excess amino acids cannot be dealt with properly by the damaged liver, so eating less protein reduces the amount of excess amino acids (and nitrogenous waste) building up.
    1 mark: liver cannot process excess amino acids; 1 mark: less protein reduces the build-up.
