  1. (a) What are the products of anaerobic respiration in yeast? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C ethanol and carbon dioxide
    Yeast respires anaerobically to ethanol (alcohol) and carbon dioxide. Lactic acid is the product in human muscle; carbon dioxide and water are the products of aerobic respiration.

  2. (b) State one way in which anaerobic respiration in yeast differs from anaerobic respiration in muscle. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) glucose → lactic acid
    B1.
  2. (b) yeast produces ethanol and carbon dioxide; muscle produces lactic acid (no carbon dioxide)
    B1.
Q3Anaerobic respiration & oxygen debt · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) State two differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration in humans. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) aerobic uses oxygen, anaerobic does not; aerobic releases much more energy per glucose; aerobic produces carbon dioxide and water, anaerobic produces lactic acid
    1 mark each for any two, both sides stated or clearly implied.
Q4Anaerobic respiration & oxygen debt · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) For each type of respiration, state whether each substance is used or produced, or is not involved.Type: aerobic (human); anaerobic (human muscle); anaerobic (yeast).Substances: oxygen; carbon dioxide; lactic acid; ethanol. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) aerobic: oxygen used, CO₂ produced, no lactic acid, no ethanol; anaerobic muscle: no oxygen, no CO₂, lactic acid produced, no ethanol; anaerobic yeast: no oxygen, CO₂ produced, no lactic acid, ethanol produced
    B1 for each correct substance across all three types (oxygen; carbon dioxide; lactic acid; ethanol).

Q6Anaerobic respiration & oxygen debt · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Lactic acid bacteria, used to make yoghurt, respire anaerobically on the sugar in milk. Which product is made? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B lactic acid

Q7Anaerobic respiration & oxygen debt · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks

  1. (a) the oxygen supply to the muscles cannot meet the demand for aerobic respiration, so glucose is broken down anaerobically to release energy more quickly
    1 mark: oxygen supply is too slow to meet demand. 1 mark: anaerobic respiration can still release some energy without oxygen.

Q9Anaerobic respiration & oxygen debt · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) After vigorous exercise, what happens to the lactic acid that has built up in the muscles? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B It is carried in the blood to the liver and broken down by aerobic respiration.
    Lactic acid is transported in the blood to the liver, where it is broken down by aerobic respiration, using the extra oxygen taken in (the oxygen debt). It is not mainly excreted by the kidneys.
Q10Anaerobic respiration & oxygen debt · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark

Show answer
  1. (a) A Anaerobic respiration releases much less energy per glucose molecule.
    Glucose is only partly broken down in anaerobic respiration, so much less energy is released per glucose molecule. It takes place in the cytoplasm, not mitochondria.

    1 mark each for any three.

Q19Anaerobic respiration & oxygen debt · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

During 8 minutes of jogging, a student's body built up an oxygen debt of 6.0 dm³. After she stopped, her breathing used 1.5 dm³ of oxygen per minute more than it did at rest.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of minutes she needs to repay her oxygen debt. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the oxygen debt has to be repaid after exercise. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4
    Oxygen debt ÷ extra oxygen per minute = 6.0 ÷ 1.5 = 4 minutes.

    1 mark: lactic acid is broken down aerobically or converted to glucose. 1 mark: oxygen restores the body's energy stores and reduces lactic acid levels.
