Q1Sense organs: the eye · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the function of each part of the eye:(i) cornea(ii) iris(iii) lens(iv) retina(v) optic nerve [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) refracts (bends) light entering the eye; (ii) controls how much light enters (changes pupil size); (iii) focuses light onto the retina (fine focusing); (iv) contains light receptors (rods and cones); (v) carries nerve impulses from the retina to the brain.
    B1 each.
Q2Sense organs: the eye · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Compare rods and cones, stating for each the light conditions in which it works best, whether it detects colour, and where it is mainly found in the retina. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why it is difficult to see the colours of objects at night. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rods: work in dim light; black and white only (no colour); found throughout the retina but not at the fovea. Cones: work in bright light; detect colour (three types); concentrated in the fovea.
    B1 rods light / colour; B1 rods distribution; B1 cones light / colour; B1 cones concentrated at fovea.
  2. (b) Cones do not work in dim light; only rods respond, and rods do not detect colour.
    B1.
Q3Sense organs: the eye · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) When the eye focuses on a close object, which structure contracts so that the lens becomes fatter? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A ciliary muscle
    Ciliary muscles contract, slackening the suspensory ligaments, so the lens becomes fatter and refracts light more strongly. Suspensory ligaments do not contract; they are pulled tight or slackened by the ciliary muscles.
Q4Sense organs: the eye · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Name the layer at the back of the eye that contains light-sensitive cells. [1]
  2. (b) Name the two types of light-sensitive cell found in the retina. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) retina
    retina
  2. (b) rods and cones
    1 mark each: rods (work in dim light, no colour); cones (work in bright light, detect colour).
Q5Sense organs: the eye · Core · Paper 3 style · 3 marks

A student walks from a dark cinema into bright sunlight.

  1. (a) State what happens to the radial muscles of the iris when the pupil constricts. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the pupil constricts in bright light. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) They relax.
    1 mark: radial muscles relax (circular muscles contract).
  2. (b) Less light enters the eye, which protects the retina from damage or overstimulation.
    1 mark: less light enters the eye; 1 mark: protects the retina from damage/overstimulation.
Q6Sense organs: the eye · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student leaves a dark cinema hall in Islamabad and walks out into bright sunlight.

  1. (a) Describe and explain the changes in the iris when the student walks into the bright sunlight. [3]
  2. (b) State the type of response this is and why it is useful that it is automatic. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Circular muscles of the iris contract; radial muscles relax; pupil becomes smaller (constricts), so less light enters and the retina is protected from damage.
    B1 circular contract; B1 radial relax; B1 pupil smaller so less light enters / protects retina.
  2. (b) A reflex (involuntary) action; it is very fast and does not need thought, so the retina is protected immediately.
    B1 reflex; B1 fast / no conscious thought.

Q10Sense organs: the eye · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

A student shines a torch into one eye of a partner in a dim room and measures the pupil diameter with a ruler. Time 0 s is when the torch is switched on. Results: 0 s 4.0 mm; 5 s 3.2 mm; 10 s 2.7 mm; 15 s 2.6 mm; 20 s 2.6 mm.

  1. (a) Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage decrease in pupil diameter between 0 s and 10 s. [2]
  3. (c) Give one control variable the student should keep the same, and explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Independent: time after the torch is switched on (s). Dependent: pupil diameter (mm).
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) 32.5 %
    (4.0 − 2.7) ÷ 4.0 × 100 = 32.5 %. 1 mark method, 1 mark answer.
  3. (c) Distance of the torch from the eye (or the same torch); this keeps the light intensity the same so that only time changes.
    1 mark for a valid control variable; 1 mark for a reason linked to a fair test.
Q11Sense organs: the eye · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A man on a train is reading messages on his phone, then looks out of the window at hills several kilometres away.

  1. (a) Describe the changes in the eye that allow the man to focus on the distant hills after looking at his phone. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the lens must change shape for the image to be focused on the retina. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ciliary muscles relax; suspensory ligaments are pulled tight (tension increases); lens is pulled thinner / less convex.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) Light from a distant object needs less refraction (rays almost parallel); a thinner lens refracts light less, so the image is focused on the retina rather than in front of it.
    B1 distant object needs less refraction / bending; B1 thinner lens bends light less to focus on retina.
Q12Sense organs: the eye · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A volunteer's pupil was photographed after two minutes at each light intensity and its diameter measured.Light intensity / lux : pupil diameter / mm50 : 6.4200 : 4.8500 : 3.61000 : 2.4

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage decrease in pupil diameter between 50 lux and 1000 lux. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the relationship between light intensity and pupil diameter. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the volunteer stayed at each light intensity for two minutes before the photograph was taken. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest one variable, other than light intensity, that should be kept constant. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 62.5 %
    (6.4 − 2.4) ÷ 6.4 × 100 (M1) = 62.5 % (A1).
  2. (b) As light intensity increases, pupil diameter decreases (not in direct proportion).
    B1.
  3. (c) To allow time for the pupil to adjust fully / reach a steady size.
    B1.
  4. (d) Distance of the light from the eye / same eye measured / same volunteer / same direction of light / looking at the same object at the same distance.
    B1.
Q13Sense organs: the eye · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A bird flies across a field. Light from the bird enters one eye.

  1. (a) Describe the pathway taken by information from light entering the eye to the brain. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Light is refracted by the cornea and lens onto the retina; rods and cones are stimulated; impulses pass along the optic nerve (sensory neurone) to the brain, which interprets the image.
    1 mark each: refraction by cornea and lens focuses the image on the retina; rods/cones stimulated (receptors); impulses along the optic nerve; brain interprets the image.
Q14Sense organs: the eye · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student measured one pupil in bright light (2.0 mm diameter) and in dim light (6.5 mm diameter).

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in pupil diameter from bright light to dim light. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the role of the radial muscles of the iris in dim light. [2]
  3. (c) State why this change in pupil diameter is an example of a reflex action. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 225 %
    (6.5 − 2.0) ÷ 2.0 × 100 = 225 %. 1 mark method, 1 mark answer.
  2. (b) The radial muscles contract, pulling the iris open so the pupil dilates and more light enters the eye.
    1 mark: radial muscles contract; 1 mark: pupil dilates / more light enters.
