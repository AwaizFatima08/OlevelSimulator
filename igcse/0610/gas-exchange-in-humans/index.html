Q1Breathing mechanism & air composition · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which row shows the approximate percentages of oxygen and carbon dioxide in expired air?A oxygen 21 %, carbon dioxide 0.04 %B oxygen 16 %, carbon dioxide 4 %C oxygen 4 %, carbon dioxide 16 %D oxygen 0.04 %, carbon dioxide 21 % [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B oxygen 16 %, carbon dioxide 4 %
    Expired air still contains about 16 % oxygen; carbon dioxide rises to about 4 %. A gives inspired air.
Q2Breathing mechanism & air composition · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State two differences, other than gas percentages, between inspired and expired air. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why expired air contains less oxygen and more carbon dioxide than inspired air. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Expired air is warmer; expired air contains more water vapour (is saturated).
    B1 temperature higher in expired; B1 more water vapour in expired.
  2. (b) Oxygen diffuses from alveoli into the blood and is used in aerobic respiration in cells; carbon dioxide is produced by respiration and carried to the lungs, where it diffuses into the alveoli and is breathed out.
    B1 O₂ diffuses into blood (at alveoli); B1 O₂ used in respiration / CO₂ made by respiration; B1 CO₂ diffuses from blood into alveoli.
Q3Breathing mechanism & air composition · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Name the gas that makes up about 78 % of both inspired and expired air. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the percentage of nitrogen hardly changes between inspired and expired air. [1]
  3. (c) State the percentage of carbon dioxide in inspired air. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Nitrogen
    B1 nitrogen.
  2. (b) Nitrogen is not used (or produced) by the body in respiration, so it is breathed out unchanged.
    B1 not used / not absorbed by the body.
  3. (c) 0.04 %
    B1 0.04 % (accept 0.03–0.04 %).
Q4Breathing mechanism & air composition · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What happens during inspiration?A The diaphragm relaxes and becomes dome-shaped.B The external intercostal muscles contract and the ribs move up and out.C The volume of the thorax decreases and the pressure increases.D The internal intercostal muscles contract and the ribs move down. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The external intercostal muscles contract and the ribs move up and out.
    Contraction of the external intercostals raises the ribcage while the diaphragm contracts and flattens, increasing thoracic volume and lowering pressure. D describes forced expiration.
Q5Breathing mechanism & air composition · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the roles of the diaphragm and the intercostal muscles in inspiration. [4]
  2. (b) Describe how forced expiration, for example when blowing up a balloon, differs from quiet expiration. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The diaphragm muscle contracts and the diaphragm flattens (moves down); the external intercostal muscles contract; the ribs move up and out; the volume of the thorax increases, so pressure falls below atmospheric and air enters.
    B1 diaphragm contracts / flattens; B1 external intercostals contract; B1 ribcage up and out; B1 volume increases and pressure decreases (air moves in).
  2. (b) In forced expiration the internal intercostal muscles contract, pulling the ribs down and in; abdominal muscles contract pushing the diaphragm up – in quiet expiration the muscles simply relax (elastic recoil).
    B1 internal intercostals contract (ribs down and in); B1 quiet expiration is passive / muscles relax / elastic recoil.
Q6Breathing mechanism & air composition · Standard · Paper 6 style · 7 marks

A student breathes in and out gently through a mouthpiece connected to two boiling tubes of limewater, X and Y. Valves make air breathed in pass through tube X and air breathed out pass through tube Y.

  1. (a) Describe the colour change expected in tube Y (breathed out into) and explain it. [2]
  2. (b) Tube X (air breathed in through) also turned slightly cloudy after a much longer time. Explain why. [1]
  3. (c) State two variables that should be controlled to make the comparison fair. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest a safer and more sensitive indicator that could replace limewater, and state its colour in expired air. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Limewater turns milky / cloudy quickly, because expired air contains a high concentration (about 4 %) of carbon dioxide.
    B1 milky / cloudy; B1 more CO₂ in expired air.
  2. (b) Inspired (atmospheric) air contains a small amount of carbon dioxide (0.04 %).
    B1 atmosphere has a little CO₂.
  3. (c) Volume of limewater; concentration of limewater; number of breaths / time of breathing; size of tubes.
    1 mark each for any two.
  4. (d) Hydrogencarbonate indicator; turns yellow.
    B1 hydrogencarbonate indicator; B1 yellow (from red/orange).
Q7Breathing mechanism & air composition · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Q8Breathing mechanism & air composition · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Air moves into the lungs during inspiration becauseA the lungs expand by themselves and suck air in.B the pressure inside the lungs falls below atmospheric pressure.C the alveoli actively pump air inwards.D oxygen diffuses down a concentration gradient into the trachea. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B the pressure inside the lungs falls below atmospheric pressure.
    Increasing thoracic volume lowers the pressure in the lungs below atmospheric pressure, so air flows in down the pressure gradient. Lungs have no muscle of their own (A).
Q9Breathing mechanism & air composition · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Calculate the volume of oxygen taken into the blood each minute, in cm³, when the person breathes 12 times per minute with a tidal volume of 500 cm³. Use inspired air 21 % oxygen and expired air 16 % oxygen. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the oxygen percentage of expired air is not close to 0 %, even though the blood leaving the lungs is almost saturated with oxygen. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 300 cm³ per minute
    M1 volume breathed per minute 12 × 500 = 6000 cm³; M1 oxygen removed 21 − 16 = 5 % of it; A1 6000 × 0.05 = 300 cm³.
  2. (b) Some air (in the trachea, bronchi and bronchioles – dead space) never reaches the alveoli and is exhaled unchanged; air in the alveoli is not fully replaced in each breath, and exchange stops when the blood is saturated.
    B1 dead space / air in airways does not take part in exchange; B1 mixes with alveolar air / haemoglobin becomes saturated.
Q10Breathing mechanism & air composition · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

A student analysed samples of air with a gas analyser. Mean results: inspired air – oxygen 21.0 %, carbon dioxide 0.04 %; expired air – oxygen 16.0 %, carbon dioxide 4.0 %.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the inspired oxygen that was absorbed by the body. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate how many times greater the carbon dioxide concentration was in expired air than in inspired air. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the student analysed three samples of each and calculated a mean. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 23.8 % (3 s.f.)
    M1 (21.0 − 16.0) ÷ 21.0 × 100; A1 23.8 %.
  2. (b) 100 times
    B1 4.0 ÷ 0.04 = 100.
  3. (c) To identify anomalies and give a more reliable result.
    B1 reliability / spot anomalies.
Q11Breathing mechanism & air composition · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain what happens to breathing if the external intercostal muscles were paralysed but the diaphragm still worked. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why a puncture wound through the chest wall into the space around one lung (pleural cavity) can cause that lung to collapse. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Breathing in would still occur, by contraction of the diaphragm, but the ribs would not move up and out, so the increase in thoracic volume (and the volume of air inhaled) would be smaller.
    B1 diaphragm alone can still increase volume; B1 smaller volume change / shallower breaths.
  2. (b) Air enters the pleural cavity, so the pressure around the lung becomes equal to atmospheric pressure; when the thorax expands the pressure in the lung no longer falls below atmospheric, so the lung does not inflate and collapses (elastic recoil).
    B1 air enters / pressure equalises; B1 pressure in the lung cannot be reduced so no air enters / lung recoils.
Q12Gas exchange system & surfaces · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which sequence shows the correct pathway of air into the lungs?A larynx → trachea → bronchioles → bronchi → alveoliB larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoliC trachea → larynx → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoliD larynx → bronchi → trachea → bronchioles → alveoli [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B larynx → trachea → bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli
    Air passes the larynx, then the trachea, which divides into two bronchi, which branch into bronchioles ending in alveoli. A puts bronchioles before bronchi.

Q15Gas exchange system & surfaces · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the function of the C-shaped rings of cartilage in the trachea?A They trap dust and pathogens.B They keep the trachea open during changes in pressure.C They produce mucus.D They allow gas exchange with the blood. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B They keep the trachea open during changes in pressure.

Q16Gas exchange system & surfaces · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State the function of (i) goblet cells and (ii) ciliated cells in the trachea. [2]
  2. (b) Tobacco smoke paralyses cilia. Explain why smokers are more likely to suffer from chest infections. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Make mucus, which traps dust and pathogens; (ii) cilia beat (waft) to move mucus up to the throat, where it is swallowed.
    B1 goblet – mucus traps particles / pathogens; B1 ciliated – cilia beat to move mucus upwards / away from lungs.
  2. (b) Cilia stop beating, so mucus is not removed; mucus containing bacteria / pathogens collects in the airways and lungs; pathogens multiply and cause infection (bronchitis / coughing to remove mucus).
    B1 cilia do not move mucus; B1 mucus builds up / trapped pathogens remain; B1 pathogens multiply in lungs → infection.
Q17Gas exchange system & surfaces · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a single spherical alveolus of radius 0.1 mm. Use surface area = 4πr² and volume = 4/3πr³. Give your answer in mm⁻¹. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why lungs made of millions of small alveoli are more efficient than lungs made of one large sac of the same total volume. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 30 mm⁻¹ (ratio 30 : 1)
    M1 SA/V = 3/r; A1 3 ÷ 0.1 = 30.
  2. (b) Small alveoli have a much larger surface area to volume ratio, so the total surface area for diffusion is far greater.
    B1 larger SA:V / larger total surface area; B1 faster / more diffusion of gases.
Q18Gas exchange system & surfaces · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the gas exchange surface in the lungs must be kept moist. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how carbon dioxide moves from the blood plasma into the alveolus. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the concentration gradient for oxygen between alveolar air and the blood is maintained. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Oxygen must dissolve in water before it can diffuse across the cell membranes.
    B1 gases dissolve before diffusing.
  2. (b) By diffusion, from a high concentration in the blood (capillary) to a low concentration in alveolar air, across the capillary and alveolus walls.
    B1 diffusion; B1 down concentration gradient from blood to alveolus.
  3. (c) Breathing (ventilation) keeps bringing fresh air with high oxygen; blood flow constantly carries oxygenated blood away and brings deoxygenated blood.
    B1 ventilation; B1 blood circulation / flow.
Q19Gas exchange system & surfaces · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the structure of an alveolus and its capillary make diffusion of oxygen into the blood rapid. [4]
  2. (b) In emphysema the walls between neighbouring alveoli break down, forming fewer, larger air sacs. Explain why people with emphysema become breathless during mild exercise. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Alveolus wall and capillary wall are each one cell thick, giving a short diffusion distance; moist lining – oxygen dissolves; huge total surface area of millions of alveoli; blood flow carries oxygen away, and breathing replaces alveolar air, maintaining a steep concentration gradient.
    1 mark each for any four of: thin walls / short distance; moist surface; large surface area (many alveoli); rich capillary network; blood flow maintains gradient; ventilation maintains gradient; red cells pressed close to wall.
  2. (b) The surface area for gas exchange is reduced, so less oxygen diffuses into the blood per minute; not enough oxygen reaches the muscles for aerobic respiration.
    B1 reduced surface area; B1 less oxygen absorbed / less oxygen for respiration.

Q21Gas exchange system & surfaces · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) An adult's lungs contain about 480 million alveoli, each with a surface area of 0.15 mm². Calculate the total surface area in m². (1 m² = 1 000 000 mm²) [2]
  2. (b) In cystic fibrosis the mucus in the airways is very thick and sticky. Suggest and explain two effects this has on gas exchange and health. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 72 m²
    M1 480 000 000 × 0.15 = 72 000 000 mm²; A1 72 m².
  2. (b) Mucus blocks bronchioles, so less air reaches some alveoli – less oxygen diffuses into blood / lower effective surface area; cilia cannot move the thick mucus, so trapped bacteria stay in the lungs and cause frequent infections; a thicker layer increases diffusion distance.
    Two effects, each B1 effect + B1 explanation: blocked airways → less ventilation / less O₂ uptake; mucus not cleared by cilia → pathogens remain → infections; increased diffusion distance → slower diffusion.

Q29Exercise & control of breathing · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

Q30Exercise & control of breathing · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

Two students did the same 3-minute exercise. Breathing rates / breaths per minute:Resting (3 readings): A – 16, 15, 17; B – 12, 14, 22Minutes after exercise 2, 4, 6, 8: A – 30, 22, 16, 16; B – 20, 13, 13, 12

  1. (a) Identify the anomalous result and calculate the mean resting breathing rate of student B without it. [2]
  2. (b) State how many minutes after exercise each student's breathing rate first returned to within 1 breath per minute of their resting mean (A resting mean 16). [2]
  3. (c) Suggest which student is fitter and give one reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Anomaly: 22 (third resting reading of B); mean 13 breaths per minute.
    B1 22 identified; B1 (12 + 14)/2 = 13.
  2. (b) A: 6 minutes; B: 4 minutes.
    B1 A: 6 min (22 at 4 min is too high; 16 at 6 min); B1 B: 4 min (13 equals resting mean of 13).
  3. (c) Student B; lower resting breathing rate and faster return to resting rate after exercise (smaller oxygen debt).
    B1 B; B1 lower resting rate or quicker recovery.
