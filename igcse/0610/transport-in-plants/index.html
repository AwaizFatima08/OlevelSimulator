Q1Transpiration · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is transpiration? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B the loss of water vapour from leaves
    Transpiration is the loss of water vapour from the leaves (water evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces and diffuses out through the stomata). A is water uptake; C is translocation.
Q2Transpiration · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the pathway taken by water from the soil to the air around a leaf. Name the cells or tissues it passes through. [4]
  2. (b) State the name given to the loss of water vapour from leaves. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water enters root hair cells (by osmosis), passes through the root cortex cells to the xylem, moves up the xylem in the root and stem into the leaf veins, passes into mesophyll cells, evaporates from their surfaces into the air spaces and diffuses out through the stomata.
    1 mark each for any four in correct order: root hair cell; root cortex; xylem (root → stem → leaf); mesophyll cells; evaporation into air spaces; diffusion out through stomata.
  2. (b) Transpiration.
    B1.
Q3Transpiration · Core · Paper 6 style · 7 marks

A potted cotton plant was weighed every 2 hours during a sunny day in Faisalabad, and the rate of water loss was calculated.Time: 06:00, 08:00, 10:00, 12:00, 14:00, 16:00, 18:00Rate of water loss (g per hour): 0.4, 1.6, 3.2, 4.1, 3.6, 1.8, 0.5

  1. (a) State the time at which the rate of water loss was highest. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean rate of water loss for the seven readings. Give your answer to two decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two reasons why the rate was low at 06:00. [2]
  4. (d) State the most suitable type of graph to display these results and how the axes should be arranged. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12:00 (4.1 g per hour).
    B1.
  2. (b) 2.17 g per hour
    M1: (0.4 + 1.6 + 3.2 + 4.1 + 3.6 + 1.8 + 0.5) ÷ 7 = 15.2 ÷ 7; A1: 2.17 g/h.
  3. (c) Low temperature; low light so stomata were (partly) closed; higher humidity in early morning.
    1 mark each for any two.
  4. (d) A line graph with time on the x-axis and rate of water loss (g per hour) on the y-axis.
    1 mark: line graph; 1 mark: time on x-axis, rate on y-axis with units.
Q4Transpiration · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In a leaf, where does liquid water change into water vapour during transpiration? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A on the surfaces of the mesophyll cells, into the air spaces
    Water evaporates from the wet cell walls of mesophyll cells into the air spaces, then water vapour diffuses out through the stomata. Guard cells control the stoma but are not the main evaporating surface; the cuticle is waterproof.
Q5Transpiration · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Under which set of conditions will a well-watered maize plant with open stomata lose water vapour fastest? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D 35 °C, dry air, windy
    High temperature increases evaporation and diffusion; dry air gives a steep water vapour gradient; wind removes water vapour from around the leaf, keeping the gradient steep. C has a high temperature but humid, still air reduces the gradient.
Q6Transpiration · Standard · Paper 6 style · 10 marks

A leafy shoot of a guava plant was set up in a potometer. The air bubble in the capillary tube moved 36 mm in 5 minutes in still air. A fan was then switched on and the bubble moved 62 mm in 5 minutes. The internal radius of the capillary tube is 0.5 mm.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of water taken up in still air in 5 minutes. Use the formula volume = πr²l, where r is the internal radius of the tube and l the distance moved. Give your answer in mm³ to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the rate of water uptake in still air in mm³ per minute. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage increase in the distance moved when the fan was switched on. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the fan increased the rate of water uptake. [3]
  5. (e) State two precautions when setting up a potometer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 28.3 mm³
    M1: π × 0.5² × 36; A1: 28.3 mm³.
  2. (b) 5.7 mm³/min
    28.27 ÷ 5 = 5.7 mm³/min (allow follow-through).
  3. (c) 72 %
    (62 − 36) ÷ 36 × 100 = 72 %.
  4. (d) Moving air removes water vapour from around the leaf surface, keeping a steep water vapour concentration (diffusion) gradient between the air spaces and the outside air; more water vapour diffuses out through the stomata, so more water is pulled up the xylem (transpiration pull) and taken up from the potometer.
    1 mark each: wind removes water vapour near the leaf; steeper diffusion gradient; faster loss through stomata / more transpiration pull.
  5. (e) Cut the shoot under water; make all joints airtight (e.g. with petroleum jelly); dry the leaves; let the shoot settle for a few minutes before taking readings.
    1 mark each for any two.
Q7Transpiration · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the rate of transpiration increases when the temperature rises. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the rate of transpiration is lower on a humid day. [2]
  3. (c) State the feature of the leaf interior that provides a large surface for evaporation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water molecules gain more kinetic energy, so more water evaporates from the surfaces of the mesophyll cells; water vapour diffuses faster out through the stomata; warm air can also hold more water vapour, so the diffusion gradient is steeper.
    1 mark each for any three: more kinetic energy; faster evaporation from mesophyll surfaces; faster diffusion of water vapour through stomata; steeper gradient.
  2. (b) Humid air contains more water vapour, so the concentration gradient of water vapour between the air spaces in the leaf and the air outside is smaller; less water vapour diffuses out.
    1 mark: more water vapour in the air outside; 1 mark: smaller diffusion gradient, slower diffusion out of stomata.
  3. (c) The large internal surface area of the mesophyll cells facing the interconnecting air spaces.
    B1.

  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how wind speed affects the rate of transpiration of a leafy shoot. You are provided with a potometer, an electric fan and normal laboratory apparatus.In your plan, include: what you will vary and how; what you will measure; the variables you will keep constant; how you will make the results reliable. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Set up a potometer with a leafy shoot cut under water and airtight joints; vary wind speed by placing the fan at different distances (e.g. 25, 50, 75, 100 cm) or using fan speed settings, plus a no-fan control; measure the distance the bubble moves in a fixed time (e.g. 5 min) and calculate the rate; keep the same shoot, temperature, light intensity and humidity constant; allow 5 minutes to settle at each speed; repeat three times at each speed and calculate a mean.

Q9Transpiration · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) On a hot afternoon in Multan the leaves of a pumpkin plant droop, even though the soil is moist. By evening they have recovered. Explain why the leaves droop. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest one advantage to the plant of wilting. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the leaves recover in the evening. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The rate of transpiration is greater than the rate of water uptake by the roots; the leaf cells lose water and become flaccid (lose turgor pressure); so the leaves are no longer supported and wilt.
    1 mark each: water loss faster than uptake; cells lose turgor / become flaccid; loss of support → wilting.
  2. (b) The leaves hang down so less surface faces the sun / less heat absorbed, reducing further water loss.
    B1.
  3. (c) Lower temperature and light, so transpiration falls (stomata close); water uptake now exceeds loss so cells take in water by osmosis and become turgid again.
    1 mark: less transpiration in evening; 1 mark: cells regain water / become turgid.
Q10Transpiration · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

    1 mark: paper is sensitive to any water; 1 mark: fingers/air moisture would change colour → invalid result.

    B1.

Q12Transpiration · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how water moves up the xylem of a tall tree such as a shisham tree. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest why the trunk of a tree becomes very slightly narrower in diameter at midday than at night. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water evaporates from mesophyll cells and diffuses out of the leaf; this creates a transpiration pull (tension) on the water in the xylem; water molecules are attracted to each other (cohesion), so a continuous column of water is pulled up from the roots; the xylem vessels are narrow, continuous, hollow tubes.
    1 mark each for any four: evaporation/transpiration from leaf; creates transpiration pull / tension; cohesion between water molecules; continuous column of water; pulled up through xylem (continuous hollow tubes) from roots.
  2. (b) At midday the transpiration rate is highest, so the tension in the xylem is greatest; the walls of the xylem are pulled inwards, slightly reducing the diameter.
    1 mark: high transpiration at midday; 1 mark: greater tension/pull on xylem walls draws them inwards.

Q14Transpiration · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A researcher measured the rate of water loss from a leafy shoot of a mulberry tree in still air and in moving air.Still air: 2.4 g per hourMoving air: 4.2 g per hour

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in the rate of water loss in moving air compared with still air. [2]
  2. (b) The researcher then covered the shoot with a clear plastic bag in moving air, and the rate fell to 0.6 g per hour. Explain this result. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why plants in very windy places, such as the northern mountain passes, often have small leaves. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 75 %
    M1: (4.2 − 2.4) ÷ 2.4 × 100; A1: 75 %.
  2. (b) Water vapour collects inside the bag, so the air around the leaf becomes very humid / saturated; the bag also prevents wind removing water vapour; the diffusion gradient between leaf air spaces and the air is very small, so little water vapour diffuses out.
    1 mark each: humidity in bag rises; wind cannot remove water vapour; small diffusion gradient → slow loss.
  3. (c) Smaller leaf surface area means fewer stomata exposed and less water vapour lost, which reduces the risk of wilting when wind increases transpiration.
    1 mark: smaller surface area / fewer stomata; 1 mark: reduces water loss by transpiration.
Q15Transpiration · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Water moves up the xylem of a tall eucalyptus tree in a continuous column. Which statement best explains this? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Loss of water vapour from the leaves pulls water up, and water molecules are attracted to each other.
    Transpiration pull: evaporation and diffusion of water vapour from leaves creates a pull on the water column; cohesion (attraction between water molecules) keeps the column continuous. Xylem cells are dead and cannot contract; water enters roots by osmosis, not active transport of water; xylem carries no sucrose.
Q16Xylem & phloem structure · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which is a function of xylem? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B transport of water and mineral ions and support
    Xylem transports water and mineral ions and its lignified walls help support the plant. Sucrose and amino acids travel in phloem (A).

  2. (b) State the positions of xylem and phloem in a transverse section of a root. [2]
