Q1Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is a sustainable resource? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B a resource that is produced as rapidly as it is removed, so it does not run out
    A sustainable resource is replaced as fast as it is used (e.g. fish caught within quotas, forests replanted). A is wrong: renewable resources can still run out if over-used.
Q2Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State four reasons why species become endangered or extinct. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any four: habitat destruction / loss; hunting / poaching / over-harvesting; pollution; introduced (invasive) species; climate change.
    1 mark each for four different reasons; 'pollution' accepted once.

  1. (a) State two materials that can be recycled. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how recycling paper helps to conserve forests. [2]
  3. (c) State why sewage is treated before the water is returned to rivers. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two: paper; glass; plastic; metal (e.g. aluminium).
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Fewer trees need to be cut down to make new paper, so habitats are kept.
    B1 fewer trees felled; B1 habitats / species conserved (or less energy / CO₂).
  3. (c) To remove pathogens and organic matter / to make the water safe to reuse.
    B1.
Q4Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which is an example of in situ conservation? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C protecting a forest as a national park
    In situ conservation protects a species in its natural habitat, such as a national park or reserve. Seed banks, zoos and gene banks are ex situ conservation, because the organisms are removed from their habitat.
Q5Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

The markhor is an endangered wild goat found in the mountains of northern Pakistan.

  1. (a) Give three ways in which hunting restrictions and protected areas can help the markhor population recover. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Protected areas keep the habitat safe from development; hunting restrictions reduce the number killed; breeding is not disturbed so the population can grow.
    1 mark for each of any three valid ways, such as: protected habitat; fewer animals killed; undisturbed breeding; patrols against poaching.
Q6Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Why does increasing the minimum mesh size of fishing nets help to conserve fish stocks? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Young fish escape and can reach breeding age.
    Larger mesh lets small, young fish escape so they can grow and reproduce, keeping the population up. A is wrong: adult fish are still caught.
Q7Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The Indus river dolphin is an endangered mammal found only in the Indus River system. Its population was split into small groups when barrages were built across the river.

  1. (a) Suggest three ways in which the Indus river dolphin could be conserved. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why a species with a very small population is at greater risk of extinction, even if it is protected. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three: protect / make reserves of its river habitat; ban hunting and the use of harmful fishing nets; reduce pollution entering the river; ensure enough water flow (manage dams / barrages, provide passages); rescue stranded dolphins from canals; education of local communities; monitoring the population.
    1 mark each for any three.
  2. (b) Small population has low genetic variation; less chance that some individuals have alleles to survive a disease or environmental change (also inbreeding).
    B1 reduced genetic variation / inbreeding; B1 less able to adapt to change / disease.
Q8Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A forest in the Murree hills contains 12 000 trees. Each year, 900 trees are cut down for timber and fuel, and 650 young trees are planted.

  1. (a) Calculate how many years it will take for the number of trees to fall to half of its present number at these rates. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest three ways the forest could be managed sustainably. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 24 years
    net loss = 900 − 650 = 250 trees per year (M1); 6000 ÷ 250 = 24 years (A1).
  2. (b) Any three: replant at least as many trees as are felled; selective felling instead of clear-felling; legal quotas / permits for felling; protect some areas as reserves; education of local people; use alternative fuels.
    1 mark each.

  1. (a) Calculate the mean number of orchids per quadrat in each area. [2]
  2. (b) Each area is 1200 m². Estimate the total number of orchids in each area. [2]

  4. (d) Suggest one reason for the difference between the two areas. [1]

  1. (a) protected: 1.1; unprotected: 0.15
    protected: 22 ÷ 20 = 1.1 (B1); unprotected: 3 ÷ 20 = 0.15 (B1).
  2. (b) protected: 1320; unprotected: 180

Q10Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which forestry method is most sustainable? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C selective logging of mature trees with replanting of young trees
    Selective logging removes only mature trees and replanting keeps the forest renewing itself, so the resource lasts. The other methods remove the forest faster than it can recover or destroy the habitat.
Q11Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A count of a bird species in a wetland gave: 2000: 480 birds; 2005: 360 birds; 2010: 200 birds.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage decrease from 2000 to 2010. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean decrease per year between 2000 and 2010. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest one reason for the decline and one way to reverse it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 58.3 %
    1 mark for (480 − 200) ÷ 480 × 100; 1 mark for 58.3 %.
  2. (b) 28 birds per year
    1 mark for 280 ÷ 10 = 28 birds per year.
  3. (c) Reason: loss of wetland habitat through drainage or pollution. Reverse: protect the wetland from drainage and restore the habitat.
    1 mark for a valid reason; 1 mark for a valid way to reverse it.
Q12Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how artificial insemination (AI) and in vitro fertilisation (IVF) can help in captive breeding programmes for endangered mammals. [4]
  2. (b) Explain the benefits of seed banks for conserving plant species. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) AI: sperm collected from a male and placed into a female, so animals do not need to be moved or mate naturally; sperm can be stored / transported between zoos. IVF: eggs fertilised outside the body and embryos implanted, possibly into a surrogate mother of a related species; increases offspring numbers; allows breeding between individuals in different places, increasing genetic variation.
    1 mark each, max 4: AI – sperm inserted artificially; no need for animals to meet / transport; IVF – fertilisation outside the body; embryos implanted into female / surrogate; frozen sperm / embryos stored; more offspring; mixes genes from different populations / maintains variation.
  2. (b) Seeds are stored (dry and cold) for many years, so species can be regrown if they become extinct in the wild; they keep a range of genetic variation for future breeding / research.
    1 mark each for two benefits.
Q13Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Scientists estimated the total mass of a species of fish in part of the Arabian Sea. Fishing quotas and a closed breeding season were introduced in 2016.2010: 52 000 tonnes2016: 31 000 tonnes2024: 46 500 tonnes

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage decrease in the stock from 2010 to 2016 (to 2 significant figures) and the percentage increase from 2016 to 2024 (to 1 decimal place). [3]
  2. (b) Explain how quotas and closed seasons help a fish population to recover. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest one reason why the stock in 2024 had not returned to its 2010 level. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) decrease: 40 %; increase: 50.0 %
    (52 000 − 31 000) ÷ 52 000 × 100 = 40.4 → 40 % (M1 A1); (46 500 − 31 000) ÷ 31 000 × 100 = 50.0 % (B1).
  2. (b) Quotas limit the mass of fish caught, so more remain to breed; closed seasons stop fishing in the breeding season, so fish can reproduce; population increases because birth rate exceeds death rate.
    1 mark each: fewer fish removed; more fish reach breeding age / breed; closed season protects breeding; birth rate > death rate / numbers replaced.
  3. (c) Time needed for fish to grow and breed / illegal fishing / pollution / climate change.
    Any one.
Q14Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

The Arabian oryx became extinct in the wild in 1972 because of hunting. It was saved by captive breeding in zoos and has since been released into protected reserves.

  1. (a) Suggest why the Arabian oryx released into a reserve came from several different zoos rather than one zoo. [2]
  2. (b) Give three reasons why it is important to conserve species such as the Arabian oryx. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why scientists continue to monitor a reintroduced population. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Animals from different zoos are less closely related, so there is more genetic variation in the released population; reduces inbreeding / increases the chance of surviving disease or environmental change.
    B1 more genetic variation / less related; B1 less inbreeding / better able to adapt.
  2. (b) Any three: to maintain biodiversity; to keep food chains / ecosystems stable; possible future sources of medicines or genes; ecotourism / economic value; moral responsibility; future generations.
    1 mark each for any three.
  3. (c) To check the population is breeding and increasing / to detect threats such as poaching or disease early.
    Any one.
Q15Conservation, sustainability & endangered species · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Only 40 individuals of a rare mammal remain in a reserve, and they are all closely related.

  1. (a) Explain why a small, closely related population has a greater risk of extinction. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest how a conservation programme could reduce this risk. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) There is little genetic variation, so the population cannot adapt to disease or environmental change; inbreeding increases harmful recessive alleles, reducing fertility and survival.
    1 mark each for any three of: little genetic variation; cannot adapt to change or disease; inbreeding; harmful recessive alleles; reduced fertility or survival.
  2. (b) Introduce unrelated individuals from other populations to increase genetic diversity; manage breeding so close relatives do not mate.
    1 mark for introducing unrelated individuals; 1 mark for managing breeding to avoid inbreeding.
Q16Pollution & eutrophication · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State three sources of water pollution. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why untreated sewage in a river is a danger to people living downstream. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three: untreated sewage; fertiliser run-off from farms; chemical waste from industry; discarded rubbish / plastics; pesticides.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Sewage contains pathogens (e.g. cholera bacteria); people who drink or wash in the water can become infected.
    B1 pathogens; B1 transmitted through drinking / using water.
Q17Pollution & eutrophication · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which substance, washed off farmland into a lake, is the main cause of eutrophication? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B nitrate ions
    Nitrate ions from fertiliser cause rapid growth of algae. Carbon dioxide (A) is not usually limiting to the same extent and does not come from fertiliser run-off.
Q18Pollution & eutrophication · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Nitrate fertiliser washes from farmland into a lake after heavy rain.

  1. (a) State two ways in which a farmer could reduce fertiliser run-off. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how a strip of vegetation next to the lake helps to reduce nitrate entering the lake. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Apply the correct amount of fertiliser at the right time (not before heavy rain); use slow-release fertiliser; leave buffer strips of vegetation along the river bank.
    1 mark for each of any two valid ways.
  2. (b) Roots absorb some of the nitrate before it reaches the water; the vegetation slows the run-off so less nitrate is carried into the lake.
    1 mark: roots absorb nitrate; 1 mark: slows run-off so less nitrate enters the lake.
Q19Pollution & eutrophication · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which gas is released by burning sulfur-containing fuels and causes acid rain? [1]
Show answer
