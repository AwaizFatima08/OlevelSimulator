Q1Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) For which disease would a doctor prescribe an antibiotic? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C cholera
    Cholera is caused by a bacterium, and antibiotics act on bacteria. Influenza, measles and colds are caused by viruses, which antibiotics do not affect.

  1. (a) State what an antibiotic is used for. [1]
  2. (b) A student with a bad cold asks a doctor for antibiotics. Explain why the doctor refuses. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) To treat bacterial infections (kill bacteria or stop them growing) in the body.
    B1 treat bacterial infection.
  2. (b) Colds are caused by viruses; antibiotics have no effect on viruses (and unnecessary use encourages resistant bacteria).
    B1 cold caused by virus; B1 antibiotics do not kill viruses / encourage resistance.

  1. (a) Tuberculosis (TB) is treated with antibiotics for at least six months. Suggest why the treatment takes so long. [1]
  2. (b) Patients with TB are usually given three or four different antibiotics at the same time. Explain how this reduces the chance of resistant TB bacteria developing. [2]

Q24Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

Q26Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the use of an antibiotic leads to an increase in the proportion of resistant bacteria in a population. [4]
  2. (b) A student writes 'the antibiotic makes the bacteria become immune'. Explain why this statement is wrong. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) There is variation in a bacterial population; some bacteria have (by random mutation) an allele giving resistance; the antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria; resistant bacteria survive and reproduce, passing on the resistance allele; over many generations most of the population is resistant.
    B1 variation / random mutation already present (not caused by antibiotic); B1 non-resistant killed; B1 resistant survive and reproduce; B1 pass on allele / proportion increases over generations.
  2. (b) Bacteria do not have an immune system / immunity refers to the body's response to pathogens; the antibiotic does not cause the resistance – it selects bacteria that were already resistant.
    B1 'immune' is wrong term (immunity is in the host); B1 resistance pre-exists, selected not caused.
Q27Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Calculate the time taken for one resistant bacterium to produce over 4000 bacteria if it divides every 20 minutes. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Use your answer to explain why resistance can spread through a bacterial population very quickly when an antibiotic is used. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4 hours (12 divisions give 2¹² = 4096)
    M1 2¹² = 4096 > 4000 (2¹¹ = 2048 < 4000); A1 12 × 20 min = 240 min = 4 hours.
  2. (b) Bacteria reproduce very rapidly (asexually, by binary fission), so a single surviving resistant bacterium can give rise to a large resistant population in hours; each offspring inherits the resistance allele.
    B1 rapid reproduction / many generations quickly; B1 all offspring inherit resistance.
