Q1Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) For which disease would a doctor prescribe an antibiotic?A influenzaB measlesC choleraD the common cold [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C cholera
    Cholera is caused by a bacterium, and antibiotics act on bacteria. Influenza, measles and colds are caused by viruses, which antibiotics do not affect.
Q2Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State what an antibiotic is used for. [1]
  2. (b) A student with a bad cold asks a doctor for antibiotics. Explain why the doctor refuses. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) To treat bacterial infections (kill bacteria or stop them growing) in the body.
    B1 treat bacterial infection.
  2. (b) Colds are caused by viruses; antibiotics have no effect on viruses (and unnecessary use encourages resistant bacteria).
    B1 cold caused by virus; B1 antibiotics do not kill viruses / encourage resistance.
Q3Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State one disease, caused by bacteria, that can be treated with antibiotics. [1]
  2. (b) State why some bacterial infections are now difficult to treat with antibiotics. [1]
  3. (c) Name one example of a strain of bacteria that is resistant to several antibiotics. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) e.g. cholera, tuberculosis, typhoid, bacterial pneumonia, strep throat.
    B1 any bacterial disease. Do not accept viral diseases (e.g. HIV, flu, COVID-19).
  2. (b) Some bacteria are resistant to antibiotics, which reduces the effectiveness of the antibiotics.
    B1 resistance.
  3. (c) MRSA
    B1 MRSA.
Q4Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Antibiotics affect bacteria but do not affect viruses. Name one structure found in bacteria that is not found in viruses. [1]
  2. (b) State whether each disease can be treated with antibiotics: (i) tuberculosis; (ii) HIV/AIDS; (iii) cholera; (iv) influenza. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cell wall / cell membrane / ribosomes / cytoplasm.
    B1 any one.
  2. (b) (i) yes; (ii) no; (iii) yes; (iv) no.
    2 marks all four correct; 1 mark for two or three correct.
Q5Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State whether each statement about antibiotics is true or false: (i) Antibiotics kill bacteria. (ii) Antibiotics are effective against all pathogens. (iii) Antibiotics should be taken until the course is finished. (iv) Antibiotics can be used to treat the common cold. [2]
  2. (b) Antibodies and antibiotics are often confused. State one difference between them. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) true; (ii) false; (iii) true; (iv) false.
    2 marks all four correct; 1 mark for three correct.
  2. (b) Antibodies are proteins made by lymphocytes in the body; antibiotics are drugs (often made by microorganisms) taken to kill bacteria.
    B1 antibodies made by lymphocytes / white blood cells vs antibiotics are drugs taken into the body.
Q6Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a person should not take antibiotics left over from a previous illness, or share them with a family member. [3]
  2. (b) State why antibiotics are sometimes called 'a precious resource'. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The illness may be viral, so the antibiotic will not work; it may be the wrong antibiotic / not enough for a full course / wrong dose; this exposes bacteria to low doses so resistant bacteria survive and multiply.
    1 mark each for any three of: illness may be viral; wrong antibiotic for that bacterium; incomplete course / wrong dose; increases resistance; may cause allergy / side effects (not the prescribed patient).
  2. (b) As resistance increases, fewer antibiotics work and very few new antibiotics are being developed.
    B1 resistance making them ineffective / few new ones.
Q7Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) The first antibiotic, penicillin, was obtained from a mould. State the kingdom to which moulds belong. [1]
  2. (b) State what happens to bacteria when they are exposed to an antibiotic to which they are not resistant. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why a doctor may change to a different antibiotic if a patient's infection does not improve. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fungi
    B1 fungi / fungus kingdom.
  2. (b) They are killed or their growth (reproduction) is stopped.
    B1 killed / growth prevented.
  3. (c) The bacteria may be resistant to the first antibiotic.
    B1 resistance.
Q8Antibiotics & their use · Core · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

Equal volumes of a culture of a harmless bacterium were spread on agar plates with and without an antibiotic and incubated at 25 °C. Number of colonies: without antibiotic – mean 180; with antibiotic – 17, 20, 17.

  1. (a) Calculate the mean number of colonies on plates with antibiotic. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of colonies that survived on antibiotic plates compared with plates without antibiotic. [2]
  3. (c) State what the colonies growing on the antibiotic plates show about those bacteria. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18
    B1 (17 + 20 + 17)/3 = 18.
  2. (b) 10 %
    M1 18 ÷ 180 × 100; A1 10 %.
  3. (c) They are resistant to the antibiotic.
    B1 resistant.
Q9Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Why do antibiotics not cure diseases caused by viruses?A Viruses are too small to be seen by the immune system.B Viruses lack the structures and processes, such as cell walls, that antibiotics act on.C Viruses are always resistant to antibiotics because of mutations.D Antibiotics only work in the gut, and viruses live in the lungs. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Viruses lack the structures and processes, such as cell walls, that antibiotics act on.
    Antibiotics damage bacterial structures or processes (e.g. cell wall formation); viruses have no cell wall or cell processes of their own and reproduce inside host cells. Resistance (C) is a property of some bacteria, not the reason viruses are unaffected.
Q10Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is MRSA?A a virus that causes pneumoniaB a strain of bacteria resistant to several commonly used antibioticsC an antibiotic used in hospitalsD a fungus that produces penicillin [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B a strain of bacteria resistant to several commonly used antibiotics
    MRSA is a strain of Staphylococcus aureus that is resistant to methicillin and several other antibiotics, making infections hard to treat.
Q11Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the development of resistant bacteria, such as MRSA, can be minimised. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why patients are told to finish the full course of antibiotics even when they feel better. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Use antibiotics only when essential (not for viral infections); doctors prescribe only when needed / avoid unnecessary prescriptions; patients complete the full course so all bacteria are killed; do not use antibiotics routinely in farm animals; good hygiene in hospitals reduces spread of resistant strains.
    1 mark each for any four of: only use when essential; not for viral infections; complete the course; do not share / keep leftover; limit use in livestock; hospital hygiene / hand washing / isolate infected patients; use the correct antibiotic (after testing).
  2. (b) Some bacteria may still be alive (the more resistant ones survive longest); if the course is stopped they can multiply, causing the infection to return and spreading resistance.
    B1 not all bacteria killed / surviving bacteria; B1 they multiply / infection returns / resistant bacteria increase.
Q12Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The number of antibiotic prescriptions per 1000 people per day in one district was 5.2 in 2021 and 3.9 in 2024, after a campaign to reduce unnecessary use.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage decrease in antibiotic prescriptions between 2021 and 2024. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest two reasons why health authorities wanted to reduce the number of prescriptions. [2]
  3. (c) Over the same period, the percentage of infections caused by resistant bacteria in the district did not fall. Suggest one reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25 %
    M1 (5.2 − 3.9) ÷ 5.2 × 100; A1 25 %.
  2. (b) To slow the development / spread of antibiotic-resistant bacteria; many prescriptions were for viral infections where antibiotics have no effect; to save money / reduce side effects.
    1 mark each for any two.
  3. (c) Resistant bacteria already present continue to spread (e.g. in hospitals); antibiotics still used in livestock / bought without prescription; resistance takes many years to decline.
    B1 any sensible reason.
Q13Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

A student placed discs soaked in antibiotics A and B on agar plates spread with a harmless bacterium and incubated them at 25 °C for 2 days. Diameter of clear zone / mm:A: 26, 24, 25B: 14, 15, 31, 13

  1. (a) Calculate the mean diameter of the clear zone for antibiotic A. [1]
  2. (b) Identify the anomalous result for antibiotic B and calculate the mean without it. [2]
  3. (c) State which antibiotic would be most effective against this bacterium. Explain your answer. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why the plates were incubated at 25 °C rather than 37 °C. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25 mm
    B1 (26 + 24 + 25)/3 = 25.
  2. (b) Anomaly 31 mm; mean 14 mm.
    B1 31; B1 (14 + 15 + 13)/3 = 14.
  3. (c) Antibiotic A; it has the largest clear zone, showing the most bacteria were killed / prevented from growing.
    B1 A; B1 largest zone / more inhibition.
  4. (d) To reduce the chance of growing bacteria that are pathogenic to humans (which grow best at body temperature).
    B1 avoids growth of human pathogens.
Q14Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student prepared an agar plate to test the effect of penicillin on a harmless bacterium.

  1. (a) Explain why the student flamed the inoculating loop until it glowed red before use. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the lid was held with two pieces of tape but not sealed all the way round. [2]
  3. (c) State why the plate was stored upside down during incubation. [1]
  4. (d) After incubation a clear zone appeared around a disc containing penicillin. Explain what this shows. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) To kill any microorganisms on the loop (sterilise it), so the plate is not contaminated.
    B1 sterilise / kill microorganisms.
  2. (b) Tape stops the lid being opened accidentally (safety); not sealing allows oxygen in, preventing the growth of harmful anaerobic bacteria.
    B1 prevents lid being lifted / contamination; B1 oxygen can enter so anaerobic (pathogenic) bacteria do not grow.
  3. (c) Prevents condensation dripping onto the agar surface and spreading colonies.
    B1 condensation does not drip onto agar.
  4. (d) The penicillin diffused into the agar and killed the bacteria / stopped them growing near the disc.
    B1 antibiotic diffused out and prevented bacterial growth.
Q15Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A survey in a city found that many people bought antibiotics for coughs and fevers without seeing a doctor.

  1. (a) In some places antibiotics can be bought from a pharmacy without a doctor's prescription. Explain two ways this can increase antibiotic resistance. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest one advantage of a doctor testing a sample of bacteria from the patient before choosing an antibiotic. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) People take antibiotics for viral illnesses where they have no benefit, exposing their bacteria to antibiotics unnecessarily; people buy only a few tablets / do not complete a full course, so some bacteria survive; wrong antibiotic or dose may be chosen, so bacteria are not killed. In each case resistant bacteria are favoured and multiply.
    Two ways, each B1 practice + B1 link to resistance (surviving / selected resistant bacteria multiply).
  2. (b) The doctor can choose an antibiotic that the bacteria are not resistant to, so it will work and unnecessary antibiotics are avoided.
    B1 choose effective antibiotic / avoid ineffective use.
Q16Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A doctor in Peshawar prescribed amoxicillin capsules of 500 mg, one capsule three times a day for 7 days.

  1. (a) Calculate the total mass of antibiotic taken during the full course and the number of capsules needed. [2]
  2. (b) After 3 days the patient feels better and stops taking the capsules. Calculate the number of capsules not taken. [1]
  3. (c) Explain the risk of stopping the course early. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10 500 mg (10.5 g); 21 capsules
    B1 500 × 3 × 7 = 10 500 mg; B1 3 × 7 = 21 capsules.
  2. (b) 12
    B1 21 − 9 = 12.
  3. (c) Not all the bacteria have been killed; the surviving ones (often the least sensitive / resistant) multiply, so the infection can return and is harder to treat.
    B1 bacteria survive; B1 infection returns / resistant bacteria increase.
Q17Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

The number of bacteria in samples from a patient with a throat infection (arbitrary units): day 0 – 48; day 3 – 12; day 5 – 25; day 7 – 40.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage decrease in bacteria between day 0 and day 3. [2]
  2. (b) The patient stopped taking the antibiotic on day 3. Describe and explain the change in the number of bacteria from day 3 to day 7. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 75 %
    M1 (48 − 12) ÷ 48 × 100; A1 75 %.
  2. (b) The number rose from 12 to 40 (arbitrary units); the bacteria that survived were not killed by the antibiotic (more resistant) and multiplied once the antibiotic was no longer present.
    B1 increase with data; B1 surviving bacteria multiply; B1 survivors likely more resistant / antibiotic no longer present.
Q18Antibiotics & their use · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Tuberculosis (TB) is treated with antibiotics for at least six months. Suggest why the treatment takes so long. [1]
  2. (b) Patients with TB are usually given three or four different antibiotics at the same time. Explain how this reduces the chance of resistant TB bacteria developing. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) TB bacteria grow / reproduce slowly and some are hidden inside cells / tissues, so antibiotics take a long time to kill them all.
    B1 slow-growing bacteria / hard to reach.
  2. (b) A bacterium is unlikely to be resistant to all the antibiotics at once; any bacterium resistant to one antibiotic is killed by another, so resistant strains do not survive to multiply.
    B1 unlikely to have resistance to all; B1 bacteria resistant to one killed by another.
Q19Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which action is most likely to reduce the development of antibiotic-resistant bacteria in a community?A taking antibiotics at the first sign of any sore throatB stopping a course of antibiotics as soon as symptoms disappearC using antibiotics only when essential and finishing the full courseD giving leftover antibiotics to family members with similar symptoms [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C using antibiotics only when essential and finishing the full course
    Using antibiotics only when needed reduces the chance of selecting resistant bacteria, and finishing the course kills as many bacteria as possible so fewer survive to multiply. Stopping early (B) leaves the less susceptible bacteria alive.
Q20Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Paper discs soaked in four antibiotics, P, Q, R and S, were placed on agar spread with one type of bacterium. After incubation the diameters of the clear zones were P 6 mm, Q 21 mm, R 0 mm, S 14 mm (all discs 6 mm wide). Which conclusion is correct?A P is the most effective antibiotic.B The bacteria are resistant to R, and Q is the most effective.C S is more effective than Q.D All four antibiotics killed the bacteria. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B The bacteria are resistant to R, and Q is the most effective.
    The largest clear zone (Q, 21 mm) shows the most bacteria killed or prevented from growing. A zone no wider than the disc (P 6 mm) or none at all (R) means no effect, i.e. resistance.
Q21Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

In one country, 202 tonnes of antibiotics were used in a year: 71 tonnes in human medicine and 131 tonnes in farm animals.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the total antibiotics used that were given to farm animals. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why giving antibiotics routinely to healthy farm animals can affect human health. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 65 %
    M1 131 ÷ 202 × 100 = 64.85; A1 65 %.
  2. (b) Bacteria in animals are exposed to antibiotics, so resistant bacteria survive and multiply; resistant bacteria (or their resistance) can pass to humans through meat, contact or the environment; antibiotics may then not work against human infections.
    B1 routine use selects for resistant bacteria; B1 resistant bacteria spread to humans (food / contact / manure); B1 infections in humans harder to treat.
Q22Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

Clear zones around two antibiotic discs on a bacterial lawn had diameters of 2.2 cm (antibiotic X) and 1.1 cm (antibiotic Y).

  1. (a) Calculate the area of each clear zone. Use area = πr² and give your answers to 2 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate how many times larger the clear zone area is for X than for Y. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why it is better to compare areas rather than diameters. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Antibiotic X: 3.8 cm²; antibiotic Y: 0.95 cm².
    B1 X: π × 1.1² = 3.80 → 3.8 cm²; B1 Y: π × 0.55² = 0.950 → 0.95 cm².
  2. (b) 4 times
    B1 3.80 ÷ 0.950 = 4.
  3. (c) The area shows the actual region where bacteria failed to grow; doubling the diameter gives four times the area, so diameters underestimate the difference.
    B1 area represents amount of agar cleared / area ∝ diameter².
Q23Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how the concentration of an antibiotic affects the growth of a harmless bacterium on agar plates. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Prepare at least five concentrations of antibiotic by dilution (e.g. 0, 25, 50, 75, 100 %); spread bacteria evenly on agar plates using aseptic technique (sterile spreader, flame neck of bottle, lift lid only slightly); soak equal-sized filter paper discs in each concentration for the same time; place on plates at equal spacing; tape the lid with two strips (not sealed), incubate upside down at 25 °C for 48 hours; measure diameter of clear zone with a ruler; repeat three times and calculate means; control: disc soaked in sterile water; controlled variables: same bacterium, same agar volume, disc size, temperature, time; safety: do not open plates after incubation, dispose by autoclaving, wash hands.
    1 mark each, max 6: range of ≥5 concentrations with how made; aseptic technique (one detail); same-size discs soaked for equal time; incubate at 25 °C for stated time; measure diameter / area of clear zone; two controlled variables; control disc (water); repeats and mean; safety (taped lid not sealed / do not open / autoclave).
Q24Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the use of an antibiotic leads to an increase in the proportion of resistant bacteria in a population. [4]
  2. (b) A student writes 'the antibiotic makes the bacteria become immune'. Explain why this statement is wrong. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) There is variation in a bacterial population; some bacteria have (by random mutation) an allele giving resistance; the antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria; resistant bacteria survive and reproduce, passing on the resistance allele; over many generations most of the population is resistant.
    B1 variation / random mutation already present (not caused by antibiotic); B1 non-resistant killed; B1 resistant survive and reproduce; B1 pass on allele / proportion increases over generations.
  2. (b) Bacteria do not have an immune system / immunity refers to the body's response to pathogens; the antibiotic does not cause the resistance – it selects bacteria that were already resistant.
    B1 'immune' is wrong term (immunity is in the host); B1 resistance pre-exists, selected not caused.
Q25Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Calculate the time taken for one resistant bacterium to produce over 4000 bacteria if it divides every 20 minutes. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Use your answer to explain why resistance can spread through a bacterial population very quickly when an antibiotic is used. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4 hours (12 divisions give 2¹² = 4096)
    M1 2¹² = 4096 > 4000 (2¹¹ = 2048 < 4000); A1 12 × 20 min = 240 min = 4 hours.
  2. (b) Bacteria reproduce very rapidly (asexually, by binary fission), so a single surviving resistant bacterium can give rise to a large resistant population in hours; each offspring inherits the resistance allele.
    B1 rapid reproduction / many generations quickly; B1 all offspring inherit resistance.
Q26Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A hospital introduced compulsory hand cleaning with alcohol gel and stricter rules on prescribing antibiotics. MRSA infections per 10 000 patients: 2020 – 6.8; 2024 – 1.7.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage decrease in MRSA infections between 2020 and 2024. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how each of the two measures could have contributed to the decrease. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 75 %
    M1 (6.8 − 1.7) ÷ 6.8 × 100; A1 75 %.
  2. (b) Hand washing / alcohol gel kills or removes MRSA from hands, so fewer bacteria are passed between patients; reduced prescribing lowers selection pressure, so resistant strains have less advantage and become less common.
    B1 hygiene removes / kills bacteria on hands; B1 so less transmission between patients; B1 fewer antibiotics → less selection for resistant bacteria; B1 resistant bacteria do not increase in proportion.
Q27Antibiotics & their use · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Data for four countries – antibiotic doses per 1000 people per day and % of E. coli infections resistant to one antibiotic: Q – 10, 9 %; R – 18, 20 %; S – 25, 29 %; T – 32, 41 %.

  1. (a) Describe the relationship shown in the data. [2]
  2. (b) A student concludes that 'antibiotic use is the only cause of resistance'. Evaluate this conclusion. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Countries with higher antibiotic use have a higher percentage of resistant E. coli; e.g. country T (32 doses) 41 % against country Q (10 doses) 9 % – a positive correlation.
    B1 positive correlation; B1 data quote with units.
  2. (b) The data show a correlation but do not prove cause; other factors could affect resistance, e.g. use in farm animals, hospital hygiene, sanitation, travel; only four countries were studied; but it is consistent with selection of resistant bacteria by antibiotic use.
    1 mark each, max 3: correlation not proof of causation; other factors named (livestock use / hygiene / sanitation / spread between countries); small sample of countries; supported by mechanism of selection.
Q28Drug definition & other drugs · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which is the best definition of a drug?A any substance that kills bacteriaB any substance taken into the body that modifies or affects chemical reactions in the bodyC any substance made by a doctorD any substance that is addictive [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B any substance taken into the body that modifies or affects chemical reactions in the body
    This is the general definition; antibiotics (A) are only one type of drug, and many drugs are neither prescribed nor addictive.
Q29Drug definition & other drugs · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why both a painkiller such as paracetamol and an antibiotic such as amoxicillin are drugs. [2]
  2. (b) State the difference in what these two drugs are used for. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both are substances taken into the body that modify or affect chemical reactions in the body.
    B1 taken into the body; B1 modify / affect chemical reactions in the body.
  2. (b) Paracetamol relieves symptoms (pain / fever) but does not kill the pathogen; amoxicillin kills bacteria, treating the cause of a bacterial infection.
    B1 painkiller relieves symptoms; B1 antibiotic kills bacteria / treats bacterial infection.
Q30Drug definition & other drugs · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A herbal tea is advertised as a cure for influenza. Using the definition of a drug, explain what would need to be true for the tea to be classed as a drug. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why it would not be useful for a patient with influenza to take an antibiotic instead. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest how scientists could test fairly whether the tea reduces the length of the illness. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A substance in the tea would need to be taken into the body and modify or affect chemical reactions in the body.
    B1 substance taken into body; B1 affects / modifies chemical reactions in the body.
  2. (b) Influenza is caused by a virus; antibiotics only act on bacteria, so they would not help (and could encourage resistance in bacteria in the body).
    B1 influenza is viral; B1 antibiotics do not affect viruses.
  3. (c) Compare two large groups of similar patients – one given the tea and one given a similar-tasting tea without the herb (placebo), without knowing which they get; record the number of days of illness and compare means.
    B1 large groups with a control / placebo group; B1 measure length of illness / compare means; accept blind trial.
