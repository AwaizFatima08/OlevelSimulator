Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Genetic modification · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the term genetic modification. [1]
  2. (b) Give three examples of genetic modification and state the benefit of each. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Changing the genetic material of an organism by removing, changing or inserting individual genes.
    B1.
  2. (b) Any three of: bacteria given the human insulin gene → produce human insulin; crops resistant to herbicides → weeds can be sprayed without killing crop; crops resistant to insect pests → less insecticide, higher yield; crops with improved nutritional quality (e.g. rice with extra vitamin A) → reduce deficiency.
    1 mark each for example + benefit.
Q2Genetic modification · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which enzyme is used to join a human gene into a bacterial plasmid?A amylaseB DNA ligaseC proteaseD restriction enzyme [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B DNA ligase
    DNA ligase joins the sticky ends of the gene and plasmid together. A restriction enzyme cuts DNA; it does not join it.
Q3Genetic modification · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why rice genetically modified to contain extra vitamin A precursor could improve health in some countries. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest one reason why some people prefer to increase vitamin A intake by eating a more varied diet (for example carrots and leafy vegetables) rather than growing GM rice. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) In places where people eat mainly rice, the diet may lack vitamin A; GM rice provides it, reducing vitamin A deficiency (e.g. poor night vision / blindness).
    B1 rice is a staple / main food; B1 reduces deficiency.
  2. (b) Any one of: concerns about the safety or environmental effects of GM crops; a varied diet also provides other vitamins and minerals; cost of GM seed / dependence on seed companies.
    B1.
Q4Genetic modification · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A maize variety has been genetically modified to be resistant to a herbicide (weedkiller).

  1. (a) Explain how growing a herbicide-resistant GM crop helps a farmer. [2]
  2. (b) State two concerns people have about growing herbicide-resistant crops. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The whole field can be sprayed with herbicide; weeds are killed but the crop is not, so there is less competition and a higher yield.
    B1 herbicide kills weeds but not crop; B1 less competition / higher yield.
  2. (b) Any two of: the resistance gene could spread to wild plants / weeds (making 'superweeds'); more herbicide may be used; reduced biodiversity; possible effects on human health not fully known.
    1 mark each.
Q5Genetic modification · Core · Paper 4 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) The steps below describe how a gene is transferred to bacteria. Put them in the correct order (write the letters).P Recombinant plasmids are taken up by bacteria.Q The gene and plasmid are joined using DNA ligase.R The gene is cut out of human DNA with a restriction enzyme.S The bacteria multiply and make the human protein.T A plasmid is cut open with the same restriction enzyme. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) R, T, Q, P, S
    B2 all correct; B1 one pair reversed (accept T before R).
Q6Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how bacteria can be genetically modified to produce human insulin. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Human insulin gene is cut out using a restriction enzyme, leaving sticky ends; a bacterial plasmid is cut with the same restriction enzyme; the gene and plasmid are joined with DNA ligase to form a recombinant plasmid; the recombinant plasmid is inserted into a bacterium; the bacteria multiply (in a fermenter) and express the gene, making human insulin, which is extracted and purified.
    1 mark each, max 5: restriction enzyme cuts out gene; sticky ends; plasmid cut with same enzyme; ligase joins; recombinant plasmid put into bacteria; bacteria reproduce / grown in fermenter and make insulin.
Q7Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by 'sticky ends'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the same restriction enzyme must be used to cut both the human DNA and the plasmid. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Short lengths of single-stranded DNA left at the cut ends after a staggered cut by a restriction enzyme; their exposed bases can pair with complementary bases.
    B1 single-stranded overhang; B1 unpaired bases that can pair with complementary bases.
  2. (b) The enzyme cuts at a specific base sequence and leaves the same sticky ends; so the sticky ends of the gene and plasmid are complementary and can pair up.
    B1 same sequence cut / same sticky ends; B1 complementary so they join.
Q8Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement about a plasmid is correct?A It is a small circle of DNA found in bacteria, separate from the main bacterial chromosome.B It is a chromosome found in the nucleus of a human cell.C It is an enzyme that joins pieces of DNA together.D It is a protein coat that surrounds the genetic material of a virus. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A It is a small circle of DNA found in bacteria, separate from the main bacterial chromosome.
    Plasmids are small circular loops of DNA in bacterial cytoplasm; they are used as vectors to carry genes into bacteria. C describes DNA ligase, and bacteria have no nucleus, so B is wrong.
Q9Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Before GM bacteria were used, insulin for people with diabetes was extracted from the pancreases of cattle and pigs. Suggest three advantages of using insulin made by GM bacteria. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any three of: identical to human insulin, so fewer allergic or immune reactions; can be made in large quantities; cheaper / quicker to produce; consistent purity; acceptable to people who avoid pig or animal products for religious or ethical reasons; no risk of disease passed from animals.
    1 mark each.
Q10Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 6 style · 4 marks

Fish have been genetically modified with an extra growth hormone gene. At a research farm the mean mass of GM fish at 12 months was 2.8 kg and of non-GM fish of the same species 1.2 kg.

  1. (a) Calculate how much greater, as a percentage, the mean mass of the GM fish was than the non-GM fish. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest one benefit and one concern of farming such GM fish. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 133 %
    (2.8 − 1.2) ÷ 1.2 × 100 (M1) = 133.3 → 133 % (A1).
  2. (b) Benefit: more food produced in less time / cheaper protein. Concern: GM fish escaping and breeding with or out-competing wild fish / effects on ecosystems / unknown health effects.
    B1 benefit; B1 concern.
Q11Genetic modification · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Wheat is the main crop in parts of Sindh and Balochistan where rainfall is low.

  1. (a) Suggest how genetically modified drought-tolerant wheat could help farmers in a region with low and unreliable rainfall. [2]
  2. (b) Give two reasons why some people are against growing GM crops. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Wheat survives / grows with less water, so yields are higher in dry years; more reliable food supply and income / less irrigation needed.
    B1 survives dry conditions; B1 higher / more reliable yield or less water used.
  2. (b) Any two of: genes could transfer to wild plants; possible unknown effects on health; reduced biodiversity; seeds are expensive and farmers depend on big companies; ethical objection to altering organisms.
    1 mark each.
Q12Genetic modification · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Many cotton farmers in Punjab grow cotton that has been genetically modified to contain a gene from a bacterium. The gene makes a protein that is toxic to caterpillars that feed on cotton.

  1. (a) Discuss the advantages and disadvantages to farmers and the environment of growing insect-resistant GM cotton. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Advantages: crop makes its own toxin so pests die; less insecticide sprayed (lower cost, less harm to farm workers and to non-target insects); higher yield / less damage. Disadvantages: GM seed is expensive and must be bought each season; pests may become resistant to the toxin; non-target insects may be harmed; reduced biodiversity in a monoculture; genes may spread to wild relatives.
    Max 4 for one side only. 1 mark each, max 6, with at least 2 advantages and 2 disadvantages for full marks.
Q13Genetic modification · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

A farmer near Bahawalpur grew GM insect-resistant cotton in one field and non-GM cotton in a similar field in the same season.Cotton type : yield / kg per hectare : number of insecticide spraysGM : 2250 : 3non-GM : 1800 : 8

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in yield of the GM cotton compared with the non-GM cotton. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage reduction in the number of insecticide sprays. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two reasons why the farmer should not conclude from these results alone that GM cotton is better on all farms. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25 %
    (2250 − 1800) ÷ 1800 × 100 (M1) = 25 % (A1).
  2. (b) 62.5 %
    (8 − 3) ÷ 8 × 100 (M1) = 62.5 % (A1).
  3. (c) Only one farm / one season — results may not be repeatable; other factors (soil, rainfall, pests that year) may differ between fields; cost of GM seed not included.
    1 mark each for any two valid limitations.
Q14Genetic modification · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

Under ideal conditions in a fermenter, a bacterium divides into two every 20 minutes.

  1. (a) Calculate the number of bacteria present after 4 hours, starting with one GM bacterium, if every cell divides once every 20 minutes. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest two reasons why the number of bacteria in a real fermenter after 4 hours is usually smaller than your answer to (a). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4096
    4 h = 240 min; 240 ÷ 20 = 12 divisions (M1); 2^12 = 4096 (A1).
  2. (b) Any two of: not every cell divides every 20 minutes (some cells die); nutrients (e.g. glucose) start to run out; toxic waste products build up / pH changes; the bacteria need time to adjust to the new conditions before dividing quickly.
    1 mark each for any two valid reasons.
Q15Genetic modification · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Suggest why a gene taken from a human can still be used by a bacterium to make the same human protein. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why the human protein made by bacteria must be purified before it can be used as a medicine. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The base sequence of the gene codes for the order of amino acids; the same base sequence codes for the same amino acids in all organisms, so the bacterium makes a protein with the same amino acid sequence.
    B1 gene / base sequence codes for amino acid sequence; B1 code is the same (universal) in bacteria and humans.
  2. (b) To remove bacterial cells, other bacterial proteins and toxins / nutrients from the fermenter that could cause harmful reactions.
    B1.
Q16Genetic modification · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A restriction enzyme cuts DNA only at the base sequence GAATTC. A linear piece of DNA has this sequence on one strand:TTGAATTCAGCCGAATTCTTAGGCTAGAATTCCGAHow many fragments are produced when this DNA is cut completely by the enzyme?A 2B 3C 4D 5 [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 4
    GAATTC occurs 3 times, and each cut in a linear molecule adds one fragment: 3 + 1 = 4. Answer B (3) counts the cut sites, not the fragments.

Q18Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A baker in Peshawar mixes flour, water, sugar and yeast to make dough for naan.

  1. (a) Write the word equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the dough rises when left in a warm place. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest what happens to the ethanol when the naan is baked. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide
    B1 ethanol; B1 carbon dioxide (with glucose as reactant).
  2. (b) Yeast respires and produces carbon dioxide gas; bubbles of gas are trapped in the dough, making it expand.
    B1 carbon dioxide produced; B1 gas trapped in dough.
  3. (c) It evaporates (because of the high temperature).
    B1.
Q19Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A factory in Swat makes apple juice.

  1. (a) Explain how the enzyme pectinase is used in the production of fruit juice. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pectinase breaks down pectin in plant cell walls; cells break open more easily, so more juice is released; the juice is clearer (less cloudy).
    B1 breaks down pectin; B1 cell walls; B1 more juice / clearer juice.
Q20Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the enzyme lactase used to produce?A biological washing powderB clear fruit juiceC lactose-free milkD ethanol for biofuel [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C lactose-free milk
    Lactase breaks down lactose into glucose and galactose, so the milk can be drunk by people who are lactose intolerant. Clear fruit juice is made using pectinase.
Q21Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Yeast is used to produce a biofuel from sugar cane. Which product of yeast respiration is used as the fuel?A carbon dioxideB ethanolC glucoseD lactic acid [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B ethanol
    Anaerobic respiration in yeast produces ethanol, which can be burned as a fuel. Lactic acid is made by anaerobic respiration in muscles, not yeast.
Q22Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

Equal samples of bread dough were placed in measuring cylinders at different temperatures. Each started at 50 cm³. The volume was read after 30 minutes.Temperature / °C : volume after 30 min / cm³20 : 6230 : 8440 : 9860 : 51

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate of increase in volume, in cm³ per minute, at each temperature. Give your answers to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the result at 60 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest how the student could find the optimum temperature more accurately. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20 °C: 0.40; 30 °C: 1.13; 40 °C: 1.60; 60 °C: 0.03
    (final − 50) ÷ 30 (M1); all four correct (A1): 0.40, 1.13, 1.60, 0.03.
  2. (b) Yeast enzymes are denatured (or yeast cells killed) at high temperature; so little or no respiration / carbon dioxide produced.
    B1 enzymes denatured; B1 little respiration / CO₂.
  3. (c) Repeat with smaller temperature intervals between 30 °C and 60 °C (e.g. 35, 40, 45, 50, 55 °C).
    B1.
Q23Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the enzymes in biological washing powders remove stains of curry and egg from clothes. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why biological washing powders are less effective in very hot water. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lipases break down fats / oils in curry into fatty acids and glycerol; proteases break down proteins in egg into amino acids; the products are small and soluble, so they wash away in water.
    B1 lipase / fats; B1 protease / protein; B1 soluble products washed away.
  2. (b) The enzymes are denatured at high temperatures; the shape of the active site changes so substrates no longer fit.
    B1 denatured; B1 active site changes shape / substrate does not fit.
Q24Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

Pieces of cloth stained with egg yolk were placed in a biological washing powder solution at different temperatures. The time for the stain to disappear was recorded.Temperature / °C : time / s20 : 60030 : 30040 : 16050 : 24060 : 900

  1. (a) The rate of stain removal can be calculated as 1 ÷ time. Calculate the rate at 40 °C, in s⁻¹. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) State the temperature at which the washing powder worked best and explain the result at 60 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one way of making the end point of this experiment easier to judge. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.00625 s⁻¹
    1 ÷ 160 = 0.00625 (M1) → 0.00625 s⁻¹ (A1).
  2. (b) 40 °C; at 60 °C the enzymes are (partly) denatured so the stain takes much longer to remove.
    B1 40 °C; B1 denatured at 60 °C.
  3. (c) Compare each cloth with a clean, unstained piece of the same cloth / use a light sensor or colorimeter / place cloth against a white background.
    B1.
Q25Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why each of the following is controlled in an industrial fermenter producing insulin from GM bacteria:(i) temperature(ii) pH(iii) oxygen supply(iv) nutrient supply [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the fermenter is cleaned with steam before use. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) enzymes work best at an optimum temperature / too high denatures them; (ii) enzymes have an optimum pH; (iii) for aerobic respiration of the microorganisms; (iv) provides glucose for respiration and raw materials (e.g. amino acids / nitrogen) for growth and product.
    B1 each.
  2. (b) To kill microorganisms (aseptic conditions); contaminating microorganisms would compete with the GM bacteria / reduce yield / contaminate the product.
    B1 kills microorganisms / sterilises; B1 contamination would reduce yield or spoil product.
Q26Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 6 style · 7 marks

Samples of 100 g of apple pulp were mixed with different volumes of pectinase and left at 40 °C for 20 minutes, then filtered.Pectinase / cm³ : volume of juice / cm³0.0 : 121.0 : 212.0 : 27, 29, 18, 283.0 : 304.0 : 30.5

  1. (a) Identify the anomalous result for 2.0 cm³ of pectinase and calculate the mean volume of juice without it. [2]
  2. (b) Using your mean, calculate the percentage increase in juice volume with 2.0 cm³ of pectinase compared with no pectinase. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the volume of juice hardly increases above 3.0 cm³ of pectinase. [1]
  4. (d) State two variables that should be kept constant. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18 cm³; mean = 28 cm³
    anomaly 18 (B1); (27 + 29 + 28) ÷ 3 = 28 (B1).
  2. (b) 133 %
    (28 − 12) ÷ 12 × 100 (M1) = 133.3 → 133 % (A1).
  3. (c) All the pectin has already been broken down / another factor (amount of pulp or time) is now limiting.
    B1.
  4. (d) Any two of: mass of apple pulp; temperature; time; type of apple; total volume (add water to make volumes equal); filter paper / funnel type.
    1 mark each.
Q27Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Penicillin is made by a fungus grown in a fermenter.Day : penicillin concentration / g per dm³0 : 02 : 0.54 : 4.16 : 8.98 : 10.2

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate of penicillin production between day 2 and day 6, in g/dm³ per day. [2]
  2. (b) The fermenter has a water jacket through which cold water is pumped. Explain why. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the production rate between day 6 and day 8 is lower than between day 2 and day 6. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.1 g/dm³ per day
    (8.9 − 0.5) ÷ (6 − 2) (M1) = 2.1 (A1).
  2. (b) The fungus respires and releases heat energy; cooling stops the temperature rising above the optimum, which would denature enzymes / kill the fungus.
    B1 respiration releases heat; B1 prevents temperature rising / denaturing enzymes.
  3. (c) Nutrients (e.g. glucose) run low; waste products build up / pH changes; so fewer cells are active.
    B1 nutrients used up; B1 waste builds up.
Q28Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how the concentration of sugar affects the rate of carbon dioxide production by yeast. Include apparatus, method, variables to control, how results are recorded and how a conclusion is reached. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Yeast suspension + sugar solutions of different concentrations; collect CO₂ in a gas syringe / inverted measuring cylinder or count bubbles for a fixed time; constant temperature water-bath; repeats; calculate rate.
    1 mark each, max 6: at least five sugar concentrations (e.g. 0, 2, 4, 6, 8 %); same volume and concentration of yeast suspension; same volume of sugar solution; water-bath at a fixed temperature (e.g. 35 °C, thermostatically controlled); allow time to equilibrate; measure CO₂ volume in a gas syringe (or count bubbles / measure balloon) over a fixed time; repeat three times and calculate mean; rate = volume ÷ time; table of concentration against volume of CO₂; graph and describe trend for conclusion.
Q29Biotechnology, yeast, enzymes & fermenters · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A farm grows 65 tonnes of sugar cane per hectare on 12 hectares. Each tonne of cane can yield 72 dm³ of ethanol.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of ethanol that could be produced from the farm's sugar cane. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how the sugar from cane is converted into ethanol. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest one disadvantage of using large areas of land to grow sugar cane for biofuel. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 56160 dm³
    65 × 12 = 780 tonnes (M1); 780 × 72 = 56160 dm³ (A1).
  2. (b) Sugar solution is mixed with yeast in a fermenter without oxygen (anaerobic); yeast respires anaerobically to produce ethanol (and carbon dioxide); ethanol is separated (distilled).
    B1 yeast; B1 anaerobic respiration / fermentation produces ethanol.
  3. (c) Less land for food crops / higher food prices / habitat destruction and loss of biodiversity / heavy water use.
    B1.

What is the main cause of this rise?A The stirrer rubs against the walls.B The microorganisms release heat energy during respiration.C Steam used to sterilise the fermenter stays inside.D The nutrient solution reacts with the oxygen supplied.
