Q1Formulae & equations (incl. ionic) · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is the formula of aluminium sulfate? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D Al₂(SO₄)₃
    Al³⁺ and SO₄²⁻: two Al³⁺ (+6) balance three SO₄²⁻ (−6), so Al₂(SO₄)₃. B ignores the charges.

Use these ions: Ca²⁺, Fe³⁺, Mg²⁺, NH₄⁺, OH⁻, O²⁻, CO₃²⁻, N³⁻.

  1. (a) Write the formula of each compound.(i) calcium hydroxide(ii) iron(III) oxide(iii) ammonium carbonate(iv) magnesium nitride [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Ca(OH)₂ (ii) Fe₂O₃ (iii) (NH₄)₂CO₃ (iv) Mg₃N₂
    B1 each.
Q3Formulae & equations (incl. ionic) · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) How many atoms are shown in the formula of calcium nitrate, Ca(NO₃)₂? [1]
  2. (b) How many atoms are shown in the formula of hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·5H₂O? [1]
  3. (c) How many different elements are in ammonium phosphate, (NH₄)₃PO₄? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9
    B1 1 Ca + 2 N + 6 O
  2. (b) 21
    B1 1 + 1 + 4 + 5 × 3
  3. (c) 4 (N, H, P, O)
    B1
Q4Formulae & equations (incl. ionic) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Aqueous silver nitrate is added to aqueous sodium chloride. A white precipitate forms. Which is the ionic equation for this reaction? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A Ag⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq) → AgCl(s)
    The precipitate is silver chloride; Na⁺ and NO₃⁻ are spectator ions. C is the full (not ionic) equation; B shows a soluble salt as solid.
Q5Formulae & equations (incl. ionic) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Balance this equation for the reduction of iron(III) oxide:Fe₂O₃ + CO → Fe + CO₂ [1]
  2. (b) Balance this equation for the complete combustion of propane, the main fuel in LPG cylinders:C₃H₈ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O [1]
  3. (c) Balance this equation for aluminium burning in oxygen:Al + O₂ → Al₂O₃ [1]
  4. (d) Balance this equation and add state symbols for sodium reacting with water:Na + H₂O → NaOH + H₂ [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
    B1
  2. (b) C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O
    B1
  3. (c) 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃
    B1
  4. (d) 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)
    B1 balancing; B1 all state symbols.
Q6Formulae & equations (incl. ionic) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Magnesium ribbon reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid to form aqueous magnesium chloride and hydrogen gas.

  1. (a) Write the balanced symbol equation, including state symbols, for this reaction. [2]
  2. (b) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. [2]
  3. (c) Name the spectator ion in this reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)
    B1 formulae and balancing; B1 state symbols.
  2. (b) Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g)
    B1 species; B1 balanced (charge and atoms).
  3. (c) Chloride ion, Cl⁻
    B1
Q7Formulae & equations (incl. ionic) · Standard · Paper 6 style · 5 marks

A student heats green copper(II) carbonate in a hard-glass test tube. The gas given off is bubbled through limewater.

  1. (a) Describe two observations the student makes. [2]
  2. (b) Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the decomposition. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the delivery tube must be removed from the limewater before heating stops. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Green solid turns black; bubbles / gas given off (limewater turns milky).
    B1 green to black; B1 limewater milky / gas.
  2. (b) CuCO₃(s) → CuO(s) + CO₂(g)
    B1 equation; B1 state symbols.
  3. (c) To stop limewater being sucked back into the hot tube (suck-back), which could crack it.
    B1
Q8Formulae & equations (incl. ionic) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Aqueous sodium hydroxide is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate. A light blue precipitate forms.

  1. (a) Write the full balanced equation, with state symbols, for this reaction. [2]
  2. (b) Write the ionic equation for the formation of the blue precipitate. [2]
  3. (c) Write the ionic equation for the neutralisation of any acid by any alkali. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why sodium ions and sulfate ions do not appear in the ionic equation in (b). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CuSO₄(aq) + 2NaOH(aq) → Cu(OH)₂(s) + Na₂SO₄(aq)
    B1 formulae and balancing; B1 state symbols.
  2. (b) Cu²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Cu(OH)₂(s)
    B1 species; B1 balanced with state symbols.
  3. (c) H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l)
    B1
  4. (d) They are spectator ions: they are in solution before and after the reaction and are not changed.
    B1

Q12Moles & reacting masses · Standard · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

In a lime kiln, calcium carbonate is heated strongly:CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂[Ar: C = 12, O = 16, Ca = 40]

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of calcium oxide that can be made from 50 kg of pure calcium carbonate. [3]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide released at the same time. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 28 kg
    M1 Mr CaCO₃ 100, CaO 56; M1 moles CaCO₃ = 50 000 ÷ 100 = 500 mol (1:1); A1 500 × 56 = 28 000 g = 28 kg
  2. (b) 22 kg
    B1 50 − 28 or 500 × 44 g
Q13Moles & reacting masses · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is the maximum mass of iron that can be made from 320 g of iron(III) oxide?Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂[Ar: O = 16, Fe = 56] [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C 224 g
    Mr Fe₂O₃ = 160, so 320 g = 2.0 mol; 2 mol Fe₂O₃ → 4 mol Fe = 4 × 56 = 224 g. A forgets the 1:2 ratio.
Q14Moles & reacting masses · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

4.8 g of magnesium is added to dilute hydrochloric acid containing 0.30 mol of HCl.Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂[Ar: Mg = 24, Cl = 35.5]

  1. (a) Show by calculation which reactant is in excess. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of magnesium chloride formed. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of magnesium left unreacted. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mg: 4.8 ÷ 24 = 0.20 mol needs 0.40 mol HCl; only 0.30 mol HCl, so magnesium is in excess.
    M1 moles Mg = 0.20; A1 Mg in excess with reason.
  2. (b) 14.25 g (14.3 g)
    M1 moles MgCl₂ = 0.30 ÷ 2 = 0.15; A1 0.15 × 95 = 14.25 g
  3. (c) 1.2 g
    B1 (0.20 − 0.15) × 24
Q15Moles & reacting masses · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A student heated copper(II) carbonate in a crucible until there was no further change in mass.Mass of empty crucible = 18.62 gMass of crucible + copper(II) carbonate = 21.10 gMass of crucible + solid after heating = 20.22 g[Ar: C = 12, O = 16, Cu = 64]

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of copper(II) carbonate used and the mass of copper(II) oxide formed. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the number of moles of each substance. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how the results support the equation CuCO₃ → CuO + CO₂. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the crucible was heated, cooled and weighed repeatedly until the mass stayed the same. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CuCO₃ 2.48 g; CuO 1.60 g
    B1 each.
  2. (b) CuCO₃: 2.48 ÷ 124 = 0.020 mol; CuO: 1.60 ÷ 80 = 0.020 mol
    B1 each (Mr 124 and 80).
  3. (c) The mole ratio CuCO₃ : CuO is 1 : 1, as in the equation.
    B1
  4. (d) To make sure all the copper(II) carbonate had decomposed (reaction complete).
    B1
Q16Empirical & molecular formulae · Core · Paper 4 style · 3 marks

A sample of a hydrocarbon contains 2.4 g of carbon and 0.60 g of hydrogen. [Ar: H = 1, C = 12]

  1. (a) Calculate the empirical formula of the hydrocarbon. [2]
  2. (b) The relative molecular mass is 30. Deduce the molecular formula. [1]
