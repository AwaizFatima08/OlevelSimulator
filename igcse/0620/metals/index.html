Q1Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What is the name of the main ore of aluminium?A bauxiteB cryoliteC hematiteD limestone [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A bauxite
    Aluminium is extracted from bauxite (impure aluminium oxide). Cryolite is the solvent used in the electrolysis; hematite is an iron ore.
Q2Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Name the three solid raw materials added at the top of the blast furnace. [2]
  2. (b) Write equations for: (i) carbon burning in hot air to form carbon dioxide; (ii) carbon dioxide reacting with more carbon to form carbon monoxide; (iii) carbon monoxide reducing iron(III) oxide. [3]
  3. (c) State why the reaction in (i) is important for the working of the furnace. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Hematite (iron ore), coke (carbon), limestone.
    2 marks all three; 1 mark for two.
  2. (b) (i) C + O₂ → CO₂ (ii) CO₂ + C → 2CO (iii) Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
    B1 each.
  3. (c) It is exothermic and heats the furnace.
    B1
Q3Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Core · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Five metals: aluminium, copper, gold, iron, potassium.

  1. (a) Which metal is found uncombined (native) in the ground? [1]
  2. (b) Which two metals must be extracted by electrolysis of their molten compounds? [2]
  3. (c) Which two metals can be extracted by heating their oxides with carbon? [2]
  4. (d) State the link between the position of a metal in the reactivity series and its method of extraction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gold
    B1
  2. (b) Potassium and aluminium
    B1 each.
  3. (c) Iron and copper (copper also from its sulfide ores)
    B1 each.
  4. (d) The more reactive the metal, the harder it is to extract; metals above carbon need electrolysis.
    B1
Q4Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Why is limestone added to the blast furnace?A It reduces iron(III) oxide to iron.B It decomposes to calcium oxide, which reacts with silicon dioxide impurities to form slag.C It burns to provide heat for the furnace.D It lowers the melting point of the iron ore. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B It decomposes to calcium oxide, which reacts with silicon dioxide impurities to form slag.
    CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂; CaO (basic) + SiO₂ (acidic) → CaSiO₃ (slag). Carbon monoxide, not limestone, reduces the ore (A).
Q5Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why aluminium cannot be extracted by heating aluminium oxide with carbon. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbon anodes have to be replaced regularly. [2]
  4. (d) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the cathode. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot remove the oxygen from aluminium oxide.
    B1
  2. (b) This lowers the operating temperature (aluminium oxide has a very high melting point), saving energy, and lets the ions move.
    B1 lowers temperature/ melting point; B1 saves energy/cost.
  3. (c) Oxygen is formed at the anode; at the high temperature it reacts with the carbon to form carbon dioxide, so the anodes burn away.
    B1 oxygen produced at anode; B1 reacts with carbon to form CO₂.
  4. (d) Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al
    B1

A steel works near Karachi uses an iron ore that contains 80 % iron(III) oxide by mass; the rest is mainly silicon dioxide. [Ar: O = 16, Fe = 56]

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of iron(III) oxide in 1000 tonnes of the ore. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be produced from 1000 tonnes of the ore.Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ [2]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the formation of slag. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 800 tonnes
    B1 80 % of 1000
  2. (b) 560 tonnes
    M1 Mr Fe₂O₃ = 160, contains 112 of Fe; A1 800 × 112 ÷ 160 = 560 t
  3. (c) CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃
    B1
Q7Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Standard · Paper 6 style · 7 marks

A student reduces 3.20 g of copper(II) oxide by heating it in a horizontal glass tube while a stream of hydrogen passes over it. Excess hydrogen is burned at a small hole at the far end of the tube. [Ar: O = 16, Cu = 64]

  1. (a) Describe the colour change seen in the tube. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why hydrogen is passed through the tube for a while before heating starts. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why hydrogen is kept flowing while the tube cools. [1]
  4. (d) Calculate the number of moles of copper(II) oxide used and the mass of copper expected. [2]
  5. (e) Write the equation for the reaction and state which substance is reduced. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Black solid turns pink-brown.
    B1
  2. (b) To push out all the air; a mixture of hydrogen and air could explode when heated.
    B1
  3. (c) To stop the hot copper reacting with oxygen in the air and turning back into copper(II) oxide.
    B1
  4. (d) 0.040 mol; 2.56 g
    M1 3.20 ÷ 80; A1 0.040 × 64 = 2.56 g
  5. (e) CuO + H₂ → Cu + H₂O; copper(II) oxide is reduced.
    B1 equation; B1 CuO reduced.
Q8Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Iron from the blast furnace contains about 4 % carbon. State one property of this iron that makes it unsuitable for most uses. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how this iron is converted into steel. Include the role of oxygen and of calcium oxide. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is brittle.
    B1
  2. (b) Oxygen is blown into the molten iron; it oxidises carbon to carbon monoxide/carbon dioxide gas which escapes; it also oxidises impurities such as silicon and phosphorus to acidic oxides; calcium oxide (a basic oxide) is added and reacts with these acidic oxides to form slag, which is removed.
    B1 oxygen blown into molten iron; B1 carbon removed as CO/CO₂; B1 impurities form acidic oxides; B1 CaO neutralises them to form slag.
Q9Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite. [Ar: O = 16, Al = 27]

  1. (a) Write the ionic half-equation for the reaction at the anode. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the maximum mass of aluminium that can be obtained from 51 tonnes of aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the process is described as a redox reaction. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why aluminium smelters are often built near hydroelectric power stations. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻
    B1
  2. (b) 27 tonnes
    M1 Mr Al₂O₃ = 102, Al = 54 per formula; A1 51 × 54 ÷ 102 = 27 t
  3. (c) Aluminium ions gain electrons (reduction) at the cathode; oxide ions lose electrons (oxidation) at the anode.
    B1 reduction at cathode; B1 oxidation at anode.
  4. (d) Electrolysis needs very large amounts of electricity; hydroelectric power is cheap (and renewable).
    B1
Q10Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State the oxidation number of iron in Fe₂O₃ and in Fe. [2]
  2. (b) State the oxidation number of carbon in CO and in CO₂. [2]
  3. (c) Use your answers to identify the oxidising agent and the reducing agent in the reaction Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) +3 in Fe₂O₃; 0 in Fe
    B1 each.
  2. (b) +2 in CO; +4 in CO₂
    B1 each.
  3. (c) Fe₂O₃ is the oxidising agent (iron's oxidation number decreases; it is reduced); CO is the reducing agent (carbon's oxidation number increases; it is oxidised).
    B1 Fe₂O₃ oxidising agent with reason; B1 CO reducing agent with reason.

[Ar: C = 12, Al = 27]

Q12Extraction (blast furnace, aluminium) · Challenge · Paper 5 style · 6 marks

A student heated a mixture of each metal oxide with powdered carbon in a crucible for ten minutes.Copper(II) oxide: pink-brown solid formedLead(II) oxide: silvery beads of metal formedMagnesium oxide: no changeAluminium oxide: no change[Ar: O = 16, Pb = 207]

  1. (a) Use the results to place carbon in the reactivity series relative to copper, lead, magnesium and aluminium. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of lead that could be obtained from 2.23 g of lead(II) oxide, PbO. [2]
  3. (c) Write the equation for the reaction of lead(II) oxide with carbon, forming carbon dioxide. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest why aluminium oxide gave no reaction with carbon. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Order (most reactive first): magnesium/aluminium > carbon > lead > copper
    B1 carbon above lead and copper; B1 carbon below magnesium and aluminium.
  2. (b) 2.07 g

  3. (c) 2PbO + C → 2Pb + CO₂
    B1
  4. (d) Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide.
    B1
Q13Reactivity series & displacement · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) A metal reacts vigorously with cold water, floats and moves about on the surface, and gives off hydrogen. Which metal could it be?A copperB ironC sodiumD zinc [1]

Q17Reactivity series & displacement · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

Equal masses of four metal powders were added separately to 50 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. The volume of hydrogen collected in a gas syringe in the first 60 seconds was recorded.Magnesium: 48 cm³Zinc: 21 cm³Iron: 6 cm³Copper: 0 cm³

  1. (a) Calculate the average rate of gas production, in cm³/s, for magnesium, zinc and iron. [2]
  2. (b) State the order of reactivity shown by the results. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why copper gave no gas. [1]
  4. (d) State two variables that must be controlled. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Mg 0.80 cm³/s; Zn 0.35 cm³/s; Fe 0.10 cm³/s
    2 marks all three; 1 mark two.
  2. (b) Magnesium > zinc > iron > copper
    B1
  3. (c) Copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series, so it cannot displace hydrogen from the acid.
    B1
  4. (d) Mass/surface area of metal; volume and concentration of acid; temperature.
    1 mark each for any two.

Q19Reactivity series & displacement · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A metal M forms M²⁺ ions. M displaces iron from aqueous iron(II) sulfate but does not react with aqueous zinc sulfate.

  1. (a) Write the ionic equation for the reaction of M with iron(II) ions. [2]
  2. (b) Place M in the reactivity series relative to zinc, iron and copper. [1]
  3. (c) Predict whether M would react when heated with copper(II) oxide. Explain. [2]
  4. (d) Predict whether the oxide of M could be reduced by heating it with carbon. Explain. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) M(s) + Fe²⁺(aq) → M²⁺(aq) + Fe(s)
    B1 species; B1 balanced.
  2. (b) Zinc > M > iron > copper
    B1
  3. (c) Yes; M is more reactive than copper so it removes oxygen from copper(II) oxide.
    B1 yes; B1 reason.
  4. (d) Yes; M is below zinc (which can be extracted with carbon), so M is less reactive than carbon.
    B1

Q21Corrosion & rust prevention · Standard · Paper 6 style · 6 marks

Four identical iron nails were set up for one week:Tube A: nail in tap water, open to air. Mass increase 0.12 g.Tube B: nail in boiled water with a layer of oil on top. Mass increase 0.00 g.Tube C: nail in air with anhydrous calcium chloride, stoppered. Mass increase 0.00 g.Tube D: nail in salt water, open to air. Mass increase 0.21 g.

  1. (a) In which tubes did the nails rust? [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the nail in tube B did not rust. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the nail in tube C did not rust. [1]
  4. (d) Calculate how many times greater the mass increase was in tube D than in tube A. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [1]
  5. (e) Suggest why car bodies rust faster in coastal cities such as Karachi. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tubes A and D
    B1
  2. (b) Boiling removes dissolved oxygen (air); the oil layer stops oxygen dissolving back in.
    B1 boiling removes oxygen; B1 oil stops air.
  3. (c) Anhydrous calcium chloride absorbs water vapour, so the air is dry.
    B1
  4. (d) 1.8 times
    B1 0.21 ÷ 0.12 = 1.75 → 1.8
  5. (e) Salt (sodium chloride) from sea spray speeds up rusting.
    B1
Q22Corrosion & rust prevention · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how painting a steel gate prevents rusting. [1]
  2. (b) Name the metal used to galvanise steel roof sheets. [1]
  3. (c) A galvanised roof sheet is scratched, exposing the steel. Explain why the steel still does not rust. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why a scratched painted gate does rust. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It forms a barrier that keeps water and oxygen away from the iron.
    B1
  2. (b) Zinc
    B1
  3. (c) Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it corrodes/is oxidised instead of the iron (sacrificial protection).
    B1 zinc more reactive; B1 zinc oxidised in preference to iron.
  4. (d) The paint is only a barrier; once broken, water and oxygen reach the iron.
    B1
Q23Corrosion & rust prevention · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Blocks of zinc are bolted to the steel hulls of ships in Karachi port, below the waterline.

  1. (a) Name the type of protection given by the blocks. [1]
  2. (b) Write the half-equation for the reaction of the zinc blocks and explain why it protects the steel. [2]
  3. (c) An engineer suggests using copper blocks instead. Explain why this would not work. [2]
  4. (d) State the chemical name of rust. [1]
  5. (e) Explain why the zinc blocks must be replaced from time to time. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sacrificial protection
    B1
  2. (b) Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻; zinc loses electrons more easily than iron, so it is oxidised instead of the iron.
    B1 equation; B1 explanation.
  3. (c) Copper is less reactive than iron, so the iron would be oxidised instead (rusting faster).
    B1 copper less reactive; B1 iron corrodes.
  4. (d) Hydrated iron(III) oxide
    B1
  5. (e) They are used up as the zinc corrodes.
    B1

Show answer
  1. (a) P 1350 g; Q 4450 g; R 3950 g; S 2250 g
    2 marks all four; 1 mark two or three (mass = density × volume).

    B1 P; B1 reason using data.

Q28Alloys · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define an alloy. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why an alloy is usually harder and stronger than the pure metal it is made from. [3]
  3. (c) Give one use of brass and the property it depends on. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A mixture of a metal with other elements (metals or non-metals).
    B1
  2. (b) In the pure metal, atoms are the same size and arranged in layers that slide easily; in the alloy, atoms of different sizes disrupt the layers; so the layers cannot slide over each other easily.
    B1 pure metal layers slide; B1 different-sized atoms in alloy; B1 layers can no longer slide easily.
