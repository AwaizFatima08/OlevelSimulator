Q1Bond-energy calculations · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

At the high temperatures inside a car engine, nitrogen reacts with oxygen: N≡N + O=O → 2 N=OBond energies in kJ/mol: N≡N 945, O=O 498, N=O 630

  1. (a) Calculate the energy needed to break the bonds in the reactants. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the energy released when the bonds in the products form. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the energy change, ΔH, for the reaction and state whether it is exothermic or endothermic. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1443 kJ
    B1 945 + 498
  2. (b) 1260 kJ
    B1 2 × 630
  3. (c) ΔH = +183 kJ/mol; endothermic
    M1 1443 − 1260; A1 +183 kJ/mol with endothermic (more energy taken in to break bonds than released making bonds).
Q2Bond-energy calculations · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

In the Haber process: N≡N + 3 H–H ⇌ 2NH₃ (each NH₃ has three N–H bonds)Bond energies in kJ/mol: N≡N 945, H–H 436, N–H 391

  1. (a) Calculate the energy change, ΔH, for the reaction in kJ/mol. [3]
  2. (b) State the sign of ΔH for the reverse reaction, the decomposition of ammonia. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) −93 kJ/mol
    M1 broken: 945 + 3 × 436 = 2253; M1 formed: 6 × 391 = 2346; A1 2253 − 2346 = −93
  2. (b) Positive (+93 kJ/mol, endothermic).
    B1
Q3Bond-energy calculations · Core · Paper 2 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement about bond energies is correct?A Breaking bonds releases energy and making bonds takes in energy.B Breaking bonds takes in energy and making bonds releases energy.C Both breaking and making bonds release energy.D Both breaking and making bonds take in energy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Breaking bonds takes in energy and making bonds releases energy.
    Energy must be supplied to pull atoms apart (endothermic); energy is released when bonds form (exothermic). A is the common reversal.

Show answer
  1. (a) −250 kJ/mol
    B1 1850 − 2100
  2. (b) Exothermic; more energy is released in making bonds than is taken in to break bonds (ΔH negative).
    B1 exothermic; B1 explanation.
  3. (c) It rises.
    B1

  2. (b) Calculate the total energy released when the bonds in the products form. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the energy change for the reaction, in kJ/mol. [1]
  4. (d) Explain, in terms of bond breaking and bond making, why this reaction is exothermic. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2648 kJ
    B1 4 × 413 + 2 × 498
  2. (b) 3466 kJ
    B1 2 × 805 + 4 × 464
  3. (c) −818 kJ/mol
    B1 2648 − 3466

Q15Energy profiles & activation energy · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define activation energy, Ea. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the energy profile for an endothermic reaction. Say where the reactants and products are, and show how Ea and ΔH are measured. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The minimum energy that colliding particles must have in order to react.
    B1
  2. (b) Products higher than reactants; curve rises to a peak above both; Ea = height from reactants to peak; ΔH = difference from reactants to products (upward, positive).
    B1 products above reactants; B1 Ea from reactants to peak; B1 ΔH reactants to products.

Q28Exothermic & endothermic · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A physiotherapist at a cricket match uses an instant cold pack. Squeezing it lets ammonium nitrate dissolve in water. Explain how this cools the injury. [2]
  2. (b) A hand warmer contains iron powder that reacts with oxygen from the air. State whether this reaction is exothermic or endothermic, and describe the energy transfer. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the iron in the hand warmer is a fine powder rather than a lump. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Dissolving is endothermic; it takes in thermal energy from the surroundings (the injured skin), lowering its temperature.
    B1 endothermic; B1 energy taken from surroundings/skin.
  2. (b) Exothermic; thermal energy is transferred from the reacting chemicals to the surroundings (hands).
    B1 exothermic; B1 energy to surroundings.
  3. (c) Larger surface area so it reacts faster and releases heat quickly.
    B1

Excess zinc powder was added to 50 cm³ of aqueous copper(II) sulfate in an insulated cup at time 0. The temperature was recorded every 30 seconds.Time/min: 0.0, 0.5, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0, 3.5Temperature/°C: 21.0, 27.5, 32.0, 34.0, 34.5, 34.0, 33.5, 33.0

  1. (a) Calculate the maximum temperature rise. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the temperature falls after 2.0 minutes. [1]
  3. (c) Write the ionic equation for the reaction. [1]
  4. (d) The experiment is repeated with magnesium powder instead of zinc, using the same number of moles of metal. Predict how the maximum temperature rise would compare. Explain. [2]
  5. (e) Suggest why zinc was used in excess. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 13.5 °C
    B1 34.5 − 21.0
  2. (b) The reaction has finished and heat is lost to the surroundings.
    B1
  3. (c) Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s)
    B1
  4. (d) Larger rise; magnesium is further above copper in the reactivity series, so more energy is released.
    B1 larger; B1 magnesium more reactive.

    B1
