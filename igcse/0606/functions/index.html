Q1Graphs of a function and its inverse · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) The function f is defined by f(x) = 2x + 5 for x ∈ ℝ. The graph of y = f(x) passes through (1, 7) and (−2, 1). Write down the coordinates of two points on the graph of y = f⁻¹(x). [2]
  2. (b) Find f⁻¹(x). [2]
  3. (c) Describe the geometrical relationship between the graphs of y = f(x) and y = f⁻¹(x). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (7, 1) and (1, −2)
    B1 B1 (swap coordinates)
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = (x − 5)/2
    M1 x = 2y + 5; A1
  3. (c) Each is the reflection of the other in the line y = x
    B1
Q2Graphs of a function and its inverse · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = eˣ + 2 for x ∈ ℝ. State the range of f. [1]
  2. (b) Find f⁻¹(x) and state its domain. [2]
  3. (c) The graph of y = f(x) crosses the y-axis at P. Write down the coordinates of the point where the graph of y = f⁻¹(x) crosses the x-axis, and the equation of the asymptote of y = f⁻¹(x). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) f(x) > 2
    B1
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = ln(x − 2), x > 2
    M1 x − 2 = e^y; A1 with domain
  3. (c) (3, 0); x = 2
    B1 P = (0, 3) reflects to (3, 0); B1 x = 2
Q3Graphs of a function and its inverse · Core · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) g(x) = ln(2x − 1) for x > ½. Find the coordinates of the point where y = g(x) crosses the x-axis, and state the equation of its asymptote. [2]
  2. (b) Find g⁻¹(x). [2]
  3. (c) Write down the coordinates of the point where y = g⁻¹(x) crosses the y-axis and the equation of its asymptote. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (1, 0); x = ½
    B1 B1
  2. (b) g⁻¹(x) = (eˣ + 1)/2
    M1 2y − 1 = eˣ; A1
  3. (c) (0, 1); y = ½
    B1 B1
Q4Graphs of a function and its inverse · Core · Paper 2 style · calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = √(x + 4) for x ≥ −4. State the range of f. [1]
  2. (b) Find f⁻¹(x) and state its domain. [2]
  3. (c) The graph of y = f(x) passes through (−4, 0), (0, 2) and (5, 3). Write down three points on the graph of y = f⁻¹(x), and find f⁻¹(3). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) f(x) ≥ 0
    B1
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = x² − 4, x ≥ 0
    M1; A1
  3. (c) (0, −4), (2, 0), (3, 5); f⁻¹(3) = 5
    B1; B1
Q5Graphs of a function and its inverse · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = (x − 2)² + 1 for x ≥ 2. Find f⁻¹(x) and state its domain. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact coordinates of the point where the graphs of y = f(x) and y = f⁻¹(x) meet. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) f⁻¹(x) = 2 + √(x − 1), x ≥ 1
    M1 x − 1 = (y − 2)²; A1 positive root since y ≥ 2; B1 x ≥ 1
  2. (b) ((5 + √5)/2, (5 + √5)/2)
    M1 they meet on y = x: (x − 2)² + 1 = x; M1 x² − 5x + 5 = 0; A1 x = (5 ± √5)/2; A1 reject (5 − √5)/2 < 2
Q6Graphs of a function and its inverse · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = 4 − e^(2x) for x ∈ ℝ. State the range of f and find the exact coordinates of the points where y = f(x) meets the axes. [3]
  2. (b) Find f⁻¹(x), stating its domain. [2]
  3. (c) Write down the coordinates of the points where y = f⁻¹(x) meets the axes and the equation of its asymptote. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) f(x) < 4; (0, 3) and (ln 2, 0)
    B1; B1 (0, 3); B1 e^(2x) = 4 ⇒ x = ½ ln 4 = ln 2
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = ½ ln(4 − x), x < 4
    M1; A1
  3. (c) (3, 0) and (0, ln 2); x = 4
    B1 B1

  2. (b) Find h⁻¹(x). [2]
  3. (c) State the equations of the asymptotes of y = h(x) and y = h⁻¹(x). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (−2, 0) and (0, 2.20)
    B1 (−2, 0); M1 2 ln 3; A1 2.20
  2. (b) h⁻¹(x) = e^(x/2) − 3
    M1; A1
  3. (c) x = −3 and y = −3
    B1 B1
Q8Graphs of a function and its inverse · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = (2x + 1)/(x − 3) for x ≠ 3. Find f⁻¹(x), stating the value of x for which it is not defined. [3]
  2. (b) Write down the equations of the asymptotes of y = f(x) and of y = f⁻¹(x). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) f⁻¹(x) = (3x + 1)/(x − 2), x ≠ 2
    M1 xy − 3x = 2y + 1; M1 rearrange; A1
  2. (b) f: x = 3, y = 2; f⁻¹: x = 2, y = 3
    B1 B1
Q9Graphs of a function and its inverse · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = x² + 4x for x ≥ −2. Explain why f has an inverse, and find f⁻¹(x), stating its domain. [4]
  2. (b) Find f⁻¹(12). [1]
  3. (c) Find the coordinates of every point where the graphs of y = f(x) and y = f⁻¹(x) meet. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) f is one-one on x ≥ −2 (the vertex is at x = −2); f⁻¹(x) = −2 + √(x + 4), x ≥ −4
    B1 one-one reason; M1 (x + 2)² − 4; A1 positive root; B1 x ≥ −4
  2. (b) 2
    B1
  3. (c) (0, 0) only
    M1 x² + 4x = x ⇒ x = 0 or −3; A1 −3 rejected (not ≥ −2)
Q10Graphs of a function and its inverse · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = 1 + ln x for x > 0. Find f⁻¹(x). [2]
  2. (b) Show that the graphs of y = f(x) and y = f⁻¹(x) meet at (1, 1). [1]
  3. (c) Find the gradients of both graphs at (1, 1) and hence describe how the two graphs meet there. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) f⁻¹(x) = e^(x − 1)
    M1; A1
  2. (b) f(1) = 1 + 0 = 1 and f⁻¹(1) = e⁰ = 1
    B1
  3. (c) Both gradients are 1, so the graphs touch at (1, 1) with common tangent y = x
    M1 f′(x) = 1/x = 1; M1 (f⁻¹)′(x) = e^(x − 1) = 1; A1 touch, tangent y = x

Q14Composite functions · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = 1/(x − 2) for x ≠ 2. Show that ff(x) = (x − 2)/(5 − 2x) and state the value of x, other than 2, for which ff is not defined. [3]
  2. (b) Solve ff(x) = x, giving your answers in exact form. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) ff(x) = (x − 2)/(5 − 2x); x = 5/2
    M1 1/(1/(x − 2) − 2); A1 simplified; B1 x = 5/2
  2. (b) x = 1 ± √2
    M1 x − 2 = 5x − 2x²; M1 x² − 2x − 1 = 0; A1
Q15Composite functions · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = 2x + k and g(x) = x² − 3 for x ∈ ℝ, where k is a constant. Given that gf(1) = 6, find the possible values of k. [3]
  2. (b) For the positive value of k, find fg(x). [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 1 or k = −5
    M1 (2 + k)² − 3 = 6; M1 2 + k = ±3; A1
  2. (b) fg(x) = 2x² − 5
    B1 2(x² − 3) + 1

Q17Composite functions · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = x² + 2x for x ∈ ℝ and g(x) = eˣ − 1 for x ∈ ℝ. Show that fg(x) = e^(2x) − 1. [3]
  2. (b) State the range of fg. [1]
  3. (c) Solve fg(x) = 8, giving your answer in exact form. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (eˣ − 1)² + 2(eˣ − 1) = e^(2x) − 2eˣ + 1 + 2eˣ − 2 = e^(2x) − 1
    M1 substitute; M1 expand; A1
  2. (b) fg(x) > −1
    B1
  3. (c) x = ln 3
    M1 e^(2x) = 9; A1
Q18Composite functions · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 7 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = 3x + 2 for x ∈ ℝ and g(x) = (x + 4)/(x − 1) for x ≠ 1. Show that gg(x) = x and state what this tells you about g⁻¹. [3]
  2. (b) Find gf(x) and solve gf(x) = f(x), giving your answers correct to 3 significant figures. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) gg(x) = [(x + 4) + 4(x − 1)]/[(x + 4) − (x − 1)] = 5x/5 = x; so g⁻¹ = g (g is self-inverse)
    M1 substitute; A1 simplify; B1
  2. (b) gf(x) = (3x + 6)/(3x + 1); x = 0.412 or x = −1.08
    B1 gf(x); M1 3x + 6 = (3x + 2)(3x + 1); M1 9x² + 6x − 4 = 0; A1 x = (−1 ± √5)/3

Q22Inverse functions · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

Q30Domain and range · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) f(x) = √(x − 1) for x ≥ 1 and g(x) = 5 − x² for x ∈ ℝ. Find the set of values of x for which fg(x) is defined. [2]
  2. (b) Find the range of fg for these values of x. [2]
  3. (c) Find gf(x), simplifying your answer, and state the range of gf. [2]
Show answer
