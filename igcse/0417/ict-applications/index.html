Q1Monitoring, measurement and control · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A weather station in Gilgit records the air temperature and rainfall every 10 minutes. A greenhouse near Quetta keeps its temperature between 22 °C and 26 °C. Explain why the weather station is an example of a monitoring system and the greenhouse is an example of a control system. [2]
  2. (b) Name two sensors, other than temperature sensors, that could be used in the greenhouse. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The weather station only takes/records/displays readings from sensors and does not change anything. The greenhouse system uses the readings to switch devices (heater, fan, window motor) on or off through actuators to change the conditions.
    1 mark: monitoring = readings recorded/displayed, no action taken. 1 mark: control = computer uses readings to operate actuators/devices to change conditions.
  2. (b) Any two: humidity/moisture sensor; light sensor; soil moisture sensor; pH sensor; CO₂/gas sensor.
    1 mark each. Do not accept brand names or 'heat sensor'.

Q5Monitoring, measurement and control · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A pH sensor is connected to a computer in a water treatment plant. Explain why an analogue-to-digital converter (ADC) is needed. [2]
  2. (b) The computer opens a valve to add chemicals when the pH is too low. State why a digital-to-analogue converter (DAC) may be needed between the computer and the valve's motor. [1]
  3. (c) Name the type of device that physically opens the valve. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The sensor produces an analogue signal; the computer can only process digital data, so the ADC converts the signal to digital.

  2. (b) The motor/actuator needs an analogue signal, but the computer outputs digital signals.
    B1: actuator needs analogue signal / computer's digital output must be converted.
  3. (c) An actuator (e.g. a motor or solenoid).
    B1: actuator / motor / solenoid.

Q7Monitoring, measurement and control · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A hatchery in Punjab uses a microprocessor-controlled incubator to keep eggs at 37.5 °C. Describe how the microprocessor controls the temperature. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) A temperature sensor constantly measures the temperature; the analogue reading is converted to digital by an ADC; the microprocessor compares the reading with the preset value (37.5 °C) stored in memory; if it is lower, a signal is sent (through a DAC/actuator) to switch the heater on (and fan off); if it is higher, the heater is switched off/the fan is switched on; this process repeats continuously.
    1 mark each for any five: sensor measures temperature; ADC converts analogue to digital; compares with preset value; below → heater on; above → heater off/fan on; actuator/DAC used to operate device; process continuous loop.
Q8Monitoring, measurement and control · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) A water level/pressure sensor sends readings to the microprocessor, which keeps the inlet valve open until the preset level is reached, then closes it. A temperature sensor sends readings; the microprocessor compares them with 40 °C and switches the heater on if lower and off when 40 °C is reached; this repeats continuously.
    1 mark each for any four of: water level/pressure sensor used; microprocessor opens inlet valve / keeps it open while level below preset value; closes valve when level reached; temperature sensor used; reading compared with stored value 40 °C; heater switched on if below, off if at/above; ADC needed for sensor readings; process repeats / continuous loop.

The microprocessor receives these three temperature readings, one after another: 19.5 °C, 21.6 °C, 20.8 °C. The heater is off before the first reading. Describe what the microprocessor does after each reading. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the system uses a continuous loop of readings rather than one reading each hour. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 19.5 °C: below 21 °C, so it switches the heater on. 21.6 °C: above 21 °C, so it switches the heater off. 20.8 °C: below 21 °C, so it switches the heater on again.
    1 mark per reading. Each answer must link the comparison with the stored value 21 °C to the action (on / off / on).

    B1: temperature changes between readings; B1: quicker response / keeps temperature steady / avoids large swings.
Q10Monitoring, measurement and control · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A chemical factory in Sheikhupura must keep a reaction vessel at 70 °C with a pH between 6.5 and 7.0. Describe how a computer control system does this. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Temperature and pH sensors send readings via an ADC to the computer. The computer compares the temperature with 70 °C and switches a heater on or a cooling valve on as needed. It compares the pH with 6.5 and 7.0: if below 6.5 it opens a valve to add alkali; if above 7.0 it opens a valve to add acid. Actuators open/close the valves. The process repeats continuously.
    1 mark each for any five of: temperature sensor and pH sensor named; ADC converts readings; readings compared with stored values (70 °C, 6.5–7.0); heater/cooler switched on/off by computer; if pH < 6.5 alkali added; if pH > 7.0 acid added; actuators/valves operated by computer (DAC if needed); loop repeats continuously / until process stopped.

Q13Monitoring, measurement and control · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A busy crossroads in Rawalpindi has traffic lights controlled by a microprocessor. Describe how the sensors and microprocessor control the lights. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sensors (e.g. induction loops or cameras/infrared) detect vehicles waiting on each road. Q163Medical applications · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how 3D printing is used in tissue engineering to make an artificial blood vessel, and explain one benefit to patients. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) The patient is scanned (e.g. CT or MRI) to find the exact size and shape needed. A 3D model is designed in CAD software.
