Q1Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Write down the most suitable storage medium for each use.(i) Backing up all the data on a bank's servers every night and keeping the copies for ten years.(ii) Selling a high-definition feature film in shops.(iii) Carrying homework files between home and school.(iv) Storing the operating system in a thin, light laptop. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Magnetic tape(ii) Blu-ray disc(iii) USB flash drive / pen drive (accept memory card)(iv) Solid-state drive (SSD)
    1 mark each. Do not accept brand names.
Q2Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how data is stored on a magnetic hard disk drive. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The disk has platters coated in a magnetic material; the platters spin at high speed; a read/write head moves across the surface on an arm; the head magnetises tiny areas in one of two directions to represent 1s and 0s; data is stored in concentric tracks/sectors.
    1 mark per point, max 3.
Q3Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by direct access to data. [1]
  2. (b) State what is meant by serial access to data. [1]
  3. (c) Give one example of a storage medium that uses each type of access. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Data can be read straight from where it is stored without reading through all the data before it.
    1 mark.
  2. (b) Data is read in the order it was stored, starting from the beginning, until the required item is found.
    1 mark.
  3. (c) Direct: hard disk/SSD/DVD/Blu-ray/flash drive. Serial: magnetic tape.
    1 mark each.

Q9Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks

Q11Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Bilal is a university student who carries his laptop to lectures every day. He is choosing between a laptop with a magnetic hard disk drive (HDD) and one with a solid-state drive (SSD) of the same capacity. Compare HDDs and SSDs for Bilal's laptop. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both are non-volatile backing storage that store the OS and files with direct access. An SSD has no moving parts whereas an HDD has spinning platters and a moving head, so the SSD is less likely to be damaged if the laptop is dropped while carried; an SSD reads and writes data faster so the laptop starts up and loads files quicker; an SSD uses less power so the battery lasts longer; an SSD is lighter and thinner; an SSD is silent and runs cooler; an HDD is cheaper per gigabyte, so more storage for the same price; an HDD can be rewritten more times/has a longer lifespan for frequent writes.
    1 mark per comparison point, max 6. At least one similarity for full marks. Points must be linked to both devices.
Q12Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how data is read from an optical disc such as a DVD. [3]
  2. (b) State which colour of laser is used to read a DVD. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The disc spins; a laser beam is shone onto the surface; the surface has pits and lands (in a spiral track); light reflects differently from pits and lands (or at the change between them); a sensor/light detector reads the reflections and converts them into 1s and 0s.
    1 mark per point, max 3.
  2. (b) Red laser.
    1 mark.
Q13Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A large insurance company in Karachi backs up its database onto magnetic tape every night. Explain two reasons why magnetic tape is used for this. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why magnetic tape is not suitable for looking up one customer's record while the customer waits on the telephone. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two explained, e.g. very large capacity so the whole database fits on one/few tapes; low cost per gigabyte; tapes are robust and can be stored off-site for many years; fast to write large amounts of data in one continuous stream.
    1 mark each for an explained reason.
  2. (b) Tape uses serial access; the tape must be wound through from the start until the record is reached, so finding one record is very slow.
    1 mark for serial access, 1 mark for consequence.
Q14Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a solid-state drive stores data. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why smartphones use solid-state storage. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It uses flash memory chips (NAND) made of transistors; electrons are trapped in each cell (in the floating gate) to represent 1 or 0; data stays when power is off; there are no moving parts.
    1 mark per point, max 2.
  2. (b) Any two explained, e.g. very small and light so fits in a thin phone; no moving parts so not damaged when dropped; low power use so longer battery life; fast access so apps open quickly.
    1 mark each.
Q15Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between a DVD-ROM, a DVD-R and a DVD-RW. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest a suitable use for a DVD-RW. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) DVD-ROM: data is written when the disc is made and can only be read, not changed. DVD-R: can be written (recorded) once by the user, then only read. DVD-RW: can be written, erased and re-written many times.
    1 mark for each correct description.
  2. (b) Any suitable use where data changes, e.g. regular backups that are overwritten; recording TV programmes to watch and then record over; moving files between computers.
    1 mark.
Q16Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A photography club in Lahore wants to back up 60 GB of photos onto single-layer DVD-R discs. Each disc holds 4.7 GB.

  1. (a) Calculate the smallest number of discs needed. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a portable hard disk drive would be a better choice for this backup. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 13 discs
    60 ÷ 4.7 = 12.77… (M1); round up to 13 discs because 12 discs only hold 56.4 GB (A1).
  2. (b) One portable HDD holds all 60 GB so there is no need to split files across 13 discs; it can be overwritten for each new backup whereas DVD-R can only be written once; it is quicker to copy to and to restore from.
    1 mark each for any two explained points.
Q17Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

A news reporter in Dhaka records video on a camera with a 32 GB memory card. One minute of video uses 120 MB. Use 1 GB = 1024 MB.

  1. (a) Calculate the largest number of whole minutes of video that can be stored on the card. [2]
  2. (b) The reporter copies the video to her laptop at the end of each day. Give one reason why she should also keep a second copy on a different storage device. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 273 minutes
    32 × 1024 = 32 768 MB (M1); 32 768 ÷ 120 = 273.06…, so 273 whole minutes (A1).
  2. (b) If the laptop is lost/stolen/fails the video is not lost (backup).
    1 mark.

Q19Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A server in a factory writes sensor readings to its solid-state drive every second, all day and night. Explain why the limited number of write cycles of an SSD could be a problem for this server. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest one way the factory could reduce the risk of losing data. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Each flash memory cell can only be written/erased a limited number of times; with constant writing the cells wear out sooner, so the drive could fail and data could be lost.
    1 mark: limited write/erase cycles. 1 mark: constant writing shortens its life / risk of failure.
  2. (b) e.g. back up the data regularly to another device/cloud; use a magnetic hard disk for the constant logging; replace drives on a schedule; use two mirrored drives.
    1 mark.
Q20Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A shop in Gujranwala has 4 CCTV cameras. Each camera records 1.5 GB of video per hour, 24 hours a day, onto a 2 TB hard disk drive (1 TB = 1024 GB). Calculate the number of complete days of video the drive can hold. [3]
  2. (b) Give one reason why a magnetic hard disk drive is a suitable choice for this CCTV recorder. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 14 days
    M1: 1.5 × 4 × 24 = 144 GB per day. M1: 2 × 1024 = 2048 GB. A1: 2048 ÷ 144 = 14.2, so 14 complete days.
  2. (b) e.g. large capacity at a low cost per gigabyte; can be overwritten many times; fast enough to record video continuously.
    1 mark.
Q21Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe how data is written to a recordable DVD (DVD-R). [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A laser in the drive heats points on a dye layer in the disc; the heated spots change and reflect less light, acting like pits, while unheated areas act like lands; the pattern represents the binary data, written in a spiral track from the centre outwards.
    1 mark each for: laser heats/burns the dye layer; marked areas reflect light differently (act as pits/lands); written along a spiral track. Max 3.
Q22Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A company compares two drives: an 8 TB hard disk drive costing Rs 52 000 and a 2 TB solid-state drive costing Rs 38 000. Using 1 TB = 1024 GB, calculate the cost per gigabyte of each drive, correct to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why data centres still use many hard disk drives. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) HDD Rs 6.35 per GB; SSD Rs 18.55 per GB
    1 mark each: 52 000 ÷ 8192 = 6.35; 38 000 ÷ 2048 = 18.55.
  2. (b) Data centres need enormous capacity; HDDs are much cheaper per gigabyte (about a third of the SSD cost here), and much of the stored data is not read often, so the slower access speed matters less.
    1 mark: cost per GB/capacity linked to calculation. 1 mark: speed less important for rarely used data.
Q23Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A single-layer Blu-ray disc can hold about five times as much data as a single-layer DVD, even though both discs are the same size. Explain why. [3]
  2. (b) Give one reason why films are often sold on Blu-ray rather than DVD. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Blu-ray uses a blue/blue-violet laser which has a shorter wavelength than the red laser used for DVDs; the shorter wavelength can be focused to a smaller spot so the pits can be smaller; the tracks can be closer together; so more pits fit on the same area of disc.
    1 mark per point, max 3.
  2. (b) Higher capacity allows high-definition (or 4K/3D) video/extra content on one disc; Blu-ray discs have a protective coating so are less easily scratched.
    1 mark.

Q25Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A school in Rawalpindi plans to replace the hard disk drives in its file server with solid-state drives. Evaluate this plan. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Benefits: SSDs read/write faster so students' files open and save more quickly, especially when many users log on at once; no moving parts so less likely to fail/be damaged and quieter; lower power use and less heat so cheaper to run and cool; smaller. Drawbacks: SSDs cost more per gigabyte so the same budget buys less storage; files may need to be moved/server set up again, causing disruption; SSDs have a limited number of write cycles, which matters for a server that saves files all day; HDD capacity may be enough if current speed is acceptable. Q26Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A team of geologists will spend three months in the Thar Desert collecting data on laptops. Temperatures are very high, there is fine dust, and the vehicles travel over rough tracks. Recommend the most suitable type of backing storage for the laptops and explain your choice by comparing it with the other two types of storage. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Recommend solid-state (SSD). No moving parts, so it is not damaged by vibration on rough tracks, whereas the heads of a magnetic disk could crash into the platters; dust can stop an optical drive working/scratch discs, but sealed flash memory is unaffected; SSDs cope better with heat and use less power, important when charging is limited; optical discs have low capacity and are slow to write.
    1 mark for SSD with a reason, then 1 mark per comparison with magnetic or optical, max 4.
Q27Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a solid-state drive can find and read a file faster than a magnetic hard disk drive. [2]
  2. (b) Video games are still sold on optical discs. Explain two reasons why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) An HDD must wait for the read/write head to move to the correct track and for the platter to spin to the correct sector; an SSD has no moving parts and reads electronically from any memory cell almost instantly.
    1 mark for the HDD mechanical delay, 1 mark for the SSD electronic access.
  2. (b) Any two explained, e.g. discs are very cheap to mass-produce by pressing; ROM discs cannot be changed or erased by accident/by a virus; easy to sell and package in shops; large enough capacity (Blu-ray) for a game; buyers without fast internet do not need to download a very large file; the disc can be resold/lent.
    1 mark each.
Q28Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A film editor copies a 24 GB video file. The SSD in her laptop reads at 150 MB/s and her old external hard disk reads at 90 MB/s (1 GB = 1024 MB). Calculate how many seconds faster the file is read from the SSD. Give your answer correct to 1 decimal place. [3]
  2. (b) Her laptop SSD holds 512 GB. Explain why she still keeps finished projects on the external hard disk. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 109.2 seconds faster
    M1: 24 × 1024 = 24 576 MB. M1: 24 576 ÷ 150 = 163.84 s and 24 576 ÷ 90 = 273.07 s. A1: 273.07 − 163.84 = 109.2 s.
  2. (b) Hard disks give much more capacity for the cost, so many large video files can be archived cheaply; this keeps the smaller, more expensive SSD free for current work, and the external drive also acts as a backup copy.
    1 mark: HDD lower cost per GB / larger capacity. 1 mark: frees SSD space for current projects / acts as a backup.
Q29Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A teacher in Gilgit moves between classrooms with no internet connection. She needs to carry presentations of about 3 GB each week and update them often. Compare using a USB flash drive and a DVD-RW disc for this. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both are portable and can be rewritten. Q30Magnetic, optical and solid-state storage · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A hospital radiology department must keep patient scans for 25 years. The scans are rarely looked at after the first year. Evaluate the use of magnetic tape and hard disk drives for archiving these scans, and recommend one. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tape: very large capacity at a very low cost per GB; long shelf life and suited to archive/backup; can be stored off-site; but serial access means finding one scan is slow, and it needs a tape drive. HDD: direct access, so any scan can be found quickly; but more expensive per GB for this volume, uses power if kept online and has moving parts that can fail, so drives would need replacing during 25 years. Recommendation: tape for the long-term archive (perhaps with recent scans kept on HDD), because old scans are rarely needed and cost and capacity matter most.
    Up to 2 marks for tape points, up to 2 marks for HDD points, 1 mark for linking to the 'rarely looked at' context, 1 mark for justified recommendation. Max 6.

Q43Backing storage vs internal memory · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

(a) RAM holds the programs and data the processor is currently using; RAM is fast so the processor can work quickly; RAM is volatile so files would be lost when switched off; backing storage keeps files/programs permanently when the power is off; backing storage has a much larger capacity than RAM so it can store all programs and files; RAM is too expensive to provide that capacity.
    1 mark per point, max 4. Must address both RAM and backing storage for full marks.

Q52Backing storage vs internal memory · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe what happens to ROM, RAM and backing storage when a computer is switched on and starts up. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The processor runs the start-up (BIOS) instructions stored in ROM; these check the hardware and find the operating system on backing storage; the operating system is then loaded (copied) from backing storage into RAM, where it runs.

Q53Backing storage vs internal memory · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) All open programs and their data must be held in RAM; when RAM is full, the operating system moves some data to backing storage (virtual memory) and back again; backing storage is much slower than RAM, so the computer slows down; more RAM means more programs/data can be held in fast memory at once, so less swapping is needed.

  1. (a) ROM is non-volatile so the program is still there when the machine is unplugged/switched on again; ROM cannot be changed so the program cannot be erased or altered by accident.

  1.   1. (a) A: much more storage, so space for years of records and backups; but 4 GB RAM is not enough for three programs at once, so it would be slow, and the HDD is slower to load programs and files. Q58Backing storage vs internal memory · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A computer's hard disk drive is replaced with a solid-state drive of the same capacity. Explain why the computer now starts up and opens programs faster, but still cannot run more programs at the same time without slowing down. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The operating system and programs are loaded from backing storage into RAM; the SSD has no moving parts, so it reads data much faster than the HDD and loading takes less time. The number of programs that can run together depends on the amount of RAM, which has not changed; when RAM is full, data is swapped to backing storage, which slows the computer.
    1 mark each for: OS/programs copied from backing storage into RAM; SSD faster access (no moving parts/no seek delay); programs running at once limited by RAM capacity, which is unchanged; full RAM causes swapping and slows the computer.
Q59Backing storage vs internal memory · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A weather data logger has 64 KB of RAM for storing readings. Each reading uses 2 bytes and a reading is taken every 5 seconds (1 KB = 1024 bytes). Calculate how many hours it takes to fill the RAM. Give your answer correct to 1 decimal place. [3]
  2. (b) The data logger copies its readings to a flash memory card every hour. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 45.5 hours
    M1: 64 × 1024 ÷ 2 = 32 768 readings. M1: 32 768 × 5 = 163 840 s. A1: 163 840 ÷ 3600 = 45.5 hours.
  2. (b) RAM is volatile, so if the battery fails all readings in RAM would be lost; the memory card is non-volatile and has more capacity, so readings are kept safe and RAM space is freed.
    1 mark: RAM volatile / data lost if power fails. 1 mark: card keeps data permanently / frees RAM.
Q60Backing storage vs internal memory · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A USB flash drive and RAM both store data electronically using semiconductor chips. Compare a USB flash drive with RAM. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Both are solid-state with no moving parts. RAM is volatile and loses data when the power is off, whereas the flash drive is non-volatile and keeps data. RAM is internal memory directly accessed by the processor and is much faster, whereas the flash drive is backing storage connected through a USB port. The flash drive is portable and usually has a larger capacity per unit cost, whereas RAM is fixed inside the computer.
    1 mark for a similarity; up to 3 marks for linked differences (volatility, speed/direct access, portability, purpose/capacity).
