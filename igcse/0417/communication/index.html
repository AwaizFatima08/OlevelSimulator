Q17Internet, WWW, URLs and search engines · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe what happens after a user types a URL into a web browser and presses Enter, until the web page appears. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The browser sends the domain name to a DNS server; the DNS server looks up and returns the matching IP address of the web server; the browser sends a request (using HTTP/HTTPS) to that IP address; the web server sends back the page's HTML and other files; the browser interprets the HTML and displays the page.
    1 mark each for any four steps in a sensible order.

Q21Internet, WWW, URLs and search engines · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Ahmed downloads a 1.8 GB software update at a connection speed of 25 Mbps. Taking 1 GB = 1000 MB and 1 MB = 8 megabits, calculate the download time in minutes. [3]
  2. (b) Give one reason why the download may take longer than this. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9.6 minutes
    1.8 × 1000 × 8 = 14,400 megabits (M1); 14,400 ÷ 25 = 576 s (M1); 576 ÷ 60 = 9.6 minutes (A1).
  2. (b) Other devices are using the same connection; the web server is busy; network congestion; the actual speed is lower than advertised.
    1 mark.

Q23Internet, WWW, URLs and search engines · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a search engine finds and ranks the web pages it shows in its results. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Web crawlers (spiders/bots) automatically visit pages and follow links; the content and keywords of each page are stored in an index; when a user enters a search the engine looks up matching pages in its index rather than the live web; results are ranked by an algorithm using factors such as relevance of keywords, number and quality of links to the page, how recent it is and location/popularity.
    1 mark each for any four: crawler/spider; follows links; index built; search looks up index; ranking algorithm; ranking factors.

Q31Email and spam · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between cc and bcc when sending an email. [2]
Show answer

Q51Email and spam · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe what happens to an email from the moment the sender clicks Send until the receiver reads it. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The email is sent from the sender's device to the sender's email server; the server uses the domain after the @ to find the receiver's email server and passes the email to it; the email is stored there in the receiver's mailbox until the receiver's email software or webmail downloads/displays it.
    1 mark each: sent to sender's mail server; forwarded to receiver's mail server using the domain; stored until accessed by receiver.

Q56Email and spam · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Spammers send millions of emails even though very few people respond. Explain why spam is still profitable for them. [2]
  2. (b) Describe two ways spammers obtain email addresses. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why clicking 'unsubscribe' in an obvious spam email may increase the amount of spam received. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sending email costs almost nothing, so even a tiny percentage of replies or purchases (or stolen bank details) earns money from a huge number of messages.
    1 mark: very low cost per email; 1 mark: small response rate × huge numbers still gives profit.
  2. (b) Software that collects addresses published on websites and forums; buying lists from data breaches; guessing common addresses at a domain (e.g. info@, ali@); from chain emails where addresses are visible.
    1 mark each for any two.
  3. (c) It confirms to the spammer that the email address is real and active, so it is worth more and gets more spam.
    1 mark.
Q57Email and spam · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A small marketing team in Lahore uses email for all internal communication. The manager is considering an instant messaging app for the team. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of instant messaging compared with email for the team. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Instant messaging advantages: messages arrive and are answered in real time; good for quick questions; group chats and presence status show who is available; less formal. Q58Email and spam · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A sports equipment exporter in Sialkot wants to email its new 45 MB PDF catalogue to 300 overseas buyers. Discuss the problems it may meet and suggest better ways to send the catalogue. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Problems: many email services limit attachments to about 20–25 MB, so the email may be rejected; large emails fill buyers' inboxes; a bulk email with a large attachment may be blocked as spam; sending 300 copies uses a lot of bandwidth; putting all addresses in To/cc would reveal the buyer list to competitors. Q59Email and spam · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A clinic in Hyderabad sends an appointment reminder to 80 patients by putting all their email addresses in the 'To' box. Explain the problems this causes and what the clinic should have done. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Every patient can see the email addresses of all the others, which reveals personal data and that they are patients of the clinic – a breach of privacy/data protection; patients might 'reply all', sending messages to everyone; the visible list could be collected by spammers. The clinic should have used bcc, or better, sent individual emails through a mail-merge or appointment system.
    1 mark each for up to three problems; 1 mark for the correct alternative (bcc / individual emails). Max 4.
Q60Email and spam · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A company tells its employees that their work email accounts may be monitored. Discuss whether employers should monitor employees' work emails. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: the email system and data belong to the company; monitoring can detect leaks of confidential information, harassment or illegal activity; protects against malware and phishing; makes sure staff use time productively; may be needed to meet legal obligations. Against: invades employees' privacy, especially for personal messages; may reduce trust and morale; staff may feel stressed; monitoring must follow the law and the company should tell staff clearly; reading personal content may be disproportionate. Conclusion: acceptable if staff are told in a clear policy and monitoring is limited and proportionate.
