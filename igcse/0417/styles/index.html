Q1Corporate house style · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe what is meant by a corporate house style. [2]
  2. (b) Give three items that a corporate house style might specify. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A set of rules an organisation uses for all its documents and media, fixing items such as the logo, fonts, font sizes, colour scheme and layout, so that everything it produces looks the same.
    1 mark each for any two of: a standard/consistent style for all documents | set by the organisation | specifies fonts/sizes/colours/logo/layout/spacing | so all documents look the same / are recognisable.
  2. (b) The company logo and its position; the font type and sizes for headings and body text; the colour scheme.
    1 mark each for any three of: logo (size/position) | font/typeface | font sizes | colour scheme | margins | line spacing | paragraph spacing | text alignment | header/footer contents | page orientation/size | slogan/strapline.

Q4Corporate house style · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

You are told to create a new paragraph style called HL-body with these settings: sans-serif font, 11 point, left aligned, single line spacing, 0 pt space before and 6 pt space after.

  1. (a) Describe how to create this style in a word processor. [3]
  2. (b) State how you would apply the style to all the body text in the document. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the style name must be typed exactly as given, including the capital letters and hyphen. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Open the Styles pane and choose New Style; name it HL-body and set the style type to paragraph; choose a sans-serif font and 11 pt; set alignment to left and, in Paragraph settings, line spacing single, spacing before 0 pt and after 6 pt; save the style.
    1 mark: create new style and name it exactly HL-body. 1 mark: font sans-serif 11 pt. 1 mark: paragraph settings – left, single, 0 pt before, 6 pt after.
  2. (b) Select all the body text paragraphs and click HL-body in the styles list.
    B1.
  3. (c) A style is identified by its name; a name spelt differently (e.g. HL Body or hl-body) is a different style, so the document would not match the house style and others could not find or apply the correct style.
    B1: styles identified by exact name / different spelling = different style, so house style not followed (accuracy).

Q15Corporate house style · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A house style states that the heading style Brand-H1 must have 36 pt space before and 18 pt space after. One point (pt) is 1/72 of an inch, and 1 inch = 2.54 cm.

  1. (a) Calculate the space before in centimetres, correct to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the space after in centimetres, correct to 3 decimal places. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the house style specifies spacing in points rather than as 'one blank line'. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.27 cm
    M1 36 ÷ 72 = 0.5 inch; A1 × 2.54 = 1.27 cm.
  2. (b) 0.635 cm
    B1 18 ÷ 72 × 2.54 = 0.635 cm.
  3. (c) A blank line's height depends on the font size used, so it varies; points are an exact measurement, so every document gets exactly the same spacing.
    1 mark each: blank line size varies with font/size | points exact/consistent across documents.

Q23Corporate house style · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A company's house style for newsletters states: A4 portrait page 21.0 cm wide; left and right margins 2.0 cm each; body text in two equal columns with 1.0 cm spacing between them.

  1. (a) Calculate the width of each column. [2]
  2. (b) The title of the newsletter must stretch across the full page width above the two columns. Describe how this is done. [2]
  3. (c) The left margin is changed to 2.5 cm but the right margin stays at 2.0 cm. Calculate the new column width. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8.0 cm
    M1 (21.0 − 2.0 − 2.0 − 1.0) = 16.0 cm; A1 ÷ 2 = 8.0 cm.
  2. (b) Keep the title in a single column and insert a continuous section break after it; set the section below the break to two columns, so the title section stays one column wide.
    1 mark: continuous section break after the title. 1 mark: two columns applied only to the section after the break.
  3. (c) 7.75 cm
    B1 (21.0 − 2.5 − 2.0 − 1.0) ÷ 2 = 7.75 cm.

Q28Corporate house style · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A 40-page annual report for a textile company in Faisalabad mentions the company name 'Indus Weave' 85 times. The new house style says the company name must always appear in bold, dark green, small capitals, but the surrounding text must keep its own paragraph style.

  1. (a) Explain why a character style is more suitable than a paragraph style for this requirement. [2]
  2. (b) Describe an efficient way to apply the style to all 85 occurrences. [2]
  3. (c) Explain one risk of using Replace All and how to reduce it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A character style applies only to the selected words, so the paragraph keeps its own style (spacing, alignment, font for other text); a paragraph style would change the whole paragraph.
    1 mark: character style affects only selected text. 1 mark: paragraph style would reformat the entire paragraph.
  2. (b) Create a character style (e.g. Company-name) and use Find and Replace: find 'Indus Weave', replace with the same text but with the Company-name style as replacement formatting, then Replace All.
    1 mark: Find and Replace (Replace All). 1 mark: replacement formatting set to the character style.
  3. (c) It may change text that should not be changed, for example the name inside a quotation or a web address; review the changes afterwards (or use Find Next and replace one at a time / match case).
    1 mark: unintended replacements. 1 mark: proofread / match case / whole words / check each one.
Q29Corporate house style · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

An international aid organisation has offices in Pakistan, Jordan and Kenya. Documents are written in English, Urdu and Arabic. Head office wants one corporate house style for all offices.

  1. (a) Discuss the issues the organisation should consider when designing a single house style for all three offices. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) The chosen fonts must support Urdu and Arabic script as well as English, so a font family with matching scripts is needed; Urdu and Arabic are written right to left, so alignment, page layout and logo position rules must work in mirrored form; font sizes may need adjusting because some scripts appear smaller at the same point size; colours and images must be culturally acceptable in each country; templates and styles must be shared with every office and staff trained. Benefits: a consistent, recognisable brand worldwide. Conclusion: one house style with language-specific variations defined in it.
    Up to 5 marks for issues/benefits, 1 mark for reasoned conclusion. Points: fonts must support all scripts | right-to-left text affects alignment/layout/indents | logo placement in mirrored layouts | relative font sizes differ between scripts | cultural meaning of colours/images | templates must be distributed and kept up to date | training and checking in each office | benefit – global brand consistency.
Q30Corporate house style · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A house style for exam revision booklets states: 'No widows or orphans; no blank pages; each section heading must be on the same page as the paragraph that follows it.'

  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a widow and an orphan in a document. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how paragraph style settings can be used to meet all three parts of this house style rule. [3]
  3. (c) State how a student could check the whole booklet for these errors before printing. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A widow is the last line of a paragraph that appears on its own at the top of a new page (or column); an orphan is the first line of a paragraph left on its own at the bottom of a page (or column).
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Turn on widow/orphan control in the body text style so single lines are never left alone; turn on 'keep with next' in the heading styles so each heading moves to the page of the next paragraph; use page breaks only where required (or page break before in a style) and remove empty paragraphs so no blank pages are created.
    1 mark: widow/orphan control. 1 mark: keep with next on headings. 1 mark: remove extra blank paragraphs/unneeded page breaks to avoid blank pages.
  3. (c) Use print preview (multi-page view) and look at the top and bottom of every page.
    B1: print preview / proofread each page / show formatting marks to find empty paragraphs.
