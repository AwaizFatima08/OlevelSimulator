Q1Headers and footers · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain what is meant by a header and a footer in a document. [2]
  2. (b) Give two items commonly placed in a footer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A header is an area in the top margin of each page; a footer is an area in the bottom margin; text or fields placed in them appear automatically on every page (of the section).
    B1 header top margin / footer bottom margin. B1 repeated on every page.
  2. (b) Any two: page number; total number of pages; file name/path; date; author's name; copyright notice; company contact details.
    1 mark each.
Q2Headers and footers · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain why automated fields, rather than typed text, should be used for the page number and date in a header. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Automated fields update automatically – page numbers change when pages are added or removed and the date updates each time the document is opened/printed – so they are always correct without the user retyping them.
    B1 updates automatically. B1 example: page numbering after edits / current date when printed.
Q3Headers and footers · Core · Paper 2 style · 2 marks

A document's header has a centre tab and a right tab. The page is A4 portrait (21.0 cm wide). The margins are changed to 2.5 cm on the left and 2.5 cm on the right.

  1. (a) Calculate where, measured from the left margin, the right tab stop must be placed so that the right-aligned item lines up with the right margin. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate where the centre tab stop must be placed, measured from the left margin. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 16.0 cm
    21.0 − 2.5 − 2.5 = 16.0 cm. B1
  2. (b) 8.0 cm
    16.0 ÷ 2 = 8.0 cm. B1

Q7Headers and footers · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between a header and a heading. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A header is text placed in the top margin that repeats on every page; a heading is a title within the body text that introduces a section and appears only once where it is typed.
    B1 header – top margin, every page. B1 heading – in the body, introduces a section, appears once.
Q8Headers and footers · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between a footnote and a footer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A footnote gives extra information or a reference linked to a numbered mark in the text and appears at the bottom of the page where the mark is; a footer holds the same items (e.g. page number) on every page.
    B1 footnote linked to a reference mark/specific text. B1 footer repeated on every page.

  2. (b) The original logo is 6.0 cm wide and 2.4 cm high. Calculate the largest width it can have, with its aspect ratio maintained, so that it fits this height. Give your answer correct to 2 decimal places. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.75 cm

    M1 6.0 × 1.75 ÷ 2.4. A1 4.375 → 4.38 cm.

Q46Headers and footers · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A university thesis has 6 introductory pages (title, declaration, acknowledgements, abstract, contents and list of tables) before Chapter 1. The introductory pages must be numbered i, ii, iii …, and Chapter 1 must start at page 1.

  1. (a) Describe how to set up the page numbering. [3]
  2. (b) State the page number printed on the abstract, which is the fourth introductory page. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Insert a next-page section break before Chapter 1; in the first section set the page number format to lower-case Roman numerals (i, ii, iii); in the second section unlink the footer from the previous section, choose Arabic numerals (1, 2, 3) and set numbering to start at 1.
    B1 section break. B1 Roman numeral format in section 1. B1 Arabic format starting at 1 in section 2 (unlinked).
  2. (b) iv
    B1
Q47Headers and footers · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A portrait report contains one landscape page holding a wide table of exam statistics. After changing that page to landscape, the student notices that the right-aligned page number in the footer of the landscape page is now in the middle of the page.

  1. (a) Explain why this has happened. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how to correct it without affecting the portrait pages. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The footer's right tab stop is still at the position set for the narrower portrait page, so on the wider landscape page it is no longer at the right margin.

  2. (b) In the landscape section, unlink the footer from the previous section and move the right tab stop to the new right margin (or use right alignment).

Q49Headers and footers · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

An examination board requires candidates to put their name, centre number and candidate number in the header of every printed page of practical work.

  1. (a) Explain why the details are required on every page rather than only on the first page. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why using the header is better than typing these details at the top of the body text on each page. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pages can become separated or mixed with other candidates' work; having the details on each page means every page can be identified and returned to the right candidate.
    B1 pages may be separated. B1 each page identifiable.
  2. (b) The header repeats automatically on every page and stays in place if the body text is edited; typing on each page is slow and the text would move as content changes.
    B1 automatic repetition. B1 fixed position / saves time.

Q51Headers and footers · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A spreadsheet of 400 sales records prints on 9 pages. On pages 2 to 9 the column headings in row 1 are missing, so it is hard to tell which column is which. A student suggests putting the column headings in the header. Explain why this is not a good solution. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the setting that should be used instead. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The header is separate from the cells, so the headings would not line up exactly with the columns (and would not change if column widths change); the header should hold document details such as the name and date.
    1 mark: headings would not line up with the columns. 1 mark: header is for page information / would not adjust.
  2. (b) In page setup, set row 1 as the rows to repeat at the top of each page (print titles); the column headings then print above the columns on every page.
    1 mark: rows to repeat at top / print titles. 1 mark: row 1 printed at top of every page.
Q52Headers and footers · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A report has three sections separated by section breaks. A student edits the header in section 3 to show 'Appendix', but the headers in sections 1 and 2 also change to 'Appendix'. Explain why this happened. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how to give section 3 its own header without changing sections 1 and 2. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The headers of the sections are linked to the previous section (link to previous is on by default), so a change in one section's header is copied to the linked sections.
    1 mark: headers linked to previous. 1 mark: change applied to all linked sections.
  2. (b) Restore the earlier headers; in section 3's header turn off 'link to previous', then type 'Appendix' in the section 3 header only.
    1 mark: turn off link to previous in section 3. 1 mark: then edit only that header.

  1. (a) Turn on 'different first page'; put the logo, address and phone number in the first-page header; in the header for the other pages insert the customer's name, an automated date and a page number field; check pages 2 to 4 show the smaller header and page 1 the letterhead.

Q58Headers and footers · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A health charity in Quetta prints a two-page leaflet for the public. The designer wants to leave out the header and footer. Discuss whether a header and footer should be used on this leaflet. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) For: a footer could hold the charity's name, contact details and date of issue, so readers know who produced it and if it is up to date; page numbers or 'continued overleaf' help if the pages are separated. Against: with only two pages page numbers are hardly needed; headers take space from the main content and may make a public leaflet look like a formal report; the charity name may already be in the title. Conclusion, e.g. use a small footer with contact details and date, but no header.
    Up to 2 marks for points for, up to 2 marks for points against; a reasoned conclusion is needed for full marks (max 4).

Q60Headers and footers · Challenge · Paper 3 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A wide spreadsheet prints as 6 pages: 3 pages across and 2 pages down. The footer shows the page number. With the page order set to 'down, then over', state the page number printed on the page that holds the middle columns of the bottom rows. [1]
  2. (b) The page order is changed to 'over, then down'. State the new page number of the same page. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why automated page numbers are essential when a spreadsheet prints over several pages like this. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4
