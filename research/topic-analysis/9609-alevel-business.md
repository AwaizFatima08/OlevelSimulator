# Cambridge International AS & A Level Business (9609): topic analysis, 2022–2026

This analysis covers 9 exam sessions, from May/June 2022 to May/June 2026: 34 papers and 1,800 marks, all tagged by syllabus topic. The per-question data is in `9609-questions.csv`. Questions are described in our own words, not quoted.

## Method and sample

- **Sessions:** May/June and Oct/Nov from 2022 to 2025, plus May/June 2026. All files were local, and no network sources were used.
- **Sample:** variant 2 of every written paper in every session (components 12, 22, 32 and, from 2023, 42). That makes 302 question-part rows.
- **Marks:** taken from the `[n]` markers in each question paper and checked by a script. Every paper adds up to its cover total: 40 / 60 / 60 / 40, or 40 / 60 / 100 under the 2022 syllabus.
- **Optional questions are weighted:**
  - Paper 1 Section B in 2022 (choose 1 of 3 essays): each option counts 1/3 of its marks.
  - Paper 1 Section B from 2023 (choose 1 of 2): each option counts 1/2.
  - Paper 3 Section B in 2022 (choose 1 of 2): each option counts 1/2.
- **Topics:** these are the six syllabus areas: Business and its environment, Human resource management, Marketing, Operations management, Finance and accounting, and Strategic management.
- **Subtopics:** 43 in total, based on the syllabus sub-sections. Each part goes to the one subtopic that earns most of its marks.
- **Judgement calls in the tagging:**
  - On Paper 4, questions were tagged to the function they focus on. For example, an "operations strategy" question goes to Operations management, and an "employee performance strategy" question goes to HRM. Only questions about culture, contingency planning, corporate plans, strategic tools or blue ocean strategy were tagged *Strategic management*. This means Strategic management looks smaller than its real role on Paper 4.
  - Paper 3 investment decisions (for example "should the business automate?") were tagged by their subject, not as investment appraisal. The ARR, NPV and payback calculations that go with them are tagged *Investment appraisal*.
  - Treat the shares as estimates, accurate to about ±1–2 % per topic.
- **Syllabus change:** 2022 papers belong to the old syllabus (Short Answer and Essay / Data Response / Case Study). The 2023 syllabus kept the same subject content areas, so both are tagged on one scheme. Their structures are compared below.
- **Examiner reports** exist locally only for June 2022, Nov 2022 and June 2023. Grade thresholds exist for all 9 sessions.
- **2026 files** use long file names and carry website watermark lines. These were ignored.

## 1. Paper structure

**From 2023 (current syllabus)**

| Component | Level | Time | Marks | Format | A Level weight* |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 Business Concepts 1 | AS | 1 h 15 min | 40 | Section A (20 marks): three questions with a 2-mark define part and a 3-mark explain part, plus one 5-mark "analyse one…" question. Section B (20 marks): choose 1 of 2 essays, each with an 8-mark analyse part and a 12-mark evaluate part | 20 % |
| Paper 2 Business Concepts 2 | AS | 1 h 30 min | 60 | Two data-response questions of 30 marks each. Every question follows the same pattern: identify [1], explain a term [3], calculate [3], explain [3], analyse [8], evaluate [12] | 30 % |
| Paper 3 Business Decision-Making | A Level | 1 h 45 min | 60 | One case study in an insert, with 5 compulsory questions: Q1 and Q2 analyse [8] each; Q3 is two calculations (4 marks together) plus evaluate [12]; Q4 is one calculation [4] plus evaluate [12]; Q5 evaluate [12] | 30 % |
| Paper 4 Business Strategy | A Level | 1 h 15 min | 40 | A pre-set case (a timeline plus appendices) in the paper, with 2 compulsory 20-mark questions: Q1 "Evaluate…" looks back at past strategy; Q2 "Advise…" looks forward | 20 % |

\*For AS Level, Paper 1 counts 40 % and Paper 2 counts 60 %. The weights come from the threshold tables: the four papers' raw marks add up to the 200-mark A Level total.

**2022 (old syllabus)**

- **Paper 1 Short Answer and Essay** (1 h 15 min, 40 marks): four short questions, then one essay chosen from three. Two of the essay options were split 8 + 12 and one was a single 20-mark "Discuss" question.
- **Paper 2 Data Response** (1 h 30 min, 60 marks): two questions of 30 marks each. The pattern was a 2-mark *define*, a 3-mark *explain the term*, calculations, an 8-mark analysis and an 11-mark evaluation.
- **Paper 3 Case Study** (3 h, 100 marks): Section A was 80 marks of compulsory questions, including about 14 marks of calculations. Section B was one 20-mark strategy essay chosen from two.

**What changed in 2023**

- The 100-mark case study was split into Paper 3 (decision-making) and Paper 4 (strategy).
- "Define" (2 marks) on Paper 2 became "Identify" (1 mark), and the final evaluation went up from 11 to 12 marks.
- Paper 1 essays now always ask you to *evaluate* a claim in a stated business setting (a hotel, a bus service, a coffee shop and so on).
- The format has been identical in every session from June 2023 to June 2026.

## 3. Bankers

These came up in almost every session.

- **Costs, contribution and break-even:** examined in all 9 sessions. Paper 2 nearly always has a cost, profit, contribution or margin-of-safety calculation, often with a "one limitation of break-even" part straight after it.
- **Recruitment, selection and training:** examined in all 9 sessions. Topics include induction, on-the-job and off-the-job training, assessment centres, internal recruitment, job descriptions, person specifications and redundancy. These often appear as the "identify" mark or a Section A question.
- **Investment appraisal (ARR, payback, NPV):** a calculation on Paper 3 in 6 of 9 sessions. ARR alone came up in 2022, 2023, 2024, Nov 2025 and 2026.
- **Ratio analysis:** asked in 7 of 9 sessions. It appears on Paper 3 as a 1–4 mark calculation, sometimes followed by a 12-mark evaluation. Ratios seen include inventory turnover, current ratio, ROCE, operating profit margin, gearing, dividend yield, dividend cover and days' sales in receivables.
- **Need for finance and sources of finance:** 8 of 9 sessions. This covers capital versus revenue expenditure, grants, retained earnings, crowdfunding, sale and leaseback, and internal versus external finance.
- **Business structure and legal forms:** 8 of 9 sessions, mostly short Paper 2 items: private and public limited companies, limited liability, sectors, multinationals, co-operatives.
- **Inventory and supply chain management:** 7 of 9 sessions. This covers JIT (twice as an 8-mark question), buffer inventory, re-order level, supply chain management and inventory valuation.
- **Ethics, CSR and sustainability:** 7 of 9 sessions. It was a 12- to 16-mark evaluation on Paper 3 three times (June 2022, June 2023, Nov 2023).
- **Management and leadership:** 6 of 9 sessions. Topics include autocratic style, delegation, leaders versus managers, emotional intelligence, and the role of a manager.
- **Product decisions:** 6 of 9 sessions. This covers the product life cycle, the Boston Matrix, packaging, branding, NPD and R&D.

## 4. Rarely tested

These areas came up 1–3 times in the sample, or only for small marks. They are still in the syllabus.

- **Strategic analysis tools as a question in their own right:** PEST/SWOT was asked directly only once (2022). Blue ocean strategy came up once (Nov 2023, Paper 4). Porter's five forces and force field analysis were never the main focus of a question, although they appear in mark schemes as tools you could use.
- **Market segmentation, pricing, and promotion and distribution:** each was examined 3 times, mostly for 1–5 marks. When the marketing mix is tested, it is usually inside a larger strategy question.
- **International marketing:** only twice (Paper 4 June 2023, Paper 3 Nov 2024).
- **Operations planning (CPA, ERP):** twice (Nov 2022, Nov 2024).
- **Communication and employee relations:** 3 times. Trade unions and industrial action appeared in Nov 2024 and June 2026, and communication methods in June 2023.
- **Business objectives:** 3 times, and the stakeholders subtopic 4 times.
- **Financial statements:** you have never been asked to build an income statement or a statement of financial position from scratch. Accounts came up as interpretation, profitability and inventory valuation instead.
- **Capacity utilisation:** a 1-mark calculation (Nov 2023) and one 5-mark question about operating over capacity (2026).

## 5. Trends

- **The business environment matters more on the A Level papers.** Paper 3 had an external-environment question in five of seven new-format sessions. Three were the final Q5 (a regional trading agreement, exchange-rate changes, economic factors) and two were Q1 (economic change, more competition). Paper 4 also had a question on government policy (Nov 2025). The share for this topic rose from 14 % in 2022 to 19 % from 2023.
- **Finance's share fell from 23 % to 16 %.** This happened when the 3-hour case study went. However, finance calculations still appear on every Paper 2 and Paper 3.
- **Budgets and variances are a newer favourite.** They were examined in every session from June 2024 to June 2026: variance calculations on Paper 2, and budget-evaluation essays on Paper 1.
- **More labour-data calculations on Paper 3.** Labour productivity, absenteeism, labour turnover and span of control have all been asked since 2024. Labour turnover also turned up on Paper 2.
- **Forecasting is back.** Moving averages, seasonal variation and time-series analysis came up in June 2024, Nov 2025 and June 2026. Elasticity calculations last appeared in 2022.
- **Diversity and equality recur.** They were examined in June 2023, Nov 2023, June 2025 and June 2026, including a 12-mark evaluation on Paper 2 in 2026.
- **Paper 4 moves between functions.** It has covered leadership, marketing strategy, corporate culture, blue ocean strategy, operations strategy (twice), corporate planning, employee performance (twice), accounting data, strategic tools, government policy and contingency planning. A finance-centred Paper 4 question has appeared only once (Nov 2024).

## 6. Question styles

- **Define / Identify** (Paper 1 [2], Paper 2 [1]). Give a precise, complete definition, or a one- or two-word answer for "identify". No context is needed.
- **Explain** [3]. One mark is for knowledge and two are for applying it. On Paper 2 the application must use the business in the data.
- **Calculate** [1–5]. Paper 2 always has one calculation per question. Paper 3 always has two in Q3 and one in Q4. Show your working, because method marks are usually available.
- **Analyse** [5 / 8]. Build chains of cause and effect in context. "Two benefits" means exactly two, and you get no credit for evaluating.
- **Evaluate / Discuss / Recommend / Advise** [11–20]. Half or more of the marks are for evaluation. The question usually tests a claim ("…is the most important…", "…is the only way…", "Evaluate whether…"). You need a justified judgement tied to the business named in the question.
- **Paper 4:** Q1 asks you to judge how effective a past strategy was over a stated period on the timeline. Q2 asks you to advise on a future strategy. Both need all four assessment objectives and a clear decision.

## 7. Common mistakes

These are paraphrased from the examiner reports for June 2022, Nov 2022 and June 2023.

- **Partial definitions.** For example, describing demand without mentioning ability to pay, price or time period. Precise definitions are an easy 2 marks.
- **Weak use of context.** Repeating the case or using only the business's name is not application. Show why the point matters for *this* business.
- **Too little evaluation.** On the 12-mark questions, evaluation is worth up to 6 marks, but most answers gave only one or two weak judgements. Weigh points up as you go, and end with a decision that is supported and specific to the context.
- **Answering a different question.** Examples include writing a general marketing-mix essay when the question asked whether marketing was the *most important* factor, ignoring the stakeholder-*conflict* focus, and writing about leadership styles when the question asked about factors that *influence* style.
- **Doing too much.** Some candidates gave three points when two were asked for, used every piece of data, or listed all 4Ps with no evaluation. Fewer points, developed more fully, scored higher.
- **Skipping calculations.** Over 10 % of candidates (over 20 % on one paper) skipped at least one calculation. Common errors were:
  - not turning a total into an annual figure for ARR;
  - getting the sign wrong in an elasticity answer;
  - giving a ratio in the wrong form;
  - dropping a component, such as an inventory adjustment.
- **Poor timing on Paper 4.** Some candidates spent too long on one 20-mark answer. The examiners suggest about 25–30 minutes per question, built on three or four strong chains of analysis.
- **Wrong focus on Paper 4.** Many candidates prepared only "strategy" content, but Paper 4 can draw on the whole syllabus.

## 9. Revision priorities for Oct/Nov 2026 and 2027

1. **Calculations.** Every one of these has come up in the last six sessions:
   - break-even, contribution and margin of safety;
   - budget variances, including whether each is favourable or adverse;
   - ARR, payback and NPV;
   - inventory turnover, current ratio, ROCE, profit margins, gearing, dividend cover and dividend yield;
   - labour productivity, labour turnover and absenteeism;
   - centred moving averages and seasonal variation.

   For each one, also practise a short comment on what the result means for the decision.
2. **External environment for Papers 3 and 4:** exchange rates, trading blocs, economic change, competition and government policy. This is the most common Paper 3 theme since 2023.
3. **People topics:**
   - recruitment, selection and training;
   - leadership and delegation;
   - diversity and equality;
   - workforce planning;
   - organisational structure (delayering, span of control);
   - HRM approaches for improving performance.

   HRM is the most heavily weighted topic overall.
4. **CSR, ethics and sustainability:** an evaluation of these comes up every year or two.
5. **Operations:** inventory, JIT and supply chains; location decisions; lean production, TQM and quality assurance; automation versus labour-intensive production; operations strategy (asked twice on Paper 4).
6. **Marketing strategy:** coordinated marketing mix, the product life cycle and the Boston Matrix, NPD and R&D, market research and sampling reliability, and entering international markets.
7. **Strategic management for Paper 4:**
   - contingency planning, which came up three times;
   - corporate culture and change management;
   - corporate plans;
   - Ansoff, decision trees, SWOT, PEST, Porter and blue ocean strategy, as tools you can evaluate.

   Expect the question to be built around any function: a finance-led Paper 4 question has been rare so far, so practise using accounting data to judge a strategy.
8. **Essay technique:** practise 12-mark answers that test a claim, set in an unfamiliar context (hotel, hospital, airline, bus service, retailer), with a judgement throughout and a supported conclusion. This is where most marks are lost.

These priorities are based on frequency in this sample. They are not a prediction of any specific paper.
