Q1Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Margalla Cement operates a large cement plant near a village in northern Punjab.

  1. (a) Define 'external cost'. [2]
  2. (b) Identify two external costs that Margalla Cement's plant might create for the village. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A cost of a business's activity that is paid by people or groups outside the business (society), not by the business itself.
    1 mark: a cost resulting from the business's activity. 1 mark: borne by third parties/society, not the business.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. dust/air pollution causing breathing problems; noise from machinery; heavy lorries damaging local roads and causing congestion; damage to farmland or water supplies; loss of natural landscape from quarrying.
    1 mark each.
Q2Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Pine View is a hotel in Murree that attracts many tourists every summer.

  1. (a) Define 'sustainable development'. [2]
  2. (b) Identify two ways Pine View could reduce its impact on the environment. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Development that meets the needs of people today without reducing the ability of future generations to meet their own needs.
    1 mark: meeting present needs. 1 mark: without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. install solar panels / energy-saving lighting; reuse towels to save water and detergent; recycle waste; stop using single-use plastic; buy food from local suppliers; harvest rainwater.
    1 mark each.
Q3Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

A pressure group is campaigning against a drinks company that sells water in single-use plastic bottles in Pakistan.

  1. (a) Define 'pressure group'. [2]
  2. (b) State two methods the pressure group could use to influence the drinks company. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) An organisation of people with a common interest who try to influence the decisions of businesses or the government.
    1 mark: group of people with a shared interest/cause. 1 mark: tries to influence business or government decisions.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. organising a consumer boycott; protests or demonstrations; social media campaigns and bad publicity; petitions; lobbying the government to pass laws.
    1 mark each.
Q4Environmental and ethical issues · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

The government has built a new metro bus line through the centre of Lahore.

  1. (a) Define 'external benefit'. [2]
  2. (b) Identify two external benefits of the new metro bus line. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A gain from an activity that is received by people or groups outside the organisation carrying out the activity.
    1 mark: a benefit/gain from an activity. 1 mark: received by third parties/society.
  2. (b) Any two, e.g. less road congestion as people leave their cars; less air pollution; shorter journey times so workers reach jobs more easily; more customers for shops near stations; new jobs.

Q5Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Evergreen Oils produces palm oil on plantations in Malaysia. An environmental pressure group says the company is cutting down rainforest to plant more palm trees.

  1. (a) Identify two ways the government could control the environmental impact of Evergreen Oils. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways the pressure group's campaign could affect Evergreen Oils. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two, e.g. laws banning or limiting forest clearance; fines for illegal clearing; taxes on environmental damage; permits/licences for land use; subsidies for sustainable farming.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Boycott: food manufacturers and consumers may stop buying its palm oil, so sales and profit fall. 2) Bad publicity: the company's reputation is damaged, making it harder to attract investors or win contracts, and it may need to spend on public relations. 3) The company may change its methods, e.g. replanting forest, which raises costs but may later improve its image.
    For each of the two points: 1 mark for identifying the point, 1 mark for applying it to the business, 1 mark for developing the consequence (max 3 per point).
Q6Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

The provincial government has introduced a pollution tax of PKR 1.50 per brick on brick kilns that still use old, smoky technology. Shah Bricks produces 600,000 bricks a month using the old technology.

  1. (a) Calculate the monthly pollution tax Shah Bricks must pay. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways the pollution tax could affect Shah Bricks. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) PKR 900,000
    M1: 600,000 × 1.50. A1: PKR 900,000.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Higher costs: the PKR 900,000 a month reduces profit unless it raises brick prices, which may lose customers to cleaner kilns. 2) Incentive to change: the owner may invest in cleaner (zig-zag) kiln technology to avoid the tax; this needs finance now but saves tax and improves its image. Other: may cut output or workers.
    For each of the two points: 1 mark for identifying the point, 1 mark for applying it to the business, 1 mark for developing the consequence (max 3 per point).
Q7Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

  1. (a) Define 'ethical decision'. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why Northway Fashion might choose the more expensive supplier. [6]

    1 mark: based on moral principles/doing what is right. 1 mark: may not maximise profit.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Reputation: continuing with a supplier using child labour could lead to boycotts and lost sales, so paying more protects long-term revenue. 2) Marketing advantage: it can advertise ethically made clothes, attracting customers willing to pay higher prices. Q8Environmental and ethical issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

A government is deciding whether to build a new international airport outside a city. The following effects have been identified:1 Construction cost paid by the airport company2 Noise for families living nearby3 New jobs for local people in hotels and taxis4 Wages paid by the airport company to its own staff5 Loss of farmland and wildlife habitat6 Shorter journey times for business travellers from the whole region

  1. (a) Classify each of the six effects as a private cost, an external cost, or an external benefit. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the government might approve the airport even though it creates external costs. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Private costs: 1 and 4. External costs: 2 and 5. External benefits: 3 and 6.
    4 marks for all six correct; 3 marks for five; 2 marks for four; 1 mark for two or three.
  2. (b) The government considers the total social costs and benefits; if the external benefits (jobs, faster travel, growth) plus private benefits are greater than all the costs, society as a whole gains.
    1 mark: compare social benefits with social costs. 1 mark: development, e.g. benefits outweigh costs so society gains.
Q9Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Green Leaf Tea Company buys tea from farmers in Sri Lanka. It pays farmers above the market price and uses only recyclable packaging. Its shareholders want higher profits.

  1. (a) Outline one way ethical behaviour could increase Green Leaf's sales. [2]
  2. (b) Do you think acting ethically will reduce Green Leaf's profits? Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) e.g. Customers who care about fair treatment of farmers may choose Green Leaf over rival brands, increasing sales and allowing a higher price.
    1 mark for the point, 1 mark for development in context.
  2. (b) Reduce: paying above market prices and recyclable packaging raise costs; if prices cannot rise, profit margins fall; price-sensitive customers may buy cheaper tea. Not reduce: stronger brand image and loyalty; can charge a premium price; reliable supply from loyal farmers; avoids bad publicity. Judgement, e.g. in the short run profit may fall, but in the long run it may rise if customers value ethical tea; it depends on how many customers will pay more.
    Up to 2 marks for knowledge/analysis of points for, up to 2 for points against, 2 marks for a justified judgement that explains why one side outweighs the other in this context.
Q10Environmental and ethical issues · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

Bean There is a chain of coffee shops in Karachi. It buys 20,000 kg of coffee beans a year from Supplier X at $4.50 per kg. Supplier X has been accused of paying workers very low wages. Supplier Y is a certified fair-trade supplier that charges 12% more per kg. Bean There's customers are mostly young professionals.

  1. (a) Should Bean There switch to Supplier Y? Justify your answer using suitable calculations. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Calculations: $10,800. Switch: protects reputation; fair-trade logo can be used in promotion and may attract young professionals who care about ethics; may justify slightly higher prices; avoids risk of boycott. Stay: extra $10,800 a year raises costs and reduces profit; customers may not notice or pay more; Supplier X may improve. Judgement, e.g. switch, because the extra cost is small compared with the risk to the brand and could be covered by a very small price rise per cup.
    Calculations: 20,000 × 4.50 = $90,000. 12% × 90,000 = $10,800. Content: extra cost 12% × 20,000 × 4.50 = $10,800 a year; fair-trade logo in promotion, appeal to ethically minded young professionals, reputation and boycott risk, possible small price rise per cup; higher costs and lower profit, customers may not notice or pay more. Mark bands (12; knowledge 2, application 2, analysis 4, evaluation 4): Level 3 (9–12): developed analysis of both sides / all options applied to Bean There, using the data, and a justified decision that explains why the rejected option(s) are weaker. Level 2 (5–8): advantages and disadvantages analysed and applied to Bean There, with a decision that is only partly justified or one-sided. Level 1 (1–4): relevant knowledge with little application or analysis; a decision with no reasoning earns no evaluation marks. Q16Business and the international economy · Core · Paper 1 style · 4 marks

Sialkot Sports makes footballs that it exports to the USA. It charges PKR 3,360 per football. The exchange rate is $1 = PKR 280.

  1. (a) Calculate the price of one football in US dollars at this exchange rate. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) The exchange rate changes to $1 = PKR 260 and Sialkot Sports keeps its price at PKR 3,360. Calculate the new price in US dollars, correct to 2 decimal places, and state whether the rupee has appreciated or depreciated. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $12.00
    M1: 3,360 ÷ 280. A1: $12.
  2. (b) $12.92; the rupee has appreciated

Q18Business and the international economy · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

    M1: (22% − 18%) = 4% × 5,000,000. A1: PKR 200,000.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Fewer car sales: many customers buy cars with loans/car financing; higher interest makes monthly payments larger, so demand falls. 2) Less expansion: borrowing to open a new showroom becomes more expensive, so Prime Autos may delay investment. Other: customers may save rather than spend.
    For each of the two points: 1 mark for identifying the point, 1 mark for applying it to the business, 1 mark for developing the consequence (max 3 per point).
Q27Economic issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Tasty Karahi is a restaurant in Islamabad. The country has high inflation. The price Tasty Karahi pays for chicken has risen from PKR 1,250 per kg to PKR 1,400 per kg in one year.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in the price of chicken. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two effects of high inflation on Tasty Karahi. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12%
    M1: (1,400 − 1,250) ÷ 1,250 × 100. A1: 12%.
  2. (b) e.g. 1) Rising costs: chicken is 12% dearer, so the cost of each karahi rises; if menu prices are not raised, profit margins fall. 2) Falling demand: customers' real incomes fall, so eating out (a non-essential) may be cut back and sales fall. Other: workers demand higher wages; frequent menu price changes cost money.
    For each of the two points: 1 mark for identifying the point, 1 mark for applying it to the business, 1 mark for developing the consequence (max 3 per point).
Q28Economic issues · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Last year a country's GDP was $340 billion. This year it is $357 billion.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage growth in GDP. Show your working. [2]
  2. (b) Identify two government economic objectives other than economic growth. [2]
  3. (c) Outline two benefits to a construction company of economic growth. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5%
    M1: (357 − 340) ÷ 340 × 100. A1: 5%.
  2. (b) Any two: low unemployment; low and stable inflation (price stability); balance of payments stability (exports and imports balanced).
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) e.g. 1) Rising incomes mean more people can afford new houses, so the company gets more orders. 2) Government may receive more tax and spend more on roads and schools, giving the company more contracts.
    For each: 1 mark for the benefit, 1 mark for linking it to a construction company.
Q29Economic issues · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

The government has increased income tax rates. Luxe Motors sells expensive imported cars. Daily Needs is a grocery store selling rice, flour and cooking oil.

  1. (a) State two other ways the government could use fiscal policy to influence the economy. [2]
  2. (b) Do you think the increase in income tax will affect Luxe Motors more than Daily Needs? Justify your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Any two, e.g. change sales tax/GST; change corporation tax on profits; change government spending (e.g. on infrastructure); change import duties.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Higher income tax reduces disposable income. Luxe Motors sells luxury goods that people can delay buying, so demand is likely to fall significantly; buyers may switch to cheaper cars. Daily Needs sells necessities that people must still buy, so demand changes little, though customers may switch to cheaper brands. However, very rich customers of Luxe Motors may be little affected, while Daily Needs' low-income customers feel the tax more. Judgement, e.g. yes, Luxe Motors is affected more, because its products are not essential.
    Up to 2 marks for knowledge/analysis of points for, up to 2 for points against, 2 marks for a justified judgement that explains why one side outweighs the other in this context.
Q30Economic issues · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks

Comfort Home Furniture sells sofas and beds in Karachi. Because of a recession, it expects sales to fall. Last year it sold 2,000 items at an average price of PKR 50,000. Managers are considering two options:Option 1 – cut prices by 10%; they forecast this would lead to sales of 2,150 items;Option 2 – keep prices the same and make 8 of its 40 staff redundant. Each of these staff costs PKR 60,000 per month.

  1. (a) Which option should Comfort Home Furniture choose? Justify your answer using suitable calculations. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Calculations: PKR 96,750,000. Option 1: revenue falls from PKR 100 m to PKR 96.75 m even though more items are sold, and the forecast may be optimistic in a recession; but it keeps staff and customers. Option 2: saves 8 × 60,000 × 12 = PKR 5.76 m a year; but customer service may fall, remaining staff demotivated, redundancy payments, and staff are lost for when the economy recovers. Note: revenue is not profit – selling 150 more items also adds to costs, so Option 1's effect on profit is worse than the PKR 3.25 m revenue fall suggests. Judgement, e.g. Option 2, because Option 1 reduces revenue by PKR 3.25 m while Option 2 cuts costs by PKR 5.76 m; or a justified alternative.
    Calculations: new price 50,000 × 0.9 = PKR 45,000. 2,150 × 45,000 = PKR 96,750,000. Content: Option 1 revenue 2,150 × 45,000 = PKR 96.75 m v PKR 100 m now (fall of PKR 3.25 m, and extra items add costs); Option 2 saves 8 × 60,000 × 12 = PKR 5.76 m a year; reliability of the sales forecast in a recession; customer service, morale, redundancy pay, losing staff before recovery. Mark bands (12; knowledge 2, application 2, analysis 4, evaluation 4): Level 3 (9–12): developed analysis of both sides / all options applied to Comfort Home Furniture, using the data, and a justified decision that explains why the rejected option(s) are weaker. Level 2 (5–8): advantages and disadvantages analysed and applied to Comfort Home Furniture, with a decision that is only partly justified or one-sided. Level 1 (1–4): relevant knowledge with little application or analysis; a decision with no reasoning earns no evaluation marks. Correct calculations count towards application and analysis; follow through an earlier calculation error.
