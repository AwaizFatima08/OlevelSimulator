Q1Meaning and effect of phrases · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

Read the passage below.The bazaar woke slowly that Friday. By nine, though, it had become a living river of people, and Ayesha, clutching her mother's list, was swept along with it. Stall-holders barked out prices over one another. Bolts of silk spilled their colours across wooden counters, and the air was thick with cardamom, frying dough and diesel from the delivery rickshaws that nosed their way through the crowd like impatient goats.At the spice stall, old Karim sat as still as a stone in the middle of all the movement. He weighed out turmeric with the slow care of a jeweller, tipping the tiny brass scale until it settled exactly level. When Ayesha handed him the list, he peered at it over his glasses and smiled, as if it were a letter from an old friend. 'Your mother still buys the best saffron,' he said. 'Tell her the red chillies are good this week, but the cumin is not what it was.' Ayesha nodded seriously, as though she had been trusted with a secret.

  1. (a) Explain the meaning and effect of the phrase 'a living river of people'. [3]
  2. (b) Explain the meaning and effect of the phrase 'as still as a stone in the middle of all the movement'. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Meaning: a huge crowd moving continuously through the bazaar. Effect: the metaphor suggests the crowd flows constantly and powerfully, as if it has a will of its own; Ayesha is helpless in it ('swept along'); creates a sense of energy and bustle.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
  2. (b) Meaning: Karim does not move at all while everything around him is busy. Effect: the simile contrasts his calm with the chaos of the market, making him seem solid, dependable, timeless, perhaps wise; he becomes the fixed centre of the scene.
    1 mark for the meaning; up to 2 marks for the effect (what it makes the reader see, feel or understand). Do not credit answers that simply repeat the words of the phrase; the explanation must be in the candidate's own words.
