Q1Opinion response using a text · Core · Paper 1 style · 7 marks

Read the text below.Growing more than vegetablesWhen Gulshan Girls' School turned a dusty corner of its playground into a vegetable garden three years ago, few teachers expected much. Today the plot produces spinach, tomatoes, coriander and okra, and the harvest is sold to parents every Friday. The money buys seeds and tools for the next season.Teachers say the garden has become an outdoor classroom. Biology lessons on plant growth now happen beside real seedlings, and mathematics classes measure beds and calculate yields. Students who struggle to sit still indoors often work hardest outside, and several have discovered that they enjoy something they had never tried.There are difficulties. Watering during the summer holidays depends on a rota of volunteer families, and one year the whole crop dried out when the rota broke down. Some parents worry that time in the garden takes away from preparation for exams, and the plot needs a small budget for fencing to keep out goats from the neighbouring field.The head teacher, Mrs Rubina Saleem, is clear: 'Children who have grown food understand where it comes from and why it should not be wasted. That lesson lasts longer than any test score.'

  1. (a) According to the text, give two ways the garden is used in lessons. [2]
  2. (b) Imagine you are a parent of a student at a different school. Your school is deciding whether to start a garden like this. What is your opinion and why? Use information from the text to support your answer. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) Biology lessons on plant growth beside real seedlings; mathematics classes measure beds and calculate yields.
    1 mark each. Accept close paraphrase.
  2. (b) For: lessons become practical and memorable (biology, maths); students who find classrooms difficult can succeed; it is self-funding through sales; teaches respect for food and less waste. Against: needs reliable watering in holidays (crop failed once); takes time away from exam preparation; costs for fencing and tools; depends on volunteers. A developed answer might support it but suggest a firm holiday rota and limit garden time in exam years.

  2. (b) For: lower bills, power during cuts, selling spare power, less fuel burnt, falling panel prices; a society could negotiate bulk prices and shared cleaning.
