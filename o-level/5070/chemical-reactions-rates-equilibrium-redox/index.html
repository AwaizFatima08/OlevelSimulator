Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Rate of reaction · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Magnesium reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid. Which change does NOT increase the rate of this reaction?A raising the temperature of the acidB using magnesium powder instead of ribbonC using more concentrated acidD using larger pieces of magnesium [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D using larger pieces of magnesium
    Larger pieces have a smaller surface area, so collisions are less frequent and the rate falls. Powder (B) increases surface area and rate.
Q2Rate of reaction · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Zinc granules react with dilute hydrochloric acid to produce hydrogen gas.

  1. (a) Describe the effect on the rate of reaction of using more concentrated hydrochloric acid. [1]
  2. (b) Explain this effect in terms of particles. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The rate increases (the reaction is faster).
    B1
  2. (b) There are more acid particles in the same volume, so collisions between particles are more frequent (more collisions per second).
    B1 more particles per unit volume; B1 higher collision frequency
Q3Rate of reaction · Core · Paper 3 style · 5 marks

A student follows the rate of reaction of marble chips with dilute hydrochloric acid by measuring the decrease in mass of the flask every 30 seconds.

  1. (a) Name the apparatus used to measure the mass of the flask and its contents. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why a cotton-wool plug is put in the neck of the flask. [1]
  3. (c) State two variables that must be kept the same if the experiment is repeated with smaller marble chips. [2]
  4. (d) Describe one other way of following the rate of this reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (top-pan) balance
    B1
  2. (b) It lets gas escape but stops acid spray / liquid being lost.
    B1
  3. (c) Any two of: mass of marble; volume of acid; concentration of acid; temperature.
    1 mark each for any two
  4. (d) Collect the CO₂ in a gas syringe and measure its volume at regular time intervals.
    B1
Q4Rate of reaction · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A student mixes different concentrations of sodium thiosulfate solution with the same volume of dilute hydrochloric acid in a flask standing on a paper marked with a cross. She measures how long it takes for the cross to disappear from view.

  1. (a) Name the independent variable and the dependent variable in this experiment. [2]
  2. (b) State how the time taken is related to the rate of the reaction. [1]
  3. (c) State one way to make the experiment a fair test. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) independent: concentration of sodium thiosulfate; dependent: time for the cross to disappear
    B1 each
  2. (b) The shorter the time, the faster the rate (rate is proportional to 1/time).
    B1
  3. (c) Keep the temperature / volume of acid / concentration of acid / total volume / the same cross the same.
    B1
Q5Rate of reaction · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement explains how a catalyst increases the rate of a reaction?A It gives the particles more kinetic energy.B It provides a pathway with a lower activation energy.C It increases the concentration of the reactants.D It makes the reaction more exothermic. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B It provides a pathway with a lower activation energy.
    A catalyst provides an alternative pathway of lower activation energy, so a greater proportion of collisions are successful. A describes the effect of heating, not of a catalyst.
Q6Rate of reaction · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Sodium thiosulfate solution reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid.

  1. (a) Describe and explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the rate of this reaction. Use ideas about particles and collisions. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Rate increases. Particles gain kinetic energy and move faster, so they collide more frequently; a greater proportion of collisions have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, so there are more successful collisions per second.
    B1 rate increases; B1 particles have more kinetic energy / move faster; B1 more frequent collisions; B1 greater proportion of collisions with energy ≥ activation energy
Q7Rate of reaction · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Marble chips (calcium carbonate) react with excess dilute hydrochloric acid. The carbon dioxide is collected in a gas syringe.Time / s: 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100Volume / cm³: 0, 46, 74, 90, 98, 100

  1. (a) Calculate the average rate of reaction between 0 s and 20 s, in cm³/s. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the average rate between 40 s and 60 s. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why the rate decreases as the reaction proceeds. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest the total volume of gas that would be collected if the experiment were left to finish. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.3 cm³/s
    46 ÷ 20 = 2.3 B1
  2. (b) 0.8 cm³/s
    (90 − 74) ÷ 20 = 0.8 B1
  3. (c) The acid is used up so its concentration decreases; collisions become less frequent.
    B1 concentration of acid (reactant) decreases; B1 fewer collisions per second
  4. (d) about 100 cm³ (reaction almost complete)
    B1 accept 100–102 cm³
Q8Rate of reaction · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why fine coal dust in the air of a coal mine in Balochistan can explode when a spark occurs, but a lump of coal burns slowly. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest one other industry where fine powders could cause an explosion. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Dust has a much larger surface area (per unit mass); more coal particles are exposed to oxygen, so collisions are much more frequent; the reaction is so fast that heat and gas are released almost instantly (explosion).
    B1 larger surface area; B1 more frequent collisions with oxygen; B1 very fast reaction / rapid release of energy
  2. (b) flour mills (or sugar / custard powder / sawmills)
    B1
Q9Rate of reaction · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Hydrogen peroxide solution decomposes slowly into water and oxygen. When a little solid manganese(IV) oxide is added, oxygen is given off rapidly.2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂

  1. (a) Describe how the student could show that the manganese(IV) oxide is unchanged in mass at the end. [3]
  2. (b) The student repeats the experiment with the same mass of manganese(IV) oxide as a fine powder instead of granules. State and explain the effect on the rate. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Weigh the manganese(IV) oxide before; after the reaction filter the mixture to collect the solid; wash and dry it, then weigh again; the mass is the same.
    B1 weigh before; B1 filter, wash and dry; B1 reweigh, same mass
  2. (b) The rate increases because the powder has a larger surface area, so more collisions happen at the catalyst surface per second.
    B1 faster; B1 larger surface area / more frequent collisions
Q10Rate of reaction · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

0.50 g of calcium carbonate lumps is added to 50 cm³ of 1.0 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂ [Mr: CaCO₃, 100; molar volume 24 dm³ at r.t.p.]

  1. (a) Show that the acid is in excess and calculate the volume of carbon dioxide produced at r.t.p. [3]
  2. (b) For each change, state how the initial rate and the final volume of gas compare with the original experiment.(i) 0.50 g of powdered calcium carbonate is used.(ii) 50 cm³ of 0.50 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid is used.(iii) 1.00 g of calcium carbonate lumps is used. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 120 cm³
    CaCO₃ = 0.50 ÷ 100 = 0.0050 mol, needs 0.010 mol HCl; HCl present = 0.050 mol, so in excess M1; CO₂ = 0.0050 mol M1; × 24 000 = 120 cm³ A1
  2. (b) (i) faster, same volume (120 cm³); (ii) slower, same volume (120 cm³, acid still in excess); (iii) faster or similar rate, double volume (240 cm³)
    B1 each; in (ii) 0.025 mol HCl still exceeds the 0.010 mol needed; in (iii) acid still in excess (0.020 needed), so 0.010 mol CO₂ = 240 cm³ (allow 'faster' as larger total surface area)
Q11Rate of reaction · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why increasing the pressure increases the rate of a reaction between two gases. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why food kept in a refrigerator goes bad more slowly than food left in a hot kitchen in Hyderabad in summer. [2]
  3. (c) A student says 'a catalyst increases the energy of the colliding particles'. Explain why this is wrong. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The same number of gas particles occupy a smaller volume (higher concentration), so collisions are more frequent.
    B1 particles closer together / more per unit volume; B1 more frequent collisions
  2. (b) At low temperature the reacting particles (in the decay reactions) have less kinetic energy; fewer collisions per second have energy ≥ activation energy, so the rate of decay is lower.
    B1 less kinetic energy / move slower; B1 fewer successful collisions (fewer with energy ≥ Ea)
  3. (c) A catalyst does not change the particles' energy; it provides an alternative route with a lower activation energy, so more of the existing collisions are successful.
    B1 particle energy unchanged; B1 lower activation energy pathway
Q12Rate of reaction · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

0.12 g of magnesium ribbon reacts completely with excess dilute sulfuric acid in 40 s.Mg + H₂SO₄ → MgSO₄ + H₂ [Ar: Mg, 24; molar volume 24 dm³ at r.t.p.]

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of hydrogen produced at r.t.p. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean rate of reaction in cm³ of hydrogen per second. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the mean rate in moles of magnesium used per second. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the rate in the first 10 seconds is greater than the mean rate. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 120 cm³
    moles Mg = 0.12 ÷ 24 = 0.0050 = moles H₂ M1; × 24 000 = 120 cm³ A1
  2. (b) 3.0 cm³/s
    120 ÷ 40 = 3.0 B1
  3. (c) 1.25 × 10⁻⁴ mol/s
    0.0050 ÷ 40 = 1.25 × 10⁻⁴ mol/s B1
  4. (d) The acid concentration is highest at the start, so collisions are most frequent then.
    B1
Q13Reversible reactions & equilibrium · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement is correct for a reversible reaction at equilibrium in a closed system?A The forward and reverse reactions have both stopped.B The concentrations of reactants and products are equal.C The rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal.D Only the forward reaction is taking place. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C The rates of the forward and reverse reactions are equal.
    At dynamic equilibrium both reactions continue at equal rates, so concentrations stay constant. B is a common error: concentrations are constant, not necessarily equal.
Q14Reversible reactions & equilibrium · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

CoCl₂·6H₂O ⇌ CoCl₂ + 6H₂O

  1. (a) State the meaning of the symbol ⇌. [1]
  2. (b) Describe what is seen when pink hydrated cobalt(II) chloride is heated, and what is seen when water is then added to the product. [2]
  3. (c) State what is meant by a closed system. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The reaction is reversible.
    B1
  2. (b) It turns blue on heating; adding water turns it pink again.
    B1 pink → blue; B1 blue → pink
  3. (c) No substances can enter or leave (reactants and products cannot escape).
    B1
Q15Reversible reactions & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A sealed glass tube contains an equilibrium mixture of colourless dinitrogen tetroxide and brown nitrogen dioxide.N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) the forward reaction is endothermic

  1. (a) The sealed tube is placed in hot water. State and explain what happens to the colour of the gas. [2]
  2. (b) State and explain the effect on the position of equilibrium of increasing the pressure. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) It becomes darker brown; the forward reaction is endothermic, so increasing temperature moves the position of equilibrium to the right (more NO₂).
    B1 darker; B1 equilibrium shifts in endothermic direction / to the right
  2. (b) Moves to the left; there are fewer moles of gas on the left (1) than on the right (2).
    B1 left; B1 fewer gas molecules on left
Q16Reversible reactions & equilibrium · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)

  1. (a) State and explain the effect of increasing the pressure on the position of equilibrium. [2]

Q18Reversible reactions & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Methanol, used as a fuel, is made from carbon monoxide and hydrogen.CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g)

  1. (a) Explain why a high pressure is used. [2]
  2. (b) The forward reaction is exothermic. Explain why a moderate temperature of about 250 °C is used rather than a much lower temperature. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest one disadvantage of using a very high pressure. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) There are fewer moles of gas on the right (1) than the left (3), so high pressure moves the equilibrium to the right, increasing the yield; it also increases the rate.
    B1 fewer gas moles on right / shifts right; B1 higher yield (allow faster rate)
  2. (b) A low temperature would give a higher yield because the equilibrium shifts in the exothermic direction, but the rate would be too slow; 250 °C is a compromise giving an acceptable yield at a reasonable rate (a catalyst is also used).
    B1 lower T gives higher yield; B1 but rate too slow; B1 compromise between yield and rate
  3. (c) Expensive equipment / energy costs / safety risk.
    B1
Q19Reversible reactions & equilibrium · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Show answer

    B1 increases; B1 fewer gas moles on right
  3. (c) more than 59%
    B1 (yield at 300 °C is 59% at 200 atm and increases with pressure)
