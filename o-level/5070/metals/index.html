Q1Extraction of metals · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which metal is extracted from its oxide by electrolysis rather than by heating with carbon? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A aluminium
    Aluminium is above carbon in the reactivity series, so carbon cannot reduce aluminium oxide. Iron (B) is below carbon and is extracted with carbon (as carbon monoxide) in the blast furnace.
Q2Extraction of metals · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Iron is made in a blast furnace from hematite, coke, limestone and hot air.

  1. (a) State the purpose of each raw material:(i) coke(ii) limestone(iii) hot air [3]
  2. (b) Name the main ore of iron used. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) burns to provide heat and forms carbon monoxide, the reducing agent (ii) removes acidic impurities such as silicon(IV) oxide, forming slag (iii) provides oxygen for the coke to burn
    B1 each.
  2. (b) hematite (iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃)
    B1.

  2. (b) In the reaction in (a)(ii), name the substance that is reduced and the reducing agent. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) C + O₂ → CO₂ then CO₂ + C → 2CO (ii) Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
    B1 C + O₂ → CO₂; B1 CO₂ + C → 2CO; B1 balanced reduction equation.
  2. (b) iron(III) oxide is reduced; carbon monoxide is the reducing agent
    B1 each.
Q4Extraction of metals · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Limestone decomposes in the furnace. Write the equation. [1]
  2. (b) Calcium oxide reacts with silicon(IV) oxide (sand) to form slag. Write the equation and name the slag. [2]
  3. (c) Explain this reaction in terms of the types of oxide involved. [1]
  4. (d) Explain how the slag and the molten iron are removed separately. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
    B1.
  2. (b) CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃; calcium silicate
    B1 equation; B1 name.

    B1.
Q5Extraction of metals · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of aluminium oxide (from bauxite) dissolved in molten cryolite, using carbon electrodes.

  1. (a) Explain why aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite rather than melted on its own. [2]
  2. (b) Write the equations for the reactions at the cathode and at the anode. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the carbon anodes must be replaced regularly. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Aluminium oxide has a very high melting point (over 2000 °C); the solution in molten cryolite works at a much lower temperature (about 950 °C), saving energy and cost.
    B1 lowers the operating temperature; B1 saves energy / cost.
  2. (b) cathode: Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al; anode: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻
    B1 each.
  3. (c) The oxygen produced at the hot anodes reacts with the carbon to form carbon dioxide, so the anodes burn away.
    B1 oxygen reacts with carbon; B1 forms CO₂ / anodes wear away.
Q6Extraction of metals · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Predict which of these oxides can be reduced by heating with carbon: magnesium oxide, zinc oxide, copper(II) oxide, aluminium oxide. Explain your choice. [3]
  2. (b) A student heats black copper(II) oxide with carbon powder in a crucible. Write the balanced equation and state what is seen at the end. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) zinc oxide and copper(II) oxide; zinc and copper are less reactive than carbon, so carbon can remove the oxygen. Magnesium and aluminium are more reactive than carbon.
    B1 zinc oxide; B1 copper(II) oxide (and no others); B1 reason based on position relative to carbon.
  2. (b) 2CuO + C → 2Cu + CO₂; pink-brown (orange-brown) solid copper forms.
    B1 equation; B1 observation.
Q7Extraction of metals · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be obtained from 800 tonnes of iron(III) oxide. (Ar: Fe = 56, O = 16) [2]
  2. (b) A sample of hematite ore is 90 % iron(III) oxide by mass. Calculate the maximum mass of iron from 1000 tonnes of this ore. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the actual mass of iron produced is less than this. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 560 tonnes
    M1 Mr Fe₂O₃ = 160 and 112 t Fe in every 160 t; A1 800 × 112 ÷ 160 = 560 t.
  2. (b) 630 tonnes
    M1 900 t Fe₂O₃; A1 900 × 112 ÷ 160 = 630 t.
  3. (c) Not all the iron oxide is reduced / some iron is lost in the slag.
    B1.
Q8Extraction of metals · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The overall reaction is 2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂. Calculate the mass of aluminium produced from 51 tonnes of aluminium oxide. (Ar: Al = 27, O = 16) [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of oxygen produced at the same time. [1]
  3. (c) Give two reasons why recycling aluminium cans is better than extracting new aluminium. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 27 tonnes
    M1 Mr Al₂O₃ = 102, 54 t Al per 102 t; A1 51 × 54 ÷ 102 = 27 t.
  2. (b) 24 tonnes
    B1 51 − 27 = 24 t (conservation of mass).
  3. (c) Recycling uses much less electrical energy; it conserves bauxite ore; it reduces waste in landfill; less mining and land damage.
    1 mark each for any two.

    B1.

Data for four metals:metal: density / g/cm³, relative electrical conductivity, cost, resistance to corrosionW: 2.7, 61, low, goodX: 8.9, 100, high, goodY: 7.9, 17, low, poorZ: 19.3, 73, very high, excellent

Q14Properties & uses of metals · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Aluminium is high in the reactivity series but aluminium cooking pots do not react with water. Explain why. [2]
  2. (b) A piece of aluminium foil is placed in dilute hydrochloric acid. At first nothing happens; after a few minutes bubbles appear quickly. Suggest an explanation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Aluminium is covered by a thin, unreactive layer of aluminium oxide that sticks to the surface and stops water reaching the metal.
    B1 oxide layer; B1 layer protects / prevents further reaction.
  2. (b) The acid first reacts with / removes the oxide layer (no gas). Once the metal is exposed, the reactive aluminium reacts with the acid, giving hydrogen.
    B1 oxide layer removed by acid first; B1 then Al reacts producing hydrogen.

Q16Reactivity series & displacement · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Q17Reactivity series & displacement · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student places small pieces of metals P, Q and R in solutions of their nitrates and records whether a reaction happens (✓) or not (✗).P in Q nitrate: ✓ P in R nitrate: ✓Q in P nitrate: ✗ Q in R nitrate: ✓R in P nitrate: ✗ R in Q nitrate: ✗

  1. (a) Put the metals P, Q and R in order of reactivity, most reactive first. Explain how you decided. [2]
  2. (b) Predict whether R would react with dilute hydrochloric acid if R is copper. Explain. [1]
  3. (c) Give one safety precaution and one way to make the comparison fair. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P > Q > R. P displaces both Q and R; Q displaces only R; R displaces neither.
    B1 order; B1 explanation.
  2. (b) No; copper is below hydrogen in the reactivity series.
    B1.
  3. (c) Wear eye protection; use the same size / mass of each metal and the same volume and concentration of solution.
    B1 each.

Q21Corrosion & rust prevention · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student sets up four test tubes, each containing an iron nail, and leaves them for one week.A: nail in tap water, open to the airB: nail in boiled water with a layer of oil on topC: nail in dry air with anhydrous calcium chloride, tube stopperedD: nail in salt water, open to the air

  1. (a) In which tubes does the nail rust? [1]
  2. (b) Explain the purpose of boiling the water and adding a layer of oil in tube B. [2]
  3. (c) What do the results show is needed for iron to rust? [1]
  4. (d) Suggest which tube rusts fastest and why. [1]

  1. (a) A and D

  3. (c) Both water and oxygen (air).

    B1.

Q23Corrosion & rust prevention · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Galvanised steel sheets are widely used for roofs of sheds and cattle shelters in rural Punjab.

  1. (a) State what is meant by galvanising. [1]
  2. (b) A galvanised steel roof sheet is scratched, exposing the steel. Explain why the steel still does not rust. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why a painted steel gate starts to rust when the paint is chipped. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Coating iron or steel with a layer of zinc.
    B1.
  2. (b) Zinc is more reactive than iron, so zinc loses electrons (is oxidised) in preference to iron. The zinc corrodes instead of the iron (sacrificial protection).
    B1 zinc more reactive; B1 zinc oxidised / loses electrons instead of iron; B1 iron protected even where exposed.
  3. (c) Paint is only a barrier; once broken, water and oxygen reach the iron.
    B1.
Q24Corrosion & rust prevention · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Give the chemical name of rust and write a word equation for its formation. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why cars in Karachi rust faster than cars in a dry inland city. [2]
  3. (c) Give two barrier methods for preventing rusting of a bicycle and say which part each is used for. [2]

  1. (a) hydrated iron(III) oxide; iron + water + oxygen → hydrated iron(III) oxide

    B1 more moisture; B1 salt speeds up rusting.
  3. (c) Paint for the frame; oil or grease for the chain; plastic coating for the basket / handlebars.
    1 mark each for any two matched methods.
Q25Corrosion & rust prevention · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A student pushes damp iron wool into the bottom of a measuring cylinder and turns it upside down in a trough of water. The volume of air trapped is 100.0 cm³ at the start. After eight days, when the reading has stopped changing, the volume is 79.2 cm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of the air used up. [2]
  2. (b) Name the gas that was used up and explain why the water level rose. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the student waited until the reading stopped changing. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest one source of error and one improvement. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20.8 %
    M1 100.0 − 79.2 = 20.8 cm³; A1 20.8 ÷ 100.0 × 100 = 20.8 %.
  2. (b) Oxygen; it reacted with the iron to form rust, so the volume of gas fell and water moved up to take its place.
    B1 oxygen; B1 explanation.
  3. (c) To make sure all the oxygen had reacted.
    B1.
  4. (d) Error: temperature or pressure changes alter the gas volume; reading not at eye level / meniscus. Improvement: keep at constant temperature and read at the same temperature; use more iron wool (or wet it) so all the oxygen reacts; repeat and average.
    B1 error; B1 matching improvement.
Q26Alloys · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Sialkot is known for making surgical instruments and cutlery from stainless steel.

Q28Alloys · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain, in terms of structure, why brass is harder than pure copper. You may describe a diagram in words. [3]
  2. (b) Brass is used for door handles and musical instruments. Give one reason, other than hardness, why brass is used rather than iron. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) In pure copper the atoms are all the same size and arranged in regular layers that slide easily. In brass the zinc atoms are a different size; they distort the layers so the layers cannot slide over each other easily, so brass is harder.
    B1 pure metal: regular layers slide easily; B1 alloy: atoms of different sizes disrupt layers; B1 layers cannot slide easily → harder.
  2. (b) It does not rust / resists corrosion; attractive gold colour.
    B1.

Q30Alloys · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A 250 g sample of brass contains 64 % copper by mass. Calculate the masses of copper and zinc in the sample. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the ratio of copper atoms to zinc atoms. Give your answer as x : 1 to 3 significant figures. (Ar: Cu = 64, Zn = 65) [2]
  3. (c) Explain why brass is described as a mixture and not a compound. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) copper 160 g; zinc 90 g
    B1 0.64 × 250 = 160; B1 250 − 160 = 90.
  2. (b) 1.81 : 1
    M1 moles Cu = 2.50, moles Zn = 1.385; A1 2.50 ÷ 1.385 = 1.81.
  3. (c) The metals are not chemically combined in a fixed ratio; the composition of brass can vary (and it keeps the metallic properties of its components).
    B1 not chemically bonded / no fixed formula; B1 variable composition.
