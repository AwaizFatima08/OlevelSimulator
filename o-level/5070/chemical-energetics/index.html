Q6Energy profiles & activation energy · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A mixture of hydrogen and oxygen can be kept at room temperature without reacting, but it explodes when a spark is applied. Explain this using the idea of activation energy. [2]
  2. (b) The reaction is 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O and it is strongly exothermic. Describe the reaction pathway diagram for this reaction. Say where reactants and products are and how ΔH and the activation energy are shown. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) At room temperature too few colliding molecules have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy, so the rate is negligible. The spark gives molecules enough energy to exceed the activation energy; the energy released then supplies other molecules.
    B1 particles at room temperature do not have energy ≥ Ea; B1 spark supplies the activation energy (then the reaction sustains itself).
  2. (b) Reactants (2H₂ + O₂) on the left at a higher level; a curve rising to a peak then falling to products (2H₂O) at a lower level. Activation energy: arrow from reactants up to the peak. ΔH: arrow from reactants down to products (negative).
    B1 products below reactants; B1 Ea from reactant level to top of curve; B1 ΔH arrow from reactants down to products.
Q7Energy profiles & activation energy · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Hydrogen peroxide decomposes: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. ΔH = −98 kJ per mole of H₂O₂. The activation energy is 75 kJ/mol without a catalyst and 58 kJ/mol with manganese(IV) oxide.

  1. (a) Calculate the decrease in activation energy caused by the catalyst. [1]
  2. (b) State the value of ΔH for the catalysed reaction. [1]
  3. (c) Explain, in terms of particles and energy, why the catalysed reaction is faster. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 17 kJ/mol
    B1 75 − 58 = 17.
  2. (b) −98 kJ/mol
    B1 a catalyst does not change ΔH.
  3. (c) With a lower activation energy, a larger fraction of colliding particles have energy equal to or above the activation energy, so there are more successful collisions per second.
    B1 more particles have energy ≥ Ea; B1 more successful collisions per unit time.

Q11Energy profiles & activation energy · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Data for two reactions:Reaction A: activation energy 30 kJ/mol, ΔH = −20 kJ/molReaction B: activation energy 180 kJ/mol, ΔH = −400 kJ/mol

  1. (a) Which reaction would you expect to be faster at room temperature? Explain. [2]
  2. (b) Which reaction releases more energy per mole? Calculate how many times more. [2]
  3. (c) A student says: 'Reaction B has a very negative ΔH, so it must be very fast.' Evaluate this statement. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Reaction A: its activation energy (30 kJ/mol) is much lower, so many more colliding particles have enough energy to react.
    B1 A; B1 lower Ea → greater proportion of collisions successful.
  2. (b) Reaction B; 20 times more
    B1 B; B1 400 ÷ 20 = 20.
  3. (c) The statement is wrong. ΔH only tells how much energy is released overall; rate depends on the activation energy. B has a high activation energy, so it is slow at room temperature even though it is very exothermic.
    B1 ΔH does not determine rate; B1 rate depends on Ea, which is high for B.

Q13Exothermic & endothermic · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

  1. (a) 158 kJ/mol
    M1 ΔH = (436 + F–F) − 2 × 568; M1 −542 = 436 + x − 1136; A1 x = 158.
  2. (b) The F–F bond is weak (needs little energy to break), and strong bonds form in the products.
    B1.

Bond energies in kJ/mol: C–H 413, C–C 347, C–O 358, O–H 464, O=O 498, C=O 805

  1. (a) List the bonds broken in one molecule of ethanol, C₂H₅OH. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate ΔH for the complete combustion of ethanol: C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O. [3]
  3. (c) The data-book value is −1367 kJ/mol. Suggest why the value calculated from bond energies is different. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5 C–H, 1 C–C, 1 C–O, 1 O–H
    B1 all four types with correct numbers.
  2. (b) −1276 kJ/mol
    M1 broken 5 × 413 + 347 + 358 + 464 + 3 × 498 = 4728; M1 made 4 × 805 + 6 × 464 = 6004; A1 −1276.
  3. (c) Bond energies are average values from many compounds (and the data assume all substances are gases).
    B1.
Q30Bond energy calculations · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Bond energies in kJ/mol: O–H 464, O–O 146, O=O 498

  1. (a) Hydrogen peroxide decomposes: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. Each H₂O₂ molecule has the structure H–O–O–H. Calculate ΔH for the equation as written. [3]
  2. (b) Show that, in this reaction, the only bonds that really change are two O–O single bonds broken and one O=O double bond made, and that this gives the same answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) −206 kJ/mol
    M1 broken 2 × (2 × 464 + 146) = 2148; M1 made 4 × 464 + 498 = 2354; A1 −206.
  2. (b) The four O–H bonds are present on both sides and cancel. ΔH = 2 × 146 − 498 = −206 kJ/mol.
    B1 O–H bonds cancel; B1 292 − 498 = −206.
