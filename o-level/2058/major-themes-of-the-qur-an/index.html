Q1Thematic essay on the set passages · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Using at least two of the set passages, describe the blessings that God has given to human beings. [6]
  2. (b) Explain one way Muslims can show thanks for these blessings. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Creation of humans and earlier peoples, the earth as a resting place, the sky as a canopy, rain and fruit (2.21–22); creation from a clot, knowledge and the pen (96.1–5); mercy, guidance to the straight path (Sura 1); protection from evil (114); acceptance of repentance (2.37).
    1 mark each for any six relevant blessings correctly linked to a set passage, e.g.: life / creation (2.21, 96.2); earth spread out (2.22); sky as a canopy (2.22); rain and produce (2.22); knowledge and writing (96.4–5); guidance (1.6); mercy (1.1–3); refuge from evil whispers (114); forgiveness (2.37); shelter, guidance and provision (93.6–8). Maximum 3 if only one passage is used.
  2. (b) For example, by using the gift of knowledge to help others, such as teaching children to read.
    1 mark for a relevant action, 1 for explaining how it shows gratitude.
Q2Thematic essay on the set passages · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Using Sura 112 and Sura 2.255, write about how God describes Himself in the Qur'an. [6]
  2. (b) Why is it important for Muslims to know how God describes Himself? Give one reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) He is One, eternal and without children, parents or equal (112); He is the Ever-Living, Self-Sustaining, never sleeping; owner of the heavens and earth; all-knowing; none can intercede without His permission; His authority covers everything; the Most High, the Supreme (2.255).
    1 mark each for any six: One; eternal / self-sufficient; neither begets nor is begotten; none comparable (all 112); Ever-Living; Self-Subsisting; no sleep or slumber; owns everything; intercession only by His leave; knows past and future; His throne / authority over heavens and earth; never tires; Most High, Most Great (2.255). At least two from each passage for full marks.
  2. (b) So they worship Him correctly and do not attribute to Him human weaknesses or partners.
    1 mark for a reason, 1 for development (e.g. avoids shirk; builds trust because He never sleeps or tires).
Q3Thematic essay on the set passages · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe what the set passages say about angels. [4]
  2. (b) Explain what Muslims can learn from the angels' obedience in these passages. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) God told the angels about creating a vicegerent; they questioned and admitted their knowledge came from God; they prostrated to Adam at God's command (2.30–34); they glorify God and seek forgiveness for those on earth (42.5); Jesus was strengthened by the Holy Spirit (5.110).
    1 mark each for any four: angels informed of Adam's appointment; they asked about corruption and bloodshed; they knew only what God taught them; they bowed to Adam; Iblis refused (credit as contrast); angels praise God; they ask forgiveness for people (42.5); support of Jesus by the Holy Spirit (5.110).
  2. (b) Muslims should obey God humbly, admit the limits of their knowledge and avoid arrogance like Iblis's; they can also pray for others as angels do.
    Up to 4 marks for developed points, e.g.: obedience to God's commands without arrogance (1+1); humility about knowledge — saying 'I don't know' (1); praying for forgiveness for others (1).
Q4Thematic essay on the set passages · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about God's relationship with the Prophets Adam and Abraham as shown in Sura 2.30–37 and Sura 6.75–79. [6]
  2. (b) What do these two stories teach Muslims about learning and faith? Explain briefly. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Adam: chosen as vicegerent, taught the names, honoured by the angels' prostration, warned about Satan, forgiven after repentance. Abraham: shown the kingdom of the heavens and earth, guided by reflection to reject the star, moon and sun, made certain in faith and turned to God alone.
    Up to 6 marks: 1 mark per accurate point, maximum 4 from one prophet. Adam: appointed khalifa; given knowledge of names; angels ordered to bow; told to avoid the tree; forgiven when he repented. Abraham: shown the dominion of heaven and earth; given certainty; guided through observing heavenly bodies; rejected false gods; declared pure monotheism. Credit a link showing God as Teacher and Guide in both.
  2. (b) Both prophets were taught or guided by God, showing that knowledge and reflection lead to stronger faith.
    1 mark for the shared lesson (God gives knowledge / guidance), 1 for applying it to Muslims today.

Q6Thematic essay on the set passages · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the set passages present the natural world as signs of God. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how this teaching should affect the way Muslims treat the environment, for example during a water shortage in Quetta or a heatwave in Spain. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Night, day, sun and moon are His signs (41.37); He made the earth a resting place, the sky a canopy and sent rain to bring forth fruit (2.21–22); He originated the heavens and earth (6.101); the heavenly bodies Abraham saw pointed to their Creator (6.75–79); the creation of humans from a clot (96.2).
    1 mark each for any five accurate points linked to passages: 41.37 night/day; 41.37 sun/moon not to be worshipped; 2.22 earth and sky; 2.22 rain and fruit; 6.101 Originator of heavens and earth; 6.76–78 star, moon, sun set — they are not gods; 96.2 human creation; 2.255 heavens and earth belong to God.
  2. (b) If nature is a sign and gift of God held in trust, Muslims should conserve water, avoid pollution and share resources fairly.
    Up to 3 marks: humans are vicegerents / trustees (1); waste and damage misuse God's signs (1); practical action, e.g. saving water at wudu, planting trees, supporting relief (1).

(a) God is One with no equal (112); people must not set up rivals to Him (2.22); do not prostrate to the sun or moon but to their Creator (41.37); Abraham rejected the star, moon and sun and turned to God alone (6.79); 'You alone we worship' (Sura 1); there is no god but Him (6.102); pray and sacrifice to your Lord (108).

  2. (b) For example, letting the desire for wealth or social-media fame control their decisions more than God's guidance.

  2. (b) 'Guidance is a gift from God, not something people can earn.' To what extent do the set passages support this view? [3]

(a) He is asked for guidance to the straight path (Sura 1); He taught Adam the names and forgave him (2.31, 2.37); He showed Abraham the kingdom of the heavens and earth (6.75); He taught humans by the pen (96.4–5); He found the Prophet (pbuh) unaware and guided him (93.7); He gives refuge from misleading whispers (114).

Q12God's relationship with the created world · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the main themes of Sura 1 (al-Fatiha). [4]
  2. (b) Al-Fatiha is recited in every unit (rak'a) of prayer. Explain why this sura is so important for Muslims. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) All praise belongs to God, Lord of all the worlds; He is the Most Merciful; He is Master of the Day of Judgement; worship and help are sought from Him alone; believers ask Him for guidance to the straight path, the path of those He has blessed, not of those who earned His anger or went astray.
    1 mark each for any four of: praise and thanks are due to God alone; He is Lord / Sustainer of all the worlds; He is Most Gracious, Most Merciful; He is Master of the Day of Judgement (accountability); worship is for Him alone; help is sought only from Him; prayer for guidance to the straight path; the path of those He has favoured, not of those who incurred anger or went astray.
  2. (b) It sums up the core of Islam — praise, God's mercy, accountability, sole worship and the request for guidance — so repeating it many times a day keeps a Muslim's relationship with God alive.
    Up to 4 marks for developed points, e.g.: prayer is not complete without it (1); it renews the believer's promise to worship God alone several times a day (1); it reminds them of the Day of Judgement and so of their conduct (1); the daily request for guidance shows humans depend on God, applied to an example such as choices at work or school (1).
Q13God's relationship with the created world · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the main themes of Sura 114 (an-Nas). [4]
  2. (b) Hamza, a student in Karachi, is tempted to copy a friend's answers in a test. Explain how the teaching of Sura 114 could help him. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Believers are told to seek refuge with God, who is Lord, King and God of mankind, from the evil of the sneaking whisperer who puts evil suggestions into hearts, whether from jinn or from people.
    1 mark each for any four of: seek refuge / protection in God; He is Lord (Sustainer) of mankind; King of mankind; God (the only one worthy of worship) of mankind; protection from the evil of the whisperer; the whisperer withdraws / retreats when God is remembered; it whispers into human hearts; evil suggestions come from jinn and from human beings.
  2. (b) He can recognise the temptation as an evil whisper, turn to God for protection by remembering Him, and choose honesty knowing God is his Lord and King.
    Up to 4 marks: recognising the urge as a whisper from Shaytan or from bad company (1); seeking refuge in God / reciting the sura or remembering God makes the whisperer retreat (1); remembering God is King of mankind and sees him (1); acting honestly and avoiding friends who push him to cheat (1).

Maximum 2 if only one sura is used.

Q15God's relationship with the created world · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the themes of Sura 2.21–22. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how farmers and families in Pakistan can respond to the blessings described in this passage. [4]

Q17God's relationship with the created world · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the themes of Sura 99 (az-Zalzala). [4]
  2. (b) Explain what lessons Muslims can learn from this sura about small actions. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) On the Last Day the earth will be shaken violently and throw out its burdens; people will ask what is happening; the earth will report what happened on it, as God inspired it; people will come forward in groups to be shown their deeds; every atom's weight of good or evil will be seen.
    1 mark each for any four of: the great earthquake at the end of the world; the earth throws out its burdens (the dead / what it contains); humans are bewildered and ask what is wrong with it; the earth will tell its news / bear witness, by God's command; people will emerge in separate groups; they will be shown their deeds; even an atom's weight of good will be seen; even an atom's weight of evil will be seen (complete accountability).

    Up to 4 marks, e.g.: small good deeds such as smiling, removing litter or helping a neighbour carry shopping are rewarded (1+1); small wrongs such as minor lies or rudeness are not ignored, so Muslims should avoid them (1); this encourages sincerity and constant self-examination, since God and even the earth witness everything (1).

Q22God's relationship with His messengers · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) God showed Abraham the kingdom of the heavens and earth so he would be certain in faith; Abraham saw a star, the moon and the sun, and as each set he rejected it as a god because it disappears; he declared himself free of what his people associated with God and turned to the Creator of the heavens and earth alone.
    1 mark each for any four of: God showed Abraham the dominion of the heavens and earth; to make him firm / certain in faith; he observed a star, said 'this is my Lord', then rejected it when it set; the same with the moon; the same with the sun, though it was greater; he did not love things that set / disappear; he declared himself free of shirk; he turned his face to the Creator of the heavens and earth, upright, not among the polytheists.
  2. (b) Faith can be strengthened by thinking and observing creation; created things are limited and cannot be God; believers should be prepared to stand against wrong beliefs around them with courage.
    Up to 4 marks for developed points, e.g.: using reason and observation to strengthen faith, e.g. studying astronomy (1+1); anything that changes or disappears, including wealth or fame, cannot be worthy of worship (1); courage to hold to the truth even when family or society believe otherwise (1).
Q23God's relationship with His messengers · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the favours and miracles that God mentions in Sura 5.110 regarding the Prophet Jesus. [5]
  2. (b) The phrase 'by God's permission' is repeated in this passage. Explain why this is important for Muslim belief about Jesus. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) God reminds Jesus son of Mary of His favour on him and his mother; strengthened him with the Holy Spirit; he spoke to people in the cradle and as an adult; he was taught the Book, wisdom, the Torah and the Gospel; by God's permission he made a bird of clay that became a living bird, healed the blind and the leper and brought the dead to life; God held back the Children of Israel from harming him when they called his miracles magic.
    1 mark each for any five of: favour on Jesus and on his mother Mary; support with the Holy Spirit (Jibril); speaking in the cradle and in maturity; taught writing / the Book and wisdom; taught the Torah and the Gospel; shaped a bird of clay and breathed into it and it became a bird, by God's leave; healed those born blind and lepers, by God's leave; raised the dead, by God's leave; protected from the Children of Israel who said his signs were magic.
  2. (b) It shows that the miracles came from God's power, not Jesus's own, so Jesus is honoured as a great prophet but is a human servant of God, not divine.
    Up to 3 marks: the power belonged to God alone (1); Jesus was a chosen messenger, deeply respected in Islam (1); this protects tawhid by rejecting the idea that Jesus is God or the son of God (1).
Q24God's relationship with His messengers · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the themes of Sura 108 (al-Kawthar). [4]
  2. (b) Explain how the message of this sura can encourage Muslims who are mocked for their faith. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) God has given the Prophet (pbuh) abundant good (al-Kawthar); in thanks he should pray to his Lord and offer sacrifice; it is his enemy who will be cut off, not him.
    1 mark each for any four of: God has granted the Prophet (pbuh) abundance / al-Kawthar (a river or great blessing in Paradise); God's generosity and care for His messenger; so pray to God alone; and sacrifice for Him alone; worship and sacrifice should be sincere for God; the one who hates / mocks the Prophet (pbuh) will be the one cut off; reassurance against those who said he had no lasting legacy.
  2. (b) God's support is greater than people's insults; Muslims should respond with prayer, thankfulness and good character rather than anger, trusting that truth lasts and mockery fades.
    Up to 4 marks for developed points, e.g.: God honoured the Prophet (pbuh) when he was mocked, so believers are not truly harmed by insults (1); respond by worship and gratitude rather than revenge (1); example, e.g. a student mocked for praying at school continues calmly (1); those who oppose truth lose in the end — patience is rewarded (1).
Q25God's relationship with His messengers · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Suras 93 and 108 were both revealed to reassure the Prophet (pbuh) during difficult times. Explain how each sura shows God's care for him. [6]
  2. (b) Which of these two suras do you think gives the stronger message of hope? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sura 93: God has not abandoned him; the future is better; God will satisfy him; reminders of shelter as an orphan, guidance and provision. Sura 108: he has been given abundance; he is to pray and sacrifice; his enemy, not he, will be cut off.
    3 marks per sura. 93: assurance that God has neither left him nor become displeased (1); promise that the later life / Hereafter will be better and he will be pleased (1); reminder of past care — orphanhood, guidance, wealth (1). 108: gift of al-Kawthar / abundance (1); direction to respond with prayer and sacrifice (1); his enemies will be the ones without legacy (1).
  2. (b) A reasoned choice, e.g. Sura 93, because it links God's past care to a promise for the future.
    1 mark for a clear judgement, 1 for a supported reason drawn from the sura's content (either sura may be chosen).
Q26God in Himself · Core · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the main themes of Sura 112 (al-Ikhlas). [4]
  2. (b) Explain how a Muslim can show belief in the teaching of this sura in daily life. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) God is One and unique; He is eternal and absolute, needing nothing while all depend on Him; He has no children and no parents; nothing is comparable to Him.
    1 mark each for any four of: God is One (tawhid); He is unique / without partner; He is eternal / self-sufficient, the One all creatures turn to in need; He has no offspring; He was not born of anyone (has no beginning); nothing in creation is equal or similar to Him; the sura rejects ideas of God having a family. Do not credit background about the sura's name or place of revelation.
  2. (b) By worshipping and relying on God alone, avoiding anything that gives His position to another, and treating all people as equal servants of the one God.
    Up to 4 marks: 1 per relevant action, +1 for developing it with a reason or example. Points may include: praying and making du'a to God alone, not to any created being; relying on God in difficulty rather than on luck or superstition; not letting money, status or people take first place in one's life; treating all people as equal because all are created by the one God; sincerity (doing good for God's pleasure, not show).
Q27God in Himself · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about what Sura 2.255 (Ayat al-Kursi) teaches about God's power and knowledge. [5]
  2. (b) Many Muslims recite this passage before sleeping or before a journey. Explain why its teaching might give a believer comfort. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) God is ever-living and sustains everything; He never tires or sleeps; He owns all that is in the heavens and earth; no one can intercede with Him except by His permission; He knows everything past and future while people know only what He allows; His authority covers the heavens and earth and guarding them does not tire Him; He is the Most High, the Supreme.
    1 mark each for any five of: God is the Ever-Living; He is self-subsisting and keeps all creation in existence; He is never overtaken by drowsiness or sleep; everything in the heavens and earth belongs to Him; no one can intercede except with His permission; He knows what is before and behind people (complete knowledge); humans know only what He wills; His throne/authority extends over the heavens and earth; preserving creation does not weary Him; He is the Most High, the Most Great.
  2. (b) Because God never sleeps or tires and controls everything, the believer feels protected and can rely on Him when vulnerable.
    Up to 3 marks for a developed explanation, e.g.: God is awake and watchful when the believer is asleep (1); He guards the heavens and earth without tiring, so nothing is beyond His protection (1); this reduces fear and builds trust (tawakkul) during travel, illness or exams (1).
Q28God in Himself · Standard · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the themes of Sura 6.101–103. [4]
  2. (b) The passage says that no human vision can grasp God. Explain how this teaching should affect the way Muslims think about God. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) God is the Originator of the heavens and earth; He cannot have a son as He has no partner; He created everything and knows everything; He alone deserves worship and takes care of all affairs; no vision can grasp Him but He sees all; He is the Subtle, the All-Aware.
    1 mark each for any four of: God originated the heavens and the earth from nothing; the idea of God having a son is rejected (He has no consort); He created all things; He has knowledge of all things; He is the Lord and there is no god but Him, so worship Him; He is the guardian/disposer of all affairs; eyes cannot perceive Him; He perceives all eyes / sees everything; He is Subtle and Well-Acquainted.
  2. (b) Muslims should not picture God as a created form or make images of Him; they understand Him through His names, attributes and signs, while knowing He sees them at all times.
    Up to 4 marks for developed points, e.g.: God is beyond human imagination, so Muslims do not make images or statues of Him (1+1); they come to know Him through His attributes and the signs of creation rather than physical form (1); because He sees everything even though He cannot be seen, believers should behave well in private as in public (1+1).
Q29God in Himself · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Outline what each of the following passages teaches about God: (i) Sura 41.37, (ii) Sura 42.4–5. [6]
  2. (b) A classmate says that admiring the sun and moon is the same as worshipping them. Using Sura 41.37, explain why a Muslim would disagree. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Night, day, sun and moon are signs of God; people should not prostrate to the sun or moon but only to God who created them. (ii) Everything in the heavens and earth belongs to God, the Most High and Great; the heavens almost burst from His majesty; the angels glorify Him and seek forgiveness for those on earth; He is the Forgiving, the Merciful.
    3 marks for each passage. 41.37: night and day are among His signs (1); sun and moon are signs too, created by Him (1); worship / prostration belongs only to God the Creator, not to created objects (1). 42.4–5: all in the heavens and earth belong to Him / He is Most High, Most Great (1); the heavens nearly split apart from above, showing His majesty (1); angels praise Him and ask forgiveness for people on earth, and He is Oft-Forgiving, Most Merciful (1).
  2. (b) Admiring creation as a sign that points to God is encouraged; worship and prostration are directed only to the Creator, not to what He made.
    1 mark: the passage presents the sun and moon as signs that lead people to recognise God. 1 mark: it forbids prostrating to them; reflection on nature is praise of the Creator, not worship of the creation.
Q30God in Himself · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Sura 112 and Sura 6.101–103 both deny that God has offspring. Explain how each passage expresses God's uniqueness. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how belief in God's uniqueness can help a Muslim living in a multi-faith city such as Toronto or Karachi to relate to people of other faiths. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sura 112: God is One, self-sufficient, neither begets nor was begotten, and nothing is like Him. 6.101–103: as Originator of everything He has no consort and so no son; He is Creator of all and beyond sight.
    2 marks per passage. Sura 112: He is One and absolute / eternal (1); He neither has children nor parents and has no equal (1). 6.101–103: He originated the heavens and earth and has no partner, so cannot have a son (1); He created and knows everything and no vision can encompass Him, so He is unlike His creation (1).
  2. (b) It gives the Muslim a clear identity in belief while reminding them that all people are God's creation, so they can live respectfully and justly alongside others without compromising their faith.
    Up to 4 marks for a developed answer, e.g.: belief is clear and firm, so the Muslim can explain it calmly and confidently (1); all humans are created by the one God, so neighbours of other faiths deserve kindness and fair treatment (1); no compulsion in religion — respect does not mean adopting others' beliefs (1); co-operation on shared good works (charity, community service) with a concrete example (1).
