  1. (a) Describe the events of the first revelation to the Prophet (pbuh) and what happened immediately afterwards. [10]
  2. (b) What can Muslim husbands and wives learn from Khadija's response? Explain your answer. [4]

    Indicative content: Age about 40 / month of Ramadan; retreat in the cave of Hira on Mount Nur; Jibril appeared and commanded 'Read'; the Prophet (pbuh) replied that he could not read; the angel embraced / squeezed him three times; the first verses of Sura 96.1–5 were revealed; he returned trembling and asked to be wrapped up; Khadija reassured him (he is kind to relatives, helps the weak, honours guests); visit to Waraqa bin Naufal; Waraqa confirmed it was the angel who came to Moses and predicted opposition.   1. (a) Write about the first three years of the Prophet's (pbuh) preaching, when he invited people to Islam privately. Name the first people who accepted his message. [10]

Show answer
  1. (a) Preaching was private for about three years; the first believers were Khadija, 'Ali, Zayd ibn Haritha and Abu Bakr; Abu Bakr brought others such as 'Uthman, al-Zubayr, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas and Talha; believers met quietly, including at the house of al-Arqam.
    Content points (credit any relevant, accurate point): after the first revelation (610 CE) the Prophet (pbuh) first told those closest to him; Khadija was the first to believe; 'Ali, a boy in his household, accepted; Zayd ibn Haritha, his freed slave and adopted son; Abu Bakr, his close friend, a respected merchant; Abu Bakr used his influence to bring 'Uthman ibn 'Affan, al-Zubayr ibn al-'Awwam, 'Abd al-Rahman ibn 'Awf, Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas, Talha ibn 'Ubaydullah; early converts included poor people and slaves such as Bilal; they prayed in secret, sometimes in the valleys outside Makka; the house of al-Arqam ibn Abi al-Arqam became a meeting and teaching place; the number of believers grew slowly (a few dozen); the period ended when God commanded open preaching to his relatives.     Points may include: the believers were few and weak; Makkan leaders were powerful and could crush the message early; time was needed to teach the basics of belief and prayer; a firm core group could later spread the message; it shows wisdom and planning alongside trust in God; Muslims today can learn to plan carefully before starting a big task. 4 marks: a well-developed, reasoned answer with a relevant example; 3: good reasoning, partly developed; 2: some relevant reasoning; 1: a simple statement.

Show answer
  1. (a) Most Arabs worshipped idols, about 360 around the Ka'ba, alongside a vague belief in Allah; a few hanifs kept Ibrahim's faith; there were some Jews and Christians; society was tribal with constant feuds; drinking, gambling and usury were common; some buried baby daughters alive; women, slaves and orphans had few rights; but Arabs valued hospitality, courage and poetry.
