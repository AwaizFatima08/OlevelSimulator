Q1Revelation of the Qur'an · Core · Paper 1 style · 14 marks
  1. (a) Describe the events of the first revelation to the Prophet (pbuh) in the cave of Hira. [10]
  2. (b) What lesson can a Muslim student today draw from the fact that the first word revealed was a command to read? [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Prophet (pbuh), aged about 40, used to retreat to the cave of Hira on Mount Nur near Makka to reflect and worship; in 610 CE, during Ramadan, the angel Jibril appeared and commanded him to read/recite; he replied that he could not read; the angel pressed him tightly three times; then the angel conveyed the first verses (Sura 96, al-'Alaq, ayat 1–5) about God who created humans from a clot and taught by the pen; the Prophet (pbuh) returned home trembling and asked Khadija to cover him.
    Indicative content: Retreat/meditation in Hira; Mount Nur, near Makka; age about 40 / 610 CE; month of Ramadan (Night of Power); angel Jibril appeared; command to read/recite; reply 'I cannot read'; squeezed three times; first verses were Sura 96.1–5; meaning (God creates, teaches by the pen); went home frightened; asked to be wrapped up / covered. Level 3 (6–8): a mostly accurate answer covering the main points, with some detail but gaps or little supporting evidence. Level 2 (3–5): a general answer with some relevant points but limited detail or some errors. Level 1 (1–2): a few basic or partly relevant points. 0: nothing creditworthy.
  2. (b) Seeking knowledge is a religious duty, so a student should treat study (of religion and of the world) as worship and keep learning throughout life, using knowledge to recognise God and serve others.
    Mark points to credit: Lesson (knowledge/learning is valued/duty); developing it with a practical example, e.g. regular study, helping others learn, reading the Qur'an with understanding. Model answer: Seeking knowledge is a religious duty, so a student should treat study (of religion and of the world) as worship and keep learning throughout life, using knowledge to recognise God and serve others. Level 1 (1): a basic, general statement. 0: nothing creditworthy.
Q2Revelation of the Qur'an · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe what Khadija and Waraqa ibn Nawfal did after the Prophet (pbuh) returned from the cave of Hira. [4]
  2. (b) Why is Khadija's response an example for Muslim families today? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Khadija covered him and calmed him; she reassured him that God would never disgrace him because he was kind to relatives, helped the weak and poor, was generous to guests and supported people in hardship; she took him to her cousin Waraqa ibn Nawfal, an elderly Christian who knew the earlier scriptures; Waraqa said the being who came was the same angel (Namus) who came to Moses, and warned that his people would drive him out.
    1 mark each for any four of: Khadija comforted/covered him; reassured him that God would not humiliate him; listed his good qualities; took him to Waraqa; Waraqa was her cousin / a Christian scholar of earlier scriptures; identified the angel as the one sent to Moses; foretold that his people would turn against him / expel him; wished he could support him.
  2. (b) She supported her husband when he was afraid, believed in him and sought wise advice; families today should support each other in difficult times with trust and comfort and seek knowledgeable help when facing something new.
    1 mark for identifying a quality (support, trust, calm, seeking advice); 1 mark for applying it to family life today.
Q3Revelation of the Qur'an · Core · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) State the name of the angel who brought the revelation, the month in which revelation began and the place where it began. [3]
  2. (b) The Prophet (pbuh) received his first revelation in 610 CE and died in 632 CE. Calculate how many years passed between these two events. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why Muslims give special importance to the month of Ramadan because of the revelation. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Jibril (Gabriel); Ramadan; the cave of Hira on Mount Nur (Jabal al-Nur) near Makka.
    1 mark each: Jibril; Ramadan; cave of Hira (Mount Nur / near Makka).
  2. (b) 22 years (about 23 lunar years, which is why the period is usually given as 23 years).
    B1: 632 − 610 = 22 (accept 'about 23 lunar years' with the 22 shown).
  3. (c) The Qur'an began to be revealed in Ramadan (on the Night of Power, which is better than a thousand months); so Muslims fast in this month, recite and complete the Qur'an, pray extra night prayers and seek the Night of Power in the last ten nights, renewing their link with God's guidance.
    1 mark: Qur'an first revealed in Ramadan / Night of Power; 1 mark: fasting/extra recitation; 1 mark: seeking Laylat al-Qadr / extra prayers / renewed commitment.

Q10Revelation of the Qur'an · Standard · Paper 1 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe the experiences of the Prophet (pbuh) when revelation came upon him, as reported by those who were with him. [4]
  2. (b) A friend in Karachi says the Qur'an is just a wise book written by a good man. Using the experience of revelation, how might a Muslim respond politely? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) He sometimes heard a sound like a ringing bell; he became very heavy (a camel he rode would kneel under the weight); his forehead sweated even on cold days; his face changed colour; he seemed absorbed and unaware of his surroundings; afterwards he recited the words he had received clearly.
    1 mark each for any four of: sound like a bell; heaviness (camel knelt / pressure on a Companion's thigh); sweating on cold days; change of face/colour; absorbed, cut off from surroundings; afterwards recited exactly what he received.
  2. (b) A Muslim could explain calmly that the Prophet (pbuh) did not control when or how revelation came, that its arrival was a heavy physical experience, and that he himself distinguished it from his own words (his Hadith), so Muslims believe it is the word of God; the reply should be respectful and invite further discussion.
    1 mark: Prophet (pbuh) did not control revelation; 1 mark: distinction between Qur'an and his own sayings / physical signs; 1 mark: respectful manner of response (wisdom and good speech).

Q17Revelation of the Qur'an · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the Night of Power (Laylat al-Qadr) and its link with the revelation of the Qur'an. [4]
  2. (b) Why do you think the exact date of the Night of Power is not known? Explain how this affects the way Muslims worship. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is the night in Ramadan on which the Qur'an began to be revealed; Sura 97 says it is better than a thousand months and that angels and the Spirit descend by God's permission, and that it is peace until dawn; its exact date is not fixed, and Muslims seek it in the odd nights of the last ten nights of Ramadan; many spend these nights in prayer, recitation and i'tikaf.
    1 mark each for any four of: revelation began on this night; in Ramadan; better than a thousand months (Sura 97); angels and the Spirit descend; peace until dawn; date not certain, sought in last ten (odd) nights; Muslims pray, recite, make supplication, i'tikaf.
  2. (b) Because it is hidden, Muslims make extra effort across several nights rather than one, which increases worship, sincerity and self-discipline; it keeps believers hopeful and humble; communities in mosques from Lahore to London fill the last ten nights with prayer, so the whole end of Ramadan becomes a time of spiritual effort.
    1 mark: plausible reason (to encourage effort / sincerity); 1 mark: effect on worship across all last ten nights; 1–2 marks: developed explanation with example and judgement.
Q18Revelation of the Qur'an · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Surah al-Anfal (8:1) opens with a question about the spoils (anfal) of a battle. Name the surah and verse, and name the battle the verse is linked to. [2]

    1 mark for surah and verse; 1 mark for the battle of Badr.
  2. (b) The spoils belong to God and His Messenger, who decide how they are divided. Believers should be conscious of God, settle their disputes by putting things right among themselves, and obey God and His Messenger. The answer was given because the Companions disagreed about the division.

Q57The Qur'an as a source of Islamic law · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Give examples of concessions (rulings that make things easier in hardship) found in the Qur'an. [4]
  2. (b) The Qur'an says 'God intends ease for you, and does not intend hardship' (Sura 2.185). To what extent is this principle important in Islamic law today? Give reasons. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A sick person or traveller may miss fasts in Ramadan and make them up later, and those who cannot fast may feed a poor person instead (Sura 2.184–185); travellers may shorten prayers when on a journey (Sura 4.101); when water is not available or would cause harm, tayammum with clean earth replaces washing (Sura 4.43, 5.6); a person forced by necessity may eat otherwise forbidden food to stay alive (Sura 2.173); hajj is required only of those who are able (Sura 3.97); a person forced to deny faith under threat is excused while the heart remains firm (Sura 16.106).
    1 mark each for any four accurate examples: fasting concession for sick/traveller; fidya for those unable to fast; shortening prayer on a journey; tayammum; forbidden food under necessity; hajj only for those able; compulsion excused (16.106). Verse references credit-worthy but not required.
  2. (b) Very important: scholars use it to give relief in new situations, e.g. allowing medical treatment during a fast when needed, or easing rules for patients, travellers and workers; it keeps the law practical for people in all conditions. However, ease does not mean dropping duties whenever they are inconvenient: concessions apply only to genuine hardship and within the limits set by the Qur'an and Sunna, so the principle must be applied by qualified scholars. A reasoned judgement is required.
    1 mark: judgement; 1 mark: argument with modern example of the principle in use; 1 mark: limitation (genuine hardship only / not an excuse to abandon duties); 1 mark: reasoned conclusion (role of scholars / balance).

Q60The Qur'an as a source of Islamic law · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Assess how far the Qur'an gave women rights to own and inherit property in the seventh century, compared with the customs of the time. [10]
Show answer
  1. (a) A balanced judgement. Points for: the Qur'an sets fixed shares for women in inheritance (4:7, 4:11, 4:12) and gives women the right to own what they earn (4:32); the mahr is the wife's right; these rights were a major change from customs that often excluded women. Points of caution: women's shares in inheritance are often half the men's share in the same relationship (4:11), and the scholars' explanation of the reasons (duties of maintenance) is part of the debate.
    Content points (credit any relevant, accurate point): women's inheritance right in 4:7; the fixed shares in 4:11–12; women's right to own their earnings (4:32); the mahr as the wife's own right; the change from customs that excluded women; the unequal share in some cases and its explanation; a judgement on 'how far' with reasons.
