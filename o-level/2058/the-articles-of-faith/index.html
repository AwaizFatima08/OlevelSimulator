Q1Oneness of God (tawhid) · Core · Paper 2 style · 14 marks
  1. (a) Explain the meaning of tawhid and describe how Sura 112 expresses it. [10]
  2. (b) Why is tawhid called the foundation of all the other Articles of Faith? Explain. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tawhid is the belief that God is One, unique and without partner, the only Creator and Sustainer, the only one worthy of worship, and unlike anything in creation. Sura 112 states that He is One, that He is eternal and absolute, that He has no children and no parents, and that nothing is comparable to Him.
    Indicative content: Tawhid means the oneness of God; God has no partner or equal; He alone creates and sustains; He alone is worshipped; His attributes are unique and unlike creation. Sura 112: He is One; eternal / self-sufficient, all depend on Him; He neither begets nor is begotten; none is comparable to Him. Both the meaning and Sura 112 are needed for Level 3 and above; one part only cannot go above Level 2. Marked by levels out of 10. Level 2 (3–5): a general answer with some relevant points but limited detail or some errors. Level 1 (1–2): a few basic or partly relevant points. 0: nothing creditworthy.
  2. (b) Because belief in angels, books, prophets, the Last Day and decree all depend on believing in the one God who created the angels, sent the books and prophets, will judge on the Last Day and decrees all things.
    Mark points to credit: The other articles come from God; e.g. prophets and books bring His message; the Last Day and decree depend on His power and knowledge. Model answer: Because belief in angels, books, prophets, the Last Day and decree all depend on believing in the one God who created the angels, sent the books and prophets, will judge on the Last Day and decrees all things. Q2Oneness of God (tawhid) · Core · Paper 2 style · 14 marks
  1. (a) Describe what is meant by shirk and give examples of its different forms. [10]
  2. (b) Explain how a Muslim can guard against hidden shirk in everyday life. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shirk is associating partners with God, the opposite of tawhid. Forms include worshipping idols or created things such as the sun or moon; believing God has a son, wife or partners; giving God's unique attributes, such as complete knowledge of the unseen or power to create, to others; and hidden or minor shirk, such as doing good deeds to show off rather than for God.
    Indicative content: Meaning — associating partners with God / opposite of tawhid; idol worship; worship of nature (sun, moon, stars); believing God has children or a consort; giving God's attributes to created beings; obeying anything in a way that places it above God; minor / hidden shirk — showing off (riya') in worship; superstition that something other than God controls fate.
