Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Oneness of God (tawhid) · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain the meaning of tawhid and describe how Sura 112 expresses it. [5]
  2. (b) Why is tawhid called the foundation of all the other Articles of Faith? Explain. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tawhid is the belief that God is One, unique and without partner, the only Creator and Sustainer, the only one worthy of worship, and unlike anything in creation. Sura 112 states that He is One, that He is eternal and absolute, that He has no children and no parents, and that nothing is comparable to Him.
    1 mark each for any five of: tawhid means oneness of God; God has no partner or equal; He alone creates and sustains; He alone is worshipped; His attributes are unique / unlike creation; Sura 112 — He is One; eternal / self-sufficient, all depend on Him; neither begets nor is begotten; none is comparable to Him. Maximum 3 for meaning only or Sura 112 only.
  2. (b) Because belief in angels, books, prophets, the Last Day and decree all depend on believing in the one God who created the angels, sent the books and prophets, will judge on the Last Day and decrees all things.
    Up to 3 marks: the other articles come from God (1); e.g. prophets and books bring His message (1); the Last Day and decree depend on His power and knowledge (1).
Q2Oneness of God (tawhid) · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe what is meant by shirk and give examples of its different forms. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how a Muslim can guard against hidden shirk in everyday life. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shirk is associating partners with God, the opposite of tawhid. Forms include worshipping idols or created things such as the sun or moon; believing God has a son, wife or partners; giving God's unique attributes, such as complete knowledge of the unseen or power to create, to others; and hidden or minor shirk, such as doing good deeds to show off rather than for God.
    1 mark each for any five of: meaning — associating partners with God / opposite of tawhid; idol worship; worship of nature (sun, moon, stars); believing God has children or a consort; giving God's attributes to created beings; obeying anything in a way that places it above God; minor / hidden shirk — showing off (riya') in worship; superstition that something other than God controls fate.
  2. (b) By keeping a sincere intention for God in all deeds, not doing good to gain praise, and relying on God rather than luck charms or superstition.
    Up to 3 marks: sincere intention / checking motives (1); avoiding showing off, e.g. not advertising charity online (1); trusting God rather than superstitions or wealth (1).
Q3Oneness of God (tawhid) · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the acts of worship in Islam express belief in tawhid. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why sincerity is essential in worship for a Muslim who believes in tawhid. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) In salat Muslims bow and prostrate only to God and recite that they worship Him alone; du'a is made directly to Him; the shahada declares His oneness; zakat recognises that wealth belongs to God; fasting is done only for God's pleasure; Hajj gathers all believers in the worship of the one God around the Ka'ba built by Abraham for that purpose.
    1 mark each for any five of: shahada declares oneness; salat — bowing and prostrating to God alone; al-Fatiha recited — worship and help from God alone; du'a directly to God; zakat — wealth belongs to God; fasting — sincere for God alone; Hajj — talbiya declares God has no partner; Ka'ba built by Abraham for worship of one God.
  2. (b) Worship done to impress others gives a share of it to someone other than God, so only sincere worship is truly for God alone and is accepted.
    Up to 3 marks: worship must be for God alone (1); showing off is a form of hidden shirk (1); God accepts deeds based on sincere intention, with example (1).
Q4Oneness of God (tawhid) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the Names and Attributes of God, giving the meanings of at least four of His names. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how knowing God's names can influence the way a Muslim behaves. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) God has many beautiful names (al-asma' al-husna), traditionally ninety-nine, which describe His perfect qualities. Examples: al-Rahman (the Most Gracious), al-Rahim (the Most Merciful), al-Khaliq (the Creator), al-Razzaq (the Provider), al-'Alim (the All-Knowing), al-Basir (the All-Seeing), al-Ghafur (the Forgiving), al-'Adl (the Just). His attributes are perfect and unlike those of creation.
    1 mark for explaining that God's names describe His attributes / beautiful names (traditionally 99); 1 mark each for up to four correctly translated names; maximum 5.
  2. (b) Knowing He is All-Seeing makes a Muslim careful in private; knowing He is the Forgiving encourages repentance; knowing He is the Provider removes greed and anxiety about earning.
    Up to 3 marks: 1 for each name linked to a specific change in behaviour, with development.
Q5Oneness of God (tawhid) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the Qur'an uses the natural world to point to the oneness of God. [5]
  2. (b) How can studying science strengthen a Muslim's belief in tawhid? Explain. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) It invites people to reflect on the creation of the heavens and earth, the alternation of night and day, the sun and moon, rain bringing dead land to life, plants and fruits, animals and the creation of humans; the order and balance in creation show a single Creator; if there were more than one god there would be disorder.
    1 mark each for any five of: heavens and earth as signs; night and day; sun and moon following their courses; rain reviving the earth; crops, fruit and plants; animals; creation of human beings in stages; order and harmony of the universe point to one Creator; argument that many gods would cause chaos; repeated invitation to think and reflect.
  2. (b) Science reveals the order, precision and balance of the universe, which a Muslim sees as signs of a single wise Creator, increasing wonder and gratitude.
    Up to 3 marks: science reveals order / design (1); Muslims see this as signs (ayat) of God (1); example, e.g. the water cycle, human development, the motion of planets (1).
Q6Oneness of God (tawhid) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the ways in which belief in tawhid affects the life of an individual Muslim. [6]
  2. (b) Which of these effects do you think is most important for a young Muslim today? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The believer worships God alone and prays and makes du'a only to Him; relies on God in difficulty; is free from fear of other created powers; is humble, knowing all comes from God; is grateful in good times and patient in hard times; acts sincerely; feels accountable since God sees everything; avoids superstition.
    1 mark each for any six of: worship only for God; du'a to God alone; tawakkul; freedom from fear of people, spirits or luck; humility; gratitude; patience; sincerity; accountability / God-consciousness; avoiding superstition and fortune-telling; contentment.
  2. (b) A reasoned choice, e.g. freedom from fear of people, because peer pressure is strong and tawhid reminds them to seek God's approval first.
    1 mark for judgement, 1 for developed reason.
Q7Oneness of God (tawhid) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) The Qur'an teaches both that God is far above His creation and that He is closer to a person than their jugular vein (Sura 50.16). Explain what these two ideas mean. [5]
  2. (b) How can belief that God is close to them help a Muslim who feels lonely, for example a student studying abroad? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Transcendence: God is beyond creation, not limited by time, space or form, and nothing resembles Him; no vision can grasp Him. Closeness: God knows every thought and whisper of the heart, hears every prayer, and is always near through His knowledge, power and care; believers can call on Him directly without any go-between.
    1 mark each for any five, at least two for each idea: God is above / beyond creation; not limited by space or time; unlike anything created; cannot be seen in this world; close through His knowledge of every thought; hears and answers prayer; always present with His servants by His power and care; no intermediary is needed for du'a.
  2. (b) Knowing God hears every prayer and knows their feelings, they can turn to Him in du'a and prayer and feel comforted and supported.
    Up to 3 marks: God hears and knows their situation (1); turning to prayer and remembrance brings comfort (1); practical action, e.g. regular prayer and joining a local mosque community (1).
Q8Oneness of God (tawhid) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain how belief in tawhid affects Muslim society as a whole. [5]
  2. (b) 'Belief in one God should lead to a fairer society.' To what extent is this true in the world today? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) If there is one Creator, all humans are equal before Him, so racism and class pride are rejected; it unites Muslims as one ummah with one direction of prayer; laws come from the one God, so justice applies equally to rich and poor; leaders are accountable to God; it encourages brotherhood and care for the weak.
    1 mark per point, +1 for development, up to 5: equality of all people (1+1); unity of the ummah — one qibla, one book (1+1); justice and the rule of God's law for all (1); accountability of leaders (1); care for the weak and brotherhood (1).
  2. (b) A reasoned judgement, e.g. tawhid demands equality and justice, and examples such as the mixing of races at Hajj show this; but injustice still exists in many Muslim societies, showing belief must be put into practice.
    Up to 3 marks: judgement (1); supporting example (e.g. Hajj, zakat) (1); counter-point / evaluation with modern example (1).
Q9Oneness of God (tawhid) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the declaration of faith (shahada) expresses both tawhid and belief in prophethood. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why it is not enough simply to say the shahada without acting on it. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The first part declares that there is no god but God, rejecting all false gods and affirming that only He deserves worship; the second part declares that Muhammad (pbuh) is His messenger, meaning that God's guidance comes through him and that following his example is the way to worship the one God correctly; together they join belief in God with obedience to His messenger.
    1 mark each for any five of: first part — denial of all false gods; affirmation of God alone as worthy of worship; the shahada is the basis of Islam / entry into Islam; second part — Muhammad (pbuh) is God's messenger; guidance and the Qur'an come through him; following the Sunna is how tawhid is lived; prophethood depends on tawhid — prophets call to the one God; the two parts cannot be separated.
  2. (b) The shahada is a commitment that must be shown in worship, obedience and character; words without action, like the signs of hypocrisy, show weak or false faith.
    Up to 3 marks: faith includes belief, words and actions (1); acting through the pillars and good character (1); reference to hypocrisy / sincerity with example (1).
Q10Oneness of God (tawhid) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain why shirk is regarded as the most serious sin in Islam. [5]
  2. (b) 'Hidden shirk is a greater danger to Muslims today than idol worship.' Evaluate this statement. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) It denies the basic truth of tawhid on which all faith rests; it gives the rights of the Creator to created things; it is an injustice to God and lowers human dignity by making people serve what cannot help them; the Qur'an states that God does not forgive shirk if a person dies upon it, though He forgives other sins as He wills; it leads to false values and superstition.
    1 mark per point, +1 for development, up to 5: contradicts tawhid, the foundation of Islam (1+1); gives God's rights to creation (1); the Qur'an says God does not forgive it if one dies without repenting, unlike other sins (1); described as a great injustice (zulm) (1); degrades humans and leads to superstition (1).
  2. (b) Agree to an extent: few Muslims worship idols, but love of wealth, fame and showing off can take God's place without people noticing; however, all forms are serious and open shirk remains the gravest.
    Up to 3 marks: judgement (1); support — showing off, materialism, social-media fame (1); counter-point and conclusion (1).
Q11Prophets and revealed books · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the role and qualities of prophets in Islam. [6]
  2. (b) Why do you think God chose human beings, rather than angels, as prophets? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Prophets are human beings chosen by God to convey His message, call people to worship Him alone and show them how to live; they are truthful, trustworthy and protected from major sin; they convey the message completely; they set the perfect example; some performed miracles by God's permission; they were sent to every nation; they warned of the Day of Judgement and gave good news of reward.
    1 mark each for any six of: chosen by God; human, not divine; sent to every nation; call to tawhid; convey the message fully; truthful and trustworthy; protected from sin (ma'sum); best examples of conduct; miracles by God's permission; warn and give good news; the Qur'an names 25 prophets; Muhammad (pbuh) is the last.
  2. (b) So that people could follow a practical example of how to live, since humans share the same needs and feelings.
    1 mark for the point, 1 for development (e.g. a human prophet showed how to deal with family, work and hardship).
Q12Prophets and revealed books · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Name the revealed books mentioned in the Qur'an and the prophet to whom each was given, and describe what Muslims believe about them. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why belief in the earlier books is important for Muslims. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Scrolls (Suhuf) of Abraham; the Torah (Tawrat) given to Moses; the Psalms (Zabur) given to David; the Gospel (Injil) given to Jesus; the Qur'an given to Muhammad (pbuh). Muslims believe all were revealed by God with the same basic message of tawhid; earlier books were for particular peoples and times and are not preserved in their original form; the Qur'an is the final, complete and preserved revelation.
    1 mark for each correct book–prophet pair up to 4; 1 further mark for a correct belief (same source / message; earlier books not preserved in original form; Qur'an final and preserved). Maximum 5.
  2. (b) It shows that God has always guided humanity, connects Islam to the message of earlier prophets and encourages respect towards Jews and Christians as People of the Book.
    Up to 3 marks: continuity of God's guidance (1); Islam confirms earlier revelation (1); respect for the People of the Book (1).
Q13Prophets and revealed books · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between a nabi and a rasul, and describe the prophets known as the 'Possessors of Firm Resolve' (ulu'l-'azm). [5]
  2. (b) What can Muslims learn from the patience of these great messengers? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A nabi is a prophet who receives revelation from God; a rasul is a messenger who is also given a message or law to deliver, often with a book; every messenger is a prophet but not every prophet is a messenger. The ulu'l-'azm are the great messengers known for their patience and determination: Noah, Abraham, Moses, Jesus and Muhammad (pbuh).
    1 mark: nabi receives revelation / guides people; 1 mark: rasul brings a new message / law / book; 1 mark: every rasul is a nabi but not the reverse; up to 2 marks for the ulu'l-'azm — meaning (firm resolve / great patience) and correct names (all five for full credit, 1 mark for at least three).
  2. (b) That carrying out God's work involves hardship and rejection, so believers should stay firm, patient and hopeful, as Noah did for centuries and the Prophet (pbuh) did in Makka.
    Up to 3 marks: patience in delivering the message despite rejection (1); example from one of the prophets (1); application today, e.g. persevering in doing good when others mock (1).
Q14Prophets and revealed books · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain why Muslims believe the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) is the final prophet (the Seal of the Prophets). [5]
  2. (b) Why is belief in the finality of prophethood important for Muslim unity? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Qur'an calls him the Seal of the Prophets (Sura 33.40); his message is for all humanity, not one people; the religion was completed in his time; the Qur'an revealed to him is preserved, so no new revelation is needed; he himself said there would be no prophet after him; his Sunna is recorded in detail as a complete example.
    1 mark each for any five of: Qur'anic title Seal of the Prophets (33.40); universal message for all people and all times; religion perfected / completed; Qur'an preserved unchanged; Hadith that there is no prophet after him; complete record of his life and teaching; earlier messages confirmed and completed.
  2. (b) It keeps all Muslims following one final message and one example, so no later claimant can divide the community with a new religion.
    Up to 3 marks: one final source of guidance (1); protection against false claimants (1); unity of the ummah worldwide (1).
Q15Prophets and revealed books · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the ways in which the Qur'an differs from the earlier revealed books, according to Muslim belief. [5]
  2. (b) How should this belief affect the way Muslims treat the Qur'an? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) It is the final revelation; it is for all humanity, while earlier books were for particular peoples; it has been preserved in its original words and language, memorised and written from the start, while the earlier books are not available in their original form; it confirms the truth in earlier books and acts as a criterion over them; it is a complete guide for all aspects of life; God has promised to protect it.
    1 mark each for any five of: final revelation; universal; preserved in original Arabic; memorised by many and written during the Prophet's (pbuh) lifetime; earlier books not preserved in original form; confirms earlier scriptures; criterion (furqan); complete guidance; God's promise to guard it.
  2. (b) They should read it regularly with understanding, follow its guidance, treat copies with respect and teach it to others.
    Up to 3 marks for developed points: regular recitation and study, including translation (1); acting on its teachings (1); respect for the text and teaching children, e.g. supporting Qur'an classes (1).
Q16Prophets and revealed books · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe what Muslims believe about the Prophet Jesus (Isa). [6]
  2. (b) Explain why respect for Jesus is important in Muslim belief. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) He was a great prophet and messenger, one of the ulu'l-'azm; he was born miraculously to the Virgin Mary without a father; he spoke as a baby to defend his mother; he was given the Gospel (Injil); by God's permission he performed miracles such as healing the blind and lepers and raising the dead; he called people to worship the one God; he is a human servant of God, not God or the son of God; Muslims believe he was not crucified but raised up by God, and that he will return before the Last Day.
    1 mark each for any six of: prophet and messenger; born to Mary without a father; Mary honoured in the Qur'an; spoke in the cradle; given the Injil; miracles by God's leave (examples); called to tawhid; human, not divine; not the son of God; not crucified, raised up by God; will return before the Day of Judgement.
  2. (b) Because believing in and honouring all prophets is part of faith; denying or disrespecting Jesus would contradict the Qur'an.
    1 mark for the point (belief in all prophets), 1 for development (e.g. Muslims say 'peace be upon him' after his name).
Q17Prophets and revealed books · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain why Muslims must believe in all the prophets and not only in the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh). [5]
  2. (b) Evaluate how this belief can help relations between Muslims, Jews and Christians. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Qur'an requires belief in all God's messengers without making any distinction between them in believing them (Sura 2.285); all prophets came from the same God with the same message of tawhid; rejecting any prophet is rejecting God's guidance; the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) came to confirm and complete the earlier messages; belief in all prophets is one of the Articles of Faith.
    1 mark per point, +1 for development, up to 5: Qur'anic command / no distinction (2.285) (1+1); same source and message (1+1); rejecting one means rejecting God's choice (1); Muhammad (pbuh) completes the line of prophets (1); part of iman (1).
  2. (b) It gives common ground, since Muslims honour Abraham, Moses and Jesus, which can lead to respect and dialogue; but differences remain, for example about the nature of Jesus, so respect does not mean agreement on everything.
    Up to 3 marks: judgement (1); shared respect for prophets as common ground, with example such as interfaith events (1); recognition of real differences (1).
Q18Prophets and revealed books · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe what lessons Muslims can learn from the lives of any three prophets other than Muhammad (pbuh). [6]
  2. (b) Which of these lessons do you think is most relevant today? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Examples: Noah — patience in preaching for many years despite rejection; Abraham — rejecting idols, readiness to sacrifice for God; Moses — courage in standing up to Pharaoh's oppression and leading his people; Joseph — patience in trials, honesty and forgiving his brothers; Jesus — humility, kindness to the poor and sick; Job — patience in illness; Jonah — repentance after leaving his people.
    2 marks per prophet: 1 for accurate events, 1 for the lesson drawn from them. Credit any three prophets named in the Qur'an.
  2. (b) A reasoned choice, e.g. Joseph's forgiveness, because family disputes are common and forgiveness restores relationships.
    1 mark for judgement, 1 for developed reason.
Q19Life after death and the Day of Judgement · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the stages of life after death in Islamic belief. [6]
  2. (b) How can belief in life after death change the way a person spends their time? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) At death the soul is taken by the Angel of Death; the person enters barzakh, the period between death and resurrection, and is questioned in the grave; at the end of the world the trumpet is blown and all creation dies; at the second blowing everyone is resurrected; all are gathered for the Day of Judgement, where deeds are weighed and records given; people cross the bridge (sirat); they go to Paradise or Hell.
    1 mark each for any six of: death and the Angel of Death taking the soul; barzakh; questioning in the grave by two angels; the trumpet (Israfil); end of the world; resurrection of bodies; gathering on the plain; book of deeds; scales (mizan); bridge (sirat); Paradise (Janna); Hell (Jahannam).
  2. (b) Knowing life is short and deeds will be judged, a person uses time for worship, useful work and helping others rather than wasting it.
    1 mark for the link to accountability, 1 for a specific example.
Q20Life after death and the Day of Judgement · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about what Muslims believe will happen on the Day of Judgement. [6]
  2. (b) Explain why many Muslims find the idea of a final judgement comforting. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The trumpet will be blown and the universe will end; all people will be raised from their graves and gathered before God; each person's record of deeds will be given — in the right hand to the successful and in the left hand to the losers; deeds will be weighed on the scales; witnesses will speak, including people's own limbs and the earth itself; people will cross the bridge over Hell; God will judge with perfect justice and mercy; the Prophet (pbuh) will intercede by God's permission.
    1 mark each for any six of: trumpet / destruction of the universe; resurrection; gathering before God; book of deeds in right or left hand; scales; even an atom's weight counted (Sura 99); limbs and earth bear witness; bridge (sirat); intercession by God's permission; justice — no one wronged; mercy; final destination.
  2. (b) Because injustices not put right in this life will be corrected, and every good deed, however small, will be rewarded.
    1 mark for justice for the oppressed, 1 for reward of unseen good.
Q21Life after death and the Day of Judgement · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the Qur'an presents Paradise and Hell. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how a Muslim should balance hope of Paradise with fear of Hell. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Paradise: eternal gardens with rivers flowing beneath, peace and no sorrow, fruits and pleasures, being with loved ones, and above all God's pleasure; it is the reward for faith and good deeds. Hell: a place of fire and severe punishment, regret and despair, for those who rejected God and did great evil; both are described vividly to encourage good and warn against evil.
    1 mark each for any five, at least two for each: Paradise — gardens with rivers; eternal; peace / no fatigue or sorrow; rewards beyond imagination; God's pleasure; for believers who did good. Hell — fire; boiling water / severe suffering; regret; for disbelievers and wrongdoers; warning to people to change while they can.
  2. (b) A Muslim should hope in God's mercy so they do not despair, and fear His punishment so they do not become careless; together these keep them striving to do good and repent.
    Up to 3 marks: hope prevents despair (1); fear prevents complacency (1); balance leads to good actions and repentance, with example (1).
Q22Life after death and the Day of Judgement · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain why belief in life after death is important for Muslims. [5]
  2. (b) 'Belief in the Hereafter is the main reason Muslims behave morally.' To what extent do you agree? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) It gives meaning and purpose to life as a test; it guarantees final justice, since many wrongs are not punished in this world; it motivates good deeds and discourages sin; it gives comfort in suffering and loss, as the believer hopes to meet loved ones; it reminds people that wealth and status are temporary; without it, belief in God's justice would be incomplete.
    1 mark per point, +1 for development, up to 5: purpose / life as a test (1+1); final justice (1+1); moral motivation (1); comfort in suffering / bereavement (1); worldly things temporary (1).
  2. (b) Partly: accountability is a strong motive, but Muslims are also moved by love of God, gratitude and concern for others, and many good deeds are done for God's pleasure rather than fear or reward alone.
    Up to 3 marks: judgement (1); support for the Hereafter as motive (1); other motives — love of God, conscience, community (1).
Q23Life after death and the Day of Judgement · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe what Muslims believe about barzakh and the questioning in the grave. [5]
  2. (b) How might belief in the questioning in the grave affect the way a Muslim lives? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Barzakh is the barrier or stage between death and the resurrection; after burial two angels, known in tradition as Munkar and Nakir, question the dead person about who their Lord is, what their religion is and who their prophet was; believers answer correctly and their grave becomes spacious and peaceful; those who rejected faith cannot answer and suffer; the soul remains in this state until the Day of Resurrection.
    1 mark each for any five of: barzakh means barrier / interval; between death and resurrection; questioning by two angels (Munkar and Nakir); questions about Lord, religion and prophet; believers answer and are at peace; disbelievers fail and suffer; foretaste of the final outcome; no return to the world for a second chance.
  2. (b) It reminds them that faith must be real and lived, not just words, because only sincere belief and practice will allow them to answer; this encourages regular worship and good deeds.
    Up to 3 marks: faith must be genuine (1); motivates daily worship and good character (1); urgency — death can come at any time, with example (1).
Q24Angels · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe what Muslims believe about the nature of angels. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how belief in angels can affect a Muslim's behaviour. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Angels are created by God from light; they are unseen beings, without free will, who always obey God; they do not eat, drink, marry or tire; they worship and praise God constantly; they can take different forms, including human form, by God's permission; they have different duties; belief in them is an Article of Faith.
    1 mark each for any five of: created from light; unseen; no free will — always obey; do not sin; no physical needs (food, sleep, family); constant worship; can appear in human form (e.g. Jibril); vast numbers; different duties; belief in angels is part of iman.
  2. (b) Knowing angels record every deed and are always present encourages a Muslim to act well even in private, and their obedience is an example of devotion.
    Up to 3 marks: recording angels — accountability (1); behaving well when alone (1); angels' obedience as a model / comfort that angels pray for believers (1).
Q25Angels · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Name four angels and describe the duties of each. [6]
  2. (b) Why do you think Muslims find comfort in belief in angels? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Jibril (Gabriel) brings revelation to the prophets; Mika'il (Michael) is responsible for rain and provision; Israfil will blow the trumpet at the end of the world and for the resurrection; the Angel of Death (often called 'Izra'il) takes souls; the noble recording angels (Kiraman Katibin) write each person's deeds; Munkar and Nakir question the dead in the grave; Malik guards Hell; Ridwan guards Paradise.
    Up to 6 marks: 1 mark for each correctly named angel with a correct duty up to four, plus up to 2 marks for extra detail (e.g. Jibril brought the Qur'an; recording angels on the right and left). Maximum 6.
  2. (b) Because angels pray for believers and carry out God's care for creation, reminding Muslims they are not alone.
    1 mark for the point, 1 for development.
Q26Angels · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Describe the work of the recording angels and the Angel of Death. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how belief in these angels could help a teenager resist peer pressure. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The recording angels (Kiraman Katibin, 'noble writers') accompany each person; one records good deeds and the other bad deeds; nothing is missed, and the record will be presented on the Day of Judgement. The Angel of Death takes each person's soul at the time decreed by God, gently for believers and harshly for evildoers; no one can delay or bring forward that moment.
    1 mark each for any five: recording angels with every person; one on the right writes good, one on the left writes bad (in tradition); nothing too small to be written; record shown on the Day of Judgement; Angel of Death takes the soul; at the appointed time; cannot be delayed; manner differs for believers and wrongdoers.
  2. (b) Knowing every act is recorded and death can come at any time, the teenager is more likely to refuse harmful activities such as smoking or cheating, even when friends pressure them.
    Up to 3 marks: accountability — deeds recorded (1); awareness of death's uncertainty (1); specific example of resisting pressure (1).
Q27Angels · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Write about the role of the angel Jibril in the life of the Prophet (pbuh). [6]
  2. (b) Explain why Jibril's role is important for Muslim belief in the Qur'an. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Jibril brought the first revelation in the cave of Hira and continued to bring the Qur'an over about twenty-three years; he accompanied the Prophet (pbuh) on the Night Journey and Ascension; he taught him how to perform the prayers; he came in human form to ask about Islam, faith and excellence, teaching the Companions; he reviewed the Qur'an with the Prophet (pbuh) every Ramadan, twice in the final year; he supported him at difficult times, such as at Ta'if with the angel of the mountains.
    1 mark each for any six of: first revelation at Hira; brought the Qur'an over about 23 years; Night Journey and Ascension; taught the timings and method of prayer; the Hadith of Jibril (Islam, iman, ihsan); appeared in human form; reviewed the Qur'an each Ramadan; twice in the last year; support at Ta'if; called the Holy Spirit (Ruh al-Qudus) in the Qur'an.
  2. (b) Because the Qur'an came through a trustworthy angel directly from God, Muslims believe it is God's word and not the Prophet's (pbuh) own composition.
    1 mark for the point, 1 for development (e.g. revision with Jibril ensured accuracy).
Q28Angels · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain the differences between angels, jinn and human beings in Islamic belief. [5]
  2. (b) Why do you think human beings, with free will, can be honoured above angels? Explain. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Angels are made from light, have no free will and always obey God; jinn are made from smokeless fire, are unseen, have free will and can be believers or disbelievers — Iblis was one of the jinn; humans are made from clay, have free will and are given the role of vicegerent; jinn and humans will be judged on their choices, while angels are not tested in the same way.
    1 mark each for any five: angels — light; no free will; always obey. Jinn — smokeless fire; free will; can be good or bad; Iblis was a jinn. Humans — clay / earth; free will; vicegerents (khalifa); humans and jinn judged on the Last Day.
  2. (b) Because humans choose to obey God despite temptation, while angels obey by nature; choosing goodness freely is a greater achievement, which is why angels bowed to Adam.
    Up to 3 marks: free choice makes obedience meaningful (1); humans struggle against desires and Shaytan (1); evidence — angels told to bow to Adam / Adam taught the names (1).
Q29Predestination and divine decree · Standard · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain what Muslims mean by belief in al-qadr (divine decree). [5]
  2. (b) Explain how belief in al-qadr can help a Muslim whose family business has failed. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) God knows everything that has happened and will happen; everything is recorded in the Preserved Tablet (al-Lawh al-Mahfuz); nothing happens except by God's will; God is the Creator of everything, including human actions; yet humans have been given free will to choose and are responsible for their choices; belief in decree, good and bad, is an Article of Faith.
    1 mark each for any five of: God's complete knowledge of past, present and future; recorded in the Preserved Tablet; everything happens by His will; He creates all things; humans still choose and are accountable; belief in qadr, both good and bad, is part of iman (mentioned in the Hadith of Jibril); decree does not excuse wrongdoing.
  2. (b) They can accept the loss patiently as part of God's plan and a test, avoid despair or blaming others, and still make fresh efforts, trusting God for the outcome.
    Up to 3 marks: patience and acceptance — a test from God (1); hope, since God's plan may contain good not yet seen (1); continued effort and trust (tie the camel), e.g. restarting with better planning (1).
Q30Predestination and divine decree · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) 'If God has decreed everything, humans cannot be responsible for what they do.' Explain how Muslims answer this argument. [5]
  2. (b) Explain how belief in al-qadr should affect a Muslim's attitude to both success and hardship. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) God's knowledge of what people will choose does not force them to choose it; humans are given intellect, guidance and free will, and they experience making real choices; God's justice requires accountability only for what people chose; people are not held responsible for what is outside their control; therefore decree and responsibility exist together, and the decree cannot be used as an excuse for sin.
    1 mark per point, +1 for development, up to 5: God's foreknowledge does not compel (1+1); humans are given reason, guidance (prophets, books) and free will (1+1); accountability only for chosen actions / not for what is beyond control (1); God is just (1); decree is no excuse for sin (1).
  2. (b) In success they should be grateful and humble, knowing it came by God's will; in hardship they should be patient and avoid despair, knowing it is a test and that God's plan is wise.
    Up to 3 marks: gratitude and humility in success, not arrogance (1); patience in hardship (1); example applied to modern life, e.g. exam results or illness (1).
