Q1Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) What are the products of anaerobic respiration in yeast?A lactic acid onlyB alcohol (ethanol) and carbon dioxideC carbon dioxide and waterD lactic acid and carbon dioxide [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B alcohol (ethanol) and carbon dioxide
    Yeast respires glucose anaerobically to ethanol and carbon dioxide. Lactic acid (A) is made by human muscle, not yeast.
Q2Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Write the word equation for anaerobic respiration in human muscle cells. [1]
  2. (b) State when muscle cells respire anaerobically. [1]
  3. (c) Anaerobic respiration releases much less energy per glucose molecule than aerobic respiration. Suggest why. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) glucose → lactic acid
    B1
  2. (b) During vigorous exercise, when oxygen cannot be supplied fast enough for aerobic respiration.
    B1 vigorous exercise / not enough oxygen.
  3. (c) Glucose is only partly broken down; lactic acid still contains a lot of energy.
    B1 incomplete breakdown of glucose.
Q3Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Give three differences between aerobic respiration and anaerobic respiration in human muscle. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Aerobic needs oxygen, anaerobic does not; aerobic produces carbon dioxide and water, anaerobic produces lactic acid; aerobic releases much more energy per glucose; aerobic is a complete breakdown of glucose, anaerobic is incomplete.
    1 mark each for any three comparative differences.

Q5Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) After a race, lactic acid is removed from the blood. In which organ is most of it broken down using oxygen?A kidneyB liverC lungsD stomach [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B liver
    Blood carries lactic acid from the muscles to the liver, where it is broken down by aerobic respiration (paying back the oxygen debt). The kidneys (A) do not remove lactic acid this way.
Q6Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A baker in Multan mixed flour, water, sugar and yeast to make 50 cm³ of dough. After 1 hour in a warm place the dough had a volume of 120 cm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in volume of the dough. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the dough rises. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the dough was left in a warm place rather than a refrigerator. [2]
  4. (d) State what happens to the alcohol made by the yeast when the dough is baked. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 140 %
    M1 (120 − 50) ÷ 50 × 100; A1 140 %.
  2. (b) Yeast respires anaerobically, producing carbon dioxide gas; the bubbles are trapped in the dough, so it expands.
    B1 yeast respiration produces carbon dioxide; B1 gas trapped / bubbles make dough expand.
  3. (c) Warmth increases the activity of the yeast's enzymes, so respiration and carbon dioxide production are faster; in the cold enzymes work slowly.
    B1 enzymes work faster at warm temperature (near optimum); B1 faster respiration / more gas.
  4. (d) It evaporates (boils off) in the oven heat.
    B1
Q7Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

An athlete in Islamabad ran a 400 m race. At the end of the race her blood lactic acid concentration was 12.0 mmol per dm³. After resting for 45 minutes it was 3.0 mmol per dm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate at which the lactic acid concentration fell during the 45 minutes. Give your answer in mmol per dm³ per minute. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why lactic acid built up in the athlete's blood during the race. [2]
  3. (c) Describe how the oxygen debt is repaid after the race. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.2 mmol per dm³ per minute
    M1 (12.0 − 3.0) ÷ 45; A1 0.2.
  2. (b) Muscles needed more energy than aerobic respiration could supply; oxygen could not reach them fast enough, so they respired anaerobically, producing lactic acid.
    B1 not enough oxygen to muscles; B1 anaerobic respiration produces lactic acid.
  3. (c) Heart rate stays high to carry lactic acid in the blood from the muscles to the liver; breathing stays fast and deep to supply oxygen; lactic acid is broken down by aerobic respiration in the liver.
    B1 fast heart rate transports lactic acid to liver; B1 deep/fast breathing supplies extra oxygen; B1 aerobic respiration of lactic acid in liver.

Q10Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Relative molecular masses: glucose = 180, ethanol = 46, carbon dioxide = 44.

  1. (a) Write the balanced chemical equation for anaerobic respiration in yeast. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of ethanol produced when 90 g of glucose is completely respired anaerobically by yeast. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide produced from the same 90 g of glucose. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂
    B1 correct formulae; B1 balanced.
  2. (b) 46 g
    M1 90 ÷ 180 = 0.5 mol glucose → 1.0 mol ethanol; A1 1.0 × 46 = 46 g.
  3. (c) 44 g
    1.0 mol × 44 = 44 g. B1
Q11Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

A flask of glucose solution and yeast, closed with a cotton wool plug, was placed on a top-pan balance at 30 °C.Time / min: 0 | 15 | 30 | 45 | 60Mass of flask / g: 250.00 | 249.86 | 249.62 | 249.36 | 249.12Assume that all the mass lost is carbon dioxide released by anaerobic respiration of the yeast.

  1. (a) Calculate the total mass of carbon dioxide lost in 60 minutes. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mean rate of mass loss over the 60 minutes, in g per minute, to 3 significant figures. [1]
  3. (c) Use the equation C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ to calculate the mass of glucose respired. (Mr: glucose 180, CO₂ 44) [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the cotton wool plug was used instead of a rubber bung. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.88 g
    250.00 − 249.12 = 0.88 g. B1
  2. (b) 0.0147 g per minute
    0.88 ÷ 60 = 0.0147 g/min. B1
  3. (c) 1.80 g
    M1 180 g glucose → 2 × 44 = 88 g CO₂; A1 0.88 × 180 ÷ 88 = 1.80 g.
  4. (d) It lets the carbon dioxide escape, so the loss in mass can be measured; it still keeps out microorganisms (and limits air movement), while a bung would trap the gas (and could be forced out by the pressure).
    B1 lets carbon dioxide escape (mass loss measured); B1 keeps out microorganisms / bung would trap gas and build up pressure.
Q12Anaerobic respiration (yeast/muscle) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

At a distillery near a sugar mill in Sindh, molasses (a sugary waste from sugar cane) is diluted and mixed with yeast in closed tanks to make ethanol, which is sold as a fuel.

  1. (a) Explain why air is kept out of the fermentation tanks. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the temperature of the tanks is controlled at about 30–35 °C. [2]
  3. (c) Fermentation stops after some days even though the yeast is still present. Suggest two reasons. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) With oxygen, yeast would respire aerobically, producing carbon dioxide and water instead of ethanol; without oxygen it respires anaerobically and makes ethanol.
    B1 aerobic respiration with oxygen gives no ethanol; B1 anaerobic conditions make ethanol.
  2. (b) This is near the optimum for yeast enzymes, so respiration is fast; higher temperatures would denature the enzymes / kill the yeast.
    B1 optimum for enzymes; B1 high temperature denatures / kills yeast.
  3. (c) The sugar is used up; the ethanol concentration becomes high enough to poison/kill the yeast.
    B1 sugar used up; B1 ethanol toxic to yeast (or pH falls).

Q18Respiration investigations & respirometers · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

Q29Aerobic respiration · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Relative molecular masses: glucose 180, oxygen 32, carbon dioxide 44, water 18.C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of oxygen needed to respire 36 g of glucose completely. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide produced, to 1 decimal place. [1]
  3. (c) Show that mass is conserved in this reaction. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 38.4 g
    M1 36 ÷ 180 = 0.2 mol glucose needs 1.2 mol O₂; A1 1.2 × 32 = 38.4 g.
  2. (b) 52.8 g
    1.2 × 44 = 52.8 g. B1
  3. (c) 36 + 38.4 = 74.4 g; 52.8 + 21.6 = 74.4 g
    B1 water = 1.2 × 18 = 21.6 g and totals equal (74.4 g).
Q30Aerobic respiration · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

For this question, assume that aerobic respiration releases 16.0 kJ per gram of glucose and anaerobic respiration in muscle releases 0.8 kJ per gram of glucose.

  1. (a) Calculate how many times more energy is released per gram of glucose by aerobic respiration than by anaerobic respiration. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of glucose needed to release 400 kJ by (i) aerobic respiration and (ii) anaerobic respiration. [2]
  3. (c) Use these figures to explain why muscles cannot rely on anaerobic respiration for long periods. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20 times
    16.0 ÷ 0.8 = 20. B1
  2. (b) (i) 25 g; (ii) 500 g
    B1 400 ÷ 16.0 = 25 g; B1 400 ÷ 0.8 = 500 g.
  3. (c) Anaerobic respiration releases far less energy per gram, so glucose would be used up very quickly; lactic acid also builds up and causes fatigue.
    B1 little energy / glucose used rapidly; B1 lactic acid accumulates / oxygen debt.
