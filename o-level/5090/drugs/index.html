Q1Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which is the best definition of a drug? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B any substance taken into the body that modifies or affects chemical reactions in the body
    A drug is any substance taken into the body that modifies or affects chemical reactions in the body; this covers medicines and misused drugs. A is too narrow because it describes only antibiotics.
Q2Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) For which disease would a course of antibiotics NOT be effective? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B influenza
    Influenza is caused by a virus, and antibiotics do not affect viruses. Cholera, tuberculosis and typhoid are bacterial diseases, so the other options are treated with antibiotics.
Q3Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Core · Paper 4 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the inoculating loop is heated in a Bunsen flame until it glows red before and after use. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the lid of the Petri dish is held with two short pieces of tape and not sealed all the way round. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why plates in a school laboratory are incubated at 25 °C and not at 37 °C. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the plates must not be opened after incubation. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) To kill any microorganisms on it, so the plate is not contaminated (and no bacteria escape afterwards).
    B1.
  2. (b) Oxygen can still enter, so harmful anaerobic bacteria are not encouraged to grow; the lid cannot be opened accidentally.
    B1 allows oxygen in / prevents anaerobic conditions; B1 lid stays shut.
  3. (c) At 37 °C (body temperature) bacteria that cause disease in humans could grow.
    B1.
  4. (d) Large numbers of bacteria have grown, which could include harmful ones; opening would release them.
    B1.

Q5Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

  2. (b) State two examples of medicinal drugs other than antibiotics and what each is used for. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why antibiotics should not be shared with a friend who has similar symptoms. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) They act on structures or processes found in bacterial cells but not in human cells, such as the bacterial cell wall.

    B1 each for any two.
Q6Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Poultry farms sometimes add small amounts of antibiotics to chicken feed to keep the birds healthy and growing quickly. Explain why this practice can be a risk to human health. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest one alternative way the farmer could keep the chickens healthy. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Constant low doses kill susceptible bacteria and select for resistant ones; resistant bacteria multiply in the chickens; they can pass to humans through meat or the environment, so human infections become harder to treat.
    B1 selection for resistance; B1 resistant bacteria increase in the animals; B1 transfer to humans / antibiotics less effective for people.
  2. (b) Vaccinate the birds; keep sheds clean; give birds more space; use antibiotics only to treat sick birds.
    B1.

Q10Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which sequence describes how a population of bacteria becomes resistant to an antibiotic? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B random mutation gives resistance → antibiotic kills non-resistant bacteria → resistant bacteria reproduce
    Resistance arises by random mutation; the antibiotic acts as a selection pressure, so resistant bacteria survive and reproduce (natural selection). A is wrong because bacteria do not 'learn' resistance; it is genetic.
Q11Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

A student spread a harmless bacterium on a nutrient agar plate and placed five paper discs (6 mm diameter) on it. Four discs were soaked in different antibiotics and one in sterile water. After two days at 25 °C the diameters of the clear zones were measured.penicillin 26 mm; streptomycin 18 mm; tetracycline 30 mm; erythromycin 12 mm; sterile water: no clear zone beyond the disc.

  1. (a) Calculate the area of the clear zone around each antibiotic disc in mm², using area = πr². Give each answer to the nearest whole number. [3]
  2. (b) Rank the antibiotics from most to least effective against this bacterium. [1]
  3. (c) Explain the purpose of the disc soaked in sterile water. [2]
  4. (d) State two precautions the student should take to avoid contaminating the plate or himself. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) penicillin 531 mm²; streptomycin 254 mm²; tetracycline 707 mm²; erythromycin 113 mm²
    M1 radius = diameter ÷ 2; A2 all four correct (A1 for two or three): π × 13², π × 9², π × 15², π × 6².
  2. (b) tetracycline > penicillin > streptomycin > erythromycin
    B1.
  3. (c) It is a control; it shows that the clear zones are caused by the antibiotics and not by the paper disc or the water.
    B1 control; B1 shows effect is due to antibiotic.
  4. (d) Flame the forceps / loop; open the lid only slightly and briefly; tape the lid (do not seal fully) and do not open after incubation; wash hands; incubate at no more than 25 °C.
    B1 each for any two.
Q12Medicinal drugs & antibiotics · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the use of an antibiotic can lead to a population of bacteria that is resistant to it. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why bacteria can become resistant much faster than animals become adapted to a new condition. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A random mutation makes some bacteria resistant; the antibiotic kills the non-resistant bacteria; the resistant bacteria survive and reproduce; the resistance gene is passed on so the proportion of resistant bacteria rises (natural selection).
    B1 mutation / variation; B1 non-resistant killed; B1 resistant survive and reproduce; B1 allele / gene passed on, population becomes resistant.
  2. (b) Bacteria reproduce very quickly (some divide every 20 minutes), so many generations occur in a short time; large populations mean more mutations.
    B1 rapid reproduction / short generation time; B1 large numbers so more mutations.

Show answer

  2. (b) 500

In one country the legal limit for driving is 80 mg of alcohol per 100 cm³ of blood. The table (figures for this question) shows the relative risk of a crash compared with a driver with no alcohol.blood alcohol / mg per 100 cm³: 0, 50, 80, 100, 150relative risk of crash: 1, 2, 4, 7, 25

  1. (a) Calculate how many times greater the risk of a crash is at 150 mg per 100 cm³ than at the legal limit of 80 mg per 100 cm³. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how the risk changes as blood alcohol concentration increases. [2]
  3. (c) Some countries have lowered their limit to 50 mg per 100 cm³. Use the data to argue for this change. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the risk of a crash increases. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6.25 times
    A1 25 ÷ 4 = 6.25.
  2. (b) The risk rises as blood alcohol rises; it rises slowly at first and then very steeply above about 100 mg per 100 cm³.
    B1 risk increases; B1 increase is faster at higher concentrations (with data).
  3. (c) At 50 mg the risk is 2 times, half the risk at 80 mg (4 times); lowering the limit could prevent many crashes.
    B1 quotes data; B1 conclusion.
  4. (d) Alcohol slows reaction time; it reduces judgement / self-control, so drivers take more risks.
    B1 slower reactions; B1 poor judgement / more risk-taking.
