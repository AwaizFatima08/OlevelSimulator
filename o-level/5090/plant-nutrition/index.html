Q4Limiting factors & rate investigations · Standard · Paper 4 style · 9 marks

A student placed a piece of pondweed in a test tube of water containing sodium hydrogencarbonate. A lamp was placed at different distances from the tube, and a beaker of water was placed between the lamp and the tube. After 5 minutes at each distance, the student counted the bubbles released in one minute, three times.Distance / cm: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50Trial 1: 52, 38, 22, 14, 8Trial 2: 48, 35, 25, 12, 7Trial 3: 50, 35, 22, 13, 9

  1. (a) Calculate the mean number of bubbles per minute at 20 cm and at 40 cm. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the relationship between the distance of the lamp and the rate of bubbling. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why the rate of bubbling changes as the lamp is moved further away. [2]
  4. (d) State two variables that the student should keep the same. [2]
  5. (e) Suggest why the student placed a beaker of water between the lamp and the test tube. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 20 cm: 36; 40 cm: 13 bubbles per minute
    M1 add the three readings and divide by 3; A1 both means correct (36 and 13)
  2. (b) As the distance increases the number of bubbles per minute decreases; from 50 per minute at 10 cm to 8 per minute at 50 cm.
    B1 further lamp → fewer bubbles (inverse relationship); B1 quotes paired data with units
  3. (c) Light intensity falls with distance; light is the limiting factor, so less photosynthesis and less oxygen is released.
    B1 lower light intensity further away; B1 less photosynthesis so less oxygen (gas) produced / light is limiting
  4. (d) Any two of: temperature of water; carbon dioxide (hydrogencarbonate) concentration; same piece/length of pondweed; same lamp/bulb power; time allowed to acclimatise.
    1 mark each for any two valid controlled variables
  5. (e) To absorb heat from the lamp so the temperature of the pondweed stays constant.
    B1 acts as a heat shield / keeps temperature constant
Q5Limiting factors & rate investigations · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A grower near Murree produces tomatoes in a glasshouse during winter. Paraffin heaters are burned inside the glasshouse.

  1. (a) Explain how burning paraffin in the heaters can increase the yield of tomatoes. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why the grower switches on extra lamps on cloudy winter days. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why the grower does not keep increasing the carbon dioxide concentration in the glasshouse. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Burning releases heat, raising the temperature towards the optimum for photosynthesis enzymes; it also releases carbon dioxide, a raw material; so photosynthesis is faster and more sugar is made for growth.
    B1 raises temperature (enzymes work faster); B1 adds carbon dioxide (raw material/limiting factor); B1 faster photosynthesis → more glucose/growth/yield
  2. (b) Light intensity is low, so light is the limiting factor; lamps increase the rate of photosynthesis.
    B1 light limiting on cloudy days
  3. (c) Above a certain concentration another factor (light or temperature) becomes limiting so yield does not increase further; the extra cost would then be wasted.
    B1 another factor becomes limiting / rate levels off; B1 extra cost gives no extra profit
Q6Limiting factors & rate investigations · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Hydrogencarbonate indicator is red when in equilibrium with air, yellow when carbon dioxide concentration rises and purple when it falls. Four tubes were set up and left for 3 hours:Tube A: leaf, bright lightTube B: leaf, tube covered in black paperTube C: no leaf, bright lightTube D: leaf, tube covered with thin gauze (dim light)All tubes started red. After 3 hours the indicator in tube D was still red.

  1. (a) State the colour of the indicator in tubes A, B and C after 3 hours. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the colour in tube A and in tube B. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why tube C was set up. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest why the indicator in tube D stayed the same colour. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A purple; B yellow; C red (unchanged)
    2 marks all three correct; 1 mark for two correct
  2. (b) A: the leaf photosynthesised faster than it respired, taking in carbon dioxide, so CO₂ fell and the indicator turned purple. B: in the dark there is no photosynthesis; respiration released carbon dioxide so the indicator turned yellow.
    B1 A photosynthesis > respiration; B1 CO₂ removed → purple; B1 B only respiration, CO₂ added → yellow
  3. (c) As a control, to show that the leaf caused the colour change (light alone does not change the indicator).
    B1 control to show the change is due to the leaf
  4. (d) In dim light the rate of photosynthesis equalled the rate of respiration, so there was no net change in carbon dioxide concentration.
    B1 photosynthesis rate = respiration rate (compensation point); B1 no net uptake or release of CO₂
Q7Limiting factors & rate investigations · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

In a pondweed investigation, relative light intensity is calculated using the formula: relative light intensity = 10 000 ÷ d², where d is the distance of the lamp from the plant in cm.

  1. (a) Calculate the relative light intensity at 25 cm and at 50 cm. [2]
  2. (b) State how many times greater the light intensity is at 25 cm than at 50 cm. [1]
  3. (c) The volume of oxygen collected at 25 cm was only 2.5 times the volume collected at 50 cm. Suggest why it was not 4 times greater. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25 cm: 16.0 units; 50 cm: 4.0 units
    M1 10 000 ÷ 25² = 10 000 ÷ 625; A1 16.0 and 4.0
  2. (b) 4 times
    B1 16 ÷ 4 = 4 (halving the distance gives four times the light)
  3. (c) At the higher light intensity another factor, such as carbon dioxide concentration or temperature, was limiting, so the rate could not rise in proportion to light.
    B1 another factor limiting at high light (CO₂ / temperature named); B1 so rate is not proportional to light intensity / light no longer the only limiting factor

  3. (c) Calculate the percentage increase in rate at a light intensity of 8 units when the carbon dioxide concentration is raised from 0.04% to 0.10%. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]

  1. (a) Light intensity; the rate is the same at both carbon dioxide concentrations (and rises when light increases).

Q14Mineral ions · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Nitrate ions are often at a higher concentration inside root hair cells than in the soil water. Explain how root hair cells absorb nitrate ions. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why a crop growing in waterlogged soil absorbs fewer nitrate ions. [3]
  3. (c) Explain how adding nitrate fertiliser increases the yield of a wheat crop. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) By active transport; ions move against the concentration gradient (from lower to higher concentration) using carrier proteins in the membrane; energy is released by respiration in the root cells.
    B1 active transport; B1 against the concentration gradient; B1 energy from respiration (carrier proteins)
  2. (b) Waterlogged soil contains little oxygen (air spaces filled with water); root cells carry out less aerobic respiration; so less energy is released for active transport of ions.
    B1 less oxygen in soil; B1 less aerobic respiration in roots; B1 less energy for active transport
  3. (c) Nitrate is used to make amino acids and proteins; these are needed for growth of new cells (e.g. enzymes, more leaves), so the plant grows larger and produces more grain.
    B1 amino acids/proteins made; B1 more growth / more leaves / more photosynthesis → greater yield

    B1 makes carbohydrates/glucose from CO₂ and water; B1 using light energy
  2. (b) Chlorophyll absorbs light energy; and transfers it into chemical energy (for making carbohydrates).
    B1 absorbs light; B1 converts/transfers it to chemical energy

Q26Photosynthesis equation & products · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A maize crop was kept in a sealed chamber with 12 hours of light and 12 hours of darkness. In the light, the crop took in carbon dioxide at a rate of 30 units per hour. In the dark, it released carbon dioxide at a rate of 6 units per hour.

  1. (a) Calculate the net uptake of carbon dioxide by the crop over the whole 24 hours. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the crop releases carbon dioxide at night. [2]
  3. (c) The measured uptake of 30 units per hour in the light is less than the true rate of photosynthesis. Explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 288 units
    M1 (30 × 12) − (6 × 12) = 360 − 72; A1 288
  2. (b) There is no light so no photosynthesis; respiration continues all the time and releases carbon dioxide.
    B1 no photosynthesis in dark; B1 respiration releases CO₂
  3. (c) During the day the plant also respires; some of the carbon dioxide used in photosynthesis comes from respiration inside the plant, so the measured uptake = photosynthesis − respiration.
    B1 respiration also happens in the light; B1 CO₂ from respiration used, so uptake underestimates gross photosynthesis

Q29Leaf structure (option) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe how the arrangement of tissues in a leaf allows as much light as possible to be absorbed. [4]
  2. (b) Describe how guard cells control the opening of a stoma during the day and explain why this is important for photosynthesis. [3]

  1. (a) Leaf is broad/flat with large surface area; thin so light reaches all cells; upper epidermis and cuticle are transparent; palisade layer with most chloroplasts is just below the upper surface; spongy cells also contain chloroplasts to absorb light passing through.

    B1 guard cells absorb water/turgid; B1 stoma opens; B1 CO₂ enters for photosynthesis (O₂ leaves)
Q30Leaf structure (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the vascular bundles in a leaf support photosynthesis and transport its products. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why most leaves are thin. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why a leaf has a waxy cuticle even though this reduces gas exchange through the surface. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Xylem brings water (a raw material) and mineral ions such as magnesium to the mesophyll; phloem carries sucrose (and amino acids) made in photosynthesis away from the leaf to other parts of the plant; the network of veins means every cell is close to a vein; xylem also helps to support the leaf so it is held out flat to the light.
    1 mark each for any four: xylem supplies water; xylem supplies mineral ions; phloem removes sucrose/amino acids; translocation to growing/storage parts; branching veins close to all cells; support/lignin keeps blade spread
  2. (b) Short diffusion distance for gases (CO₂ in, O₂ out) to and from all cells; light can reach all the cells.
    B1 short diffusion distance for gases; B1 light penetrates to all cells
  3. (c) It reduces water loss by evaporation; gases can still enter through the stomata.
    B1 reduces water loss (gases use stomata)
