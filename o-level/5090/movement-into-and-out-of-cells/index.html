Q1Osmosis · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which statement describes osmosis? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A the net movement of water molecules from a dilute solution to a concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane
    Osmosis is the net movement of water molecules from higher water potential (dilute solution) to lower water potential (concentrated solution) through a partially permeable membrane. B describes diffusion in general, not osmosis.

Q3Osmosis · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Define osmosis. [2]
  2. (b) A plant cell is placed in pure water. Describe and explain what happens to the cell. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the plant cell in (b) does not burst. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The net movement of water molecules from a region of higher water potential (dilute solution) to a region of lower water potential (concentrated solution) through a partially permeable membrane.
    B1 net movement of water (molecules) from higher to lower water potential / dilute to concentrated; B1 through a partially permeable membrane.
  2. (b) Water enters the cell by osmosis because the cell sap has a lower water potential; the vacuole swells and pushes the cytoplasm against the cell wall; the cell becomes turgid.
    B1 water enters by osmosis; B1 vacuole gets bigger / cytoplasm pushed against wall; B1 cell becomes turgid / firm.
  3. (c) The strong cell wall resists the pressure and stops further expansion.
    B1 cell wall is strong / rigid / resists expansion.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in mass of egg A after 2 days. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Egg B had a mass of 70.6 g at the start and 61.3 g after 2 days. Calculate its percentage change in mass to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Explain the results for egg A and egg B. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 17.5 %
    M1 (85.1 − 72.4) ÷ 72.4 × 100; A1 17.5.

Q6Osmosis · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Limp spinach (palak) leaves become crisp again after being placed in a bowl of cold tap water for an hour. Explain why. [3]
  2. (b) The cook then sprinkles a lot of salt on the crisp leaves. Predict and explain what happens to the leaves. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Tap water has a higher water potential than the leaf cells; water enters the cells by osmosis; cells become turgid, making the leaves firm.
    B1 water potential comparison; B1 water enters by osmosis; B1 cells become turgid.
  2. (b) The leaves go limp again: the salt solution on the surface has a low water potential, so water leaves the cells by osmosis and the cells become flaccid (or plasmolysed).
    B1 leaves become limp; B1 water lost by osmosis to lower water potential.

Q9Osmosis · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A farmer in lower Sindh irrigates a field of tomato plants using water from a well that has become salty. After a few days the plants wilt. Explain, in terms of water potential, why the plants wilt. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest one way the farmer could reduce this problem. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The salty soil water has a lower water potential than the root hair cells; water moves out of the root hair cells (or not into them) by osmosis; less water reaches the leaves through the xylem; cells lose turgor / become flaccid, so the plant wilts.
    B1 soil water has lower water potential than cell sap; B1 water leaves root cells by osmosis / less absorbed; B1 cells become flaccid / lose turgor; B1 so leaves and stems are not supported and droop.
  2. (b) Irrigate with fresh (canal or rain) water to wash salt out of the soil; improve drainage; grow a more salt-tolerant crop.
    B1 any one sensible way.
Q10Osmosis · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Lemon and mango pickle (achar) is made by covering fruit pieces with a large amount of salt, and sometimes oil. Explain how the salt stops bacteria and fungi from spoiling the pickle. [3]
  2. (b) Jam contains a very high concentration of sugar. Suggest why jam keeps for months in a closed jar. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The salt makes a solution with a very low water potential; water leaves the bacterial and fungal cells by osmosis; the microorganisms are dehydrated so they cannot grow / reproduce / they die.
    B1 low water potential outside the microbes; B1 water lost from microbial cells by osmosis; B1 cells cannot carry out reactions / grow / die.
  2. (b) The concentrated sugar also gives a low water potential, so microorganisms lose water by osmosis and cannot grow.
    B1 same idea applied to sugar.
Q11Osmosis · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Red blood cells are placed in distilled water. Describe and explain what happens to them. [3]
  2. (b) Red blood cells are placed in a concentrated salt solution. State what happens to them. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why red blood cells do not change shape when placed in blood plasma. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water enters the cells by osmosis because the cytoplasm has a lower water potential than the water; the cells swell; they burst because they have no cell wall.
    B1 water enters by osmosis (higher to lower water potential); B1 swell; B1 burst as there is no cell wall.
  2. (b) They lose water by osmosis and shrink (become shrivelled / crenated).
    B1.
  3. (c) Plasma has the same water potential as the cytoplasm, so there is no net movement of water.
    B1 same water potential / no net movement.

Q18Osmosis · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Sea water has a much higher concentration of salts than blood plasma. Explain why a person who drinks a large amount of sea water may suffer from dehydration of body cells. [3]
  2. (b) A hospital drip fluid is made with the same water potential as blood plasma. Suggest what would happen to the red blood cells if a drip fluid with a much lower water potential than plasma were given by mistake. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why drinking fresh water helps the dehydrated body cells in (a) to recover. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Salts are absorbed into the blood, so the plasma and tissue fluid have a lower water potential than the cytoplasm of body cells; water leaves the cells by osmosis through the partially permeable cell membranes; the cells shrink / become dehydrated and cannot work properly.
    B1 plasma / tissue fluid has lower water potential than cells; B1 water leaves cells by osmosis (through partially permeable membrane); B1 cells shrink / dehydrated.
  2. (b) Water would leave the red blood cells by osmosis into the plasma; the cells would shrink (become crenated) and carry oxygen less well.
    B1 water leaves red cells by osmosis; B1 cells shrink / shrivel / less oxygen carried.
  3. (c) Fresh water is absorbed and raises the water potential of the plasma and tissue fluid above that of the cells, so water re-enters the cells by osmosis.
    B1 water potential outside raised so water enters cells by osmosis.

  3. (c) Some ions still diffused in where they were more concentrated outside than inside; or some energy came from anaerobic respiration / the poison did not stop all respiration.
    B1.
Q26Active transport · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Compare diffusion, osmosis and active transport by stating, for each process: (i) whether energy from respiration is needed, (ii) the direction of movement in relation to the concentration gradient (or water potential gradient), (iii) whether a partially permeable membrane is needed. [6]
  2. (b) Glucose is absorbed into the blood from the small intestine partly by active transport. Suggest why active transport is useful here even after a meal. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Diffusion: no energy; down the gradient (high to low concentration); no membrane needed. Osmosis: no energy; water moves from higher to lower water potential; partially permeable membrane needed. Active transport: energy needed; against the gradient (low to high); takes place across a cell membrane using carrier proteins.
    1 mark each for any six correct entries out of the nine (energy, direction and membrane for each of the three processes).
  2. (b) As absorption continues the glucose concentration in the gut falls below that in the cells / blood; active transport can still absorb it against the gradient so none is wasted.
    B1 concentration in gut becomes lower than in blood; B1 active transport allows all glucose to be absorbed.
Q27Diffusion · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) The smell of biryani cooking in a kitchen soon reaches people in the next room, even though the air is still. Which process is responsible? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B diffusion
    Scent molecules spread by random movement from a region of higher concentration to lower concentration, which is diffusion. Osmosis (C) involves only water moving across a partially permeable membrane.
Q28Diffusion · Standard · Paper 4 style · 8 marks

Agar cubes containing a pink indicator were placed in dilute acid. The indicator turns colourless when acid reaches it. The time for each cube to become completely colourless was recorded.cube side 1 cm: 4 mincube side 2 cm: 9 mincube side 3 cm: 15 min

  1. (a) Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. Give each as a number to 1 decimal place. [3]
  2. (b) Describe the relationship between surface area to volume ratio and the time taken for the colour to change completely. [1]
  3. (c) Explain this relationship. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest how this result helps to explain why large organisms need specialised exchange surfaces and transport systems. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 cm: 6.0; 2 cm: 3.0; 3 cm: 2.0
    B1 each: surface area = 6 × side², volume = side³ (6 ÷ 1, 24 ÷ 8, 54 ÷ 27).
  2. (b) The larger the ratio, the shorter the time (the faster the cube changes colour).
    B1.
  3. (c) Acid diffuses in through the surface; smaller cubes have more surface for their volume and a shorter distance from surface to centre, so acid reaches the centre sooner.
    B1 more surface area per unit volume for diffusion; B1 shorter diffusion distance to centre.
  4. (d) Large organisms have a small surface area to volume ratio and long diffusion distances; diffusion through the body surface alone is too slow to supply all cells.
    B1 small SA:V ratio in large organisms; B1 diffusion too slow / distance too long.
Q29Diffusion · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Define diffusion. [2]
  2. (b) State and explain two factors that increase the rate of diffusion of oxygen into the blood in the lungs. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The net movement of particles from a region of higher concentration to a region of lower concentration (down a concentration gradient), as a result of their random movement.
    B1 net movement from higher to lower concentration; B1 random movement of particles.
  2. (b) Large surface area of alveoli, so more molecules cross at once; thin walls (short distance), so molecules travel less far; steep concentration gradient kept by breathing and blood flow; higher temperature, as particles have more kinetic energy.
    B1 factor + B1 explanation, for any two.
Q30Diffusion · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A crystal of potassium manganate(VII) was placed in the centre of a dish of water at 10 °C and another at 40 °C. The distance the purple colour had spread from the crystal was measured.time / min: 10, 20, 30distance at 10 °C / mm: 6, 11, 17distance at 40 °C / mm: 15, 29, 44

  1. (a) Calculate the mean rate at which the purple colour spread over 30 minutes at each temperature, in mm per minute. Give answers to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the difference between the two rates. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two variables that should be controlled. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why the dishes must not be moved or stirred during the experiment. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10 °C: 0.57 mm/min; 40 °C: 1.47 mm/min
    A1 17 ÷ 30 = 0.57; A1 44 ÷ 30 = 1.47.
  2. (b) At the higher temperature the particles have more kinetic energy and move faster, so diffusion is faster.
    B1 more kinetic energy; B1 faster random movement so faster net spread.
  3. (c) Size / mass of crystal; volume and depth of water; same type of dish; dish not disturbed or stirred; same time of measurement.
    B1 each for any two.
  4. (d) Stirring would spread the dye by mixing (convection), not by diffusion.
    B1.
