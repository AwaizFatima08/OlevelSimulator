Q10Eye & sense organs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Before examining the retina, an eye doctor puts drops into a patient's eyes. The drops relax the circular muscles of the iris. State and explain the effect on the pupil. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why this makes it easier for the doctor to see the retina. [1]
  3. (c) The patient is advised to wear sunglasses and not to drive for a few hours. Suggest why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The pupil dilates (widens): the circular muscles are relaxed so they cannot constrict the pupil, and the radial muscles pull it wider open.
    1 mark dilates; 1 mark explanation linked to muscles.
  2. (b) A wider pupil lets the doctor see more of the retina / lets more light in to see the retina.
    B1.
  3. (c) The pupil cannot become smaller, so too much light enters in bright conditions; this causes glare/dazzle and could damage the retina, and vision may be unclear for driving.
    1 mark each: pupil reflex cannot reduce light; glare / risk to retina / poor vision when driving.
Q11Eye & sense organs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A pharmacist looks at a distant shelf and then reads the small print on a medicine label held 25 cm from her eyes.

  1. (a) Describe the changes in the eye that allow the label to be seen clearly. [3]
  2. (b) As people age, the lens becomes less elastic. Suggest why older people often find it difficult to read small print but can still see distant objects clearly. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Ciliary muscles contract; suspensory ligaments slacken / become loose; lens becomes thicker / more convex (and refracts light more).
    1 mark each
  2. (b) The lens cannot become fat / round enough on its own when the ligaments slacken; so light from near objects is not focused on the retina, whereas the thin lens shape for distant vision is still achieved.
    B1 lens cannot become thicker / more convex; B1 near image not focused on retina

Q23Homeostasis: temperature & skin · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark

    1 mark each.

Q116Hormones (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

  2. (b) Explain why the control of blood glucose is an example of homeostasis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) Nervous: electrical impulses; along neurones; very fast; short-lived response (muscle contracts). (ii) Hormonal: chemical (insulin); carried in the blood plasma; slower; longer-lasting; can affect several target organs (liver, muscles).
    1 mark each for any six comparison points, at least one from each response: electrical vs chemical; neurones vs blood; fast vs slow; short vs long-lasting; specific effector vs target organs; reflex via spinal cord vs pancreas detects change

    B1 constant internal conditions; B1 negative feedback / returns to normal
Q117Hormones (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A student takes an early lunch of rice and dal at 12:00, sleeps from 14:00 to 15:00 and runs a 400 m race at 17:00. Describe and explain the roles of insulin, glucagon and adrenaline in controlling her blood glucose during the day. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) After lunch: glucose absorbed, blood glucose rises; pancreas secretes insulin; liver/muscles convert glucose to glycogen; glucose falls back to set point. During the nap/long gap: glucose used in respiration falls below set point; pancreas secretes glucagon; liver breaks down glycogen to glucose. Before/during race: excitement – adrenal glands secrete adrenaline; liver releases glucose; extra glucose for muscle respiration; negative feedback keeps glucose near set point.
    1 mark each for any six points, at least one point for each hormone for full marks.

Q120Hormones (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) Give two differences between the ways the two are controlled. [2]
