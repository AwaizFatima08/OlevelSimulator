Original questions written by HomiLabs in the style of the exam, with answers and mark schemes. They are not Cambridge past paper questions and have not been reviewed by Cambridge. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which type of cell produces antibodies? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C lymphocyte
    Lymphocytes produce antibodies. Phagocytes engulf and digest pathogens but do not make antibodies.
Q2Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by an antigen. [1]
  2. (b) Describe two ways in which antibodies help to destroy pathogens. [2]
  3. (c) Explain the difference between an antibody and an antibiotic. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) A molecule (e.g. a protein) on the surface of a pathogen / cell that stimulates antibody production.
    B1
  2. (b) Bind to antigens and mark pathogens so phagocytes can engulf them; directly destroy pathogens / cause them to clump together.
    1 mark each
  3. (c) An antibody is a protein made by lymphocytes in the body that binds to a specific antigen; an antibiotic is a drug (medicine) taken to kill bacteria (or stop their growth).
    B1 antibody: protein made by lymphocytes / body; B1 antibiotic: drug that kills bacteria
Q3Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why antibiotics cannot be used to treat measles or influenza. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how a person develops active immunity after recovering from chickenpox. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) These diseases are caused by viruses; antibiotics only kill bacteria / do not affect viruses.
    B1 caused by virus; B1 antibiotics act on bacteria only
  2. (b) Lymphocytes produced antibodies against the virus antigens during the infection; memory cells remain and give fast antibody production on re-infection.
    B1 antibodies made by own lymphocytes; B1 memory cells

Q5Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Polio vaccination campaigns in Pakistan give drops of vaccine to young children.

  1. (a) Describe what a vaccine contains. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how vaccination protects a person against polio for a long time. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Harmless / weakened / dead pathogen, or its antigens; which stimulate an immune response but do not cause the disease.
    B1 weakened / dead pathogen or antigens; B1 does not cause disease
  2. (b) Antigens in the vaccine are recognised by lymphocytes; lymphocytes produce specific antibodies; memory cells are formed which stay in the blood; on later infection antibodies are made quickly before the person becomes ill.
    1 mark each for any three
Q6Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A person bitten by a snake may be given an injection containing ready-made antibodies against the venom. State the type of immunity this gives. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why this injection gives immediate protection, but only for a short time. [3]
  3. (c) State one natural way in which a fetus gains passive immunity. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Passive immunity
    B1
  2. (b) The antibodies are already made, so act at once without waiting for lymphocytes to respond; they are not made by the person's body and are broken down; no memory cells are produced.
    B1 antibodies ready to act at once; B1 broken down / not replaced; B1 no memory cells
  3. (c) Antibodies cross the placenta from the mother.
    B1
Q7Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Why does one type of antibody protect against only one type of pathogen? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A each antibody has a shape complementary to one specific antigen
    Antibodies are proteins with a specific shape that fits one antigen. C is wrong: different pathogens carry different antigens.
Q8Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A child was given two doses of a hepatitis B vaccine, five weeks apart. Antibody concentration (arbitrary units) was measured each week after each dose.week after dose | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5after first dose | 0 | 2 | 8 | 12 | 6 | 3after second dose | 3 | 40 | 90 | 85 | 70 | 60

  1. (a) Calculate how many times higher the peak antibody concentration was after the second dose than after the first dose. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the response to the second dose is faster and greater. [3]
  3. (c) State the type of immunity produced by vaccination. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.5 times
    M1 90 ÷ 12; A1 7.5
  2. (b) The first dose caused memory cells to be made; memory cells recognise the antigen at once on second exposure; they rapidly produce many lymphocytes / large amounts of antibody.
    B1 memory cells made after first dose; B1 recognise antigen; B1 rapid / greater antibody production
  3. (c) Active (artificial) immunity
    B1
Q9Antibodies, immunity & vaccination · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In one region, 9,120,000 out of 9,600,000 children under five were given measles vaccine.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage of children vaccinated. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how vaccinating a large percentage of a population also protects people who are not vaccinated. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest one reason why some children in a population might not be vaccinated. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 95 %
    M1 9,120,000 ÷ 9,600,000 × 100; A1 95 %
  2. (b) Most people are immune, so the pathogen cannot easily spread / there are few people to infect; an infected person is unlikely to meet a non-immune person; so the disease is controlled (herd immunity).
    B1 most people immune / cannot pass on; B1 less chance unvaccinated person meets an infected person; B1 spread controlled / herd immunity
  3. (c) Too young / medical condition / weak immune system / parents refuse / live in remote areas.
    B1
Q10Pathogens & transmission · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) What is a pathogen? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B a disease-causing organism
    A pathogen is a disease-causing organism. A is wrong because most microorganisms are harmless or useful.
Q11Pathogens & transmission · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Define the term transmissible disease. [1]
  2. (b) Copy and complete the table to give one example of each method of transmission.method | example of how pathogen is passeddirect contact | ?contaminated food | ?animal (vector) | ? [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) A disease in which the pathogen can be passed from one host to another.
    B1
  2. (b) Direct contact: through blood / body fluids (e.g. sharing needles, hepatitis B). Food: eating undercooked meat or eggs, or food handled with unwashed hands (e.g. Salmonella food poisoning, typhoid). Vector: mosquito bite (malaria / dengue) or houseflies carrying pathogens from faeces to food.

Q12Pathogens & transmission · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State two ways in which hepatitis B virus can be passed from one person to another. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why barbers are advised to use a new blade for each customer. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Infected blood (e.g. sharing needles / razors, unsterilised equipment); sexual contact; from mother to baby at birth.
    1 mark each for any two
  2. (b) A used blade could carry blood containing the virus into a cut on the next customer.
    B1
Q13Pathogens & transmission · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which disease is transmitted mainly through contaminated drinking water? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A cholera
    Cholera bacteria are passed on in water contaminated with faeces. Influenza, tuberculosis and measles are spread mainly by droplets in the air.
Q14Pathogens & transmission · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how each of the following reduces the spread of disease: (i) treating sewage (ii) proper disposal of household rubbish (iii) washing hands before preparing food. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why outbreaks of diarrhoeal disease often follow floods. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) removes / kills pathogens in faeces before water is returned to rivers (ii) prevents breeding sites for flies / rats that carry pathogens (iii) removes pathogens that could enter food
    1 mark each
  2. (b) Floods mix sewage with drinking water supplies; people drink water containing pathogens.
    B1 sewage contaminates water; B1 pathogens ingested
Q15Pathogens & transmission · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Malaria is caused by a single-celled organism and transmitted by female Anopheles mosquitoes. Explain what is meant by a vector. [1]
  2. (b) Suggest two ways people in rural Sindh can reduce the spread of malaria. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) An organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another.
    B1
  2. (b) Sleep under insecticide-treated mosquito nets; remove standing water where mosquitoes breed; use insect repellent; spray insecticides; cover water containers.
    1 mark each for any two
Q16Pathogens & transmission · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A plate of cooked rice is left in a warm kitchen. It contains 50 bacteria, which divide every 20 minutes.

  1. (a) Calculate how many bacteria there would be after 4 hours if none died. [2]
  2. (b) Use this information to explain why food such as cooked rice should not be left in a warm kitchen for hours. [2]
  3. (c) State one way in which food can be stored to slow down bacterial growth. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 204,800
    M1 number of divisions = 240 ÷ 20 = 12; A1 50 × 2^12 = 204,800
  2. (b) Bacteria (pathogens) reproduce very quickly at warm temperatures; large numbers can build up and cause food poisoning when eaten.
    B1 rapid reproduction in warmth; B1 enough to cause disease
  3. (c) Refrigerate / freeze / cover it / reheat thoroughly before eating.
    B1
Q17Pathogens & transmission · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Tuberculosis (TB) is caused by a bacterium that is spread in droplets when an infected person coughs. Suggest why TB spreads more quickly in crowded housing. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why it is important for TB patients to complete their full course of antibiotics. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) People are close together, so droplets are more likely to be breathed in by others; often poor ventilation so droplets stay in the air.
    B1 close contact; B1 poor ventilation / more droplets inhaled
  2. (b) If the course is stopped early, some bacteria survive; the survivors may be resistant and multiply, spreading resistant TB.
    B1 not all bacteria killed; B1 resistant bacteria survive / spread
Q18Specific diseases (cholera etc.) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name the type of organism that causes cholera. [1]
  2. (b) Describe how cholera is usually spread. [2]
  3. (c) State two ways a city can reduce cholera outbreaks. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bacterium
    B1
  2. (b) Drinking water (or eating food) contaminated with faeces containing the bacteria.
    B1 contaminated water / food; B1 with faeces from an infected person
  3. (c) Treat / chlorinate drinking water; proper sewage treatment / sanitation; boil water; hand washing.
    1 mark each for any two
Q19Specific diseases (cholera etc.) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A recipe for oral rehydration solution uses 2.6 g of salt (sodium chloride) and 13.5 g of glucose in 1000 cm³ of water.

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of salt and the mass of glucose needed to make 250 cm³ of the solution. Give each answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest why the water used to make the solution should be boiled and cooled first. [1]
  3. (c) Suggest why a child with cholera can die within a day if not treated. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) salt 0.65 g; glucose 3.38 g
    B1 2.6 × 250/1000 = 0.65 g; B1 13.5 × 250/1000 = 3.375 = 3.38 g
  2. (b) To kill any pathogens in the water so the child is not infected again.
    B1
  3. (c) Very rapid loss of water and salts in diarrhoea; severe dehydration (low blood volume) stops organs working.
    B1 rapid water / ion loss; B1 dehydration leads to organ failure / shock
Q20Specific diseases (cholera etc.) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Dengue fever is caused by a virus carried by Aedes mosquitoes. Explain why antibiotics are not given to treat dengue. [1]
  2. (b) Aedes mosquitoes breed in small amounts of clean standing water. Suggest two actions a school in Rawalpindi could take to reduce dengue during the monsoon. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Antibiotics do not kill viruses.
    B1
  2. (b) Empty / cover water containers, flower pots, old tyres; clear blocked drains; use repellents / long sleeves; fit window screens.
    1 mark each for any two
Q21Specific diseases (cholera etc.) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Typhoid is caused by a bacterium spread in food and water contaminated by faeces. State one symptom that typhoid and cholera may both cause, and explain why this is dangerous. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a person who has recovered from cholera can still pass the disease on. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Diarrhoea (or fever / vomiting); causes dehydration / loss of water and salts.
    B1 symptom; B1 dehydration
  2. (b) They may still carry the bacteria in their gut / faeces for some time; these can contaminate water or food.
    B1 still carries bacteria; B1 in faeces → water / food
Q22Specific diseases (cholera etc.) · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Why does cholera cause severe watery diarrhoea? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B A toxin causes chloride ions to be secreted into the small intestine, and water follows by osmosis.
    The cholera toxin causes chloride ions to be released into the small intestine. This lowers the water potential of the gut contents, so water moves out of the blood into the gut by osmosis. D wrongly blames the kidneys.
Q23Specific diseases (cholera etc.) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain, using the term water potential, how cholera bacteria in the small intestine cause dehydration. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why oral rehydration solution contains salts as well as water. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Bacteria release a toxin; toxin causes chloride ions to be secreted into the lumen of the small intestine; this lowers the water potential of the intestinal contents; water moves from the blood / tissues into the gut by osmosis; lost as watery diarrhoea.
    1 mark each for any four
  2. (b) Salts (ions) are lost in the diarrhoea and must be replaced; they help the body absorb and keep water.
    B1 replace lost ions / salts; B1 help water absorption
Q24Specific diseases (cholera etc.) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Reported cholera cases in one district:year | number of cases2019 | 12402020 | 8602021 | 6102022 | 29502023 | 720

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage increase in cases from 2021 to 2022, to the nearest whole number. [2]
  2. (b) Heavy floods occurred in this district in 2022. Suggest why this explains the change. [2]
  3. (c) Describe the trend from 2019 to 2021 and suggest one reason for it. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 384 %
    M1 (2950 − 610) ÷ 610 × 100; A1 384 %
  2. (b) Floods contaminated drinking water with sewage; people drank water containing cholera bacteria / sanitation broke down.
    B1 sewage mixes with drinking water; B1 bacteria ingested / sanitation failure
  3. (c) Cases fell each year (1240 → 610); e.g. better water treatment / sanitation / vaccination / hygiene campaigns.
    B1 trend with figures; B1 reason
Q25Body defences · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which is a chemical barrier against pathogens? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B hydrochloric acid in the stomach
    Stomach acid kills many pathogens in food, so it is a chemical barrier. Nose hairs and skin are mechanical barriers; phagocytosis is a cell defence.
Q26Body defences · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how each of the following helps to prevent pathogens entering the body: (i) skin (ii) mucus in the airways (iii) hairs in the nose. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why a deep cut increases the risk of infection. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) forms a barrier that pathogens cannot pass through (ii) traps pathogens (which are then removed by cilia / swallowed) (iii) trap dust and pathogens in inhaled air
    1 mark each
  2. (b) The skin barrier is broken, so pathogens can enter the blood / tissues.
    B1
Q27Body defences · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how a phagocyte destroys a bacterium. [3]
  2. (b) State one other way in which white blood cells defend the body. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The phagocyte moves towards / is attracted to the bacterium; it engulfs the bacterium (surrounds it with its cytoplasm / membrane); it digests it with enzymes.
    1 mark each
  2. (b) Lymphocytes produce antibodies.
    B1
Q28Body defences · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Smoking damages the cilia lining the trachea. Explain why smokers get more chest infections. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how blood clotting at a wound helps defend against disease. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Cilia normally beat to move mucus containing trapped pathogens up to the throat; damaged cilia cannot do this; mucus and pathogens collect in the lungs and cause infection.
    B1 cilia move mucus; B1 mucus traps pathogens; B1 mucus / pathogens stay in lungs
  2. (b) The clot seals the wound; preventing pathogens from entering (and blood loss).
    B1 seals / blocks wound; B1 stops entry of pathogens
Q29Pathogens & transmission (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Describe how influenza spreads from person to person, and suggest three measures that could reduce its spread in a school. [5]
  2. (b) Explain why a new influenza vaccine is needed most years. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Spread: infected person coughs / sneezes droplets containing the virus, which are breathed in by others (or touched on surfaces then transferred to mouth/nose). Measures: cover mouth when coughing / wear masks; wash hands regularly; keep sick pupils at home; ventilate classrooms; vaccination.
    B1 droplets from coughing / sneezing; B1 inhaled / via surfaces; 1 mark each for three measures
  2. (b) The virus changes (mutates) and its antigens change; antibodies / memory cells from the old vaccine no longer fit the new antigens.
    B1 virus mutates / antigens change; B1 old antibodies / memory cells do not recognise it
Q30Pathogens & transmission (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why diseases such as cholera and typhoid are much more common where there is no clean water supply or sewage system. [3]
  2. (b) A village in Balochistan gets a new well and a simple filter-and-chlorine system. Explain how each part reduces disease. [2]
  3. (c) State one way in which the body's own defences protect against swallowed bacteria. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Faeces from infected people contain pathogens; without sewage treatment these get into rivers / wells used for drinking; people drink untreated water and are infected; flies may carry pathogens from faeces to food.
    1 mark each for any three
  2. (b) Filter removes particles / some microorganisms; chlorine kills remaining pathogens.
    B1 filtering; B1 chlorination
  3. (c) Hydrochloric acid in the stomach kills many bacteria.
    B1
