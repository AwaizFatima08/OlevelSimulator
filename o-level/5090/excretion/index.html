Original questions written by HomiLabs in the style of the exam, with answers and mark schemes. They are not Cambridge past paper questions and have not been reviewed by Cambridge. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Kidney & nephron · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which tube carries urine from a kidney to the bladder?A renal veinB urethraC ureterD renal artery [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C ureter
    The ureter carries urine from each kidney to the bladder. The urethra carries urine from the bladder out of the body, so B is the main distractor.
Q2Kidney & nephron · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which substance is normally found in the glomerular filtrate but NOT in the urine of a healthy person?A glucoseB saltsC ureaD water [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A glucose
    All glucose is reabsorbed in the proximal tubule. Urea passes into urine, so C is wrong.
Q3Kidney & nephron · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Define excretion. [2]
  2. (b) Name the organ that excretes carbon dioxide and the organ that excretes urea. [2]
  3. (c) Name the tube that carries urine out of the body from the bladder. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) removal from the body of toxic waste products of metabolism (and substances in excess of requirements)
    B1 removal of (toxic) waste of metabolism / made by cells; B1 substances in excess
  2. (b) lungs; kidneys
    B1 each
  3. (c) urethra
    B1
Q4Kidney & nephron · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Name the blood vessel that brings blood to the kidney. [1]
  2. (b) Name the three regions seen when a kidney is cut lengthwise, starting from the outside. [3]
  3. (c) State where the glomeruli are found. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) renal artery
    B1
  2. (b) cortex, medulla, pelvis
    B1 each, in order
  3. (c) cortex
    B1
Q5Kidney & nephron · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State two substances that are reabsorbed into the blood from the kidney tubules. [2]
  2. (b) Name the structure that stores urine. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) glucose, water, salts (ions), amino acids
    1 mark each for any two
  2. (b) bladder
    B1
Q6Kidney & nephron · Core · Paper 4 style · 4 marks

A sheep kidney was cut lengthwise. It has a dark outer layer (cortex) about 5 mm thick, a paler inner region (medulla) made of cone-shaped pyramids, and a white funnel-shaped space (pelvis) at the concave side, from which the ureter leaves.

  1. (a) State which region of the kidney described contains:(i) the glomeruli and Bowman's capsules(ii) the loops of Henle and collecting ducts [2]
  2. (b) The actual length of the kidney was 60 mm. A student drew the section 150 mm long. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) cortex; (ii) medulla
    B1 each
  2. (b) 150 ÷ 60 = ×2.5
    M1 drawing ÷ actual; A1 ×2.5
Q7Kidney & nephron · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State the function of the glomerulus. [1]
  2. (b) State the function of the collecting duct. [1]
  3. (c) Name the blood vessel that carries filtered blood away from the kidney. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) ultrafiltration of blood (forcing water and small solutes into the capsule)
    B1
  2. (b) reabsorbs water; carries urine to the pelvis
    B1
  3. (c) renal vein
    B1
Q8Kidney & nephron · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Why are proteins absent from the filtrate in the Bowman's capsule?A They are actively absorbed by the glomerulus.B Their molecules are too large to pass through the filter.C They are digested in the kidney.D They are converted to urea in the glomerulus. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B Their molecules are too large to pass through the filter.
    Ultrafiltration lets only small molecules through the capillary wall and capsule lining. Urea is made in the liver, not the glomerulus, so D is wrong.
Q9Kidney & nephron · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Concentrations in g per 100 cm³:substance | blood plasma | filtrate in Bowman's capsule | urinewater | 92 | 98 | 96protein | 7.0 | 0.0 | 0.0glucose | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.00urea | 0.03 | 0.03 | 2.00salts | 0.72 | 0.72 | 1.50

  1. (a) Use the table to name the substance that is not filtered. Explain why. [2]
  2. (b) Glucose is present in the filtrate but not in the urine. Name the process responsible and where it happens. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate how many times more concentrated urea is in urine than in plasma. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why urea becomes more concentrated in urine. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) protein; molecules too large to pass through the capillary wall/basement membrane
    B1 protein; B1 too large
  2. (b) selective reabsorption; in the (proximal convoluted) tubule
    B1 (selective) reabsorption; B1 proximal (first) tubule
  3. (c) 2.00 ÷ 0.03 = 67 times
    M1 2.00 ÷ 0.03; A1 67
  4. (d) water is reabsorbed from the filtrate (but urea mostly is not)
    B1
Q10Kidney & nephron · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Describe how filtrate is formed in the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule. [3]
  2. (b) State the name of this process. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) blood arrives at high pressure (afferent vessel wider than efferent); small molecules such as water, glucose, salts and urea are forced out through the capillary walls into the capsule; large proteins and blood cells stay in the blood
    B1 high pressure; B1 small molecules named pass out; B1 proteins/cells remain (ultrafiltration)
  2. (b) ultrafiltration
    B1
Q11Kidney & nephron · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A healthy adult produces urine at a mean rate of 1.2 cm³ per minute. The urine contains 20 g of urea per dm³.

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of urea excreted in one day (24 hours). Give your answer in grams to 3 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) On one day this person's urine gives an orange colour with Benedict's solution. Suggest an explanation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1.2 × 60 × 24 = 1 728 cm³ = 1.728 dm³; 1.728 × 20 = 34.6 g
    M1 volume per day 1 728 cm³ (1.728 dm³); A1 34.6 g
  2. (b) glucose is present in the urine; blood glucose concentration was too high (e.g. diabetes), so not all the glucose in the filtrate could be reabsorbed
    B1 glucose in urine; B1 blood glucose too high / not all reabsorbed in the tubule (diabetes)
Q12Kidney & nephron · Standard · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Artificial urine samples from three patients were tested.sample 1: Benedict's blue; biuret bluesample 2: Benedict's orange; biuret bluesample 3: Benedict's blue; biuret purple

  1. (a) Identify which sample is from the person likely to have diabetes. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Sample 3 contains protein. Suggest what this shows about the kidneys of person 3. [1]
  3. (c) Describe how the biuret test was carried out on the samples. [2]
  4. (d) State one safety precaution when testing urine. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) sample 2; it gave a positive (orange) Benedict's result showing glucose in urine
    B1 sample 2; B1 glucose present
  2. (b) damage to the glomerulus/filter (proteins leak through)
    B1
  3. (c) add biuret solution (sodium hydroxide then copper sulfate) to equal volume of urine; purple = protein
    B1 reagent; B1 purple positive
  4. (d) wear gloves / goggles; dispose of samples safely; wash hands
    B1
Q13Kidney & nephron · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A man runs a marathon in hot weather in Dubai and drinks little water. Predict how the volume and colour of his urine will change. Explain your answer. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why urine is still produced even when the body is short of water. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) smaller volume; darker / more concentrated; much water lost as sweat, so more water reabsorbed in the tubules/collecting ducts
    B1 smaller volume; B1 darker/more concentrated; B1 more water reabsorbed due to sweating
  2. (b) urea (toxic waste) must still be removed in solution
    B1
Q14Kidney & nephron · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) The blood in the renal vein, compared with the blood in the renal artery, hasA less urea and less oxygen.B more urea and less oxygen.C less urea and more oxygen.D more glucose and more oxygen. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A less urea and less oxygen.
    The kidney removes urea, and kidney cells use oxygen in respiration. Oxygen does not increase as blood passes through an organ, so C is wrong.
Q15Kidney & nephron · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In an adult, about 125 cm³ of filtrate passes from the blood into the nephrons every minute.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of filtrate formed per day in dm³. [2]
  2. (b) On the same day the person produced 1.5 dm³ of urine. Calculate the percentage of the filtrate that was reabsorbed. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the volume of urine produced is larger on a cool, rainy day than on a hot day. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 125 × 60 × 24 = 180 000 cm³ = 180 dm³
    M1 125 × 1440; A1 180
  2. (b) (180 − 1.5) ÷ 180 × 100 = 99.2 %
    M1; A1 99.2
  3. (c) less water lost in sweat on a cool day; so less water is reabsorbed by the kidneys and more urine is made
    B1 less sweating; B1 less water reabsorbed / more dilute, larger volume
Q16Kidney & nephron · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the path of a urea molecule from the renal artery until it leaves the body. [4]
  2. (b) Explain why the cells lining the proximal tubule have many mitochondria. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) renal artery → glomerulus → (ultrafiltration into) Bowman's capsule → tubule (proximal, loop of Henle, distal) → collecting duct → pelvis → ureter → bladder → urethra
    1 mark each for any four correct stages in order: glomerulus; Bowman's capsule; tubule/loop of Henle; collecting duct; ureter; bladder; urethra
  2. (b) they reabsorb some substances (e.g. glucose, salts) by active transport; active transport needs energy from (aerobic) respiration in mitochondria
    B1 active transport/reabsorption; B1 energy/ATP from respiration
Q17Kidney & nephron · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks

Three volunteers each drank 1 dm³ of water on one day and 1 dm³ of salty soup on another. Urine volume collected in the next 2 hours (cm³):water: 520, 480, 560salty soup: 210, 190, 230

  1. (a) Calculate the mean volume of urine for each drink, to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the difference in mean urine volume. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two variables that should be controlled. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) water: (520 + 480 + 560) ÷ 3 = 520.0 cm³; salty soup: (210 + 190 + 230) ÷ 3 = 210.0 cm³
    B1 each
  2. (b) salty drink raises salt concentration of blood (lowers water potential); kidneys reabsorb more water so less urine; after pure water, excess water is excreted
    B1 excess water excreted after water; B1 more water reabsorbed after salty drink
  3. (c) volume drunk; time of day; activity/temperature; body mass; food eaten before; time of last emptying of bladder
    1 mark each for any two
Q18Kidney & nephron · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how the structure of the glomerulus and Bowman's capsule is adapted for ultrafiltration. [3]
  2. (b) A person's blood pressure falls very low. Suggest the effect on filtration and urine production. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) vessel leaving the glomerulus is narrower than the one entering, creating high pressure; large surface area of capillary knot; thin walls (capillary wall + capsule lining) with pores that let small molecules pass
    1 mark each for three of: narrower efferent vessel/high pressure; large surface area/many capillaries; thin walls/short distance; pores/partially permeable filter
  2. (b) less pressure in the glomerulus so less filtrate formed; less urine produced
    B1 less filtration; B1 less urine
Q19Urea & deamination · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Where in the body is urea made?A bladderB kidneyC liverD pancreas [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C liver
    Urea is made in the liver by deamination of excess amino acids; the kidney only removes it, so B is wrong.
Q20Urea & deamination · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by deamination. [2]
  2. (b) State how urea travels from where it is made to the kidneys. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) removal of the nitrogen-containing (amino) part of excess amino acids, forming urea
    B1 removal of nitrogen/amine group; B1 from excess amino acids (to form urea)
  2. (b) dissolved in blood plasma
    B1
Q21Urea & deamination · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the body cannot store excess amino acids in the same way that it stores excess glucose. [1]
  2. (b) A wrestler eats a diet very rich in meat and eggs. Predict how the amount of urea in his urine will change, and explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) there is no storage form for amino acids (unlike glycogen for glucose), so the excess is deaminated
    B1
  2. (b) increases; more excess amino acids are deaminated in the liver, forming more urea
    B1 increase; B1 more deamination of excess amino acids
Q22Urea & deamination · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Compare urea and urine. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why urea must be removed from the body. [1]
  3. (c) After deamination, the remaining part of the amino acid is not wasted. State what happens to it. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) urea is a nitrogen-containing waste substance made in the liver; urine is the liquid made by the kidneys containing water, urea and salts
    B1 urea = substance made in liver by deamination; B1 urine = solution of urea, salts in water made by kidney
  2. (b) it is toxic (harmful) at high concentration
    B1
  3. (c) used in respiration to release energy, or converted to glycogen/fat for storage
    B1
Q23Urea & deamination · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A patient with kidney failure had a blood urea concentration of 1.2 g/dm³ before dialysis and 0.1 g/dm³ after.

  1. (a) Calculate the percentage decrease in urea in the blood during one dialysis session. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the patient's blood urea rises again between sessions. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (1.2 − 0.1) ÷ 1.2 × 100 = 91.7 %
    M1 difference ÷ 1.2 × 100; A1 91.7
  2. (b) the liver continues to deaminate excess amino acids, producing urea; the kidneys cannot remove it
    B1 urea still made in liver; B1 not removed by (failed) kidneys
Q24Dialysis & transplants (option) · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State why the dialysis fluid contains no urea. [1]
  2. (b) State why the dialysis membrane must be partially permeable. [1]
  3. (c) Give two reasons why dialysis is less convenient for patients than having a kidney transplant. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) so that urea diffuses out of the blood down a concentration gradient
    B1
  2. (b) lets small molecules (urea, salts, water) through but keeps blood cells and proteins in the blood
    B1
  3. (c) several sessions a week of several hours; must stay near hospital/machine; strict diet and fluid limits
    1 mark each for two
Q25Dialysis & transplants (option) · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Why does the dialysis fluid used in a kidney machine contain glucose at the same concentration as normal blood?A so that glucose diffuses into the bloodB so that glucose is not lost from the blood by diffusionC so that urea can diffuse into the bloodD so that the fluid gives the patient energy [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B so that glucose is not lost from the blood by diffusion
    With no concentration gradient, glucose does not diffuse out of the blood. If the fluid had extra glucose it would enter the blood, so A is wrong.
Q26Dialysis & transplants (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a dialysis machine removes urea while keeping glucose and useful salts in the blood. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate how many hours a patient spends on dialysis in one year if each session is 4 hours, three times a week. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) blood flows past a partially permeable membrane with dialysis fluid on the other side; fluid has no urea so urea diffuses out; fluid has glucose (and salts) at normal blood concentration so no net loss; excess salts diffuse out down a gradient; blood and fluid flow in opposite directions to keep gradients
    1 mark each for any four of: partially permeable membrane; urea diffuses out (none in fluid); glucose same concentration so no net movement; salt concentration controlled so excess removed; countercurrent flow/fluid constantly replaced keeps gradient
  2. (b) 4 × 3 × 52 = 624 hours
    B1 624
Q27Kidney & nephron (option) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the processes that take place in a nephron to produce urine from blood plasma. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) ultrafiltration in glomerulus at high pressure; small molecules (water, glucose, salts, urea) enter Bowman's capsule; proteins and cells stay in blood; selective reabsorption in tubule: all glucose, some salts, much water back into blood; water reabsorbed in loop/collecting duct; urine (water, urea, excess salts) passes to the collecting duct and ureter
    1 mark each for up to five of: high pressure in glomerulus; ultrafiltration; named small substances filtered; proteins/cells not filtered; glucose all reabsorbed; water reabsorbed; salts reabsorbed as needed; active transport in reabsorption; urine contains urea, salts, water
Q28Kidney & nephron (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A person in Jacobabad, one of the hottest cities in Pakistan, produces about 0.8 dm³ of urine a day in June but 1.6 dm³ in January.

  1. (a) Explain why the person needs to drink more water in the hot season to keep the same urine output. [2]
  2. (b) Kidney stones can block a ureter. Suggest two effects of a complete blockage of one ureter. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why drinking plenty of water is advised for people likely to form kidney stones. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) more water lost in sweat for cooling; kidneys reabsorb more water so less urine unless intake rises
    B1 sweating; B1 more reabsorption
  2. (b) urine cannot leave that kidney / builds up, causing pain/swelling; that kidney may be damaged; the other kidney carries out excretion (urine still produced)
    1 mark each for two
  3. (c) urine is more dilute, so salts are less likely to crystallise
    B1
Q29Dialysis & transplants · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State one advantage of a kidney transplant compared with dialysis. [1]
  2. (b) State two problems with kidney transplants. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) patient can lead a near-normal life / no regular hospital sessions / fewer diet restrictions / cheaper in the long term
    B1
  2. (b) rejection by the immune system; need for lifelong immunosuppressive drugs (risk of infection); shortage of donors / need tissue match
    1 mark each for two
Q30Dialysis & transplants · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a kidney from a close relative is more likely to be successful than one from an unrelated donor. [2]
  2. (b) Patients take drugs that suppress the immune system after a transplant. Explain one disadvantage of this. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) a relative's tissue (antigens) is more similar; less chance of the immune system recognising it as foreign and rejecting it
    B1 similar antigens/tissue type; B1 less rejection by lymphocytes/antibodies
  2. (b) the patient is less able to fight infections, so more likely to become ill from pathogens
    B1 weakened immune response; B1 more infections
