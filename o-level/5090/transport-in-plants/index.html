Q1Transpiration & factors · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) In which set of conditions will a sunflower plant lose water vapour fastest? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C warm, dry, windy
    High temperature increases evaporation, low humidity gives a steep water vapour concentration gradient and wind removes water vapour from around the leaf. B is humid, which reduces the gradient.

  2. (b) Stomata
    B1
  3. (c) Pulls water and mineral ions up the xylem / cools the leaves / supplies water for photosynthesis and support.

Q7Transpiration & factors · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the rate of transpiration increases when the temperature rises. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why the rate of transpiration decreases on a humid day in Karachi. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water molecules gain kinetic energy, so more water evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces and water vapour diffuses out faster.
    B1 more evaporation / molecules move faster; B1 faster diffusion of water vapour out of stomata
  2. (b) The air outside the leaf already contains much water vapour, so the concentration gradient between the air spaces and the outside air is smaller and diffusion is slower.
    B1 high water vapour concentration outside; B1 smaller concentration gradient so slower diffusion
Q8Transpiration & factors · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A potted chilli plant was watered and its pot and soil surface were sealed in a plastic bag. It was placed on a balance in a sunny courtyard. At 9 am its mass was 412.0 g. At 3 pm its mass was 397.6 g.

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of water lost in 6 hours. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage of the starting mass that was lost. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the mean rate of water loss in g per hour. [1]
  4. (d) Explain why the pot and soil were covered with a plastic bag. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 14.4 g
    B1 412.0 − 397.6
  2. (b) 3.50 %

  3. (c) 2.4 g/h

  4. (d) So that mass lost is due to transpiration from the plant only, not evaporation from the soil.

Q10Transpiration & factors · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which change would increase the rate of transpiration from a leafy shoot? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C placing a fan close to the shoot

Q13Transpiration & factors · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

On a very hot afternoon in Multan in June, a farmer notices that his cotton plants have drooping leaves, even though the soil is slightly damp. By the next morning the plants look normal again.

  1. (a) Explain why the plants wilted. [3]
  2. (b) When wilting occurs, the stomata close. Give one advantage and one disadvantage to the plant of closing its stomata. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water was lost by transpiration faster than it was absorbed by the roots; cells lost water and became flaccid (lost turgor); so the leaves and stems were no longer supported.
    B1 transpiration rate greater than water uptake; B1 cells lose turgor / become flaccid; B1 loss of support
  2. (b) Advantage: reduces water loss. Disadvantage: less carbon dioxide enters, so less photosynthesis (also less cooling / less mineral transport).
    B1 advantage; B1 disadvantage

Q18Water uptake & root hairs · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature of a root hair cell increases its surface area for absorption? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B a long, thin, hair-like extension
    Root hairs are long, thin outgrowths of epidermal cells, which increase the surface area for absorbing water and mineral ions. Root cells are underground and have no chloroplasts, and a thick wall does not increase surface area.

Q20Water uptake & root hairs · Core · Paper 2 style · 2 marks

Q24Water uptake & root hairs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A root hair cell has a solute concentration of 0.30 mol/dm³ and the soil water has a solute concentration of 0.10 mol/dm³. Explain which way water moves and why. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water moves from the soil into the root hair cell by osmosis. The cell has a higher solute concentration, so a lower water potential than the soil water, and water moves down this water potential gradient across the cell membrane.
    1 mark: water moves into the root hair cell; 1 mark: cell has lower water potential (higher solute concentration); 1 mark: water moves by osmosis down the water potential gradient.
Q25Water uptake & root hairs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Show answer
  1. (a) Long, thin extension: increases the surface area in contact with soil water, so more water enters per unit time. Large vacuole with concentrated cell sap: lowers the water potential inside the cell, so water enters by osmosis more readily. (Any two adaptations, each with an explanation.)

Q26Water uptake & root hairs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A farmer in Faisalabad added far too much fertiliser to a field of young wheat plants. A few days later the plants wilted, although the soil was moist.

  1. (a) Use the term water potential to explain why the plants wilted. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest what the farmer should do to help the plants recover. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The fertiliser made the soil solution very concentrated, with a lower water potential than the root cells; water moved out of the root cells into the soil by osmosis; so less water reached the leaves and cells lost turgor.
    B1 soil water potential lower than root cells; B1 water leaves root cells by osmosis / cannot enter; B1 cells / plant lose turgor
  2. (b) Water the field heavily to dilute the fertiliser.
    B1
Q27Water uptake & root hairs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe how water moves from the root hair cell to the xylem. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how water moves up the xylem to the leaves. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) By osmosis from cell to cell across the root cortex, down a water potential gradient (each cell has a lower water potential than the one before).
    B1 through cortex cells; B1 osmosis / water potential gradient
  2. (b) Water evaporates from mesophyll cells and is lost by transpiration; this creates a pull (tension) on the water in the xylem; water molecules stick together (cohesion) so a continuous column is drawn up.
    B1 transpiration / evaporation at leaves; B1 creates pull / suction; B1 water molecules stick together / continuous column
Q28Water uptake & root hairs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A chemical that stops aerobic respiration is added to the root hair cells of a plant. Explain why the uptake of water by osmosis continues, but the uptake of mineral ions falls. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Water enters by osmosis down a water potential gradient, which needs no energy. Mineral ions are taken up by active transport, which needs energy (ATP) from aerobic respiration; with respiration stopped there is less energy, so fewer mineral ions are taken up.
    1 mark: osmosis is passive and needs no energy; 1 mark: active transport of mineral ions needs energy from aerobic respiration; 1 mark: with respiration stopped, less energy is released so fewer ions are taken up.
Q29Water uptake & root hairs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Two plants have different numbers of root hairs. Plant A has 120 root hairs and absorbs 36 mm³ of water per day. Plant B has 90 root hairs and absorbs 24 mm³ of water per day.

  1. (a) Calculate the average volume of water absorbed per day by one root hair on plant A and on plant B. Give each answer to 2 decimal places, in mm³. [2]
  2. (b) Suggest one reason why the value for plant B differs from the value for plant A. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Plant A: 0.30 mm³; plant B: 0.27 mm³ (per root hair per day)
    A: 36 ÷ 120 = 0.30; B: 24 ÷ 90 = 0.267, so 0.27. 1 mark for method, 1 mark for both values.
  2. (b) Root hairs are not the only factor: root hair length, the soil water, or the rate of transpiration of each plant may differ.
    Any one sensible factor that affects water uptake other than root hair density.
Q30Water uptake & root hairs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the rate of water uptake by a plant is higher on a hot, dry, sunny day than on a cool, humid, still day. Use the ideas of water potential and transpiration. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Transpiration increases as the leaves lose water vapour faster, lowering the water potential of the leaf cells; this draws water up the xylem from the roots; the root cells then have a lower water potential than the soil, so water enters the root hairs faster by osmosis.
    Any four: more transpiration on a hot dry day; leaf water potential falls; water pulled up xylem; root cell water potential falls below soil; more osmosis into root hairs.

Q32Xylem & phloem structure · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark

Q37Xylem & phloem structure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

  2. (b) No cross walls / hollow (no cytoplasm), so water flows freely as a continuous column; lignin in walls makes them strong, giving support and stopping collapse under tension; narrow tubes help the water column hold together.

Q38Xylem & phloem structure · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which feature of xylem vessels allows water to move sideways between neighbouring vessels? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) B pits in the lignified walls
    Pits are gaps in the lignified walls where lignin is absent, so water can pass between vessels. Lignin strengthens the walls, sieve plates are in phloem, and companion cells are also phloem cells.
Q39Xylem & phloem structure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Describe the structure of a sieve tube element, and state how it is linked to a companion cell. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Sieve tube elements are living cells joined end to end to form a tube; they have no nucleus; their end walls have sieve plates with pores; the cytoplasm is continuous. Each element is linked to a companion cell that contains a nucleus and provides the energy.
    1 mark: living cells joined end to end; 1 mark: no nucleus, sieve plates with pores; 1 mark: linked to a companion cell which has a nucleus and gives energy.

Q41Xylem & phloem structure · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Q44Xylem & phloem structure · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Explain why xylem vessels are dead, whereas sieve tube elements must remain living to do their job. [3]

Q52Translocation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) In spring a stored potato tuber starts to sprout new shoots. Explain why the tuber acts as a source at this time. [2]
  2. (b) Later in summer the potato plant forms new tubers. Explain why the new tubers are sinks. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why translocation can occur in both directions in a plant, unlike water transport. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Stored starch is broken down to sucrose; the sucrose is transported in phloem to the growing shoots (sinks), which cannot yet photosynthesise enough.
    B1 starch → sucrose released; B1 transported to growing shoots / sinks
  2. (b) Sucrose from the leaves is transported to them and converted to starch for storage.
    B1 receive sucrose from leaves; B1 stored as starch
  3. (c) Sources and sinks can be above or below each other (and change with season), so phloem carries substances up and down.

Q53Translocation · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Sucrose is loaded into the sieve tubes at a source leaf by which process? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C active transport
    Sucrose is moved into the sieve tubes against a concentration gradient by active transport, using energy from respiration in companion cells. Osmosis and diffusion only move water or substances down a gradient, and evaporation loses water from leaves.
Q54Translocation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Compare the direction of movement of substances in xylem and in phloem. State where the substances move from and to in each tissue. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Xylem moves water and minerals in one direction only, upwards from the roots to the leaves. Phloem moves sugars and other substances in both directions (up and down), from a source (where sugar is made or stored) to a sink (where it is used or stored).
    1 mark: xylem one direction, upwards; 1 mark: phloem moves in both directions; 1 mark: source to sink described correctly.
Q55Translocation · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

  2. (b) Explain why sucrose moves from the source to the sink. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4 times
    0.80 ÷ 0.20 = 4.
  2. (b) The sucrose concentration is higher at the source than at the sink, so sucrose moves down this concentration gradient; the sink uses or stores sucrose, which keeps the gradient steep.
    1 mark: movement down a concentration gradient; 1 mark: sink uses or removes sucrose, maintaining the gradient.

  3. (c) Predict what would happen to the roots after a long time, and explain. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The ring removed the phloem (which is in the bark); sucrose moving down from the leaves could not pass the ring; sucrose built up above the ring, and the extra sugar caused water to enter / extra growth, so the bark swelled.

Q67Transpiration & factors (option) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) A student covers a shoot with a plastic bag and records a lower water loss. Explain why the bag reduces the water loss. [3]

    1 mark: traps water vapour; 1 mark: gradient between leaf and air is reduced; 1 mark: less diffusion out of stomata.
Q68Transpiration & factors (option) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

  1. (a) The rate of transpiration falls. With fewer leaves there are fewer stomata and less leaf surface area for water vapour to diffuse out, so less water is lost per unit time.
    1 mark: rate decreases; 1 mark: fewer stomata or less leaf surface area; 1 mark: less water vapour diffuses out per unit time.

  2. (b) 83.3 %

Q71Transpiration & factors (option) · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

Measurements on a rice plant in a field in Punjab on a clear summer day showed that water loss rose from 8 am to a peak at 2 pm and then fell. Water uptake by the roots followed a similar pattern but reached its peak about an hour later.

  1. (a) Explain why water loss from the plant is greater at 2 pm than at 8 am. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the rate of water uptake by roots lags behind the rate of transpiration in the early afternoon. [2]
  3. (c) State two ways in which a hot, dry climate could be a problem for a crop such as rice, which needs a lot of water. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Higher light intensity, so more stomata open wider; higher temperature, so faster evaporation and diffusion; lower humidity at midday, so steeper concentration gradient.

  2. (b) Water is first lost from the leaves, creating the pull; it takes time for the pull to be transmitted down the xylem and for water to cross the root, so uptake reaches its peak later.
    B1 loss from leaves comes first / drives uptake; B1 time for pull to travel / delay in uptake
  3. (c) Plants may wilt / lose turgor; stomata close so less photosynthesis and lower yield; more irrigation water needed.
    1 mark each for any two
