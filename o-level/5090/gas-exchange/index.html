Original practice questions written by HomiLabs in the exam style. They are not Cambridge past paper questions. Try each one before opening the answer.

Q1Breathing mechanism · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which events happen when a person breathes in at rest?A diaphragm contracts and flattens; external intercostal muscles contractB diaphragm relaxes and domes upwards; external intercostal muscles contractC diaphragm contracts and flattens; internal intercostal muscles contractD diaphragm relaxes and domes upwards; internal intercostal muscles relax [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A diaphragm contracts and flattens; external intercostal muscles contract
    In inspiration the diaphragm contracts and flattens and the external intercostals contract to pull the ribs up and out. B is wrong because a relaxed, domed diaphragm happens in expiration.
Q2Breathing mechanism · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the movements of the diaphragm and the ribs when a person breathes in. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how these movements cause air to enter the lungs. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The diaphragm contracts and flattens (moves down); the ribs move upwards and outwards.
    B1 diaphragm contracts/flattens; B1 ribs up and out
  2. (b) The volume of the thorax increases; so the pressure inside falls below atmospheric pressure; air moves in through the trachea down the pressure gradient.
    B1 volume increases; B1 pressure decreases (below atmospheric); B1 air flows in from higher to lower pressure
Q3Breathing mechanism · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) List, in order, the structures that air passes through from the larynx to the alveoli. [2]
  2. (b) State the function of the rings of cartilage in the trachea. [1]
  3. (c) State why the cartilage rings are C-shaped and not complete circles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Trachea → bronchus → bronchioles → alveoli
    2 marks all in correct order; 1 mark if one error
  2. (b) Keep the trachea open (stop it collapsing) when air pressure inside falls.
    B1
  3. (c) So the oesophagus behind the trachea can expand when food is swallowed (and the trachea stays flexible).
    B1
Q4Breathing mechanism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how air is breathed out when a person is resting. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how breathing out is different when a trumpet player blows hard into the instrument. [3]
  3. (c) State the relationship between the actions of the internal and external intercostal muscles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) External intercostal muscles and diaphragm relax; ribs move down and in and the diaphragm domes upwards (elastic recoil of lungs), so volume decreases and pressure rises, pushing air out.
    B1 muscles relax/ribs down and in/diaphragm domes up; B1 volume decreases, pressure increases, air out
  2. (b) The internal intercostal muscles contract; pulling the ribs down and in more forcefully; abdominal muscles contract pushing the diaphragm further up; so volume falls more and pressure rises more, forcing air out faster.
    B1 internal intercostals contract; B1 ribs pulled down and in; B1 abdominal muscles contract/push diaphragm up; max 3
  3. (c) They are antagonistic (when one contracts the other relaxes).
    B1 antagonistic
Q5Breathing mechanism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A model of the chest is made from a glass bell jar. A Y-shaped glass tube passes through a bung in the top and two balloons are attached to the branches inside the jar. The open bottom of the jar is covered by a stretched rubber sheet.

  1. (a) State which part of the human body is represented by: the balloons; the rubber sheet; the bell jar. [3]
  2. (b) Describe and explain what happens to the balloons when the rubber sheet is pulled down. [3]
  3. (c) State one way in which the model does not show breathing accurately. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Balloons – lungs; rubber sheet – diaphragm; bell jar – rib cage / thorax (chest wall)
    1 mark each
  2. (b) The balloons inflate; because the volume inside the jar increases; so the pressure falls and air enters the balloons through the tube from the atmosphere.
    B1 balloons inflate; B1 volume in jar increases/pressure falls; B1 air enters from outside
  3. (c) The bell jar is rigid so it cannot show rib movement (or the rubber sheet is pulled down, not domed and contracting; no intercostal muscles; balloons are not like alveoli).
    B1 any valid limitation
Q6Breathing mechanism · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

Five students counted their breaths for one minute at rest and again immediately after 2 minutes of skipping.At rest: 14, 16, 12, 15, 13After skipping: 27, 31, 24, 30, 26

  1. (a) Calculate the mean breathing rate at rest and after exercise. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage increase in mean breathing rate after exercise. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why the student counted breaths for one full minute rather than for 10 seconds. [1]
  4. (d) State two variables that should be kept the same for each student. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) At rest: 14.0 breaths per minute; after exercise: 27.6 breaths per minute
    M1 total ÷ 5; A1 both
  2. (b) 97.1%
    M1 (27.6 − 14.0) ÷ 14.0 × 100; A1 97.1%
  3. (c) A longer count reduces the error of starting or stopping in the middle of a breath / more accurate.
    B1
  4. (d) Any two of: type of exercise; duration of exercise (2 minutes); intensity/speed; time between stopping and counting; resting before the first count; same person counting.
    1 mark each
Q7Breathing mechanism · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which statement explains why air moves into the lungs when we breathe in?A The volume of the thorax increases so the pressure inside falls below atmospheric pressure.B The lungs expand by themselves and pull the ribs outwards.C The pressure inside the thorax rises above atmospheric pressure.D The diaphragm relaxes and domes upwards, sucking air in. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A The volume of the thorax increases so the pressure inside falls below atmospheric pressure.
    Muscle contraction increases thorax volume, lowering pressure, so air flows in from the higher atmospheric pressure. D describes breathing out, not in.
Q8Breathing mechanism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the rate and depth of breathing increase during a football match. [5]
  2. (b) Suggest why breathing rate stays high for some minutes after the match ends. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Muscles contract more so they respire faster; producing more carbon dioxide which enters the blood; the increased carbon dioxide concentration in the blood is detected by the brain; the brain sends more nerve impulses to the intercostal muscles and diaphragm; so more oxygen is supplied for aerobic respiration and carbon dioxide is removed faster.
    1 mark each for any five: more respiration in muscles; more CO₂ in blood; detected by brain; impulses to diaphragm/intercostals; more O₂ for aerobic respiration; CO₂ removed faster; (any anaerobic respiration/oxygen debt reference)
  2. (b) Lactic acid made by anaerobic respiration must be broken down/removed; this needs extra oxygen (oxygen debt), so fast breathing continues.
    B1 lactic acid/oxygen debt; B1 extra oxygen needed to break down lactic acid in liver
Q9Breathing mechanism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A trace of a person's lung volume was recorded.At rest: highest point of each breath 3.1 dm³, lowest point 2.6 dm³, 15 breaths per minute.During exercise: highest point 4.0 dm³, lowest point 2.4 dm³, 24 breaths per minute.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of air breathed in with each breath (the tidal volume) at rest and during exercise. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the total volume of air breathed in per minute at rest and during exercise. [2]
  3. (c) Explain the advantage of the change in breathing during exercise. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) At rest: 0.5 dm³; during exercise: 1.6 dm³
    M1 peak − trough; A1 both
  2. (b) At rest: 7.5 dm³ per minute; during exercise: 38.4 dm³ per minute
    M1 tidal volume × breaths per minute; A1 both
  3. (c) More air reaches the alveoli per minute, so more oxygen diffuses into the blood for aerobic respiration in muscles; and more carbon dioxide is removed.
    B1 more oxygen into blood for respiration; B1 more CO₂ removed
Q10Breathing mechanism · Challenge · Paper 4 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Plan an investigation to find out how the intensity of exercise affects the breathing rate of students. Include the method, the variables to control, how you would make the results reliable and any safety precautions. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Use stepping on/off a step at different rates (e.g. 10, 20, 30 steps per minute) set with a metronome for a fixed time (e.g. 3 minutes); measure resting breathing rate first and let it return to resting between trials; count breaths for 1 minute immediately after each exercise; keep same step height, same duration, same students (similar age/fitness), same time after exercise before counting; repeat for several students/three times and calculate means; safety: students with asthma or heart conditions do not take part / stop if dizzy.
    1 mark each for any six of: method of varying intensity; at least 3 intensities; fixed duration; resting rate measured / return to resting; breaths counted for 1 minute straight after; two controlled variables (B1 each, max 2); repeats/means; one safety point
Q11Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which is NOT a feature of an efficient gas exchange surface?A a large surface areaB a thin surfaceC a good blood supplyD a thick layer of mucus over the surface [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) D a thick layer of mucus over the surface
    A thick layer of mucus would increase the diffusion distance. The alveolar surface is moist, but only with a thin film of fluid; A, B and C are all features that speed up diffusion.
Q12Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) State four features of the gas exchange surface in human lungs. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Large surface area (many alveoli); thin walls (one cell thick); good blood supply (many capillaries); good ventilation with air; moist surface.
    1 mark each for any four
Q13Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State the function of the capillaries surrounding each alveolus. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the inside surface of each alveolus is moist. [1]
  3. (c) Name the blood vessel that brings deoxygenated blood to the lungs. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) To take up oxygen from and give carbon dioxide to the alveolus / carry blood close to the alveolus for gas exchange.
    B1
  2. (b) Gases dissolve in the moisture before diffusing across the wall.
    B1
  3. (c) Pulmonary artery
    B1
Q14Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how oxygen moves from the air in an alveolus into a red blood cell. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how a steep concentration gradient of oxygen is maintained between the alveoli and the blood. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Oxygen dissolves in the moisture lining the alveolus; diffuses through the thin alveolar wall and capillary wall; into the plasma and red blood cell where it combines with haemoglobin.
    B1 dissolves in moisture; B1 diffusion across alveolus and capillary walls; B1 into red blood cell/joins haemoglobin
  2. (b) Breathing (ventilation) keeps bringing fresh air with a high oxygen concentration into the alveoli; blood flow carries oxygenated blood away; and brings deoxygenated blood with low oxygen concentration to the alveoli.
    B1 ventilation brings fresh air; B1 blood removes oxygen; B1 deoxygenated blood continually arrives
Q15Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In a photomicrograph of lung tissue magnified 120 times, one alveolus measures 30 mm across.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual diameter of the alveolus in µm. [2]
  2. (b) The wall of the alveolus is made of a single layer of flattened cells. Explain the advantage of this. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why having millions of small alveoli is better than having a few large air sacs. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 250 µm
    M1 actual = image ÷ magnification = 30 ÷ 120 = 0.25 mm; A1 × 1000 = 250 µm
  2. (b) Short diffusion distance; so oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse quickly between the air and the blood.
    B1 short distance; B1 faster diffusion
  3. (c) Many small alveoli give a much larger total surface area (for the same volume of lung); so more gas exchange can take place.
    B1 larger total surface area; B1 more diffusion/gas exchange
Q16Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which row describes the movement of gases by diffusion in the alveoli?A oxygen: alveolus air → blood; carbon dioxide: blood → alveolus airB oxygen: blood → alveolus air; carbon dioxide: alveolus air → bloodC oxygen and carbon dioxide: both alveolus air → bloodD oxygen and carbon dioxide: both blood → alveolus air [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A oxygen: alveolus air → blood; carbon dioxide: blood → alveolus air
    Oxygen is at a higher concentration in alveolar air and diffuses into the blood; carbon dioxide is at a higher concentration in the blood and diffuses out. B reverses both directions.
Q17Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A student models organisms of different sizes as cubes with sides of 1 cm, 2 cm and 4 cm.

  1. (a) Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. [3]
  2. (b) Use your answers to explain why a large animal such as a buffalo needs lungs, while a single-celled organism does not. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1 cm cube 6 : 1; 2 cm cube 3 : 1; 4 cm cube 1.5 : 1
    M1 surface area = 6 × side²; M1 volume = side³; A1 all three ratios
  2. (b) As size increases, the surface area to volume ratio decreases; the body surface of a large animal is too small for its volume to absorb enough oxygen by diffusion and the distances to inner cells are too great; lungs provide a very large internal surface area.
    B1 larger size → smaller SA:V; B1 surface not enough to supply volume / diffusion distance too long; B1 lungs give large surface area
Q18Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A climber travels from Karachi, at sea level, to a base camp at 5000 m in the Karakoram mountains.

  1. (a) Explain why there is less oxygen available to the body in a visitor to a high mountain base camp than at sea level. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest how the visitor's breathing changes when they first arrive, and why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Air at high altitude has a lower pressure, so each breath contains less oxygen; the concentration gradient between the alveoli and the blood is smaller; so less oxygen diffuses into the blood.
    B1 less oxygen per breath / lower air pressure; B1 smaller concentration gradient; B1 slower diffusion into blood
  2. (b) Breathing becomes faster and deeper; to bring more air (oxygen) into the alveoli per minute.
    B1 faster/deeper; B1 more oxygen into alveoli/maintain gradient
Q19Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Blood arriving at the lungs at rest contains 10.0 cm³ of oxygen per 100 cm³ of blood. Blood leaving the lungs contains 19.5 cm³ of oxygen per 100 cm³ of blood.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of oxygen taken up by each 100 cm³ of blood as it passes through the lungs. [1]
  2. (b) At rest, 5 dm³ (5000 cm³) of blood passes through the lungs each minute. Calculate the volume of oxygen taken up by the blood per minute. [2]
  3. (c) During exercise the blood flow increases to 20 dm³ per minute and blood arrives at the lungs with only 5.0 cm³ of oxygen per 100 cm³. Assuming it still leaves with 19.5 cm³ per 100 cm³, calculate the oxygen taken up per minute. [2]
  4. (d) Explain why blood arriving at the lungs during exercise contains less oxygen. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9.5 cm³
    B1 19.5 − 10.0
  2. (b) 475 cm³ per minute
    M1 9.5 × (5000 ÷ 100); A1 475
  3. (c) 2900 cm³ per minute
    M1 (19.5 − 5.0) × (20 000 ÷ 100); A1 2900
  4. (d) Muscles use more oxygen in respiration, so more oxygen is removed from the blood in the tissues.
    B1

  1. (a) State three differences between inspired air and expired air. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why expired air contains more carbon dioxide than inspired air. [2]

Q22Inspired vs expired air · Standard · Paper 4 style · 5 marks

A student set up two tubes, A and B, each containing 10 cm³ of limewater. They were connected so that when the student breathed in through a mouthpiece, air from the room bubbled through tube A, and when the student breathed out, expired air bubbled through tube B. Tube B turned milky after 15 seconds. Tube A was still clear after 3 minutes.

  1. (a) Explain the results in tube B. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why tube A stayed clear for much longer. [1]
  3. (c) State one thing the student should do to make the comparison fair. [1]
  4. (d) Hydrogencarbonate indicator could be used instead of limewater. State the colour it would turn in tube B. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Expired air contains a high concentration of carbon dioxide (about 4%); carbon dioxide turns limewater milky (cloudy).
    B1 more CO₂ in expired air; B1 CO₂ turns limewater milky
  2. (b) Inspired air contains very little carbon dioxide (about 0.04%).
    B1
  3. (c) Use the same volume (and concentration) of limewater in each tube / breathe at the same rate and depth.
    B1
  4. (d) Yellow
    B1
Q23Inspired vs expired air · Standard · Paper 4 style · 7 marks

A student breathed out through 5 cm³ of limewater and timed how long it took to turn milky. This was done three times at rest and three times immediately after running for 3 minutes.At rest / s: 45, 41, 43After running / s: 19, 23, 18

  1. (a) Calculate the mean time for the limewater to turn milky at rest and after exercise. [2]
  2. (b) Explain the difference between the two mean times. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why it is difficult to judge exactly when the limewater turns milky, and one way to improve this. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) At rest: 43.0 s; after exercise: 20.0 s
    M1 total ÷ 3; A1 both
  2. (b) During exercise muscles respire faster; more carbon dioxide is produced and carried to the lungs; so expired air contains more carbon dioxide (and more is breathed out per second) and the limewater goes milky sooner.
    B1 faster respiration; B1 more CO₂ in blood/expired air; B1 limewater turns milky faster
  3. (c) The change is gradual so the end point is subjective; compare against a standard tube of milky limewater / use a black card behind the tube / use a cross under the tube.
    B1 gradual/subjective end point; B1 valid improvement
Q24Inspired vs expired air · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

At rest, a person breathes 8 dm³ of air per minute. Inspired air contains 21% oxygen and expired air contains 16% oxygen.

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of oxygen absorbed by the body each minute and in one hour. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest why expired air still contains so much oxygen. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.4 dm³ per minute; 24 dm³ per hour
    M1 21% − 16% = 5%; M1 0.05 × 8 dm³ = 0.4 dm³; A1 24 dm³ per hour
  2. (b) Some inspired air stays in the trachea and bronchi and never reaches the alveoli; air is in the alveoli only for a short time, so not all the oxygen diffuses into the blood.
    B1 air in airways not involved in gas exchange (dead space); B1 short time in alveoli / mixing with air already in lungs
Q25Inspired vs expired air · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Inspired air contains 0.04% carbon dioxide.At rest: expired air 4% carbon dioxide, 16% oxygen; volume breathed 7.5 dm³ per minuteDuring exercise: expired air 5% carbon dioxide, 15% oxygen; volume breathed 60 dm³ per minute

  1. (a) Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide added to the air breathed out per minute at rest and during exercise. Give your answers to 3 significant figures. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the expired air contains a lower percentage of oxygen during exercise. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) At rest: 0.297 dm³ per minute; during exercise: 2.98 dm³ per minute
    M1 percentage difference (4 − 0.04 = 3.96%; 5 − 0.04 = 4.96%); M1 × volume per minute; A1 both
  2. (b) Muscle cells respire faster and use more oxygen; blood returning to the lungs has less oxygen, so more oxygen diffuses into the blood from the alveoli.
    B1 more oxygen used in respiration; B1 steeper gradient/more oxygen removed from alveolar air
Q26Smoking & lungs · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 marks
  1. (a) Which component of tobacco smoke reduces the amount of oxygen the blood can carry?A tarB nicotineC carbon monoxideD smoke particles [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) C carbon monoxide
    Carbon monoxide combines with haemoglobin in red blood cells, so less oxygen is carried. Nicotine (B) is the addictive drug but does not block haemoglobin.

  1. (a) Name the component of tobacco smoke that: (i) is addictive; (ii) causes cancer. [2]
  2. (b) Name two diseases of the gas exchange system caused by smoking. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (i) nicotine; (ii) tar
    1 mark each
  2. (b) Any two of: lung cancer; chronic bronchitis; emphysema; chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD).
    1 mark each
Q28Smoking & lungs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe the roles of goblet cells and ciliated cells in the trachea. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why many smokers have a persistent cough and frequent chest infections. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Goblet cells secrete mucus; mucus traps dust and pathogens (bacteria); cilia beat/sweep the mucus up to the throat where it is swallowed.
    B1 goblet cells make mucus; B1 mucus traps particles/pathogens; B1 cilia move mucus up/away from lungs
  2. (b) Chemicals in smoke damage/paralyse the cilia; mucus is not removed and builds up in the airways; coughing is the only way to remove it; bacteria trapped in the mucus multiply, causing infections (chronic bronchitis).
    1 mark each for any three: cilia damaged/stop beating; mucus accumulates (more mucus made); cough to clear mucus; bacteria breed in mucus/infection
Q29Smoking & lungs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe what happens to the alveoli in emphysema. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why a person with emphysema becomes breathless even when walking slowly. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The walls of the alveoli break down; neighbouring alveoli merge into fewer, larger air spaces (and the lungs lose elasticity).
    B1 alveolar walls break down; B1 fewer larger spaces / loss of elasticity
  2. (b) The surface area for gas exchange is reduced; so less oxygen diffuses into the blood; muscles do not get enough oxygen for aerobic respiration, so the person breathes faster to compensate.
    B1 smaller surface area; B1 less oxygen absorbed; B1 not enough O₂ for respiration / breathe faster
Q30Smoking & lungs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A 20-year study of adults recorded deaths from lung cancer per 100 000 people per year.Non-smokers: 101–14 cigarettes per day: 7815–24 cigarettes per day: 12725 or more cigarettes per day: 251

  1. (a) Calculate how many times greater the death rate is for people who smoke 25 or more cigarettes a day than for non-smokers. [1]
  2. (b) Describe the relationship shown by the data. [2]
  3. (c) Explain how tar in cigarette smoke can lead to lung cancer. [2]
  4. (d) Suggest why some non-smokers still die of lung cancer. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 25.1 times
    B1 251 ÷ 10 = 25.1
  2. (b) The more cigarettes smoked per day, the higher the death rate from lung cancer; e.g. 10 per 100 000 for non-smokers rising to 251 per 100 000 for 25 or more a day.
    B1 positive correlation; B1 data quoted with units
  3. (c) Tar contains carcinogens; they cause mutations in cells lining the airways, leading to uncontrolled cell division and a tumour.
    B1 carcinogen/causes mutation; B1 uncontrolled cell division/tumour
  4. (d) Other causes such as breathing other people's smoke (passive smoking), air pollution, radon or inherited factors.
    B1 any valid cause
