Q1Breathing mechanism · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which events happen when a person breathes in at rest? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A diaphragm contracts and flattens; external intercostal muscles contract
    In inspiration the diaphragm contracts and flattens and the external intercostals contract to pull the ribs up and out. B is wrong because a relaxed, domed diaphragm happens in expiration.
Q2Breathing mechanism · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Describe the movements of the diaphragm and the ribs when a person breathes in. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how these movements cause air to enter the lungs. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The diaphragm contracts and flattens (moves down); the ribs move upwards and outwards.
    B1 diaphragm contracts/flattens; B1 ribs up and out
  2. (b) The volume of the thorax increases; so the pressure inside falls below atmospheric pressure; air moves in through the trachea down the pressure gradient.
    B1 volume increases; B1 pressure decreases (below atmospheric); B1 air flows in from higher to lower pressure
Q3Breathing mechanism · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) List, in order, the structures that air passes through from the larynx to the alveoli. [2]
  2. (b) State the function of the rings of cartilage in the trachea. [1]
  3. (c) State why the cartilage rings are C-shaped and not complete circles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Trachea → bronchus → bronchioles → alveoli
    2 marks all in correct order; 1 mark if one error
  2. (b) Keep the trachea open (stop it collapsing) when air pressure inside falls.
    B1
  3. (c) So the oesophagus behind the trachea can expand when food is swallowed (and the trachea stays flexible).
    B1
Q4Breathing mechanism · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Which structure separates the thorax (chest cavity) from the abdomen? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A the diaphragm
    The diaphragm is a dome-shaped sheet of muscle and tendon that forms the floor of the thorax. The intercostal muscles (C) lie between the ribs, not between thorax and abdomen.

Q6Breathing mechanism · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how air is breathed out when a person is resting. [2]
  2. (b) Describe how breathing out is different when a trumpet player blows hard into the instrument. [3]
  3. (c) State the relationship between the actions of the internal and external intercostal muscles. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) External intercostal muscles and diaphragm relax; ribs move down and in and the diaphragm domes upwards (elastic recoil of lungs), so volume decreases and pressure rises, pushing air out.
    B1 muscles relax/ribs down and in/diaphragm domes up; B1 volume decreases, pressure increases, air out
  2. (b) The internal intercostal muscles contract; pulling the ribs down and in more forcefully; abdominal muscles contract pushing the diaphragm further up; so volume falls more and pressure rises more, forcing air out faster.
    B1 internal intercostals contract; B1 ribs pulled down and in; B1 abdominal muscles contract/push diaphragm up; max 3
  3. (c) They are antagonistic (when one contracts the other relaxes).
    B1 antagonistic

Q10Breathing mechanism · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) During exercise a person breathes faster and more deeply. Which change in the blood is detected by the brain and causes this? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A a rise in the concentration of carbon dioxide
    Faster aerobic respiration in the muscles releases more carbon dioxide into the blood; the brain detects the higher carbon dioxide concentration and increases the rate and depth of breathing. Oxygen concentration does not rise during exercise (C).

A teacher makes a model of part of the rib cage. A vertical wooden stick represents the backbone and a second vertical stick represents the sternum. Two horizontal strips, joined to both sticks by pins that can turn, represent two ribs.   1. (a) State which strip of elastic (the outer band or the inner band) must shorten to raise the "ribs". Give the muscle it represents. [2]

  1. (a) The outer band (sloping down from backbone towards the front); it represents the external intercostal muscles.
    1 mark: outer band; 1 mark: external intercostal muscles.
  2. (b) The ribs move up and out, so the volume of the thorax increases; the pressure inside the thorax/lungs falls below atmospheric pressure; air flows in through the trachea.
    1 mark each: volume of thorax increases; pressure falls below atmospheric (outside) pressure; air moves in down the pressure gradient.

Q12Breathing mechanism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Explain why the rate and depth of breathing increase during a football match. [5]
  2. (b) Suggest why breathing rate stays high for some minutes after the match ends. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Muscles contract more so they respire faster; producing more carbon dioxide which enters the blood; the increased carbon dioxide concentration in the blood is detected by the brain; the brain sends more nerve impulses to the intercostal muscles and diaphragm; so more oxygen is supplied for aerobic respiration and carbon dioxide is removed faster.
    1 mark each for any five: more respiration in muscles; more CO₂ in blood; detected by brain; impulses to diaphragm/intercostals; more O₂ for aerobic respiration; CO₂ removed faster; (any anaerobic respiration/oxygen debt reference)
  2. (b) Lactic acid made by anaerobic respiration must be broken down/removed; this needs extra oxygen (oxygen debt), so fast breathing continues.
    B1 lactic acid/oxygen debt; B1 extra oxygen needed to break down lactic acid in liver

Q15Breathing mechanism · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Each lung is surrounded by two pleural membranes with a thin layer of pleural fluid between them. The space between the membranes is sealed and airtight. A man injured in a road accident has a wound through the right side of his chest wall that lets air into the space between the pleural membranes on that side.

  1. (a) State the function of the pleural fluid. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the right lung does not fill with air when the man tries to breathe in. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the left lung still works normally. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) It lubricates the pleural membranes so the lungs can move smoothly against the chest wall (reduces friction) during breathing.
    1 mark: reduces friction / lubrication between lung and chest wall.
  2. (b) Air enters the pleural cavity through the wound, so the pressure there is the same as atmospheric pressure; when the thorax volume increases the pressure around the lung does not fall below atmospheric; the lung is not pulled outwards and its elastic tissue makes it collapse, so no air is drawn in.
    1 mark each for three of: air enters the pleural cavity; pressure around the lung equals atmospheric / does not fall; lung not pulled out with chest wall; elastic lung recoils/collapses; no pressure difference to draw air in.
  3. (c) The left lung is in its own separate pleural cavity, which is still sealed / airtight.
    1 mark: separate sealed pleural cavity on the left.

Q19Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark

Q21Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe how oxygen moves from the air in an alveolus into a red blood cell. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how a steep concentration gradient of oxygen is maintained between the alveoli and the blood. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Oxygen dissolves in the moisture lining the alveolus; diffuses through the thin alveolar wall and capillary wall; into the plasma and red blood cell where it combines with haemoglobin.
    B1 dissolves in moisture; B1 diffusion across alveolus and capillary walls; B1 into red blood cell/joins haemoglobin
  2. (b) Breathing (ventilation) keeps bringing fresh air with a high oxygen concentration into the alveoli; blood flow carries oxygenated blood away; and brings deoxygenated blood with low oxygen concentration to the alveoli.
    B1 ventilation brings fresh air; B1 blood removes oxygen; B1 deoxygenated blood continually arrives
Q22Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In a photomicrograph of lung tissue magnified 120 times, one alveolus measures 30 mm across.

  1. (a) Calculate the actual diameter of the alveolus in µm. [2]
  2. (b) The wall of the alveolus is made of a single layer of flattened cells. Explain the advantage of this. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest why having millions of small alveoli is better than having a few large air sacs. [2]

  1. (a) 250 µm
    M1 actual = image ÷ magnification = 30 ÷ 120 = 0.25 mm; A1 × 1000 = 250 µm
  2. (b) Short diffusion distance; so oxygen and carbon dioxide diffuse quickly between the air and the blood.
    B1 short distance; B1 faster diffusion
  3. (c) Many small alveoli give a much larger total surface area (for the same volume of lung); so more gas exchange can take place.
    B1 larger total surface area; B1 more diffusion/gas exchange
Q23Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark

  1. (a) A oxygen: alveolus air → blood; carbon dioxide: blood → alveolus air

Q24Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 1 style · 1 mark

  1. (a) C a layer of fluid collecting inside the alveoli

Q26Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

The capillaries around an alveolus have a diameter of about 7 µm. Red blood cells are about 7.5 µm across, so they are squeezed as they pass through in single file.

  1. (a) Suggest the advantage of red blood cells being pressed against the capillary wall as they pass an alveolus. [2]
  2. (b) A person with anaemia has fewer red blood cells than normal. Explain why this person becomes breathless when climbing stairs. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) The diffusion distance from the alveolus air to the haemoglobin is very short, so oxygen diffuses into the cell quickly; the cells also move slowly in single file, giving more time for loading.
    1 mark: short diffusion distance; 1 mark: faster diffusion / more time for oxygen uptake.
  2. (b) Less haemoglobin, so the blood carries less oxygen; muscles do not get enough oxygen for aerobic respiration during exercise; breathing rate increases (to try to take in more oxygen / remove carbon dioxide), so the person feels breathless.
    1 mark each: less haemoglobin / less oxygen carried; not enough oxygen for respiration in muscles; faster/deeper breathing in response.

Q30Gas exchange surfaces & alveoli · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

In a lung disease called pulmonary fibrosis, scar tissue forms in the walls of the alveoli. In a healthy person the barrier between the air in an alveolus and the blood is about 0.5 µm thick. In one patient with fibrosis it is about 3.0 µm thick.

  1. (a) Calculate how many times thicker the barrier is in the patient with fibrosis. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why the patient has a lower concentration of oxygen in the blood leaving the lungs, especially during exercise. [3]
  3. (c) Suggest why the patient breathes faster than a healthy person even when resting. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6 times
    A1 3.0 ÷ 0.5 = 6.
