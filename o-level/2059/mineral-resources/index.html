Q16Distribution and uses of minerals · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Explain why low-grade lignite from Thar is burnt in power stations built at the coalfield rather than transported to cities. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'Pakistan should make much greater use of its coal resources.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lignite has a high moisture content and low energy per tonne, so moving it long distances would mean paying to transport much water and ash; it can catch fire easily when dried, making transport risky; it is cheaper to send the electricity through transmission lines to the national grid than to move the bulky coal.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: low energy per tonne / high moisture; transport cost; self-ignition risk; electricity easier to transmit; pithead plants.
  2. (b) For: large local reserves such as Thar can reduce imports of oil and LNG, saving foreign exchange; coal power provides reliable power day and night, unlike solar and wind; mining and power stations create jobs in poor areas. Against: burning coal releases carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change that already harms Pakistan through floods and heatwaves; air pollution and ash cause health problems; open-cast mining displaces villages and uses scarce water; renewables such as solar and wind are becoming cheaper and are plentiful in Sindh and Balochistan. Judgement: e.g. Thar coal is useful in the short term for energy security, but renewables should be expanded faster.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.

Q20Extraction methods and impacts · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by open-cast (open-pit) mining? [1]
  2. (b) Explain why coal in Balochistan is mined by underground (shaft or drift) methods, while Thar coal is mined open-cast. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Removing the soil and rock above a mineral deposit near the surface and digging the mineral out from a large open pit.
    B1.
  2. (b) In Balochistan the coal seams are thin and lie at steep angles deep within folded mountain rocks, so it would be too costly to remove all the rock above them; tunnels follow the seams instead. At Thar the lignite lies in thick, almost flat seams under sand at a depth of about 150–200 m, so it is cheaper to remove the overburden and dig out large amounts in a pit.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: Balochistan – thin, steep, folded seams; deep; tunnels follow the seams. Thar – thick flat seams; under loose sand; overburden removal economic; large output.

Show answer
  1. (a) 2 × 0.5 = 1 million tonnes
    B1 1 million tonnes.

Q28Extraction methods and impacts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Explain two environmental problems caused by open-cast coal mining in Thar. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'Open-cast mining in Thar has brought more benefits than problems to local people.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Huge amounts of overburden (sand and rock) must be removed and dumped, destroying grazing land and changing the landscape; groundwater must be pumped out of the pit, and this saline water has been stored in reservoirs, affecting nearby land and wells; dust from blasting and moving coal affects people's health and crops; coal-fired power plants nearby release smoke, ash and carbon dioxide.

Problems: some villages and grazing land have been lost and people have been relocated; saline water from mine dewatering has affected land; dust and pollution threaten health; many skilled jobs went to outsiders; traditional herding livelihoods are harder. Judgement: e.g. benefits are growing, but only if displaced families and herders share fairly in them.

  1. (a) At one block of the Thar coalfield the coal seam is about 12 m thick and lies under about 150 m of overburden. Calculate the ratio of overburden thickness to coal thickness in the form n : 1. [2]
  2. (b) Explain how the ratio you calculated affects the cost of mining. You should develop your answer. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two ways in which the land can be restored after open-cast mining ends. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 150 ÷ 12 = 12.5 : 1
    M1 150/12; A1 12.5 : 1.
  2. (b) The higher the ratio, the more sand and rock must be removed for each tonne of coal, so fuel, machinery and labour costs per tonne rise; a high ratio makes low-value lignite less profitable.
    B1 more material to remove per tonne; B1 higher cost / lower profit.
  3. (c) The pit can be refilled with stored overburden and topsoil, then levelled so it can be used for grazing or farming; trees and grasses can be planted to stabilise the soil and prevent wind erosion; old pits can be turned into water reservoirs or fish ponds; monitoring and drainage reduce salinity in restored land.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: backfilling and replacing topsoil; replanting; reservoirs / ponds; recreation use; salinity control.
Q30Extraction methods and impacts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Explain two ways in which mining can affect water supplies in nearby areas. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'Mining companies should be made to spend part of their profits on the local communities where they work.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pumping water out of mines lowers the water table, so nearby wells may dry up; waste water containing salts, chemicals or metals can flow into streams and soak into groundwater, making it unsafe for drinking and irrigation; processing ore (e.g. copper at Saindak) uses large amounts of scarce water in desert areas; sediment from mine waste clogs channels.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: dewatering lowers water table; contamination by saline/chemical effluent; heavy water use in processing; sediment / siltation.
  2. (b) For: local people bear the costs of dust, water loss, land loss and accidents, so they deserve schools, clinics, clean water and training; this reduces conflict and protests, making mining more secure; projects in Thar and Chagai show how community spending can improve living standards. Against: high charges may discourage investors and reduce national revenue from royalties and taxes; the government, not companies, should be responsible for services; money may be wasted or captured by local elites without proper monitoring. Judgement: e.g. a fixed share for community development, managed transparently with local people, is fair and helps mining succeed.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.
