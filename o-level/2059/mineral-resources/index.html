  2. (b) State the main industrial use of limestone in Pakistan. [1]

    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: bulky / heavy raw material; weight lost in processing; transport cost; other raw materials (clay, gypsum, coal) nearby.

Q24Distribution and uses of minerals · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks
  1. (a) Explain why low-grade lignite from Thar is burnt in power stations built at the coalfield rather than transported to cities. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) 'Pakistan should make much greater use of its coal resources.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Lignite has a high moisture content and low energy per tonne, so moving it long distances would mean paying to transport much water and ash; it can catch fire easily when dried, making transport risky; it is cheaper to send the electricity through transmission lines to the national grid than to move the bulky coal.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: low energy per tonne / high moisture; transport cost; self-ignition risk; electricity easier to transmit; pithead plants.
  2. (b) For: large local reserves such as Thar can reduce imports of oil and LNG, saving foreign exchange; coal power provides reliable power day and night, unlike solar and wind; mining and power stations create jobs in poor areas. Against: burning coal releases carbon dioxide, which adds to climate change that already harms Pakistan through floods and heatwaves; air pollution and ash cause health problems; open-cast mining displaces villages and uses scarce water; renewables such as solar and wind are becoming cheaper and are plentiful in Sindh and Balochistan. Judgement: e.g. Thar coal is useful in the short term for energy security, but renewables should be expanded faster.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.

Against: high charges may discourage investors and reduce national revenue from royalties and taxes; the government, not companies, should be responsible for services; money may be wasted or captured by local elites without proper monitoring. Judgement: e.g. a fixed share for community development, managed transparently with local people, is fair and helps mining succeed.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.
Q55Extraction methods and impacts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Three coal deposits (illustrative figures):Deposit P: seam 10 m thick under 120 m of overburdenDeposit Q: seam 6 m thick under 30 m of overburdenDeposit R: seam 2 m thick under 180 m of overburden

  1. (a) Calculate the stripping ratio (thickness of overburden : thickness of coal) for each deposit, in the form n : 1. [3]
  2. (b) Open-cast mining is taken to be economic if the ratio is no more than 15 : 1. State which deposits should be mined open-cast. [1]
  3. (c) Explain why deposit R would have to be mined underground, and one problem this causes. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) P: 120 ÷ 10 = 12 : 1; Q: 30 ÷ 6 = 5 : 1; R: 180 ÷ 2 = 90 : 1
    B1 each: 12 : 1; 5 : 1; 90 : 1.
  2. (b) P and Q.
    B1 P and Q.
  3. (c) To reach a 2 m seam open-cast, 90 m of rock would have to be moved for every metre of coal, so the cost would be far greater than the value of the coal; a shaft or drift is cheaper. However, underground mining is more dangerous because of gas, roof collapse and flooding, and less of the coal can be recovered because pillars must be left.
    2 marks: reason (very high ratio → cost of removing overburden exceeds value; underground cheaper); 2 marks: problem developed (danger to miners/lower recovery/higher cost per tonne/ventilation needs).

Q57Extraction methods and impacts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Coal mining in two provinces in one year (hypothetical figures):Province X: 64 deaths, production 3.2 million tonnesProvince Y: 18 deaths, production 2.0 million tonnes

  1. (a) Calculate the number of deaths per million tonnes of coal produced in each province. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why comparing deaths per million tonnes is more useful than comparing the total number of deaths. [2]
  3. (c) Suggest two reasons why mining is more dangerous in Province X than in Province Y. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) X: 64 ÷ 3.2 = 20; Y: 18 ÷ 2.0 = 9 deaths per million tonnes
    B1 20; B1 9.
  2. (b) The provinces produce different amounts of coal, so a province with more mining would be expected to have more deaths; the rate shows how dangerous mining actually is for each tonne produced.
    1 mark: different amounts produced/size of industry; 1 mark: rate allows fair comparison of safety.
  3. (c) Mines in X may be small, privately run and use hand tools with little ventilation, so methane builds up and explosions occur; safety inspections may be rare in remote areas, so rules on gas testing and roof supports are not enforced; coal seams may be deeper and more gassy.
    1 mark for each reason + 1 for development. Reasons: small/old mines; poor ventilation/gas monitoring; weak inspection/enforcement; untrained workers; geology (deep, gassy seams, weak roofs); lack of rescue services.

  2. (b) 'Using modern machinery would solve the main problems of mining in Pakistan.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 450 − 120 = 330 jobs; 330 ÷ 450 × 100 = 73.3%
    B1 330; B1 73.3%.

(b) For: machines increase output per worker, reduce waste (e.g. wire saws instead of blasting in marble quarries), improve safety by reducing the number of people underground, and make deeper or lower-grade deposits profitable. Q59Extraction methods and impacts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

In an illustrative example, 40 lorries each make one round trip a day between a mine and a railhead 180 km away (360 km in total). Each lorry uses 0.35 litres of diesel per km.

  1. (a) Calculate the fuel used by the lorries each day and in a 300-day working year. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two environmental effects of carrying ore by road in this way. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) Suggest two reasons why a railway or conveyor belt might be a better choice. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 40 × 2 × 180 × 0.35 = 5,040 litres a day; 5,040 × 300 = 1,512,000 litres a year
    B1 5,040 litres; B1 1,512,000 litres.
  2. (b) Burning so much diesel releases carbon dioxide and smoke, adding to air pollution and climate change; heavy lorries throw up dust along unpaved roads, which settles on crops and harms people's lungs; the roads are damaged and accidents increase in villages along the route.
    1 mark for each effect + 1 for development. Effects: CO₂/air pollution; dust; road damage; noise; accidents; spills of ore.
  3. (c) It uses less energy per tonne carried, so it causes less pollution; it removes heavy lorries from village roads, making them safer.
    1 mark each: less fuel/emissions per tonne; less dust; fewer accidents; lower running cost over time; reliable large capacity.

  2. (b) Read the two views below.View A: Gemstone mining in northern Pakistan should be left to small local miners.View B: Gemstone mining in northern Pakistan should be developed by large companies.Which view do you agree with more? Give reasons to support your answer. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) Heavy snow blocks the paths and covers the mine sites in winter; temperatures are far below freezing, making outdoor work impossible.

  2. (b) View A: local miners earn money in poor mountain valleys, keep control of their land and resources, and profits stay in the community. But they often use explosives and hand tools that damage the stones, work in unsafe conditions and sell rough stones cheaply to middlemen. View B: companies can invest in surveys, safer machinery and cutting and polishing, so gems fetch higher prices and exports rise; but they may take profits out of the area, employ few local people and damage the fragile mountain environment.
