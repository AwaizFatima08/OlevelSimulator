Q1Population growth and structure · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by the term death rate? [1]
  2. (b) A district has a birth rate of 27 per 1,000 and a death rate of 7 per 1,000. Calculate its rate of natural increase as a percentage. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why death rates in Pakistan have fallen since 1950. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The number of deaths in a year for every 1,000 people in the population.
    B1 deaths per 1,000 per year.
  2. (b) (27 − 7) ÷ 10 = 2% per year
    M1 27 − 7 = 20 per 1,000; A1 2%.
  3. (c) Vaccination programmes against diseases such as measles, tuberculosis and polio mean fewer children die young; more hospitals, basic health units and Lady Health Workers give people access to treatment; cleaner drinking water and better sanitation reduce diseases such as cholera and diarrhoea; improved food supply since the Green Revolution reduces malnutrition.
    1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: vaccination; medical facilities and medicines; clean water and sanitation; better nutrition; health education.

  1. (a) State what happens to birth rates and death rates in Stage 2 of the demographic transition model. [2]
  2. (b) Pakistan is often described as being between Stage 2 and Stage 3 of the model. Explain why its birth rate is now falling slowly. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer

    B1 birth rate high; B1 death rate falls rapidly.
  2.     1 mark for each point and 1 mark for its development, for two points. Points: female education / later marriage; family planning; lower infant mortality; urbanisation / cost of children; women working outside the home.
Q3Population growth and structure · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

In one year a small town in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa with a population of 80,000 recorded 2,240 live births and 560 deaths.

  1. (a) What is meant by the term birth rate? [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the birth rate and the death rate of the town, per 1,000. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the rate of natural increase as a percentage. [1]
  4. (d) Explain two reasons why the birth rate in a town like this may still be high. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The number of live births in a year for every 1,000 people in the population.
    B1 live births per 1,000 people per year (both 'per 1,000' and 'per year' idea needed).
  2. (b) Birth rate = 2,240 ÷ 80,000 × 1,000 = 28 per 1,000; death rate = 560 ÷ 80,000 × 1,000 = 7 per 1,000.
    B1 birth rate 28; B1 death rate 7.
  3. (c) (28 − 7) ÷ 10 = 2.1%
    B1 2.1% (allow ecf from b).
  4. (d) Many couples marry young, so women have more years in which to bear children; children are seen as support in old age, so parents want several, especially sons; limited use of contraception because of lack of knowledge or family pressure; high infant mortality in the past makes families have more children to be sure some survive.
    1 mark for each reason + 1 mark for development, for two reasons. Reasons: early marriage; children as old-age security/labour; low contraceptive use; religious/cultural attitudes; low female education; replacement of infant deaths.
Q4Population growth and structure · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

A population pyramid for a rural district in southern Punjab is described as follows: the 0–4 and 5–9 bars are the longest, each older age group is shorter than the one below it, and the bars for people aged 65 and over are very short.

  1. (a) Using the description, state two features of the pyramid that show a high birth rate. [2]
  2. (b) State what the narrow top of the pyramid suggests about the population. [1]
  3. (c) Explain two ways in which the shape of this pyramid is likely to change over the next 30 years if birth rates fall. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The base (0–4 and 5–9 bars) is very wide; each younger age group is larger than the one above it, so the sides slope steeply inwards from the base.
    1 mark each: wide base; large proportion of children; steady narrowing upwards from the youngest group.
  2. (b) Few people live to old age – life expectancy is fairly low (or the death rate among older people is high).
    B1 low life expectancy / few elderly people.
  3. (c) The base will become narrower because fewer babies are born each year, so the 0–14 bars shrink relative to the adult bars; the middle will bulge as today's large groups of children become working-age adults; the top will widen as better health care lets more people survive into old age.
    1 mark for each change + 1 for development. Changes: narrower base; wider middle (working ages); wider top (more elderly); sides become straighter/more vertical.
Q5Population growth and structure · Core · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

  1. (a) What is meant by the infant mortality rate? [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the infant mortality rate for the district. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two reasons why infant mortality is usually higher in rural areas of Pakistan than in cities. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) The number of babies who die before their first birthday for every 1,000 live births in a year.
    B1 deaths under 1 year old per 1,000 live births.
  2. (b) 1,800 ÷ 30,000 × 1,000 = 60 per 1,000 live births
    M1 1,800 ÷ 30,000 × 1,000; A1 60.
  3. (c) Many rural births take place at home without a trained midwife, so problems at birth are not treated; there are fewer clinics and doctors, so babies with diarrhoea or pneumonia do not get help in time; unsafe drinking water and poor sanitation spread infections; mothers may be poorly nourished, so babies are underweight.
    1 mark for each reason + 1 for development. Reasons: lack of skilled birth attendants; distance from health centres; unsafe water/sanitation; malnutrition of mothers/babies; lower vaccination rates; lower female education.

Q12Population growth and structure · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 11 marks
  1. (a) A useful estimate of the time a population takes to double is 70 ÷ (annual growth rate in %). Pakistan's population grew at about 2.5% a year between 2017 and 2023. Estimate how many years it would take to double at this rate. [1]
  2. (b) Explain two problems that rapid population growth causes for the government of Pakistan. You should develop your answer. [4]
  3. (c) 'Pakistan's large and young population is an asset rather than a problem.' Evaluate this view. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) 70 ÷ 2.5 = 28 years
    B1 28 years.
  2. (b) It must provide more schools, teachers and hospitals every year just to keep up, so quality falls and class sizes rise; millions of young people enter the labour market each year, and not enough jobs are created, so unemployment and underemployment rise; demand for food, water and housing rises, putting pressure on irrigation water and causing growth of katchi abadis.

(c) Asset: a large young workforce can raise production and attract investment if it is educated and skilled (demographic dividend); a big home market helps industries such as textiles and food; young workers abroad send remittances; young people quickly adopt technology, supporting the IT sector and freelancing. Problem: many young people lack education and skills, so they cannot find good jobs; high dependency ratio strains family incomes; pressure on schools, hospitals, water and housing; unemployment can lead to social unrest. Judgement: e.g. it can be an asset only if the government invests in education, skills and job creation.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points. L2 (3–4): developed point(s) for one view, or simple points for both. L3 (5–6): developed points for both views, a named Pakistani example and a judgement.
