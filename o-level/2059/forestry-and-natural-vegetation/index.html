Q1Deforestation and sustainable forestry · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) What is meant by 'deforestation'? [2]
  2. (b) State three causes of deforestation in Pakistan. [3]
  3. (c) Approximately what percentage of Pakistan's land area is covered by forest? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The permanent removal or clearing of forest / trees from an area, so the land is used for something else or left bare.
    1 mark removal / cutting of trees; 1 mark large-scale / permanent / not replaced.
  2. (b) Any three: cutting trees for fuelwood; illegal logging for timber ('timber mafia'); clearing land for farming; building of roads, houses and towns; overgrazing of young trees by goats and sheep; forest fires; building of hotels in tourist areas.
    1 mark each.
  3. (c) About 5% (accept 4–6%).
    1 mark.
Q2Deforestation and sustainable forestry · Core · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain how deforestation on mountain slopes can lead to soil erosion. You should develop your answer. [4]
  2. (b) Explain how deforestation in the upper Indus catchment can increase the risk of floods downstream. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Without tree cover, heavy rain hits the ground directly and loosens soil; there are no roots to bind the soil together, so it is washed downhill by run-off; on steep slopes this can cause landslides and gullies; less leaf litter means less organic matter to hold the soil.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
  2. (b) Trees intercept rain and soil absorbs it; when forest is removed more rain becomes surface run-off, which reaches the rivers quickly, so river levels rise faster and higher; eroded soil is deposited in river beds, raising them and reducing their capacity, so they overflow more easily.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
Q3Deforestation and sustainable forestry · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) What is the difference between 'afforestation' and 'reforestation'? [2]
  2. (b) What is meant by 'sustainable forestry'? [2]
  3. (c) State two methods of sustainable forest management. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Afforestation is planting trees on land that has not been forest (or not for a long time); reforestation is replanting trees where forest has recently been cut down.
    1 mark each definition.
  2. (b) Managing forests so that the trees cut are replaced by new growth, so that forests and their benefits continue for future generations.
    1 mark: using / cutting trees; 1 mark: at a rate that allows replacement / for the future.
  3. (c) Any two: replanting after felling; selective felling instead of clear-felling; fixed felling cycles / quotas; protecting areas for natural regeneration; controlling grazing; community forest management; using fuel-efficient stoves and alternative fuels.
    1 mark each.

Q6Deforestation and sustainable forestry · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A mountain district in Khyber Pakhtunkhwa has a total area of 12,500 km². Its forest area was 1,850 km² in 1990 and 1,295 km² in 2020 (hypothetical data).

  1. (a) Calculate the area of forest lost between 1990 and 2020, and express it as a percentage of the 1990 forest area. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the average area of forest lost per year. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the percentage of the district covered by forest in 2020. Give your answer to one decimal place. [1]
  4. (d) Suggest two reasons why the forest area in this district fell. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 555 km²; 30.0%
    1,850 − 1,295 = 555 km² (B1); 555 ÷ 1,850 × 100 = 30.0% (B1).
  2. (b) 18.5 km² per year
    555 ÷ 30 = 18.5 km².
  3. (c) 10.4%
    1,295 ÷ 12,500 × 100 = 10.4%.
  4. (d) Growing population needing fuelwood and timber; clearing for farmland and houses; illegal logging; new roads opening up forests; overgrazing; forest fires.
    1 mark each.

Q8Deforestation and sustainable forestry · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Q12Deforestation and sustainable forestry · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

  1. (a) Calculate the mass of fuelwood one household uses in a year (365 days). [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the mass of fuelwood used by the whole village in a year, in tonnes. [1]

  1. (a) Any two: kikar (Acacia); ber; jand (khejri); karir; frash.
    1 mark each.
  2. (b) Long tap roots reach water deep underground, so plants survive months without rain; small leaves or thorns reduce the surface area for transpiration, so less water is lost; thick bark and waxy leaves reduce water loss; thorns also protect plants from grazing animals.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
  3. (c) Fuelwood; fodder for goats and camels; timber for tools and fencing; fruit (ber); shade.
    1 mark.

  1. (a) Describe where alpine pastures are found in Pakistan. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why trees cannot grow in alpine areas. You should develop your answer. [2]
  3. (c) Explain two ways in which alpine pastures are used by people. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) High in the northern mountains above the tree line (roughly above 3,500 m), e.g. in Gilgit-Baltistan, Chitral and upper Swat or Kaghan; the Deosai Plains.
    1 mark altitude / above tree line; 1 mark named area.
  2. (b) Temperatures are very low and the growing season is too short for trees; strong winds, frozen and thin soils and snow cover for much of the year.
    1 mark point + 1 mark development.
  3. (c) Herders take sheep, goats and yaks up to graze in summer (transhumance), which provides meat, milk and wool; medicinal herbs are collected and sold; tourists visit for trekking and camping, bringing income; summer settlements make butter and cheese.

Q28Forest and vegetation types · Standard · Paper 2 style · 7 marks
  1. (a) Name two trees found in the sub-tropical dry broadleaved forests of the Pothwar Plateau and Margalla Hills. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why these forests are often thin and scattered. You should develop your answer. [4]

Q29Forest and vegetation types · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A transect up a mountainside in northern Pakistan recorded these vegetation zones (hypothetical data):900–1,800 m: chir pine forest1,800–3,000 m: deodar and blue pine forest3,000–3,600 m: fir, spruce and birchabove 3,600 m: alpine grassland and shrubsThe temperature at the valley floor (1,200 m) was 24°C on a summer afternoon.

  1. (a) Using a fall in temperature of 6.5°C for every 1,000 m rise in height, calculate the expected temperature at 3,700 m when it is 24°C at 1,200 m. Give your answer to one decimal place. [2]
  2. (b) Use the data and your answer to explain why vegetation changes as you go up the mountain. You should develop your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 7.8°C
    Height difference = 2,500 m (M1); 24 − 6.5 × 2.5 = 7.75 ≈ 7.8°C (A1).
  2. (b) Temperature falls with height, so the growing season becomes shorter higher up; broadleaved and chir pine trees need warmer conditions and grow lower down, while fir and spruce tolerate cold; above about 3,600 m it is too cold and windy, with frozen soils, for trees, so only grasses and small plants (alpine pasture) grow.
    1 mark for each of two reasons + 1 mark each for development (a consequence or 'which leads to').
Q30Forest and vegetation types · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 9 marks
  1. (a) Describe the juniper forest at Ziarat in Balochistan. [3]
  2. (b) Evaluate the extent to which the juniper forest at Ziarat is under threat. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) One of the largest juniper forests in the world; contains very slow-growing trees, some of which are thousands of years old; grows on mountain slopes in a cold, dry climate; protected as a biosphere reserve.
    1 mark per point up to 3.
  2. (b) Threats: cutting for fuelwood in very cold winters; overgrazing of seedlings by goats and sheep, so few young trees grow; very slow growth means losses cannot be replaced quickly; droughts and climate change; disease (e.g. dwarf mistletoe) and pests; pressure from tourism and roads. Protection: legal status as a protected area, conservation projects, gas supply to some villages, awareness. Judgement required.
    Level marking. L1 (1–2): simple points, one side or generic. L2 (3–4): developed points on one side, or simple points on both sides. L3 (5–6): developed points on both sides with a named Pakistani example and a reasoned judgement.
