Q1Factors affecting trade · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State three factors that make it difficult for Pakistan to increase its exports. [3]
  2. (b) Explain how load-shedding affects Pakistan's ability to export. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) High costs of electricity and gas for factories; a narrow range of export goods, mainly textiles; competition from countries such as Bangladesh, Vietnam and China.
    1 mark each for any three: high energy costs/load-shedding; narrow export base; low value addition; competition; quality/standards problems; political instability; poor infrastructure/port delays; tariffs and barriers abroad; low productivity/skills.
  2. (b) Power cuts stop factory production, so exporters miss delivery deadlines and foreign buyers may move their orders to more reliable suppliers.
    1 mark effect on production + 1 mark consequence for exports (late orders/lost buyers/higher costs → less competitive).
Q2Factors affecting trade · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by 'value addition' in trade. [1]
  2. (b) Explain why Pakistan earns more from exporting garments than from exporting the same weight of raw cotton. [3]
  3. (c) Name one Pakistani city famous for exporting sports goods and surgical instruments. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Processing a raw material into a more finished product so it can be sold for a higher price, e.g. turning raw cotton into garments.
    1 mark for processing/manufacturing to increase value.
  2. (b) Garments have gone through spinning, weaving, dyeing and stitching, so the labour, skill and processing are included in the price. Raw cotton is a primary product sold cheaply and its price changes a lot on world markets, while finished goods sell at higher and more stable prices.
    1 mark each, max 3: processing adds value; more labour/skills/jobs included; primary product prices low/volatile; brand/design increases price; idea illustrated with cotton → yarn → cloth → garments.
  3. (c) Sialkot
    1 mark.
Q3Factors affecting trade · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain two ways in which a fall in the value of the Pakistani rupee against the US dollar affects trade. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Pakistani exports become cheaper for foreign buyers, which may increase demand for goods such as textiles and rice. Imports such as oil and machinery become more expensive in rupees, which raises costs for industry and consumers and can cause inflation.
    Two developed points: exports cheaper → more competitive; imports dearer → higher import bill/inflation; imported raw materials dearer → exporters' costs rise; debt repayment cost rises.

Q6Factors affecting trade · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) State what is meant by GSP+ status. [2]
  2. (b) Explain two ways in which GSP+ status has benefited Pakistan's textile industry. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) A European Union scheme that allows a developing country to export many goods to the EU with zero or reduced import duties, in return for following international conventions on human rights, labour rights, the environment and good governance.
    1 mark reduced/zero tariffs into EU; 1 mark conditional on implementing international conventions.
  2. (b) Without import duties, Pakistani textiles and garments are cheaper in EU shops than goods from competitors who pay duties, so EU exports grew. Higher orders led factories to invest in new machinery and employ more workers.
    Two developed points: tariff-free access → price competitiveness; increased exports to EU (e.g. Germany, Spain, Netherlands); investment and jobs; foreign exchange earnings.
Q7Factors affecting trade · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain two reasons why Pakistan's exports depend heavily on the cotton crop. [4]
  2. (b) State two other natural events that can reduce Pakistan's exports. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Textiles are Pakistan's largest export, and most of them are made from locally grown cotton, so a good crop supplies mills cheaply. A poor cotton harvest, caused for example by floods, pests or heat, forces mills to import cotton at higher cost or cut production, reducing exports.
    Two developed points: textiles largest share of exports; local raw material; crop failure → imports/higher costs; cotton yarn and cloth also exported; whole chain (ginning, spinning, weaving) depends on it.
  2. (b) Floods (e.g. the 2022 floods destroying crops such as cotton and rice); droughts or heatwaves reducing crop yields.
    1 mark each for any two: floods; drought; heatwaves; pest attacks (e.g. locusts, pink bollworm); earthquakes damaging infrastructure.
Q8Factors affecting trade · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Two countries make the same shirt. Country X sells it for US$10.00 and must pay an import duty of 12% in the buying country. Country Y sells it for US$10.50 but pays no import duty because of a trade agreement (illustrative figures).

  1. (a) Calculate the amount of import duty paid on one shirt from Country X. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the final price in the importing country of a shirt from Country X and from Country Y. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why trade agreements that remove import duties are important for a country such as Pakistan. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) US$1.20
    12% of US$10.00 = 0.12 × 10.00 = US$1.20.
  2. (b) Country X: US$11.20; Country Y: US$10.50
    X: 10.00 × 1.12 = 11.20 (1); Y: 10.50 with zero duty (1).
  3. (c) Without duty its goods are cheaper than those of competitors who still pay duty, as Country Y shows, so buyers place more orders. This increases export earnings and jobs, and the small profit margins in textiles mean even a few per cent of duty can decide where orders go.
    1 mark each, max 3: price competitiveness; more orders/exports; jobs and foreign exchange; small margins in textiles; illustrated with the figures.
Q9Factors affecting trade · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain two ways in which improvements in transport and ports help Pakistan's trade. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest one reason why trade with neighbouring Afghanistan is often lower than expected. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Faster clearance and less congestion at Karachi and Port Qasim reduce the time and cost of shipping goods, making exports more competitive. Motorways and dry ports allow goods from inland cities such as Faisalabad and Sialkot to reach ships quickly and reliably.

  2. (b) Border closures at crossings such as Torkham and Chaman because of security and political tensions stop trucks from crossing, so trade is interrupted.
    1 mark reason + 1 development: border closures; security; political disputes; informal/smuggling trade; Afghan transit trade misuse; payment/banking problems.

Q16Factors affecting trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain why a narrow export base is a weakness for Pakistan's economy. [4]
  2. (b) Suggest two ways in which Pakistan could diversify its exports. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) When a country relies on a few products, mainly textiles, rice and leather, a fall in world demand or prices for those goods, or a poor cotton crop, sharply reduces total export earnings. It also means Pakistan competes head-on with many low-wage producers in the same goods, and earnings grow slowly because the goods are mostly low value.
    2 developed points: vulnerability to price/demand changes; dependence on one crop (cotton); strong competition in same products; low-value goods → slow growth in earnings.
  2. (b) Develop IT and software exports; develop high-value manufactured goods such as engineering goods, pharmaceuticals or processed foods (also: minerals from Reko Diq, fruit and halal meat).
    1 mark each for any two realistic suggestions.
Q17Factors affecting trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A Faisalabad textile exporter's cost of producing 1,000 metres of cloth (illustrative figures):Year 1: raw materials and labour Rs 250 per metre, energy Rs 150 per metreYear 2: raw materials and labour Rs 250 per metre, energy Rs 270 per metre

  1. (a) Calculate the exporter's total cost of producing 1,000 metres in each year. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the percentage increase in total cost. [1]
  3. (c) Explain how rising energy costs affect Pakistan's ability to compete in world markets. [3]
Show answer

    Year 1: (250 + 150) × 1,000 = 400,000 (1); Year 2: (250 + 270) × 1,000 = 520,000 (1).

    (520,000 − 400,000) ÷ 400,000 × 100 = 30%.

Q20Factors affecting trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate the extent to which the development of Gwadar port could increase Pakistan's trade. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gwadar is a deep-sea port on the Arabian Sea, on the shipping routes to the Gulf and the Middle East, and it is part of the China–Pakistan Economic Corridor. It could shorten shipping routes for trade with the Middle East and Africa and give landlocked Central Asia a route to the sea, and it could bring investment in roads and industry. However, trade through Gwadar depends on good road and rail links, reliable power and water, and investment in the port's facilities; trade volumes also depend on Karachi's established role and on regional security.     Level 3 (9–12): sustained judgement on Gwadar's advantages (location, CPEC, shorter routes) against infrastructure, security and competition from Karachi; clear conclusion. Level 2 (5–8): describes advantages and problems with limited judgement. Level 1 (1–4): description only.

Q36Trading partners and balance of trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between the balance of trade and the balance of payments. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two problems for Pakistan of a large and persistent trade deficit. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The balance of trade compares only the value of exports and imports of goods. The balance of payments records all money flows between a country and the rest of the world, including services, remittances, interest, loans and investment, so a country with a trade deficit may have a smaller current account deficit if remittances are large.
    1 mark trade = goods only; 1 mark payments = all transactions (services, remittances, capital/investment, loans); 1 mark example/link to remittances.
  2. (b) Foreign exchange reserves fall, so the country must borrow from abroad (e.g. the IMF) and debt rises; the rupee loses value, raising the price of imports and causing inflation.

Q38Trading partners and balance of trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Q39Trading partners and balance of trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a persistent trade deficit could affect a country's exchange rate. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) When imports are greater than exports, the country must sell its currency to buy foreign currency to pay for imports, which increases the supply of its currency; this tends to lower its value against other currencies, so the exchange rate depreciates; a weaker currency makes imports more expensive, which may increase inflation.
    1 mark each for any six linked steps, up to 6: more domestic currency sold to buy foreign currency (1); supply of domestic currency rises (1); currency depreciates (1); imports become more expensive (1); inflation or pressure on the economy (1); clear link from the deficit to the exchange rate (1).
Q40Trading partners and balance of trade · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 12 marks
  1. (a) Evaluate the extent to which a persistent trade deficit is harmful to Pakistan's economy. [12]
Show answer
  1. (a) A trade deficit means the country must borrow or use foreign reserves to pay for imports, which can increase debt, reduce reserves and put pressure on the currency, and it may weaken the country's ability to pay for development. However, a deficit can also mean that the country is importing machinery, raw materials and energy that support growth, and it can be financed by remittances, foreign investment and loans that are used productively. Judgement: a deficit is harmful when it is financed by short-term borrowing and reflects weak exports, but it is less harmful when imports build productive capacity and exports are growing.
    Level 3 (9–12): sustained judgement weighing harm (debt, reserves, currency pressure) against benefits (productive imports, financing sources); clear conclusion linked to the type of imports and financing. Level 2 (5–8): one-sided or limited judgement. Level 1 (1–4): description only.

Q47Imports and exports · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Q48Imports and exports · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
