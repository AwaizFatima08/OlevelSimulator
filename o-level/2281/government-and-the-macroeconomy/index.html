  2. (b) Explain two government policies that could reduce this type of unemployment. [4]
Show answer

Q8Employment and unemployment · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain how a recession leads to cyclical unemployment. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest one government policy to reduce cyclical unemployment. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Total spending (aggregate demand) falls, so firms sell less and produce less output. They reduce hiring, cut overtime or make workers redundant, so unemployment rises.
    1 mark: fall in aggregate demand or spending; 1 mark: firms sell less and produce less; 1 mark: fewer workers needed, so hiring stops or workers are made redundant.
  2. (b) Any one, e.g. expansionary fiscal policy such as higher government spending on roads or schools, a cut in income tax, or lower interest rates to raise spending.
    1 mark for any policy that raises aggregate demand.

Q27Fiscal policy · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks
  1. (a) Explain how expansionary fiscal policy can reduce unemployment during a recession. [4]
  2. (b) Explain two reasons why expansionary fiscal policy may fail to reduce unemployment. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Higher government spending and/or lower taxes raise aggregate demand; firms sell more and need more workers (labour is a derived demand), reducing cyclical unemployment.
    1 mark: increase spending/cut taxes; 1 mark: aggregate demand rises; 1 mark: firms raise output; 1 mark: demand for labour rises, cyclical unemployment falls.
  2. (b) e.g. time lags in planning and spending; unemployment may be structural, so extra demand does not match workers' skills
    1 mark each for two reasons + 1 mark each for explanation: time lags; structural/frictional unemployment not caused by low demand; extra income saved or spent on imports; government cannot afford more borrowing/debt; firms use spare capacity or machines rather than hiring.
Q28Fiscal policy · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Discuss whether a government should finance a major infrastructure project through a budget deficit during a recession. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) A balanced discussion. For: the multiplier effect raises output; the project creates jobs; borrowing costs may be low in a recession; the infrastructure gives long-term benefits. Against: national debt and interest payments rise, limiting future spending; if the economy is near full capacity the project could cause inflation or crowd out private spending; time lags delay the effect; the project may be badly chosen. The conclusion depends on existing debt levels, the size of the multiplier and the quality of the project.
    5–6 marks: developed arguments on both sides, uses the recession context, and a justified conclusion. 3–4 marks: one side well developed. 1–2 marks: unsupported statements.
