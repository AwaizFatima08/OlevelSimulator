Q2Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Explain the difference between an irrecoverable debt and a doubtful debt. [2]
  2. (b) State two reasons why a business creates a provision for doubtful debts. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Irrecoverable debt: certain not to be paid, so written off. Doubtful debt: may not be paid, so a provision is made but the debt stays in the customer's account.

Q9Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A bookshop's trade receivables at 31 December 2026 were $28,640 before adjustments. A debt of $640 is to be written off as irrecoverable. The provision for doubtful debts is to be 2.5% of trade receivables after the write-off. The provision on 1 January 2026 was $900.

  1. (a) Calculate the provision for doubtful debts required at 31 December 2026. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the net charge to the income statement for the irrecoverable debt and the change in the provision. [2]
  3. (c) State whether the change in the provision is an increase or a decrease in the charge, and explain why. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $700
    Receivables after write-off = 28,640 − 640 = 28,000. Provision = 2.5% × 28,000 = 700.
  2. (b) $440
    Irrecoverable debt charged = 640. The provision falls from 900 to 700, so 200 is released to the income statement and reduces the charge: 640 − 200 = 440.
  3. (c) A decrease (a release of 200), because the provision required (700) is less than the opening provision (900), so the excess is credited back to the income statement.
    1 mark for the direction (decrease); 1 mark for the reason, comparing the required provision with the opening provision.
Q10Irrecoverable debts and provision for doubtful debts · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Ravi Engineering's trade receivables at 31 August 2026 are analysed by age:Up to 30 days: $20,000 (provision 1%)31 to 60 days: $8,000 (provision 3%)Over 60 days: $3,000 (provision 10%)

  1. (a) Calculate the provision for doubtful debts required at 31 August 2026. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the charge to the income statement, if the provision brought forward on 1 September 2025 is $450. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why a business might base its provision on the age of the debts rather than as a flat percentage of all trade receivables. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) $740
    1% × 20,000 = 200; 3% × 8,000 = 240; 10% × 3,000 = 300; total 740.
  2. (b) $290 increase (charged to the income statement)
    740 − 450 = 290. The provision has increased, so the difference is charged.
  3. (c) Older debts are more likely to be irrecoverable, so a higher percentage is applied to them. This gives a more reliable and prudent estimate of the amount that will not be paid.
    1 mark: older debts are more likely to be irrecoverable; 1 mark: a higher rate on older debts gives a more reliable or prudent estimate.

Q21Depreciation and disposal of non-current assets · Core · Paper 2 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Define residual value, and state how it is used in a straight-line depreciation calculation. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Residual value is the estimated amount the asset will be worth (scrap or disposal value) at the end of its useful life. It is deducted from cost to find the depreciable amount: (cost − residual value) ÷ useful life.
    1 mark for the definition; 1 mark for the calculation method.

Q28Depreciation and disposal of non-current assets · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 8 marks

Danish bought a delivery van for $50,000. He is choosing between the straight-line method (useful life 6 years, residual value $8,000) and the reducing balance method at 30% per annum.

  1. (a) Calculate the depreciation for the first two years under each method. [4]
  2. (b) State the difference in total profit for the first two years if reducing balance is used. [1]
  3. (c) Advise Danish which method to use. Justify your answer. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Straight line $7,000 each year ($14,000 in total); reducing balance year 1 $15,000, year 2 $10,500 ($25,500 in total)
    B1 7,000; B1 15,000; B1 10,500; B1 totals.
  2. (b) Profit $11,500 lower
    25,500 − 14,000 = 11,500 (B1).
  3.     Installation is capital expenditure (part of the machine's cost), so the $3,000 must be removed from repairs. Profit increases by 3,000: 18,000 + 3,000 = 21,000. A reverses the adjustment; B is the uncorrected profit; D adds the amount twice.
Q74Capital and revenue expenditure and receipts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

A transport company buys a van for $36,000. It also pays import duty of $4,500, delivery charges of $600 and first-year insurance of $1,200.

  1. (a) Calculate the capital cost of the van. [3]
  2. (b) State the treatment of the first-year insurance of $1,200. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $41,100
    36,000 purchase price + 4,500 import duty + 600 delivery charges = 41,100. First-year insurance is not part of the cost.
  2. (b) Revenue expenditure: it is charged to the income statement as an expense for the year.
    Insurance is a running cost of using the van, not part of acquiring it.
Q75Capital and revenue expenditure and receipts · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

Kashif debited $4,800 for a new van to motor expenses. The van is depreciated at 25% a year by the reducing balance method, with the first charge in the year of purchase. Profit before the correction was $22,500.

  1. (a) Calculate the corrected profit for the year. [3]
  2. (b) State where the van appears in the statement of financial position after the correction, and its carrying amount. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) $26,100
    Profit as recorded 22,500 + expense removed 4,800 − depreciation on the van (25% × 4,800 = 1,200) = 26,100.
  2. (b) In non-current assets, at a carrying amount of $3,600 (cost 4,800 less accumulated depreciation 1,200).
    The van is a non-current asset at cost less accumulated depreciation.
