Show answer
  1. (a) A added to the profit for the year

Q12Partnerships · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

Q14Partnerships · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks

Fatima and Gul are partners in a catering business in Islamabad. Fatima works full-time; Gul works part-time. They are discussing two options for sharing profits:Option 1: Fatima receives a salary of $8,000 and the rest is shared equally.Option 2: no salary; profits shared Fatima 60% and Gul 40%.The profit for next year is expected to be $64,000. Ignore interest on capital and drawings.

  1. (a) Calculate Fatima's total share of profit under each option. [4]
  2. (b) Calculate the profit for the year at which both options would give Fatima the same amount. [2]
  3. (c) Advise Fatima which option to choose. Justify your answer. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Option 1 $36,000; option 2 $38,400
    Option 1: 64,000 − 8,000 = 56,000; ÷ 2 = 28,000; + 8,000 = 36,000 (M1 A1). Option 2: 64,000 × 60% = 38,400 (M1 A1).
  2. (b) $40,000
    Option 1: 8,000 + (P − 8,000) ÷ 2 = 4,000 + 0.5P; Option 2: 0.6P (M1); 4,000 + 0.5P = 0.6P, so P = 40,000 (A1).
  3. (c) At the expected profit of $64,000 Option 2 gives Fatima more ($38,400 v $36,000). Option 1 is better for her only if profit falls below $40,000, because the $8,000 salary is received first whatever the profit, so it is safer. Advice: choose Option 2 if she is confident profit will stay well above $40,000; choose Option 1 if profits are uncertain, since it guarantees a reward for her full-time work.
    B1 compares amounts at $64,000; B1 salary guaranteed/less risk; B1 uses the $40,000 break-even point; B1 justified decision.

Q90Sole traders · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 10 marks

Thandi runs a gift shop in Durban. This year: revenue $150,000, gross margin 35%, expenses $33,000. She could move to a larger shop. She expects revenue to rise by $60,000 at the same gross margin, rent to rise by $12,000 and other expenses to rise by $6,500 a year.

  1. (a) Calculate Thandi's profit for the current year. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the expected profit if she moves to the larger shop. [3]
  3. (c) Advise Thandi whether she should move to the larger shop. Justify your answer. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) $19,500
    Gross profit 150,000 × 35% = 52,500 (M1); − 33,000 = 19,500 (A1).
  2. (b) $22,000
    Gross profit 210,000 × 35% = 73,500 (M1); expenses 33,000 + 12,000 + 6,500 = 51,500 (M1); 22,000 (A1).
  3. (c) For: profit expected to rise by $2,500; more space for wider range/more customers. Against: extra revenue is only a forecast, and if sales rise by less than about $52,857 profit falls; higher fixed costs (rent) must be paid anyway; moving costs/disruption; existing customers may not follow. Justified decision.
    1 mark each for two points for and two against; 1 mark for a justified decision.
