Q7Stars & the Universe · Core · Paper 2 style · 4 marks

  2. (b) Explain why the star does not collapse under its own gravity while it is in its stable stage. [2]
Show answer

    B1 energy release gives an outward force / pressure; B1 this balances the inward gravitational force, so the star is stable.

Q12Stars & the Universe · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A stable star stays the same size for billions of years. Explain why, in terms of the forces acting. [2]

Q15Stars & the Universe · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

  4. (d) Some interstellar clouds contain elements such as iron and carbon as well as hydrogen. Suggest where these heavier elements came from. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gravity pulls the dust and gas together; the cloud collapses into a dense, hot ball (protostar).
    B1 gravitational attraction pulls particles together; B1 cloud contracts into a dense clump.
  2. (b) Gravitational potential energy of the falling material is converted into kinetic energy of the particles, so the temperature rises.

  3. (c) Its core must become hot enough for hydrogen nuclei to fuse into helium.
    B1

    B1

Q19Stars & the Universe · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

Astronomers measure the speed at which four distant galaxies move away from the Earth:galaxy 1: distance 2.0 × 10²⁴ m, speed 4.4 × 10⁶ m/sgalaxy 2: distance 3.5 × 10²⁴ m, speed 7.7 × 10⁶ m/sgalaxy 3: distance 5.0 × 10²⁴ m, speed 1.1 × 10⁷ m/sgalaxy 4: distance 8.0 × 10²⁴ m, speed 1.76 × 10⁷ m/s

  1. (a) Calculate the value of v ÷ d for each galaxy and state its unit. Give each value to 2 significant figures. [2]
  2. (b) State what these results show about the relationship between the distance of a galaxy and its speed of recession. [1]
  3. (c) Use the value of v ÷ d from part (a) to predict the speed at which a galaxy 1.2 × 10²⁵ m from the Earth is moving away. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second (s⁻¹) for every galaxy
    M1 v ÷ d for each galaxy (4.4 × 10⁶ ÷ 2.0 × 10²⁴ = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ etc.); A1 all 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ s⁻¹.
  2. (b) The further away a galaxy is, the faster it is moving away from the Earth (speed is proportional to distance).
    B1 greater distance gives greater recession speed; the ratio is constant.
  3. (c) 2.6 × 10⁷ m/s
    M1 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ × 1.2 × 10²⁵; A1 2.6 × 10⁷ m/s.

Q25Stars & the Universe · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Describe what happens in the core of a very massive star near the end of its life, leading to a supernova. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why scientists say 'the iron in your blood was made inside stars'. [2]

  4. (d) State why a black hole cannot be seen directly. [1]
Show answer

    B1 heavy elements made in stars / supernovae; B1 scattered into a nebula from which the Sun, Earth (and we) formed.
  3. (c) Its mass is too small; it will become a red giant then a white dwarf.
    B1
  4. (d) Its gravitational field is so strong that not even light can escape from it.
    B1

    B1

    B1 thick carbon dioxide atmosphere; B1 sunlight passes through to heat the surface; B1 atmosphere traps infrared radiation re-emitted from the surface (greenhouse effect).
  2. (b) Mercury has almost no atmosphere, so it cannot keep thermal energy in at night and the night side becomes very cold. Venus's thick atmosphere keeps its temperature nearly the same everywhere.
    B1 Mercury has little or no atmosphere so heat is lost at night; B1 Venus's atmosphere retains and spreads thermal energy, keeping its temperature steady.

Q60Earth & Solar System · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A student says: 'The seasons happen because the Earth is closer to the Sun in summer.'

  1. (a) Explain why the student's idea is wrong. Use the fact that the Earth is closest to the Sun in early January. [2]
  2. (b) Explain why it is summer in Australia in December. [2]
  3. (c) State the cause of the seasons. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The Earth is closest to the Sun in early January, which is winter in the Northern Hemisphere, so distance from the Sun is not the cause of the seasons.
    B1 closest to the Sun in early January (Northern winter), so distance is not the cause; B1 summer is caused by the tilt of the axis.
  2. (b) In December the Southern Hemisphere is tilted towards the Sun, so the Sun's rays strike the ground more directly and the days are longer.
    B1 southern hemisphere tilted towards Sun (more direct rays); B1 longer days give more energy.
  3. (c) The tilt of the Earth's axis (about 23.5°) relative to the plane of its orbit around the Sun.
    B1 axial tilt.
