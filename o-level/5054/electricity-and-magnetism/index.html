Q1Circuits (series, parallel, components) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A 6.0 Ω resistor and a 12 Ω resistor are connected in series with a 9.0 V battery.Calculate the combined resistance of the two resistors. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the current in the circuit. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the potential difference across the 12 Ω resistor. [2]
  4. (d) State how the current in the 6.0 Ω resistor compares with the current in the 12 Ω resistor. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18 Ω
    6.0 + 12 = 18 Ω B1
  2. (b) 0.50 A
    I = V/R = 9.0 ÷ 18 M1; = 0.50 A A1
  3. (c) 6.0 V
    V = IR = 0.50 × 12 M1; = 6.0 V A1
  4. (d) The same (0.50 A)
    B1 current is the same everywhere in a series circuit

Q3Circuits (series, parallel, components) · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) The same 6.0 Ω and 12 Ω resistors are now connected in parallel across a 6.0 V supply.Calculate the combined resistance. [2]
  2. (b) Calculate the current from the supply. [1]
  3. (c) Calculate the current in the 6.0 Ω resistor. [1]
  4. (d) Give two reasons why the lamps in a house are connected in parallel rather than in series. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4.0 Ω
    1/R = 1/6.0 + 1/12 M1; R = 4.0 Ω A1
  2. (b) 1.5 A
    6.0 ÷ 4.0 = 1.5 A B1
  3. (c) 1.0 A
    6.0 ÷ 6.0 = 1.0 A B1
  4. (d) Each lamp gets the full mains voltage; each can be switched on and off independently; if one fails the others stay on
    1 mark each for any two

  1. (a) A student sets up a circuit containing a 4.5 V battery, a switch, a filament lamp and a diode, all in series. The lamp does not light when the switch is closed. The diode is in good working order.Suggest why the lamp does not light, and state what change would make it light. [2]
  2. (b) The student wants to measure the current in the lamp and the potential difference across it. Describe how the ammeter and voltmeter should be connected. [2]
  3. (c) State one use of a diode in electrical circuits. [1]
Show answer

    B1 ammeter in series; B1 voltmeter in parallel with the lamp
  3. (c) e.g. converting a.c. to d.c. (rectification) / protecting a circuit from a battery connected the wrong way
    B1
