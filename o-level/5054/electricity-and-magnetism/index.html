Q1Circuits (series, parallel, components) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) A 6.0 Ω resistor and a 12 Ω resistor are connected in series with a 9.0 V battery.Calculate the combined resistance of the two resistors. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the current in the circuit. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the potential difference across the 12 Ω resistor. [2]
  4. (d) State how the current in the 6.0 Ω resistor compares with the current in the 12 Ω resistor. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 18 Ω
    6.0 + 12 = 18 Ω B1
  2. (b) 0.50 A
    I = V/R = 9.0 ÷ 18 M1; = 0.50 A A1
  3. (c) 6.0 V
    V = IR = 0.50 × 12 M1; = 6.0 V A1
  4. (d) The same (0.50 A)
    B1 current is the same everywhere in a series circuit

Q3Circuits (series, parallel, components) · Core · Paper 1 style · 1 mark
  1. (a) Four identical 20 Ω resistors are connected in parallel. What is their combined resistance? [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) A 5.0 Ω
    For n identical resistors in parallel, R = 20 ÷ 4 = 5.0 Ω. The combined resistance of a parallel set is always less than the smallest single resistor, so 80 Ω (the series value) is wrong.
Q4Circuits (series, parallel, components) · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A string of decorative lights for a wedding in Lahore has 20 identical lamps connected in series to the 230 V mains. Calculate the potential difference across each lamp. [2]
  2. (b) One lamp filament breaks. State and explain what happens to the other 19 lamps. [2]
  3. (c) State one advantage of connecting the lamps in parallel instead. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 11.5 V
    M1 p.d. shared equally: 230 ÷ 20; A1 11.5 V
  2. (b) They all go out, because the circuit is broken and there is no current in any lamp.
    B1 all lamps go out; B1 series circuit has only one path, so a break stops the current everywhere
  3. (c) If one lamp fails the others stay lit (each lamp also gets the full supply p.d.).
    B1 either point
Q5Circuits (series, parallel, components) · Core · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A 12 V battery is connected in series with three resistors of 2.0 Ω, 4.0 Ω and 6.0 Ω.

  1. (a) Calculate the total resistance of the circuit. [1]
  2. (b) Calculate the current in the circuit. [2]
  3. (c) Calculate the potential difference across each resistor. [2]
  4. (d) Show that your answers to (c) are consistent with the e.m.f. of the battery. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 12 Ω
    B1 2 + 4 + 6 = 12 Ω
  2. (b) 1.0 A
    M1 I = V ÷ R = 12 ÷ 12; A1 1.0 A
  3. (c) 2.0 V, 4.0 V and 6.0 V
    M1 V = IR for each resistor; A1 all three correct
  4. (d) 2.0 + 4.0 + 6.0 = 12 V, equal to the e.m.f.
    B1 sum of p.d.s in a series circuit equals the supply e.m.f.

Q105Electrical safety · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A student designs a table lamp with a metal base. She connects it to the mains using two-core flex (live and neutral only) and a plug with a 13 A fuse.

  1. (a) Evaluate this design from the point of view of safety. [3]
  2. (b) Suggest two changes that would make the lamp safe. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) The metal base could become live if the live wire touches it; with two-core flex there is no earth wire, so a fault would not blow the fuse and the user could get a shock.
    B1 metal base can become live; B1 no earth path; B1 fuse would not blow / shock risk
  2. (b) Use three-core flex and connect the earth wire to the metal base; or make the base from plastic (double insulation); fit a low-rated fuse (e.g. 3 A) in the plug. (any two)
    1 mark each for any two
