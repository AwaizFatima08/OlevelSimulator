Q1Combined events · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

A fair spinner is numbered 1, 2, 3, 4. A fair six-sided die is numbered 1 to 6. The spinner is spun and the die is rolled. The score is the spinner number multiplied by the die number.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the score is 12. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the score is odd. [2]
  3. (c) Find the probability that the score is greater than 15. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1/8
    Pairs (2, 6), (3, 4), (4, 3) M1; 3/24 = 1/8 A1
  2. (b) 1/4
    Both odd: 2/4 × 3/6 M1; 1/4 A1
  3. (c) 1/6
    16, 18, 20, 24: 4 outcomes M1; 4/24 = 1/6 A1

  1. (a) Find the probability that the total is 5. [2]
  2. (b) Find the probability that the total is 7 or more. [1]
  3. (c) Find the probability that both spinners show the same number. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 1/4
    16 outcomes; (1,4),(2,3),(3,2),(4,1) M1 A1
  2. (b) 3/16
    (3,4),(4,3),(4,4) B1
  3. (c) 1/4
    B1

Q15Combined events · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A bag contains n counters. 4 of them are red. Two counters are taken at random without replacement. The probability that both are red is 1/11.

  1. (a) Show that n² − n − 132 = 0. [3]
  2. (b) Solve the equation to find n. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 4/n × 3/(n − 1) = 1/11 gives n² − n = 132
    4/n × 3/(n − 1) M1; = 1/11 M1; 12 × 11 = n(n − 1) leading to n² − n − 132 = 0 A1
  2. (b) n = 12
    (n − 12)(n + 11) = 0 M1; n = 12 (n > 0) A1

Q19Relative & expected frequency · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks

A spinner has four colours. It is spun 200 times.Red: 46, Blue: 58, Green: 64, Yellow: 32

  1. (a) Find the relative frequency of green. [1]
  2. (b) The spinner is spun 500 more times. Estimate the number of times it lands on yellow. [2]
  3. (c) Is the spinner fair? Give a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.32
    64/200 B1
  2. (b) 80
    32/200 = 0.16 M1; 0.16 × 500 = 80 A1
  3. (c) Probably not: a fair spinner would give about 50 of each colour; yellow 32 and green 64 are far from 50.
    B1

Q21Relative & expected frequency · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A biased six-sided die has these probabilities.Score 1: 0.15, 2: 0.2, 3: x, 4: 0.1, 5: 0.25, 6: 2x

  1. (a) Find x. [2]
  2. (b) The die is rolled 150 times. Find the expected number of sixes. [2]
  3. (c) Find the probability of rolling an even number. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 0.1
    0.15 + 0.2 + x + 0.1 + 0.25 + 2x = 1 M1; 3x = 0.3, x = 0.1 A1
  2. (b) 30
    P(6) = 0.2 M1; 0.2 × 150 = 30 A1
  3. (c) 0.5
    0.2 + 0.1 + 0.2 B1
Q22Relative & expected frequency · Standard · Paper 2 style · calculator · 4 marks

  1. (a) Find the relative frequency of a motorbike. [1]
  2. (b) On another day, 1800 vehicles pass the junction. Estimate the number of motorbikes. [2]
  3. (c) Estimate the probability that the next vehicle is not a car. [1]

  1. (a) 87/250 (= 0.348)
    87 ÷ 250 B1
  2. (b) 626.4, so about 626
    0.348 × 1800 M1 A1
  3. (c) 66/125 (= 0.528)
    B1
Q23Relative & expected frequency · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

Bilal throws a drawing pin. In the first 40 throws it lands point up 14 times. In the next 160 throws it lands point up 66 times.

  1. (a) Find the relative frequency of 'point up' after the first 40 throws and after all 200 throws. [2]
  2. (b) Which value is the better estimate of the probability? Give a reason. [1]
  3. (c) The pin is thrown 750 times. Estimate how many times it lands point up. [1]
  4. (d) A bag contains 30 counters, some red. A counter is taken and replaced many times; the relative frequency of red settles at 0.4. Estimate the number of red counters. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.35 and 0.4
    14/40 B1; (14 + 66)/200 B1
  2. (b) 0.4, because it is based on more throws.
    B1
  3. (c) 300
    0.4 × 750 B1
  4. (d) 12
    0.4 × 30 M1; 12 A1

    B1

    187 ÷ 350 M1 A1
  4. (d) 534
    B1

Q27Single events · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A bag contains only red, white and black counters. A counter is taken at random. P(red) = 0.25 and P(white) = 0.4.

  1. (a) Find the probability that the counter is black. [1]
  2. (b) There are 14 black counters. Find the total number of counters and the number of red counters. [2]
  3. (c) Some more white counters are added so that the probability of white becomes 1/2. How many are added? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 0.35
    1 − 0.25 − 0.4 B1
  2. (b) 40 counters; 10 red
    14 ÷ 0.35 = 40 M1; 0.25 × 40 = 10 A1
  3. (c) 8
    (16 + n)/(40 + n) = 1/2 M1; n = 8 A1

Q30Single events · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A bag contains red, blue and white marbles only. The probability of taking a red marble is 0.15. There are 9 red marbles, and there are twice as many blue marbles as red marbles.

  1. (a) Find the total number of marbles and the probability of taking a white marble. [3]
  2. (b) Some white marbles are removed. The probability of taking a white marble is now ½. How many white marbles were removed? [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 60 marbles; P(white) = 11/20
    9 ÷ 0.15 = 60 M1; blue 18, white 60 − 27 = 33 M1; 33/60 = 11/20 A1
  2. (b) 6
    (33 − k)/(60 − k) = ½ M1; k = 6 A1
