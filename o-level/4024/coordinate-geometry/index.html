Q2Perpendicular & parallel lines · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the equation of the line through (3, −2) that is parallel to y = 5 − x. [2]
  2. (b) Find the equation of the line through (3, −2) that is perpendicular to y = 5 − x. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = −x + 1
    Parallel lines have the same gradient, −1. y = −x + c with (3, −2): −2 = −3 + c, so c = 1 (M1 A1).
  2. (b) y = x − 5
    Perpendicular gradient = −1 ÷ (−1) = 1 (M1). y = x + c with (3, −2): −2 = 3 + c, so c = −5 (M1 A1).

Q7Perpendicular & parallel lines · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks
  1. (a) O is the origin, P is (3, 4) and Q is (−4, 3). Show that angle POQ = 90°. [2]
  2. (b) The point R is such that OPRQ is a rectangle. Find the coordinates of R. [2]
  3. (c) Find the area of the rectangle OPRQ. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Gradient of OP = 4/3 and gradient of OQ = −3/4. Their product is (4/3) × (−3/4) = −1, so OP ⊥ OQ and angle POQ = 90°.
    Gradient OP = 4/3 and gradient OQ = −3/4 (M1). Product = −1, so the lines are perpendicular (A1).
  2. (b) R = (−1, 7)
    In a rectangle, R = P + Q (vector addition from O): (3 + (−4), 4 + 3) = (−1, 7) (M1 A1).
  3. (c) 25
    OP = √(3² + 4²) = 5 and OQ = √((−4)² + 3²) = 5, so area = 5 × 5 = 25 (M1 A1).
