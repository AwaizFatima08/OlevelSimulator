  2. (b) Find the equation of the line parallel to y = 3x − 2 that passes through (1, 7). [2]
  3. (c) Write down the gradient of a line perpendicular to y = 3x − 2. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3
    B1.
  2. (b) y = 3x + 4
    7 = 3 + c M1; y = 3x + 4 A1.
  3. (c) −1/3
    B1.
Q2Perpendicular & parallel lines · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks

Four lines have these equations.L₁: y = 4x + 1L₂: 2y = 8x − 3L₃: y = −¼x + 5L₄: 4y = x + 2

  1. (a) Which two lines are parallel? Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Which line is perpendicular to L₁? Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) L₁ and L₂: both have gradient 4
    L₂ is y = 4x − 1.5 M1; A1
  2. (b) L₃: its gradient is −¼ and 4 × (−¼) = −1
    M1 A1

Q8Perpendicular & parallel lines · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks

A is (1, 1), B is (5, 3) and C is (3, 7).

  1. (a) Find the gradients of AB and BC, and explain what they show. [3]
  2. (b) Find the area of triangle ABC. [2]
  3. (c) A circle passes through A, B and C. Find the coordinates of its centre. Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) AB: ½, BC: −2. ½ × (−2) = −1, so AB is perpendicular to BC (angle ABC = 90°).
    B1 B1; product −1 B1
  2. (b) 10 units²
    AB = BC = √20 M1; ½ × √20 × √20 A1
  3. (c) (2, 4)
    AC is a diameter (angle in a semicircle) B1; midpoint of AC B1
