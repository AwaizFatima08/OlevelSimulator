Q1Circle theorems · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks

A, B and C are points on a circle, centre O. B lies on the major arc AC. Angle AOC = 136°.

  1. (a) Find angle ABC. Give a reason for your answer. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle OAC. Give a reason for your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 68°
    136 ÷ 2 = 68 M1; reason: angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference A1.
  2. (b) 22°
    OA = OC (radii) so triangle OAC is isosceles M1; (180 − 136) ÷ 2 = 22 A1.
Q2Circle theorems · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A, C, B and D lie on a circle in that order. AB is a diameter. Angle CAB = 27°.

  1. (a) Find angle ACB. Give a reason. [1]
  2. (b) Find angle ABC. [1]
  3. (c) Find angle ADC. Give a reason. [2]
  4. (d) Find angle CDB. Give a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 90°
    Angle in a semicircle B1
  2. (b) 63°
    180 − 90 − 27 B1
  3. (c) 63°
    Equals angle ABC B1; angles in the same segment (both subtended by AC from the same arc) B1
  4. (d) 27°
    Equals angle CAB, angles in the same segment B1
Q3Circle theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

P, Q, R and S lie on a circle in that order. PR is a diameter. Angle QPR = 31° and angle PRS = 47°.

  1. (a) Find angle PQR. Give a reason. [1]
  2. (b) Find angle QRP. [1]
  3. (c) Find angle QSR. Give a reason. [2]
  4. (d) Find angle QPS. Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 90°
    Angle in a semicircle B1.
  2. (b) 59°
    180 − 90 − 31 = 59 B1.
  3. (c) 31°
    Angle QSR = angle QPR M1; angles in the same segment A1.
  4. (d) 74°
    Angle QRS = 59 + 47 = 106 M1; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°, 180 − 106 = 74 A1.
Q4Circle theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A, B, C and D lie on a circle in that order, so ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle ABC = 112°. The line TC is the tangent to the circle at C, with T on the opposite side of the line CD from A. Angle TCD = 35°.

  1. (a) Find angle ADC. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle CAD. Give a reason. [2]
  3. (c) Find angle ACD. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 68°
    180 − 112 M1; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180° A1
  2. (b) 35°
    Angle between tangent and chord equals angle in the alternate segment B2
  3. (c) 77°
    180 − 68 − 35 B1
Q5Circle theorems · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks

A, B and C lie on a circle, centre O. C lies on the major arc AB. TA and TB are tangents to the circle at A and B. Angle TAB = 58°.

  1. (a) Find angle TBA. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle ATB. [1]
  3. (c) Find angle AOB. Give a reason. [2]
  4. (d) Find angle ACB. Give a reason. [1]
  5. (e) D is a point on the minor arc AB. Find angle ADB. Give a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 58°
    TA = TB (tangents from an external point are equal) M1; triangle TAB isosceles so angle TBA = 58 A1.
  2. (b) 64°
    180 − 58 − 58 = 64 B1.
  3. (c) 116°
    Angles OAT = OBT = 90° (tangent ⟂ radius) M1; 360 − 90 − 90 − 64 = 116 A1.
  4. (d) 58°
    Alternate segment theorem (angle TAB = angle ACB), or half of angle AOB B1.
  5. (e) 122°
    180 − 58 = 122; opposite angles of cyclic quadrilateral ACBD B1.
Q6Circle theorems · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

A, B and C lie on a circle, centre O. O lies inside triangle ABC. Angle OAB = x° and angle OCB = 2x°.

  1. (a) Explain why angle OBA = x° and angle OBC = 2x°. [1]
  2. (b) Angle AOC (the angle at O facing the minor arc AC) is 132°. Find x. Give a reason. [3]
  3. (c) Find angle OAC. [1]
  4. (d) D is a point on the minor arc AC. Find angle ADC. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) OA = OB = OC (radii), so triangles OAB and OCB are isosceles and their base angles are equal.
    B1
  2. (b) x = 22
    Angle ABC = 3x B1; angle at centre is twice angle at circumference: 6x = 132 M1; x = 22 A1
  3. (c) 24°
    (180 − 132) ÷ 2, isosceles triangle OAC B1
  4. (d) 114°
    ABCD cyclic: 180 − 66 B1

Q11Angles & polygons · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks
  1. (a) The interior angle of a regular polygon is 4 times its exterior angle. Find the number of sides. [3]
  2. (b) Regular polygon P has n sides and regular polygon Q has (n + 3) sides. Each exterior angle of P is 6° larger than each exterior angle of Q. Show that n(n + 3) = 180 and hence find n. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) 10
    e + 4e = 180 M1; e = 36 A1; 360 ÷ 36 = 10 A1.
  2. (b) n = 12
    360/n − 360/(n + 3) = 6 M1; 360(n + 3) − 360n = 6n(n + 3) ⇒ 1080 = 6n(n + 3) ⇒ n(n + 3) = 180 A1; n² + 3n − 180 = 0, (n + 15)(n − 12) = 0 M1; n = 12 A1.
Q12Angles & polygons · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

The exterior angles of a hexagon are x°, (x + 10)°, (x + 20)°, (x + 30)°, (x + 40)° and (x + 50)°.

  1. (a) Find x. [2]
  2. (b) Find the largest interior angle of the hexagon. [1]
  3. (c) Show that no regular polygon has an interior angle of 100°. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 35
    6x + 150 = 360 M1; x = 35 A1
  2. (b) 145°
    Smallest exterior angle 35°, 180 − 35 B1
  3. (c) The exterior angle would be 80°, and 360 ÷ 80 = 4.5 is not a whole number.
    Exterior 80° M1; 4.5 not an integer, so impossible A1
Q13Congruence & similarity · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

  2. (b) Triangles PQR and XYZ are similar, with P corresponding to X, Q to Y and R to Z. PQ = 6 cm, QR = 8 cm, PR = 7 cm and XY = 9 cm. Find YZ and XZ. [3]

  2. (b) YZ = 12 cm, XZ = 10.5 cm
    Scale factor 9 ÷ 6 = 1.5 M1; YZ = 12 A1; XZ = 10.5 A1.

    B1
Q17Congruence & similarity · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks

In triangle ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC so that DE is parallel to BC. AD = 4 cm, DB = 6 cm and DE = 5 cm.

  1. (a) Explain why triangle ADE is similar to triangle ABC. [2]
  2. (b) Find BC. [2]
  3. (c) The area of triangle ADE is 12 cm². Find the area of the trapezium DBCE. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Angle A common; angle ADE = angle ABC (corresponding angles, DE ∥ BC), so the triangles are similar (AA).
    Angle A common B1; corresponding angles equal with reason B1.
  2. (b) 12.5 cm
    Scale factor AB/AD = 10/4 = 2.5 M1; BC = 5 × 2.5 = 12.5 A1.
  3. (c) 63 cm²
    Area factor 2.5² = 6.25 M1; area ABC = 75 A1; 75 − 12 = 63 A1.
Q18Congruence & similarity · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 8 marks

ABCD is a trapezium with AB parallel to DC. The diagonals AC and BD meet at X. AB = 6 cm, DC = 15 cm and AX = 4 cm.

  1. (a) Explain why triangles ABX and CDX are similar. [2]

  4. (d) Find the area of triangle ADX and hence the area of the trapezium ABCD. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Angle AXB = angle CXD (vertically opposite); angle BAX = angle DCX (alternate angles, AB ∥ DC). So all angles are equal.
    B1 B1
  2. (b) 10 cm
    Scale factor 15/6 = 2.5 M1; 4 × 2.5 A1
  3. (c) 50 cm²
    Area factor 2.5² = 6.25 M1 A1
  4. (d) 20 cm²; 98 cm²
    Same height from A, DX = 2.5 BX: 20 M1; ABCD = 8 + 50 + 20 + 20 A1

Q20Scale drawing & bearings · Standard · Paper 2 style · calculator · 7 marks

Q22Scale drawing & bearings · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 8 marks

A lighthouse L is 8 km from a harbour H on a bearing of 120°. A boat B is 5 km from H on a bearing of 240°.

  1. (a) Show that angle LHB = 120°. [1]
  2. (b) Find the distance LB. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
  3. (c) Find the bearing of B from L. Give your answer correct to the nearest degree. [3]
  4. (d) A map has a scale of 1 : 200 000. Find the length of LB on the map, in centimetres, correct to 1 decimal place. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 240 − 120 = 120
    240 − 120 = 120 B1.
  2. (b) 11.4 km
    LB² = 8² + 5² − 2 × 8 × 5 × cos 120° M1; = 129 A1; LB = 11.4 A1.
  3. (c) 278°
    sin HLB / 5 = sin 120° / 11.36 M1; angle HLB = 22.4° A1; bearing of H from L is 300°, 300 − 22.4 = 277.6 ≈ 278° A1.
  4. (d) 5.7 cm
    1 135 800 ÷ 200 000 = 5.68 ≈ 5.7 B1.

  2. (b) Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB. Give your answer in the form y = mx + c. [3]
  3. (c) A well W is to be dug on the y-axis so that it is the same distance from A and from B. Find the coordinates of W. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (4, 4)
    B1.
  2. (b) y = −3/2 x + 10
    Gradient AB = 4/6 = 2/3 B1; perpendicular gradient −3/2 M1; y − 4 = −3/2(x − 4) ⇒ y = −3/2 x + 10 A1.
  3. (c) (0, 10)
    Put x = 0 in the bisector B1.

  2. (b) Describe the locus of points in the field that are the same distance from AB as from AD. [1]

  2. (b) Show that the perpendicular bisector of AC has equation y = −1.5x + 13. [2]
  3. (c) A tree T is planted so that it is equidistant from AB and AD and also equidistant from A and C. Find the coordinates of T. [2]
  4. (d) Find the distance AT, correct to 3 significant figures. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) The bisector of angle DAB; y = x
    Angle bisector of angle A B1; y = x B1.
  2. (b) y = −1.5x + 13
    Midpoint of AC (6, 4) and gradient of AC 2/3 M1; perpendicular gradient −3/2, y − 4 = −1.5(x − 6) A1.

  4. (d) 7.35 m

  2. (b) Find the size of one interior angle of this polygon. [2]
  3. (c) A quadrilateral has exactly 2 lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 2. Name two quadrilaterals that fit this description. [2]

Q29Symmetry · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

A circle has centre O and radius 13 cm. AB is a chord of length 24 cm.

  1. (a) Use the symmetry of a circle to find the perpendicular distance from O to AB. [2]
  2. (b) CD is another chord, parallel to AB, with length 10 cm. Find the two possible distances between AB and CD. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 5 cm
    Perpendicular from centre bisects chord, half-chord 12 M1; √(13² − 12²) = 5 A1.
  2. (b) 17 cm or 7 cm
    Distance O to CD = √(13² − 5²) = 12 M1 A1; 12 + 5 = 17 or 12 − 5 = 7 A1.

Q33Properties of shapes · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 3 marks

The diagonals of a rhombus are 10 cm and 24 cm long.

  1. (a) Find the length of a side of the rhombus. [2]
  2. (b) Find the area of the rhombus. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 13 cm
    Diagonals bisect at right angles, half-diagonals 5 and 12 M1; √(25 + 144) = 13 A1.

Q34Properties of shapes · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 5 marks

ABCD is a kite with AB = AD and CB = CD. Angle BAD = 64° and angle BCD = 118°.

  1. (a) Find angle ABC. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle BAC. Give a reason. [1]
  3. (c) The diagonals AC and BD meet at M. Write down angle AMB and hence find angle ABD. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 89°
    Angle ABC = angle ADC (kite symmetry) M1; (360 − 64 − 118) ÷ 2 = 89 A1.
  2. (b) 32°
    The diagonal AC bisects angle BAD (line of symmetry) B1.
  3. (c) Angle AMB = 90°; angle ABD = 58°
    Diagonals of a kite cross at right angles B1; 180 − 90 − 32 = 58 B1.
Q35Properties of shapes · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks

ABCD is a parallelogram with AB = 9 cm, AD = 6 cm and angle DAB = 50°.

  1. (a) Find angle ABC. Give a reason. [1]
  2. (b) Find the area of the parallelogram, correct to 3 significant figures. [2]
  3. (c) Find the length of the longer diagonal AC, correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 130°
    Co-interior angles of a parallelogram add to 180° B1.
  2. (b) 41.4 cm²
    9 × 6 × sin 50° M1; = 41.4 A1.
  3. (c) 13.7 cm
    AC² = 9² + 6² − 2 × 9 × 6 × cos 130° M1; = 186.4 A1; AC = 13.7 A1.
