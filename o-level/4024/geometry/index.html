Q1Circle theorems · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks

A, B and C are points on a circle, centre O. B lies on the major arc AC. Angle AOC = 136°.

  1. (a) Find angle ABC. Give a reason for your answer. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle OAC. Give a reason for your answer. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 68°
    136 ÷ 2 = 68 M1; reason: angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference A1.
  2. (b) 22°
    OA = OC (radii) so triangle OAC is isosceles M1; (180 − 136) ÷ 2 = 22 A1.
Q2Circle theorems · Core · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 4 marks

A, B and C lie on a circle with centre O. Angle ACB = 35°. C lies on the major arc AB.

  1. (a) Find angle AOB. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle OAB. Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 70°
    The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference subtended by the same arc AB: 2 × 35° = 70°. M1 A1.
  2. (b) 55°
    Triangle OAB is isosceles (OA = OB, radii), so the base angles are equal: (180 − 70) ÷ 2 = 55°. M1 A1.
Q3Circle theorems · Core · Paper 2 style · calculator · 5 marks

A chord AB of length 16 cm is drawn in a circle with centre O and radius 10 cm. M is the midpoint of AB.

  1. (a) Find the distance OM. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle AOB, correct to 1 decimal place. [1]
  3. (c) A second chord CD is parallel to AB, has length 12 cm, and lies on the other side of O from AB. Find the distance between the two chords. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 6 cm
    AM = 8 cm (half of 16). OM² = 10² − 8² = 36, so OM = 6 cm. M1 A1.
  2. (b) 106.3°
    tan(AOM) = 8 ÷ 6, so AOM = 53.13°; AOB = 2 × 53.13° = 106.3°.
  3. (c) 14 cm
    The distance from O to CD is √(10² − 6²) = 8 cm. Distance between chords = 6 + 8 = 14 cm. M1 A1.

P, Q and R lie on a circle with centre O. Triangle OPQ is isosceles with OP = OQ and angle OPQ = 28°. R lies on the major arc PQ.

  1. (a) Find angle POQ. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle PRQ. Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 124°
    Triangle OPQ is isosceles (OP = OQ), so the base angles are 28° and angle POQ = 180 − 2 × 28 = 124°. M1 A1.
  2. (b) 62°
    The angle at the circumference (on the major arc) is half the angle at the centre subtended by the minor arc PQ: 124 ÷ 2 = 62°. M1 A1.

Q6Circle theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

  1. (a) Find angle PQR. Give a reason. [1]
  2. (b) Find angle QRP. [1]
  3. (c) Find angle QSR. Give a reason. [2]
  4. (d) Find angle QPS. Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 90°
    Angle in a semicircle B1.
  2. (b) 59°
    180 − 90 − 31 = 59 B1.
  3. (c) 31°
    Angle QSR = angle QPR M1; angles in the same segment A1.
  4. (d) 74°
    Angle QRS = 59 + 47 = 106 M1; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add to 180°, 180 − 106 = 74 A1.
Q7Circle theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

P, Q, R and S lie on a circle in that order. Angle PQR = 3x° and angle PSR = (x + 40)°.

  1. (a) Find the value of x. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle PQR and angle PSR. Give a reason. [2]
  3. (c) Explain why PR cannot be a diameter of the circle. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 35
    Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°: 3x + (x + 40) = 180, so 4x = 140. M1 A1.
  2. (b) PQR = 105°; PSR = 75°
    Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°. 3 × 35 = 105 and 35 + 40 = 75. M1 A1.
  3. (c) If PR were a diameter, angle PQR would be 90° (angle in a semicircle), but it is 105°.
    The angle in a semicircle is 90°; the two angles would both have to be 90°, but they are 105° and 75°. M1 A1.

A circle has centre O and radius 7 cm. T is a point outside the circle with OT = 25 cm. TP is a tangent to the circle at P.

  1. (a) Find the length TP. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle POT, correct to 1 decimal place. [2]
  3. (c) A second tangent from T touches the circle at Q. Find angle PTQ, correct to 1 decimal place. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 24 cm
    Angle OPT = 90° (tangent ⊥ radius), so TP² = 25² − 7² = 576 and TP = 24 cm. M1 A1.
  2. (b) 73.7°

Q9Circle theorems · Standard · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 6 marks

A, B, C and D lie on a circle with centre O. Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC and angle ABC = 62°. A lies on the major arc BC, and D lies on the minor arc BC.

  1. (a) Find angle BAC. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle BOC. Give a reason. [2]
  3. (c) Find angle BDC, where D lies on the minor arc BC. Give a reason. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 56°
    AB = AC, so angle ACB = angle ABC = 62°. Angle BAC = 180 − 62 − 62 = 56°. M1 A1.
  2. (b) 112°
    The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference on the same arc BC: 2 × 56° = 112°. M1 A1.
  3. (c) 124°
    ABDC is a cyclic quadrilateral (A and D are opposite), so angle BAC + angle BDC = 180°: 180 − 56 = 124°. M1 A1.

Q11Circle theorems · Challenge · Paper 1 style · no calculator · 7 marks

A, B and C lie on a circle, centre O. C lies on the major arc AB. TA and TB are tangents to the circle at A and B. Angle TAB = 58°.

  1. (a) Find angle TBA. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle ATB. [1]
  3. (c) Find angle AOB. Give a reason. [2]
  4. (d) Find angle ACB. Give a reason. [1]
  5. (e) D is a point on the minor arc AB. Find angle ADB. Give a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) 58°
    TA = TB (tangents from an external point are equal) M1; triangle TAB isosceles so angle TBA = 58 A1.
  2. (b) 64°
    180 − 58 − 58 = 64 B1.
  3. (c) 116°
    Angles OAT = OBT = 90° (tangent ⟂ radius) M1; 360 − 90 − 90 − 64 = 116 A1.
  4. (d) 58°
    Alternate segment theorem (angle TAB = angle ACB), or half of angle AOB B1.
  5. (e) 122°
    180 − 58 = 122; opposite angles of cyclic quadrilateral ACBD B1.
Q12Circle theorems · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 5 marks

A, B and C lie on a circle. TA is the tangent to the circle at A. Angle TAB = 52° and angle BAC = 61°. C lies on the arc of the circle on the opposite side of AB from T.

  1. (a) Find angle ACB. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle ABC. [1]
  3. (c) The radius of the circle is 6 cm. Find the length of the chord AB, correct to 2 decimal places. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 52°
    The angle between the tangent TA and the chord AB equals the angle in the alternate segment, angle ACB (alternate segment theorem). M1 A1.
  2. (b) 67°
    Angles in triangle ABC add up to 180°: 180 − 61 − 52 = 67.
  3. (c) 9.46 cm
    Angle AOB = 2 × 52° = 104° (angle at the centre). The perpendicular from O bisects AB, so half of AB = 6 sin 52° and AB = 12 sin 52° = 9.46 cm. M1 A1.
Q13Circle theorems · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks

A, B, C and D lie on a circle in that order, so ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle ABC = 80° and angle BAC = 40°.

  1. (a) Find angle ADC. Give a reason. [2]
  2. (b) Find angle BDC. Give a reason. [2]

Q14Circle theorems · Challenge · Paper 2 style · calculator · 6 marks

Two parallel chords PQ and RS of a circle with centre O and radius 17 cm have lengths 30 cm and 16 cm.

  1. (a) Find the distance from O to PQ. [2]
  2. (b) Find the distance from O to RS. [2]
  3. (c) Find the two possible distances between the chords PQ and RS. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 8 cm
    Half of PQ is 15 cm. Distance = √(17² − 15²) = √64 = 8 cm. M1 A1.
  2. (b) 15 cm
    Half of RS is 8 cm. Distance = √(17² − 8²) = √225 = 15 cm. M1 A1.
  3. (c) 7 cm or 23 cm
    If both chords are on the same side of O, the distance is 15 − 8 = 7 cm; if on opposite sides, 15 + 8 = 23 cm. M1 A1.
