Q5Graphs of a function and its inverse · Core · Paper 1 style · 6 marks

f(x) = 10 − 3x for x ∈ ℝ. The graph of y = f(x) passes through the points (4, −2) and (−1, 13).

  1. (a) Write down the coordinates of the points on the graph of y = f⁻¹(x) that correspond to (4, −2) and (−1, 13). [2]
  2. (b) Find f⁻¹(x), and hence find the value of x for which f⁻¹(x) = f(x). [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (−2, 4) and (13, −1)
    The graph of y = f⁻¹(x) is the reflection of y = f(x) in the line y = x, so the coordinates are swapped. M1 swap the coordinates; A1 (−2, 4) and (13, −1).
  2. (b) f⁻¹(x) = (10 − x)/3; x = 5/2
    y = 10 − 3x gives x = (10 − y)/3, so f⁻¹(x) = (10 − x)/3. Setting (10 − x)/3 = 10 − 3x gives 10 − x = 30 − 9x, so 8x = 20 and x = 5/2. M1 make x the subject; A1 f⁻¹(x); M1 equate and clear the fraction; A1 x = 5/2.
Q6Graphs of a function and its inverse · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

p(x) = x³ for all real x.

  1. (a) Explain why p has an inverse, and find p⁻¹(x). [2]
  2. (b) Write down the coordinates of all the points where the graphs of y = p(x) and y = p⁻¹(x) meet. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) p is strictly increasing for all x (or one-to-one), so it has an inverse. p(y) = x gives y = ∛x, so p⁻¹(x) = ∛x.
    B1 explain: x³ is one-to-one (strictly increasing) over all real x; B1 p⁻¹(x) = ∛x.
  2. (b) (−1, −1), (0, 0) and (1, 1)
    Setting x³ = ∛x gives x⁹ = x, so x(x⁸ − 1) = 0 and x = 0, 1 or −1 (real roots). The points are (−1, −1), (0, 0) and (1, 1). M1 set p(x) = p⁻¹(x), M1 solve x⁹ = x, A1 all three points.

Q11Graphs of a function and its inverse · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

q(x) = √(x − 2) + 1 for x ≥ 2.

  1. (a) State the range of q. [1]
  2. (b) Find q⁻¹(x) and state its domain. [3]
  3. (c) Explain why the graph of y = q⁻¹(x) does not cross the x-axis. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y ≥ 1
    √(x − 2) ≥ 0, so q(x) ≥ 1. B1 range y ≥ 1.
  2. (b) q⁻¹(x) = (x − 1)² + 2, with domain x ≥ 1
    Let y = √(x − 2) + 1, so (y − 1)² = x − 2 and x = (y − 1)² + 2. M1 rearrange, M1 square, A1 q⁻¹(x) = (x − 1)² + 2 with domain x ≥ 1 (the range of q).
  3. (c) For x ≥ 1, (x − 1)² ≥ 0, so q⁻¹(x) = (x − 1)² + 2 ≥ 2 > 0. The graph never reaches y = 0.
    M1 (x − 1)² ≥ 0 for all x in the domain; A1 q⁻¹(x) ≥ 2, so it never equals 0.
Q12Graphs of a function and its inverse · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

r(x) = 6 − x² for x ≥ 0.

  1. (a) Find r⁻¹(x) and state its domain. [3]
  2. (b) Find the exact coordinates of the point where the graph of y = r(x) meets the line y = x. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) r⁻¹(x) = √(6 − x), with domain x ≤ 6
    y = 6 − x² gives x = √(6 − y), taking the positive root since x ≥ 0. M1 rearrange, M1 positive root, A1 r⁻¹(x) = √(6 − x) with domain x ≤ 6.
  2. (b) (2, 2)
    6 − x² = x gives x² + x − 6 = 0, so (x + 3)(x − 2) = 0. With x ≥ 0, x = 2 and the point is (2, 2). M1 solve the quadratic, A1 (2, 2).

Q15Graphs of a function and its inverse · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 7 marks

s(x) = (x + 1)/(x − 1) for x ≠ 1.

  1. (a) Show that s(s(x)) = x, and hence state the inverse function s⁻¹(x). [3]
  2. (b) State the equations of the asymptotes of y = s(x). [2]
  3. (c) Write down the coordinates of the points where the graph of y = s(x) meets the axes. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) s(s(x)) = [(x + 1)/(x − 1) + 1]/[(x + 1)/(x − 1) − 1] = [(x + 1) + (x − 1)]/[(x + 1) − (x − 1)] = 2x/2 = x. So s⁻¹(x) = s(x).
    M1 substitute s(x) into s; M1 multiply top and bottom by (x − 1); A1 s(s(x)) = x, so s is its own inverse.
  2. (b) x = 1 and y = 1
    The denominator is zero at x = 1, and s(x) = (x + 1)/(x − 1) = 1 + 2/(x − 1) tends to 1 as x → ±∞. B1 x = 1, B1 y = 1.
  3. (c) (0, −1) and (−1, 0)
    s(0) = −1, so it meets the y-axis at (0, −1). s(x) = 0 gives x = −1, so it meets the x-axis at (−1, 0). M1 one intercept, A1 both.
