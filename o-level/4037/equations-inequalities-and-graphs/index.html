Q1Modulus and cubic graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the graph of y = |2x − 3| for −1 ≤ x ≤ 4, stating the coordinates of the points where the graph meets the axes. [3]
  2. (b) Hence find the values of x for which |2x − 3| = 2. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) V-shaped graph with vertex on the x-axis at (1.5, 0); meets the y-axis at (0, 3)
    B1 V shape; B1 (1.5, 0); B1 (0, 3)
  2. (b) x = 0.5, x = 2.5
    2x − 3 = ±2 M1; x = 0.5, 2.5 A1
Q2Modulus and cubic graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the graph of y = (x + 1)(x − 2)(x − 4), stating the coordinates of the points where the curve meets the axes. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) positive cubic shape (rising from bottom left to top right) crossing the x-axis at (−1, 0), (2, 0) and (4, 0); meets the y-axis at (0, 8)
    B1 correct cubic shape; B1 x-intercepts −1, 2, 4; B1 y-intercept 8
Q3Modulus and cubic graphs · Core · Paper 2 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the graph of y = (2 − x)(x + 3)², stating the coordinates of the points where the curve meets the axes. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) negative cubic shape (from top left to bottom right) touching the x-axis at (−3, 0) (a minimum there) and crossing it at (2, 0); meets the y-axis at (0, 18)
    B1 shape with negative x³ coefficient; B1 touches at −3, crosses at 2; B1 (0, 18)
Q4Modulus and cubic graphs · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the graph of y = |x + 4| − 3, stating the coordinates of its vertex and of the points where it meets the axes. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) V shape with vertex (−4, −3); meets the x-axis at (−7, 0) and (−1, 0) and the y-axis at (0, 1)
    B1 vertex; B1 x-intercepts; B1 y-intercept
Q5Modulus and cubic graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the graph of y = |(x − 1)(x − 5)|, stating the coordinates of the points where it meets the axes and the coordinates of its local maximum point. [4]
  2. (b) Find the set of values of k for which |(x − 1)(x − 5)| = k has exactly 4 solutions. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) W shape: meets the x-axis at (1, 0) and (5, 0) (cusps), meets the y-axis at (0, 5); the part between x = 1 and x = 5 is reflected, giving a maximum at (3, 4)
    B1 W shape with cusps on the x-axis; B1 (1, 0), (5, 0); B1 (0, 5); B1 (3, 4)
  2. (b) 0 < k < 4
    B1 for 4 as upper limit; B1 strict inequality 0 < k < 4
Q6Modulus and cubic graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The graph of a cubic y = f(x) crosses the x-axis at (−2, 0), touches the x-axis at (3, 0) and meets the y-axis at (0, 36). Find f(x) in the form a(x + p)(x + q)². [3]
  2. (b) Write down the set of values of x for which f(x) < 0. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) f(x) = 2(x + 2)(x − 3)²
    f(x) = a(x + 2)(x − 3)² M1; a × 2 × 9 = 36 M1; a = 2 A1
  2. (b) x < −2
    B1 (f(x) ≥ 0 at x = 3)
Q7Modulus and cubic graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the graph of y = |x³ − 4x| for −3 ≤ x ≤ 3, stating the coordinates of the points where it meets the x-axis. [3]
  2. (b) The local maximum points of the graph between x = −2 and x = 2 have y-coordinate 3.08, correct to 3 significant figures. Find the number of solutions of the equation |x³ − 4x| = 1. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) graph lies on or above the x-axis, meeting it (with cusps) at (−2, 0), (0, 0) and (2, 0), with two humps between −2 and 2
    B1 factorised x(x − 2)(x + 2); B1 shape with negative parts reflected; B1 intercepts
  2. (b) 6
    line y = 1 is below both humps (1 < 3.08), so it cuts each hump twice M1; it also cuts the rising branches for x < −2 and x > 2 once each; 6 solutions A1
Q8Modulus and cubic graphs · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) On the same axes, sketch the graphs of y = |3x + 6| and y = |x − 2|, stating where each graph meets the axes. [3]
  2. (b) Find the x-coordinates of the points where the graphs intersect. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = |3x + 6|: vertex (−2, 0), y-intercept (0, 6); y = |x − 2|: vertex (2, 0), y-intercept (0, 2)
    B1 each V shape; B1 all intercepts
  2. (b) x = −4, x = −1
    3x + 6 = x − 2 gives x = −4 M1 A1; 3x + 6 = −(x − 2) gives x = −1 A1
Q9Modulus and cubic graphs · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Show that x³ − 3x² − 9x + 27 = (x − 3)²(x + 3). [2]
  2. (b) Hence sketch y = x³ − 3x² − 9x + 27, stating the coordinates of the points where it meets the axes. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x² − 6x + 9)(x + 3) = x³ − 3x² − 9x + 27
    M1 expansion; A1
  2. (b) positive cubic crossing the x-axis at (−3, 0), touching it at (3, 0) (a minimum), y-intercept (0, 27)
    B1 shape; B1 (−3, 0) crossing and (3, 0) touching; B1 (0, 27)
Q10Modulus and cubic graphs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) The graph of y = |f(x)|, where f(x) is a cubic, touches the x-axis at x = −1, meets the x-axis at x = 4 and meets the y-axis at (0, 8). Find the two possible expressions for f(x). [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) f(x) = 2(x + 1)²(x − 4) or f(x) = −2(x + 1)²(x − 4)
    f(x) = k(x + 1)²(x − 4) M1; |k × 1 × (−4)| = 8 M1; k = ±2 A1; both expressions given A1
Q11Modulus and cubic graphs · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Sketch the graph of y = |x² − 2x − 8|, stating the coordinates of the points where it meets the axes and of its local maximum point. [4]
  2. (b) Write down the value of k for which |x² − 2x − 8| = k has exactly 3 solutions, and the set of values of k for which it has exactly 2 solutions. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) W shape: meets the x-axis at (−2, 0) and (4, 0), the y-axis at (0, 8); maximum of the reflected part at (1, 9)
    B1 shape; B1 (−2, 0), (4, 0); B1 (0, 8); B1 (1, 9)
  2. (b) k = 9; k > 9 or k = 0
    B1 k = 9; B1 k > 9 or k = 0
Q12Modulus and cubic graphs · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) On the same axes, sketch y = |2x − 5| and y = 3 − x for 0 ≤ x ≤ 4. Find the x-coordinates of the points where the graphs meet. [4]
  2. (b) Hence solve the inequality |2x − 5| < 3 − x. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 2 and x = 8/3
    both graphs sketched B1; 2x − 5 = 3 − x gives x = 8/3 M1 A1; −(2x − 5) = 3 − x gives x = 2 A1
  2. (b) 2 < x < 8/3
    B1 B1

Q17Modulus equations and inequalities · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Solve the inequality |x − 3| > |2x + 1|. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) −4 < x < 2/3
    square both sides: x² − 6x + 9 > 4x² + 4x + 1 M1; 3x² + 10x − 8 < 0 A1; (3x − 2)(x + 4) < 0 M1; −4 < x < 2/3 A1
Q18Modulus equations and inequalities · Standard · Paper 2 style · 3 marks

Show answer

    x² − 5 = 4 gives x = ±3 M1 A1; x² − 5 = −4 gives x = ±1 A1
Q19Modulus equations and inequalities · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation |x² − 3x| = 2x. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 0, 1, 5
    x² − 3x = 2x gives x = 0 or 5 M1 A1; x² − 3x = −2x gives x = 0 or 1 A1; all satisfy 2x ≥ 0, so x = 0, 1, 5 A1

Q21Modulus equations and inequalities · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Solve the inequality |2x − 1| ≤ |x + 4|. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) −1 ≤ x ≤ 5
    4x² − 4x + 1 ≤ x² + 8x + 16 M1; 3x² − 12x − 15 ≤ 0 A1; (x − 5)(x + 1) ≤ 0 M1; −1 ≤ x ≤ 5 A1
Q22Modulus equations and inequalities · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation |x² − 4x| = 3, giving any irrational answers in the form a ± √b. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 1, 3, 2 + √7, 2 − √7
    x² − 4x − 3 = 0 M1; x = 2 ± √7 A1; x² − 4x + 3 = 0 M1; x = 1, 3 A1
Q23Modulus equations and inequalities · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation |x − 4| = 2x + 1. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 1 only
    x − 4 = 2x + 1 gives x = −5, but then 2x + 1 = −9 < 0, so reject M1 A1; x − 4 = −(2x + 1) gives x = 1 (check |−3| = 3) A1
Q24Modulus equations and inequalities · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation |2ˣ − 5| = 3. [3]
  2. (b) Explain why the equation |2ˣ − 5| = 6 has only one solution, and find it correct to 3 significant figures. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 1, x = 3
    2ˣ − 5 = 3 gives 2ˣ = 8, x = 3 M1 A1; 2ˣ − 5 = −3 gives 2ˣ = 2, x = 1 A1
  2. (b) 2ˣ = −1 is impossible since 2ˣ > 0; x = log₂ 11 = 3.46
    2ˣ = 11 or 2ˣ = −1 M1; 2ˣ > 0 so reject −1 A1; x = lg 11/lg 2 = 3.46 A1

Q26Cubic inequalities · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Show that x = 1 is a root of x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 = 0 and factorise x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 completely. [3]
  2. (b) Hence solve x³ − 6x² + 11x − 6 ≤ 0. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x − 1)(x − 2)(x − 3)
    f(1) = 0 B1; quadratic factor x² − 5x + 6 M1; (x − 1)(x − 2)(x − 3) A1
  2. (b) x ≤ 1 or 2 ≤ x ≤ 3
    B1 critical values used; B1 correct set with ≤
Q27Cubic inequalities · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Show that f(x) = −x³ + 6x² − 3x − 10 can be written as (2 − x)(x + 1)(x − 5). [2]
  2. (b) Sketch y = f(x), stating the intercepts with the axes, and hence solve f(x) ≥ 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2 − x)(x² − 4x − 5) = −x³ + 6x² − 3x − 10
    M1 expansion; A1
  2. (b) negative cubic through (−1, 0), (2, 0), (5, 0) and (0, −10); x ≤ −1 or 2 ≤ x ≤ 5
    B1 shape; B1 intercepts; M1 reading regions above the axis; A1 x ≤ −1 or 2 ≤ x ≤ 5
Q28Equations reducible to quadratics · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation x⁴ − 13x² + 36 = 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = −3, −2, 2, 3
    let u = x²: u² − 13u + 36 = 0 M1; u = 4 or 9 A1; x = ±2, ±3 A1
Q29Equations reducible to quadratics · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation 2x − 7√x + 3 = 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 9, x = 1/4
    let u = √x: 2u² − 7u + 3 = 0 M1; (2u − 1)(u − 3) = 0 M1; u = 1/2 or 3 A1; x = 1/4 or 9 A1
Q30Equations reducible to quadratics · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Solve the equation x^(2/3) − 5x^(1/3) + 4 = 0. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) x = 1, x = 64
    let u = x^(1/3): u² − 5u + 4 = 0 M1; u = 1 or 4 A1; x = 1³ = 1 or 4³ = 64 M1 A1
