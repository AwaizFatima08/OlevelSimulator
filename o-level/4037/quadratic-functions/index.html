Q1Discriminant and nature of roots · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Find the values of k for which the equation x² + kx + 9 = 0 has equal roots. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) k = 6 or k = −6
    k² − 36 = 0 M1; k = ±6 A1
Q2Discriminant and nature of roots · Core · Paper 1 style · 2 marks
  1. (a) Show that the equation 2x² − 3x + 4 = 0 has no real roots. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown: discriminant = −23 < 0
    b² − 4ac = 9 − 32 M1; = −23 < 0 A1
Q3Discriminant and nature of roots · Core · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A ball is kicked upwards in a school playground in Quetta. Its height, h metres, after t seconds is h = 18t − 5t².

  1. (a) By using the discriminant, show that the ball does not reach a height of 20 m. [3]
  2. (b) Find the greatest height reached by the ball. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown: discriminant −76 < 0
    5t² − 18t + 20 = 0 M1; 324 − 400 = −76 M1; < 0 so no real t A1
  2. (b) 16.2 m
    18t − 5t² = −5(t − 1.8)² + 16.2 M1; 16.2 m A1
Q4Discriminant and nature of roots · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) State, with a reason, the nature of the roots of 4x² − 12x + 9 = 0. [1]
  2. (b) State, with a reason, the nature of the roots of 3x² + 5x − 1 = 0. [1]
  3. (c) State, with a reason, the nature of the roots of x² + x + 2 = 0. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) Equal (repeated) roots, since b² − 4ac = 0
    B1
  2. (b) Two distinct real roots, since b² − 4ac = 37 > 0
    B1
  3. (c) No real roots, since b² − 4ac = −7 < 0
    B1
Q5Discriminant and nature of roots · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the values of m for which the line y = mx + 1 is a tangent to the curve y = x² + 3x + 5. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) m = −1 or m = 7
    x² + (3 − m)x + 4 = 0 M1; (3 − m)² − 16 = 0 M1 A1; 3 − m = ±4 → m = −1, 7 A1
Q6Discriminant and nature of roots · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the set of values of c for which the line y = 2x + c does not meet the curve y = x² − 4x + 7. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) c < −2
    x² − 6x + 7 − c = 0 M1; 36 − 4(7 − c) < 0 M1 A1; c < −2 A1
Q7Discriminant and nature of roots · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Find the set of values of k for which the equation x² − (k − 2)x + 2k − 3 = 0 has two distinct real roots. Give the boundary values in exact form. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) k < 6 − 2√5 or k > 6 + 2√5
    (k − 2)² − 4(2k − 3) > 0 M1; k² − 12k + 16 > 0 A1; critical values k = (12 ± √80)/2 = 6 ± 2√5 M1 A1; outside the roots A1
Q8Discriminant and nature of roots · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Show that the equation x² + kx + k − 2 = 0 has two distinct real roots for all real values of k. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown: discriminant = (k − 2)² + 4 > 0
    k² − 4(k − 2) = k² − 4k + 8 M1; = (k − 2)² + 4 M1; > 0 for all k A1
Q9Discriminant and nature of roots · Standard · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) The curve y = 2x² + px + 8 touches the x-axis. Find the possible values of p and, for each value, the coordinates of the point where the curve touches the x-axis. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) p = 8, point (−2, 0); p = −8, point (2, 0)
    p² − 64 = 0 M1; p = ±8 A1; repeated root x = −p/4 M1; p = 8: (−2, 0) A1; p = −8: (2, 0) A1
Q10Discriminant and nature of roots · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Find the values of p for which (p − 1)x² + 4x + (p + 2) = 0 has equal roots, and find the root in each case. [6]
Show answer
  1. (a) p = 2, root x = −2; p = −3, root x = ½
    16 − 4(p − 1)(p + 2) = 0 M1; p² + p − 6 = 0 A1; p = 2 or −3 A1; p = 2: x² + 4x + 4 = 0 → x = −2 M1 A1; p = −3: −4x² + 4x − 1 = 0 → x = ½ A1
Q11Discriminant and nature of roots · Standard · Paper 2 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Show that the line y = 3x − 5 is a tangent to the curve y = x² − x − 1, and find the coordinates of the point of contact. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown; point of contact (2, 1)
    x² − 4x + 4 = 0 M1; discriminant 16 − 16 = 0 so tangent A1; (x − 2)² = 0 → x = 2 M1; y = 1 A1
Q12Discriminant and nature of roots · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Show that the equation kx² + (k + 3)x + 1 = 0, where k is a non-zero constant, has two distinct real roots for every value of k. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown: discriminant = (k + 1)² + 8 > 0
    b² − 4ac = (k + 3)² − 4k M1; = k² + 2k + 9 A1; = (k + 1)² + 8 M1; always positive, so two distinct real roots A1
Q13Discriminant and nature of roots · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Do not use a calculator in this question.Find the set of values of k for which the line y = kx − 3 meets the curve y = x² + 2x + k. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) k ≤ 4 − 2√6 or k ≥ 4 + 2√6
    x² + (2 − k)x + k + 3 = 0 M1; (2 − k)² − 4(k + 3) ≥ 0 M1; k² − 8k − 8 ≥ 0 A1; k = 4 ± 2√6 M1; k ≤ 4 − 2√6 or k ≥ 4 + 2√6 A1
Q14Discriminant and nature of roots · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) Find the set of values of k for which x² + 2kx + 3k + 4 > 0 for all real values of x. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) −1 < k < 4
    Need discriminant < 0: 4k² − 4(3k + 4) < 0 M1; k² − 3k − 4 < 0 A1; (k − 4)(k + 1) < 0 M1; −1 < k < 4 A1
Q15Discriminant and nature of roots · Challenge · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A farmer in Punjab uses 100 m of fencing to make the four sides of a rectangular pen. One side of the pen is x m long.

  1. (a) Show that the area of the pen is (50x − x²) m². [1]
  2. (b) Use the discriminant to show that the area cannot be 700 m². [3]
  3. (c) Find the greatest possible area. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) Shown
    Other side = 50 − x B1
  2. (b) Shown: discriminant −300 < 0
    x² − 50x + 700 = 0 M1; 2500 − 2800 = −300 M1; < 0, no real x A1
  3. (c) 625 m²
    50x − x² = 625 − (x − 25)² M1; 625 A1

Q18Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Core · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) A quadratic curve crosses the x-axis at x = −1 and x = 4 and crosses the y-axis at (0, −8). Find its equation in the form y = ax² + bx + c. [3]
  2. (b) Find the coordinates of the vertex of the curve. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 2x² − 6x − 8
    y = a(x + 1)(x − 4) M1; −8 = −4a → a = 2 A1; expand A1
  2. (b) (1.5, −12.5)
    x = (−1 + 4)/2 = 1.5 M1; y = 2(2.25) − 9 − 8 = −12.5 A1
Q19Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Express 5 + 4x − x² in the form p − (x − q)². [2]
  2. (b) Hence state the maximum value of f(x) = 5 + 4x − x² and the range of f for all real x. [2]
  3. (c) Hence solve 5 + 4x − x² = 0. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) 9 − (x − 2)²
    q = 2 B1; p = 9 B1
  2. (b) maximum 9 (at x = 2); range f(x) ≤ 9
    B1 B1
  3. (c) x = 5, x = −1
    (x − 2)² = 9 M1; x = 2 ± 3 A1
Q20Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Express 3x² − 12x + 7 in the form a(x − b)² + c. [3]
  2. (b) Do not use a calculator in this part.Hence solve 3x² − 12x + 7 = 0, giving your answers in the form p ± q√15, where p and q are rational. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) 3(x − 2)² − 5
    a = 3, b = 2, c = −5 B1 B1 B1
  2. (b) x = 2 ± (1/3)√15
    (x − 2)² = 5/3 M1; x − 2 = ±√(5/3) M1; √(5/3) = √15/3 so x = 2 ± √15/3 A1
Q21Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Standard · Paper 1 style · 6 marks
  1. (a) Express x² − 4x − 5 in the form (x − a)² − b. [2]
  2. (b) Describe the graph of y = x² − 4x − 5 by stating the coordinates of its vertex and of the points where it meets the axes. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x − 2)² − 9
    B1 B1
  2. (b) vertex (2, −9); x-axis (−1, 0) and (5, 0); y-axis (0, −5)
    vertex B1; (x − 2)² = 9 gives x = −1, 5 M1 A1; (0, −5) B1
Q22Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Standard · Paper 2 style · 6 marks

A company in Sialkot makes footballs. Its weekly profit, P thousand rupees, from selling x hundred footballs is P = −2x² + 48x − 160.

  1. (a) Write P in the form −2(x − a)² + b. [2]
  2. (b) State the maximum weekly profit and the number of footballs sold to achieve it. [2]
  3. (c) Find the values of x for which the company breaks even (P = 0). [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) P = −2(x − 12)² + 128
    a = 12 B1; b = 128 B1
  2. (b) 128 thousand rupees when 1200 footballs are sold
    B1 B1
  3. (c) x = 4 and x = 20 (400 and 2000 footballs)
    (x − 12)² = 64 M1; x = 4, 20 A1
Q23Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Standard · Paper 1 style · 4 marks
  1. (a) A quadratic curve has its vertex at (3, −2) and passes through the point (5, 6). Find its equation in the form y = ax² + bx + c. [4]
Show answer
  1. (a) y = 2x² − 12x + 16
    y = a(x − 3)² − 2 M1; 6 = 4a − 2 → a = 2 A1; expand 2(x² − 6x + 9) − 2 M1; y = 2x² − 12x + 16 A1
Q24Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Standard · Paper 2 style · 5 marks

A cricket ball is hit upwards. Its height, h metres, t seconds after being hit is h = 1.5 + 14.7t − 4.9t².

  1. (a) Write h in the form p − 4.9(t − q)². [3]
  2. (b) Hence state the greatest height of the ball, correct to 3 significant figures, and the time at which it occurs. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) h = 12.525 − 4.9(t − 1.5)²
    factor −4.9 from the t terms: −4.9(t² − 3t) M1; q = 1.5 A1; p = 1.5 + 4.9 × 2.25 = 12.525 A1
  2. (b) 12.5 m at t = 1.5 s
    B1 B1
Q25Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Express 4x² − 12x + 1 in the form (2x − a)² − b. [2]
  2. (b) Hence find the range of f(x) = 4x² − 12x + 1 for the domain 0 ≤ x ≤ 4. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) (2x − 3)² − 8
    a = 3 B1; b = 8 B1
  2. (b) −8 ≤ f(x) ≤ 17
    minimum −8 at x = 1.5 (inside the domain) B1; f(0) = 1, f(4) = 17 M1; range −8 ≤ f(x) ≤ 17 A1
Q26Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the values of a, b and c such that 7 − 6x − 3x² ≡ a − b(x + c)². [3]
  2. (b) Hence state the coordinates of the stationary point of y = 7 − 6x − 3x², and whether it is a maximum or a minimum. [2]
Show answer
  1. (a) a = 10, b = 3, c = 1
    b = 3 B1; c = 1 B1; a = 10 B1
  2. (b) (−1, 10), a maximum
    B1 B1
Q27Quadratic graphs and completing the square · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks

f(x) = x² + 2kx + 5, where k is a constant.

  1. (a) Express f(x) in the form (x + p)² + q, giving p and q in terms of k. [2]
  2. (b) Find the values of k for which the minimum value of f(x) is 1. [2]
  3. (c) When k = 3, state whether the curve y = f(x) meets the x-axis, giving a reason. [1]
Show answer
  1. (a) (x + k)² + 5 − k²
    p = k B1; q = 5 − k² B1
  2. (b) k = 2 or k = −2
    5 − k² = 1 M1; k = ±2 A1
  3. (c) Yes: the minimum value is 5 − 9 = −4 < 0, so the curve crosses the x-axis twice
    B1
Q28Quadratic inequalities · Core · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Solve the inequality x² − 5x − 14 < 0. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) −2 < x < 7
    (x − 7)(x + 2) M1; critical values −2, 7 A1; −2 < x < 7 A1
Q29Quadratic inequalities · Standard · Paper 1 style · 3 marks
  1. (a) Solve the inequality 2x² + x ≥ 15. [3]
Show answer
  1. (a) x ≤ −3 or x ≥ 5/2
    2x² + x − 15 ≥ 0 → (2x − 5)(x + 3) ≥ 0 M1; critical values −3, 5/2 A1; x ≤ −3 or x ≥ 5/2 A1
Q30Quadratic inequalities · Challenge · Paper 1 style · 5 marks
  1. (a) Find the set of values of x that satisfy both x² − 3x − 10 ≤ 0 and 3x² > 2x + 8. [5]
Show answer
  1. (a) −2 ≤ x < −4/3 or 2 < x ≤ 5
    (x − 5)(x + 2) ≤ 0 → −2 ≤ x ≤ 5 M1 A1; (3x + 4)(x − 2) > 0 → x < −4/3 or x > 2 M1 A1; combine: −2 ≤ x < −4/3 or 2 < x ≤ 5 A1
